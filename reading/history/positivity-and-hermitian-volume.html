Local positive currents become global geometric objects through their cohomology classes. On a compact Kähler manifold, the $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm c}$-lemma identifies closed currents in a fixed real $(1,1)$-class with quasi-plurisubharmonic potentials, up to a constant on each connected component. This is the bridge from the local theory to cohomology, positivity, and geometric loci. Nefness, bigness, and pseudo-effectivity describe different kinds of positivity; their geometric loci record where positive representatives can or cannot be made regular.

Hermitian linear algebra, Chern connections and elliptic estimates support the Hodge and vanishing theory in Demailly's book. The Frölicher spectral sequence compares Dolbeault and de Rham cohomology on general complex manifolds. Bott–Chern and Aeppli groups retain further information, while the Kähler identities impose stronger relations. The Bochner–Kodaira identity and its torsion terms distinguish the Hermitian calculation from its Kähler specialization.

The $L^2$ method of Hörmander, holomorphic division and compact vanishing theorems connect curvature to global sections and jets. Kodaira’s embedding theorem and its integral-curvature formulation then relate positivity to projectivity. Unique continuation is a separate analytic ingredient: Aronszajn's theorem is a classical source for scalar-principal second-order equations and coupled lower-order inequalities. Its hypotheses do not assert unrestricted unique continuation for every matrix-principal elliptic system.

Pliś's smooth elliptic counterexample provides a concrete obstruction. Its fourth-order two-variable construction can be prolonged to an ordinary second-order elliptic system with a nonzero smooth solution vanishing on an open set. This separates general matrix-principal ellipticity from the scalar-principal hypotheses used for harmonic forms.

The spectral proof of holomorphic Morse inequalities uses a different asymptotic from the transcendental subtraction of nef classes. Demailly's magnetic-field method counts low Dolbeault eigenvalues and gives weak and strong line-power cohomology inequalities with a fixed coefficient bundle on compact Hermitian manifolds. Its semipositive specialization yields the leading holomorphic-section growth without assuming a Kähler base.

Siu's work on semipositive line bundles over non-Kähler manifolds includes the one-point positivity variant in an added-in-proof announcement. Its geometric application joins section growth to Kähler modifications and coefficient-valued cohomological descent. The existence input for eliminating the meromorphic map is stated separately from the analytic and descent proofs.

Hopf quotients illustrate the topological restrictions imposed by the Kähler condition. The normal-coordinate curvature expansion and Wirtinger’s inequality connect the local Hermitian metric to curvature and complex-volume geometry in Demailly's exposition.

Volumes and restricted positivity turn these analytic descriptions into numerical invariants. Boucksom's work relates positive currents, divisorial decompositions, and volumes; later results compare non-Kähler loci, null loci, and restricted volumes. Such comparisons depend on the class and on the subvarieties being tested. Pome keeps the hypotheses of these statements visible rather than treating all positivity loci as interchangeable. The theory of non-pluripolar mass explains how singular potentials enter their analytic definitions.

Hermitian geometry brings a different cohomological structure. Bott–Chern and Aeppli classes retain information lost when one uses only de Rham cohomology, and their pairing is natural for positivity questions. Gauduchon metrics satisfy $\partial\bar\partial\omega^{n-1}=0$, a weaker condition than the Kähler condition $\mathrm d\omega=0$. Gauduchon's existence theorem makes this condition available within a conformal class. Lamari's positivity criterion uses dual testing against Gauduchon forms; see Lamari. These ideas organize Hermitian geometry, torsion, and Gauduchon duality.

The absence of closedness changes the meaning of a volume. In the Kähler setting, Stokes' theorem fixes the mass of bounded potentials in a class. In the Hermitian setting, upper mass bounds and positive lower mass bounds are separate properties of a background. Work of Guedj–Lu and subsequent developments studies these bounds and their role in equations and singularity volumes. The distinction between a bound for each individual potential and a uniform bound over a family is essential. It is also why a theorem for a closed big class cannot be transferred verbatim to an arbitrary positive Hermitian form.

The chapter on Gauduchon variational volume develops an additional variational viewpoint using Gauduchon data and duality. It should be read alongside, rather than identified with, the cohomological volume of a closed class or the mass attached to an ordinary singularity type. The historical strands meet in their use of positivity and nonlinear measures, but their admissible objects and normalizations differ.
