## POME-TESTFORM-ESCAPING-NET-001 — verified conditional deduction / low

**Statement and hypotheses.** Let `M` be any noncompact second-countable Hausdorff smooth real manifold, let `0 <= p <= dim M`, and equip `D^p(M)` with the locally convex test-form topology explicitly defined in §2.10.1. There exists a **net of nonzero smooth compactly supported p-forms** `u_alpha -> 0` in this topology such that for every compact `K subset M`, the support of `u_alpha` is **eventually disjoint from K**. Yet no tail of this net is a bounded set in `D^p(M)`. In contrast, §2.10.3 correctly proves that every convergent *sequence*, together with its limit, has a common compact support.

**Proof.** Choose an exhaustion `K_1 subset int K_2 subset ...` of `M`, and nonzero compactly supported smooth p-forms `b_j` supported in `M \ K_j` (each complement is nonempty by noncompactness). Let `N` be the collection of zero-neighborhoods in `D^p(M)`. Direct the pairs `(U,j)` by `(U',j') >= (U,j)` iff `U' subset U` and `j'>=j`. Intersections of zero-neighborhoods make this directed. Because every zero-neighborhood is absorbing, select a **nonzero** scalar `c(U,j)` with `u_(U,j) = c(U,j)b_j in U`. For each fixed zero-neighborhood `U_0`, all indices beyond `(U_0,1)` have `u_(U,j) in U subset U_0`; hence the net converges to zero. For each compact `K`, choose `J` with `K subset K_J`; at every index beyond `(U_0,J)`, `supp u_(U,j) subset M\K_j subset M\K`. Every tail nevertheless contains nonzero forms supported outside arbitrarily large exhaustion compacts; no single compact contains all its supports. By the *proved boundedness characterization* in Proposition 2.10.3 (lines 48–73), no tail is bounded. Thus neither eventual boundedness nor common compact support is a valid inference from convergence of arbitrary nets.

**Status.** Verified conditional deduction (conditional only on the standard, explicitly printed test-form topology), **not** a proof gap or false theorem. The printed passage explicitly refrains from claiming a common support for general convergent nets. **Optional correction:** add a post-§2.10.3 example illustrating why the sequence qualifier matters. **Dependents:** any use of arbitrary test-form nets in duality, current compactness, or distribution limits that incorrectly treats a convergent net as a bounded subset or gives it a common compact support.

## POME-CURRENT-MOVING-ORDER-001 — verified conditional deduction / low

**Statement.** On `M=R`, set `T_n = d^n delta_n/dx^n` as distributions and `A={0,T_1,T_2,...}`. Then `T_n -> 0` in the weak current/distribution topology, so `A` is weakly compact (any convergent sequence together with its limit is compact), but `A` has **no common compact support and no uniform global finite distribution order**. This gives a sharp limitation to attempts to strengthen Theorem 2.10.5 beyond its actual, correct assertion.

**Proof.** If `phi` is a compactly supported smooth test function, it vanishes near `n` for all sufficiently large `n`; thus `T_n(phi)=(-1)^n phi^(n)(n)=0` eventually. To rule out a common order `N`, fix `n>N`, choose `chi in C_c^infty((-1,1))` with `chi^(n)(0) != 0`, and put `phi_epsilon(x)=epsilon^n chi((x-n)/epsilon)`. For every `k<=N`, `||phi_epsilon^(k)||_infty = O(epsilon^(n-k)) ->0`, whereas `|T_n(phi_epsilon)|=|chi^(n)(0)|>0`. Therefore `T_n` has order at least `n` on any fixed neighborhood of its support, and no uniform order works for all `n`. On each **fixed compact K**, however, all but finitely many `T_n` vanish on tests supported in K, so a K-dependent bound of some finite order does exist, exactly as Theorem 2.10.5's Baire argument requires. Its product-compactness proof uses closure under nets/subnets and **does not** assume a global common order or support.

**Status.** Verified conditional deduction / low, **not** a printed-theorem error. **Optional correction:** a brief sharpness example after §2.10.5, without altering its valid weak-compactness statement. **Dependents:** applying its local Banach–Steinhaus order bounds to global or moving-support estimates.

## POME-FINEST-LCS-BOUNDED-SUPPORT-001 — verified conditional deduction / low

**Statement.** In `E=C^(I)`, the vector space with a fixed Hamel basis `(e_i)` and its **finest locally convex topology** of Lemma 2.23.1, a subset `B` is bounded **if and only if** all its vectors have support in one finite subset of `I` and their coordinates there are uniformly bounded. For infinite `I`, nevertheless, there is a net of nonzero finite-support vectors converging to zero that is not eventually contained in any finite-dimensional coordinate span.

**Proof.** Lemma 2.23.1 directly shows the seminorms `q_w(sum a_i e_i)=sum_i w_i |a_i|`, all positive weights `w_i`, generate the finest topology. A bounded set is bounded under each coordinate functional, so its individual coordinate values are bounded. If the union of its supports were infinite, choose distinct `i_n` and vectors `b_n in B` with nonzero coordinates `(b_n)_(i_n)`. Specify `w_(i_n)>= n/|(b_n)_(i_n)|` and any positive weights elsewhere. Then `q_w(b_n)>=n`, contradicting boundedness. Conversely finite common support plus uniformly bounded coordinates makes every `q_w` bounded. For the net, direct pairs `(U,J)`, where `U` is a zero-neighborhood and `J` finite in `I`, by reverse inclusion of neighborhoods and increasing inclusion of `J`. Pick `i notin J` and a nonzero scalar multiple of `e_i` lying in `U`. This net converges to zero but its support escapes each fixed finite coordinate subset along a tail; no tail is bounded. This explains why the **printed** proof of completeness of Lemma 2.23.1 works with arbitrary Cauchy nets and weighted sums, rather than an unjustified assertion of eventual finite-dimensional support.

**Status.** Verified conditional deduction / low, **not** a counterexample to completeness. **Optional correction:** a concise bornology/escaping-net remark next to Lemma 2.23.1. **Dependents:** direct-sum completion and topological quotient arguments, not an assertion that a convergent net itself is bounded.
