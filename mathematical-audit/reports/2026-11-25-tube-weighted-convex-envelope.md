Let omega be a nonempty connected open subset of R^n, D=conv(omega), and let phi:omega -> R be locally bounded above (not necessarily convex, measurable, or continuous). Set
\[
h(x)=\inf\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}t_j\phi(b_j): b_j\in\omega,\ t_j\ge0,\ \sum_jt_j=1,\ \sum_jt_j b_j=x\right\}.
\]
The infimum is over finite combinations; it is never +infinity on D. Let B_phi be the holomorphic functions on the original tube with |f(b+iy)| <= exp(phi(b)) for every b in omega and y in R^n. Conditional on the printed tube extension theorem, every f has a unique extension F to the convex tube and
\[
|F(x+iy)|\le \exp(h(x)).
\]
Here exp(-infinity)=0. Either h is identically -infinity on D and B_phi={0}, or h is finite and convex on D. In the finite case the bound is sharp at every x+iy, attained by a (possibly x-dependent) exponential of a complex affine function:
\[
\sup_{f\in B_\phi}|F(x+iy)|=\exp(h(x)).
\]
In particular the extension is an isometric bijection H-infinity(T_omega) -> H-infinity(T_D). This is a universal weighted bound, not an assertion that a particular f attains it.

### Proof of the bound

Fix any finite representation x=sum t_j b_j. By polygonal connectivity, choose smooth paths in omega from a common base a to all b_j. Reparametrize them flat at their ends. For each small delta>0 take a smooth real loop gamma_delta that traverses each path and its reverse in a total normalized circle time delta and otherwise dwells at the nodes b_j for positive durations mu_j(delta) with sum 1-delta and mu_j(delta)->t_j. For example mu_j=(1-delta)((1-delta)t_j+delta/k). All real boundary values lie on one fixed compact set K in omega, consisting of the path images. Local upper boundedness gives a finite C with phi<=C on K. No uniform bound on imaginary coordinates is required, since the original obstacle holds on every imaginary translate.

Take the Schwarz integral A_gamma of (9.39.1), so that the real boundary values equal gamma and its center is the real average m_delta. The Poisson kernel is a positive probability density, so the real part of the entire closed disc is in conv(K), a compact subset of D. To justify the assertion about convex hulls of means: the convex hull of a compact subset of R^n is compact by Caratheodory's theorem, or by elimination of affine dependence in convex representations to reduce to at most n+1 points. Riemann sums and closure then put every Poisson average in that hull. The boundary values themselves belong to K. Thus F(A_gamma(zeta)+iy) is holomorphic on the disc and continuous on its closure, and on the circle it agrees with f. The submean inequality for log absolute values of a holomorphic function yields
\[
\log|F(m_\delta+iy)|\le \sum_j\mu_j(\delta)\phi(b_j)+\delta C.
\]
This use of a constant C on the travel arcs does not integrate phi and hence requires no measurability of phi. At boundary zeros the logarithm may be -infinity; the inequality follows also by using log(|F|+epsilon), then sending epsilon to zero, or by the standard submean inequality for log|F|. Since m_delta tends to x, continuity of F (with the trivial zero case separated) implies log|F(x+iy)| <= sum t_j phi(b_j). Take the infimum over all finite representations. The argument uses no passage through the sup over imaginary translates.

### Sharpness and the degenerate case

Combining finite convex representations proves convexity of h in the extended sense, including when one infimum is -infinity (approximate that infimum by arbitrarily negative finite sums). If h(y)=-infinity for some y in D, then for any x in D choose z in D slightly beyond x along the ray from y, using openness. Express x as an interior convex combination of y and z to get h(x)=-infinity. Otherwise h is real-valued and convex throughout the open convex set D. A finite convex function is continuous on its open domain and has a supporting affine minorant at each interior point. For clarity, separate the boundary point (x,h(x)) from the open strict epigraph in R^{n+1}. The supporting functional has a nonnegative vertical coefficient, which must be positive since x is interior in the base and the functional is nonzero. Normalize this coefficient to obtain a real affine ell(b)=h(x)+p dot (b-x) with ell<=h on D. In particular ell<=phi on omega by the one-node representation. The entire function exp(h(x)+p dot (z-x)) belongs to B_phi and has modulus exp(h(x)) at x+iy. If h is identically -infinity the bound forces F=0. Constant obstacles (with scaling) give the H-infinity assertion and its exact norm equality, since restriction cannot increase the norm.
