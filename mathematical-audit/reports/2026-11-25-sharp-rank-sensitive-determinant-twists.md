### Exact trace-subtraction coefficient

Let T,E be positive-dimensional Hermitian spaces of dimensions n,r. Put A=tr_E C, where C is any Griffiths semipositive Hermitian form on T tensor E. For an integer m>=1 put d=min(n,r,m). Then d A tensor h - C is m-semipositive, and d is the least universal real coefficient with this property, even if C is required to be strictly Griffiths positive.

Indeed for a nonzero tensor u of rank k<=m, let F be its image under the map T* -> E. The printed proof of 10.34.2 gives C(u)<=k (tr_F C|_F tensor h_F)(u): the form D=(tr_F C|_F) tensor h_F-C|_F is Griffiths semipositive by completing a unit fiber vector to an orthonormal basis, and the phase-averaging first assertion of 10.34.2 applied to D yields k tr_F C|_F tensor h_F-C|_F >=0 in Nakano sense. The trace on F-perp is nonnegative on every base vector. Since k<=d, the claimed estimate follows. This also proves the sharper tensor-specific coefficient k, using just the image F in the trace. Applying the estimate to C-epsilon g tensor h gives the lower bound (dr-1)epsilon |u|^2 when C has Griffiths lower bound epsilon. No positivity beyond rank-one is required of C.

For sharpness choose orthonormal coordinates and set C_0(u)=|sum_{i=1}^d u_{ii}|^2. This is already Nakano semipositive. Its partial trace A_0 is the orthogonal projection onto the first d base coordinates, in quadratic-form notation. With u_0=sum_{i=1}^d t_i tensor e_i one has |u_0|^2=d, C_0(u_0)=d^2 and (A_0 tensor h)(u_0)=d. Thus any coefficient c<d fails. For C_epsilon=C_0+epsilon g tensor h, the same normalized test has value c-d+(cr-1)epsilon for c tr_E C_epsilon tensor h-C_epsilon. For sufficiently small positive epsilon this is negative. At c=d the test attains the displayed lower bound exactly. This is a distinct upper/trace-subtraction sharpness result, not the printed lower/trace-addition optimality example 10.34.5. In particular for m>=min(n,r) the optimal trace-subtraction bound is already Nakano; increasing m beyond that rank adds no tested tensors.

By negative transpose of the Chern curvature, the same constant d is a sufficient and pointwise optimal determinant exponent for m-semipositivity of E* tensor (det E)^d for a Griffiths-semipositive E of rank r over a base of dimension n. For strictly Griffiths-positive E it is strict except d=r=1, where the twist is flat. Optimality here means a universal pointwise statement for induced metrics, not an optimal global vanishing range. The algebraic examples can be realized as curvature at a point in a trivial holomorphic bundle on a small coordinate ball: prescribe the Hermitian metric's mixed quadratic Taylor coefficients as minus the desired curvature matrix, with constant term I and zero first jet, using the conventions of 10.37.3. It stays positive definite on a sufficiently small ball. A strictly Griffiths-positive value stays strictly positive in a possibly smaller ball by compactness of the unit rank-one tests. For the dual example choose the fiber transpose of C_epsilon as the curvature of E; this preserves strict Griffiths positivity by conjugation of the fiber vector.

### Optimal exponent for the subbundle twist

In 10.34.9, write s=rank S>0, a=rank Q>0 and b=min(n,s,m). For an m-semipositive E, S tensor (det Q)^b is m-semipositive, with pointwise lower bound (1+ba)epsilon under the same epsilon assumption on E. In fact, for any integer l>=b the lower bound is (1+la)epsilon. To see this, retain the exact curvature block formula of lines 188–203 with l in place of m. The first term R_E|_S has the required rank-m bound; tr_Q R_E|_Q>=a epsilon g by rank-one tests. The second fundamental form correction l tr_S C_B tensor h_S-C_B is nonnegative on tested tensors by the preceding rank-sensitive inequality, since C_B(u)=|sum_j B_j u_j|^2. The zero-rank cases retain the printed conventions.

The threshold b is optimal as a universal pointwise exponent, even when the ambient bundle is strictly Nakano positive. In a trivial bundle S_0 direct sum Q_0 of ranks s,a over a small ball in C^n, use the graph subbundle of the holomorphic linear map L(z):S_0 -> Q_0 given by L(z)v=(sum_{j=1}^b z_j v_j) q_1 for a fixed unit q_1. With the flat ambient metric, at the origin B_j(v)=v_j q_1 for j<=b and zero otherwise. Therefore C_B is exactly the preceding rank-one Gram form with d=b. On the tensor u_0=sum_{j=1}^b t_j tensor e_j, the normalized curvature of S tensor (det Q)^l is l-b. To make the ambient curvature strictly positive, use the scalar metric e^{-epsilon |z|^2} I. Its Chern curvature is epsilon times the identity product, and at the origin the metric and its first jet are unchanged, hence B_j unchanged. The normalized curvature of the twist on u_0 becomes (1+la)epsilon+l-b, negative for any integer 0<=l<b and sufficiently small positive epsilon. The tensor has rank b<=m. No global compact counterexample to cohomological vanishing is inferred.
