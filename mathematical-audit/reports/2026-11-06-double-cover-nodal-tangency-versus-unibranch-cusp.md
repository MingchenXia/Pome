## Exact assertion

Let \(m\geq 1\) be an integer, work with germs at the origin of \(\mathbb C^2\), and let \(\pi(x,y)=x\). Consider
\[
 D_m=\{y(y-x^m)=0\},\qquad
 C_m=\{y^2=x^{2m+1}\}.
\]
**Both** are reduced plane-curve germs, pure of dimension one, with a unique singular point at the origin. Their projections \(\pi\) are finite surjective degree-two maps over sufficiently small discs; both have two points over \(x\ne0\), one point over \(x=0\), and the same set-theoretic critical/singular image \(\{0\}\). In each case the length of the local normalization quotient (the \(\delta\)-invariant) is exactly \(m\). Nevertheless:

| Invariant | Tangent double branch \(D_m\) | Irreducible cusp \(C_m\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normalization | Two disjoint discs \(t\mapsto(t,0)\), \(t\mapsto(t,t^m)\) | One disc \(t\mapsto(t^2,t^{2m+1})\) |
| Points over the singular point **under normalization** | 2 | 1 |
| Preimage of punctured base disc | Two disjoint unramified copies | Connected degree-two cover |
| Monodromy around \(0\) | Identity on the two points | Transposition |
| Plane-equation discriminant in \(y\) | \(x^{2m}\) | \(4x^{2m+1}\) |
| \(\dim_{\mathbb C}(\widetilde{\mathcal O}/\mathcal O)\) | \(m\) | \(m\) |

Thus **generic degree, set-theoretic fiber cardinalities, critical-value set, and even \(\delta\) do not determine the number of local branches or monodromy**. Discriminant parity distinguishes the two models in this particular quadratic Weierstrass family; it is not being asserted that the discriminants coincide.

## Proof: tangent double branch

Write \(R=\mathbb C\{t\}\), and restrict a germ \(F(x,y)\) to its two branches:
\[
 \mathcal O_{D_m,0}
 \longrightarrow R\oplus R,\quad F\longmapsto(F(t,0),F(t,t^m)).
\]
The map is injective: its kernel is \((y)\cap(y-x^m)=(y(y-x^m))\), since the two linear factors are relatively prime in the factorial convergent power-series ring. More constructively, Weierstrass division by the monic polynomial \(y(y-x^m)\) gives the unique representative \(a(x)+y b(x)\); its two restrictions are \(a(t)\) and \(a(t)+t^m b(t)\). Consequently
\[
 A_D:=\mathcal O_{D_m,0}
 =\{(f,g)\in R^2:\ g-f\in t^mR\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The normalization is \(B_D=R\oplus R\). Indeed, its two smooth components map biholomorphically away from the origin, \(B_D=A_D[e]\) for the idempotent \(e=(1,0)\) satisfying \(e^2-e=0\), and \(B_D\) is a product of integrally closed one-variable regular local rings. The finite module \(B_D=A_D+A_De\) proves finiteness explicitly.

The quotient map \((f,g)\mapsto[g-f]\bmod t^m\) is surjective with kernel \(A_D\); therefore
\[
 B_D/A_D\simeq R/(t^m),\qquad \delta(D_m)=m.
 \tag{2}
\]
The conductor is, directly from (1),
\[
 \mathfrak c_D=\{b\in B_D:bB_D\subset A_D\}=t^mR\oplus t^mR.
 \tag{3}
\]
For the inclusion from right to left, all differences of products are divisible by \(t^m\). Conversely, multiply \(b=(f,g)\) separately by \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\): (1) forces \(t^m\mid f,g\). As an ideal of \(A_D\), the conductor is generated by \(x^m\mapsto(t^m,t^m)\) and \(y\mapsto(0,t^m)\):
\[
 \mathfrak c_D=(x^m,y)A_D.
 \tag{4}
\]
In particular \(x^mB_D\subset A_D\), and **\(m\) is optimal** among uniform denominators \(x^k\): \(x^ke=(t^k,0)\) is not in \(A_D\) for \(k<m\). Thus the branch-separating bounded weakly holomorphic idempotent cannot be holomorphic at the singular point, but is integral over the local ring and has the exact optimal denominator \(x^m\). This independently checks the multiple-branch content of Proposition 9.4.3.

The polynomial \(y(y-x^m)\) has the two single-valued roots \(0\) and \(x^m\). Both are distinct and regular when \(x\ne0\), so analytic continuation around a puncture fixes each point. The normalization over \(0\) has two points despite the original map's single set-theoretic point over \(0\).

## Proof: unibranch cusp

Set \(s=t^2\). The parametrization
\[
 \gamma(t)=(t^2,t^{2m+1})
 \tag{5}
\]
is injective and surjective onto \(C_m\) near \(0\): for \(x\ne0\) we have \(t=y/x^m\), whence \(t^2=x\) and \(t^{2m+1}=y\); at the origin \(t=0\). On a small closed disc it is proper. The cusp is irreducible because the embedding
\[
 A_C=\mathbb C\{x,y\}/(y^2-x^{2m+1})
       \hookrightarrow \mathbb C\{t\}
\]
is injective: the unique Weierstrass representative \(a(x)+y b(x)\) maps to \(a(t^2)+t^{2m+1}b(t^2)\), whose even and odd powers cannot cancel. In particular
\[
 A_C=\mathbb C\{s\}\oplus t^{2m+1}\mathbb C\{s\},
 \quad
 B_C=\mathbb C\{t\}=\mathbb C\{s\}\oplus t\mathbb C\{s\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The normalization is \(B_C\): \(t=y/x^m\) on the punctured smooth curve, \(t\) is integral because \(t^2=x\), \(B_C=A_C+A_Ct\) is finite, and the regular one-variable ring is normal. Equation (6) shows immediately that the missing monomials are precisely
\[
 t,t^3,\ldots,t^{2m-1},
\]
hence
\[
 \delta(C_m)=\dim_{\mathbb C}(B_C/A_C)=m.
 \tag{7}
\]
The conductor is exactly
\[
 \mathfrak c_C=t^{2m}B_C=(x^m,y)A_C.
 \tag{8}
\]
The inclusion \(t^{2m}B_C\subset A_C\) follows from (6). If \(a=a(s)+t^{2m+1}b(s)\in A_C\) has an even term below degree \(2m\), multiplying it by \(t\in B_C\) produces an odd term below degree \(2m+1\), not in \(A_C\); so no such \(a\) lies in the conductor. The function \(t=y/x^m\) is bounded and holomorphic on \(C_m\setminus\{0\}\), integral, but not holomorphic on \(C_m\) at \(0\), since its linear term in \(t\) does not occur in (6). As for \(D_m\), \(x^mB_C\subset A_C\) is sharp among denominators \(x^k\): \(x^kt=t^{2k+1}\notin A_C\) if \(k<m\).

The punctured curve \(C_m\setminus\{0\}\) is parametrized by a **connected** punctured \(t\)-disc, and \(\pi\circ\gamma=t^2\). A loop \(x=re^{i\theta}\), \(\theta:0\to2\pi\), lifts from \(t=\sqrt r\) to \(t=-\sqrt r\), so its monodromy is the nontrivial transposition. The quadratic \(y\)-discriminant is \(4x^{2m+1}\). All fibers and finite-cover assertions follow directly from the explicit parametrizations; no Remmert proper mapping theorem or general normalization theorem is used as a hidden step.
