### Statement and proof

Let \(n\geq1\), \(1\leq p\leq n\), and let \(\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_p:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) be finite convex nondecreasing functions. Write \(t=\log|z|^2\), \(\theta=d^ct,\ \beta=dd^ct,\ \sigma=\theta\wedge\beta^{n-1}\) on the punctured space, \(\beta_0=dd^c|z|^2\), and use the normalized \(dd^c=i\partial\bar\partial/(2\pi)\). Put \(w_i=\chi'_{i,+}\), \(a_i=\lim_{t\to-\infty}w_i(t)\geq0\), \(W=\prod_{i=1}^p w_i\), \(A=\prod_{i=1}^p a_i\). These derivatives are finite locally bounded right-continuous nondecreasing functions; their left tails are bounded. Extend \(v_i=\chi_i(t)\) to the origin by its limit (possibly minus infinity). This is psh: \(v_{i,j}=\chi_i(\max(t,-j))\), extended constantly across zero, is locally bounded psh and decreases to \(v_i\). On the punctured space the psh claim follows from convex increasing composition with \(t\); near zero each approximant is constant. The limit is finite away from zero.

On the punctured space the mixed bounded Bedford–Taylor product \(T_p\) is
\[
 T_p=W(t)\beta^p+(DW)(dt)\wedge\theta\wedge\beta^{p-1}.
\]
For \(p=n\) the first term vanishes. To prove the formula, smooth the one-dimensional profiles locally by nonnegative convolution, preserving convexity and monotonicity. For smooth profiles expand \(dd^c v_i=w_i\beta+w_i' dt\wedge\theta\); two radial factors vanish and the coefficient of one radial factor is \(\sum_i w_i'\prod_{k\ne i}w_k=W'\), with no factorial. On compact intervals the slopes converge almost everywhere and stay uniformly bounded, so their products converge in \(L^1_{\rm loc}\), and their distributional derivatives converge. The profiles converge locally uniformly, hence the bounded mixed Bedford–Taylor products converge. This proves the displayed identity, including simultaneous slope jumps, without multiplying a singular measure by an unspecified value of a discontinuous derivative.

For each \(j\), the truncated slopes are zero on \(t<-j\), and equal the original slopes on \(t>-j\) (take right-continuous values at the jump). Thus \(W_j=\mathbf1_{[-j,\infty)}W\) and the bounded mixed product \(T_{p,j}\) vanishes near zero. If \(p<n\), set \(m=n-p\). By the polar identity of 5.58.1 and the BV product rule, the exact trace on an open ball, for every real \(b\), is
\[
 \int_{|z|<e^{b/2}}T_{p,j}\wedge\beta_0^{m}
 = e^{mb}W_j(b-).
\]
For \(p=0\), use \(T_{0,j}=1,\ W_j=1\) and the same formula. In particular for \(p<n\) there is a bound \(Cr^{2m}\) on small balls, uniform in \(j\). Coefficient domination and locality away from zero imply that \(T_{p,j}\) converges weakly to the coefficient zero-extension of the punctured product; there is no residual coefficient atom. The limit is positive and closed by weak convergence, so this is also a direct proof of extension in this radial mixed class, independent of invoking the general Skoda–El Mir extension theorem. It agrees with the unique closed order-zero extension by the normal-current support theorem 5.56.3 for \(p<n\). Its normalized trace density at zero is
\[
 \lim_{r\downarrow0} r^{-2(n-p)}
 \int_{|z|<r}T_p\wedge\beta_0^{n-p}=A.
\]
The strict open-ball endpoint is \(W(b-)\), not \(W(b)\) at a radial shell atom.

For \(p=n\), on the cylinder the positive measures are \(DW_j(dt)\wedge\sigma\). Their restrictions to \(t>-j\) equal the original punctured measure and at \(t=-j\) the shell has mass \(W(-j)\). The latter tends to \(A\) and concentrates at zero, while the original measure lost on \(t\le -j\) has mass \(W(-j)-A\to0\). Testing compactly supported continuous functions gives the exact global weak limit
\[
 T_n=\widetilde{DW(dt)\wedge\sigma}+A\delta_0.
\]
Here tilde is zero extension; its finite local mass follows from the finite left tail \(W(b)-A\).

To verify that these limits are **classical recursive products**, not just chosen extensions, proceed by induction on the number of factors in any ordering. For the preceding \(p-1\) factors the small-ball trace estimate has exponent \(2(n-p+1)>0\), including when \(p=n\). On a fixed left half-line \(|\chi_i(t)|\le C(1+|t|)\), since its slopes there are bounded; the truncated profiles satisfy the same bound for \(t<0\), uniformly for positive integers \(j\). On dyadic annuli \(2^{-k-1}\le |z|<2^{-k}\), the integral of this weight against the preceding trace is at most \(C'(1+k)2^{-2(n-p+1)k}\), uniformly in \(j\). This summable tail tends to zero as the inner radius tends to zero, also for the limiting current. There is no point mass in the preceding trace. Coefficient domination therefore gives both coefficient integrability of \(v_p\) and convergence \(v_{p,j}T_{p-1,j}\to v_pT_{p-1}\): use a cutoff outside a small ball where the products and profiles are eventually unchanged, then bound the remainder by the annular series. Weak continuity of \(dd^c\) proves the recursive identity. Since the formula is symmetric, every order agrees. No general unbounded-product monotone convergence assertion is being made; the argument proves this specified radial truncation limit and recursion.

The non-pluripolar product equals the punctured product away from zero by bounded locality and has no point mass by its defining non-pluripolar property. Thus at top degree the difference between the classical and non-pluripolar products is exactly \(A\delta_0\). In particular, for \(\chi_i(t)=a_it+b_i\) with \(a_i\geq0\), the classical top product is \(A\delta_0\) whereas the non-pluripolar top product is zero. If any one left slope is zero, the two top products coincide, even if other potentials have logarithmic poles. This is a distinction between products, not a contradiction in the original text.

Dependencies retained: the bounded Bedford–Taylor mixed product and locally uniform continuity, positive-current coefficient trace domination, and non-pluripolar locality/no-polar-mass for the last comparison. The induction above avoids invoking the excluded smoothing programme or the general relative-collar theorem 7.31.2. The earlier 18 November classical radial trace-power threshold and the earlier plane-average density normalization are special logarithmic-profile cases; this increment treats arbitrary finite convex nondecreasing profiles, mixed slopes and their residual atom.
