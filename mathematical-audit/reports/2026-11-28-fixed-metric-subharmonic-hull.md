### Exact hull and relative construction

Let M be a connected noncompact complex manifold of dimension n>=1, omega any fixed smooth Hermitian metric and L the trace of the complex Hessian in that metric, as in this section. For a nonempty compact K let H(K) be K together with all components of M\K having compact closure. Then H(K) is compact by 9.52.5. For every strictly L-subharmonic smooth exhaustion f, every real b>max_K f, and every closed F disjoint from H(K), there exists a smooth strictly L-subharmonic exhaustion psi with psi>=f, psi=f on a neighborhood of K, and psi>b on F.

Proof: P=F intersect {f<=b} is compact. If it is empty take psi=f. For x in P the open component of M\K containing x has noncompact closure, and f is unbounded there by properness and boundedness below. A path in that component therefore joins x to W={f>b+1}. Take a connected relatively compact open path neighborhood D_x in M\K meeting W. Finitely many cover P. Their union D has finitely many components, each meeting W, and compact closure disjoint from K. Every component of W has noncompact closure: if relatively compact, the continuous f attains a maximum on its closure, and the boundary has value b+1 (the whole M case is already noncompact). A maximum above b+1 would be interior and contradict Lf>0. Consequently each such component contains a point outside the compact closure of D. Lemma 9.52.1 with U=D supplies v>=0, positive on P, zero outside D union W, whose negative L-locus has closure in a compact S contained in W\closure(D). The closures of D and W avoid K; v vanishes on a neighborhood of K. Choose C>0 so f+Cv>b+1 on P, possible by compactness. Choose smooth convex nondecreasing chi>=0, zero below b+1/2, with chi' on [b+1,infinity) large enough to have chi'(f)Lf>=C|Lv| on S, possible since Lf has a positive minimum there; take chi=0 for empty S. The chain rule L(chi(f))=chi'(f)Lf+chi''(f)|partial f|_omega^2 gives L(f+Cv+chi(f))>0 everywhere, on S by the chosen bound and off S by positivity of Lf and nonnegativity of the other contributions. This psi is exhaustive since psi>=f, equals f near K and exceeds b on P and on F\P. No approximation of a singular psh function is used.

It follows that the hull defined by all smooth strictly L-subharmonic exhaustions,
{ x : u(x)<=max_K u for every such exhaustion u },
is exactly H(K), for every fixed metric omega. Indeed, for a relatively compact component G of M\K its boundary lies in K; the maximum of any such u on closure(G) is attained on the boundary by strict subharmonicity. Conversely apply the relative construction to F={x} outside H(K), with any b>max_K f and a base exhaustion from 9.52.8. The same equality holds when testing all smooth strongly n-convex exhaustions (positive Levi eigenvalue somewhere in each tangent space): their local-maximum obstruction gives the same inclusion, and the fixed-metric family is a subclass. Moreover the equality persists if one tests only strictly L-subharmonic exhaustions agreeing with a preassigned f near K and dominating f. No global fixed neighborhood is asserted independently of F.

The result is a quantitative-in-level separation statement, not an estimate of derivatives or support and not a cohomology density theorem. It strengthens the geometric portion of 9.52.12, preserves the original hypotheses, and does not modify Corollary 9.52.14. Proposed optional addition after 9.52.12: the relative construction and the exact hull characterization. The cited geometric lemmas and exhaustion existence are conditional internal inputs for this deduction; no transitive certification.
