For an explicit counterexample to **well-typedness**, take `X=Delta subset C`, `p=2`, and `phi_1=phi_2=0`. The defining integral asks for `omega^{-1}` even though no negative exterior power is defined. This is a missing degree hypothesis, **not** evidence that the usual non-pluripolar theorem is false for `1 <= p <= n`.

## POME-NPP-SLICE-LIMIT-JUSTIFICATION-001 — low / proof gap with an explicit conditional repair

Example 13.11.8 states the true one-factor identity `<dd^c phi> = 1_{phi>-infty} dd^c phi`. Lines 108–116 pass from the `n`-dimensional identity to the one-dimensional Riesz result by saying “by the dominated convergence theorem”. In the presented proof, **no domination in the transverse slice parameter** for the changing measures `1_{phi_z>-k}dd^c max(phi_z,-k)` is supplied; pointwise slice convergence alone does not justify dominated convergence. The issue is a missing proof step, **not** a counterexample to the identity.

Here is a rigorous conditional replacement, using only local distributional calculus and the explicitly cited one-dimensional Riesz-measure locality input [EK24, Proposition 3.3]:

1. On a polydisc with standard Kähler `omega`, expand `dd^c u wedge omega^{n-1}` into a fixed positive dimensional constant times the sum of the directional one-variable Riesz measures, integrated against transverse Lebesgue measure. For `u=phi_k=max(phi,-k)` this disintegration follows from Lemma 13.10.5 with `Q` the selected coordinate disc, `D` the other `n-1` coordinates, and bounded Borel indicator `1_{phi>-k}`.
2. For the unbounded `phi`, the same disintegration as **positive Radon measures** follows by testing smooth compactly supported `f`: distributional integration by parts gives `<dd^c phi wedge omega^{n-1},f>=<phi,dd^c f wedge omega^{n-1}>`. Fubini for the latter is legitimate because `phi in L^1_loc`; almost all coordinate slices are subharmonic (the identically `-infty` exceptional slices have transverse measure zero), and one-variable integration by parts yields the disintegration. Equality on nonnegative smooth tests extends to Borel tests by uniqueness of Radon measures.
3. The **one-dimensional** Riesz-measure restriction/locality result gives, on almost every transverse slice, increasing restricted measures `nu_{k,z}=1_{phi_z>-k}dd^c max(phi_z,-k)` with `nu_{k,z} up-arrow 1_{phi_z>-infty}dd^c phi_z`. Inserting these restrictions into the disintegration and applying **Tonelli plus monotone convergence** (rather than unproved domination) to nonnegative test functions yields the desired `n`-dimensional formula. Repeat the directional-slice argument after arbitrary constant unitary changes of local coordinates; the resulting trace measures in every complex direction determine each diagonal Hermitian coefficient in each frame, and polarization recovers the off-diagonal coefficient measures. Hence the equality holds as a positive `(1,1)`-current, not merely after wedging one fixed Kähler form.

The monotone one-dimensional locality and its finite-part identification remain the specifically cited **external mathematical input**; this report does not certify EK24 internally. If a self-contained proof is desired, it is exactly this one-variable Riesz locality input that must be internalized, not any Demailly regularization programme. This replacement is **conditional**, not a full transitive proof certificate. It does not rely on sequential compactness of arbitrary directed nets.
