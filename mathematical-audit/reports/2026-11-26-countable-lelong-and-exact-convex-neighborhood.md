## POME-LELONG-COUNTABLE-ADD-BALL-MEAN-001

Low / verified conditional deduction and explicit proof supplement; not a false theorem.

Source: docs/sections/plurisubharmonic-functions--lelong-numbers-and-multiplier-ideal-sheaves/index.html, blob 2315574feb57cd6bf57c3d64e4221da2eabc3cc9. Proposition 5.16.4 (61D6716B27F1, source D1F02E55), lines 34–66; Proposition 5.16.8 (B9C4FE156D00, source BEBA77BB), lines 77–87; Corollary 5.16.9 (F3F797E97E9D, source D8C9CDFB), lines 92–124. The radial maximum input 5.13.11 (2FF7CC0E8132, source 83046109) is in docs/sections/plurisubharmonic-functions--the-definition-of-plurisubharmonic-functions/index.html, blob 756d3347f65bad7e06a20f52c04ee6fd466c038f, lines 122–134.

The finite addition assertion is correct. The proof's phrase "all properties follow directly" merits an explicit linear averaging argument: suprema of sums are not sums of suprema. The following argument also proves countable additivity without any approximation theorem for quasi-psh functions.

Work in a ball of positive complex dimension n and subtract constants when necessary to make each individual psh function nonpositive on a common smaller ball. Write M_u(r)=sup_{|z|<r} u(z), and B_u(r)=the normalized Euclidean volume mean on that ball. For fixed 0<s<1, take r sufficiently small that the balls below are contained in the domain. For |x|<sr, B(0,r) is contained in B(x,(1+s)r). Submean and u<=0 give
\[
 u(x)\leq ((1+s)r)^{-2n}\operatorname{Vol}(B(0,1))^{-1}
                \int_{B(x,(1+s)r)}u
 \leq (1+s)^{-2n} B_u(r).
\]
Consequently (1+s)^{2n} M_u(sr) <= B_u(r) <= M_u(r). Divide by log r^2<0, apply 5.16.4, and let s decrease to zero. Thus B_u(r)/log r^2 tends to nu(u,0). Finite additivity follows from linearity of these finite means. This also supplies an elementary proof without Siu or multiplier-ideal estimates.

More generally let u_j<=0 be psh on a common ball, let a_j>=0, and suppose U=sum_j a_j u_j is not identically minus infinity (zero coefficients are omitted). Then
\[
 \nu(U,0)=\sum_j a_j \nu(u_j,0)<\infty.
\]
Indeed U is psh by the decreasing-limit theorem for psh functions and is locally integrable. For t=log r^2, b_u(t)=B_u(exp(t/2)) is convex and increasing. To see convexity, integrate over the unit ball the circular means of zeta -> u(zeta w) at radius exp(t/2). Each such circular mean is convex in log radius by radial subharmonicity and harmonic comparison on annuli; identically minus infinity restrictions can be discarded for almost every w by local integrability and Fubini. Rotational invariance of the ball identifies this integral with b_u(t); all means at the three fixed radii in a convexity inequality are finite. Fix t1<t0. For t<=t1 the secant D_j(t)=(b_{u_j}(t0)-b_{u_j}(t))/(t0-t) is nonnegative, bounded by D_j(t1), and tends to nu(u_j,0) as t tends to minus infinity. Tonelli on the negative functions gives b_U(t)=sum_j a_j b_{u_j}(t) at every fixed t. Since both b_U(t0) and b_U(t1) are finite, sum_j a_j D_j(t1) is finite. Dominated convergence for the series therefore proves the assertion. No interchange of an unbounded sequence of slopes without this dominating summable majorant is needed. Dimension zero is trivial under the printed convention.

The arbitrary-family supremum formula of 5.16.9 has an independent direct verification avoiding the countable reduction: fix a common upper bound C on a ball of radius r0<1. Convexity of the radial maximum and its asymptotic slope imply u_i(z)<=C+nu(u_i,0) log(|z|^2/r0^2) for 0<|z|<r0. Replace the coefficient by the infimum of the Lelong numbers and take the upper semicontinuous envelope; the logarithmic upper bound persists off the center and hence controls its Lelong number. The opposite inequality is monotonicity. Thus all cardinalities, not just sequences, are covered; no uniform convergence of the family is required.

Affected dependents: generic and divisorial Lelong definitions 5.16.10–11, and later singularity-type comparisons that use finite additivity; no downstream statement is refuted. The countable conclusion requires the nontrivial negative sum on a common ball; it is not a claim of continuity under arbitrary weak limits. Proposed action: add the linear mean proof to 5.16.8 and optionally state the countable version separately.

## POME-QCONVEX-SMOOTH-SUBSPACE-EXACT-001

Low / verified conditional deduction, not a printed error.

Source: docs/sections/convex-neighborhoods-and-coherent-vanishing--convex-functions-near-analytic-subspaces/index.html, blob 9e940da0044eb3d6f16b470c0db81106506c93eb. Lemmas 9.49.1 (DD53C11960A9, source D35EA2F8), lines 11–33; 9.49.2 (71E35F5F0916, source 8BA5B243), lines 38–71; Proposition 9.49.4 (DBED5041C4BA, source 8F0EC002), lines 88–153; Theorem 9.49.8 (9EBE244C8709, source 43556780), lines 158–198.

For a smooth closed analytic subspace Y of X (with X allowed singular), every smooth strongly q-convex function psi on Y has a smooth strongly q-convex extension to some neighborhood, with EXACT restriction psi. If psi is an exhaustion, every prescribed neighborhood contains a strongly q-complete neighborhood carrying such an exhaustion with exact restriction.

Proof: take a global smooth extension f by 9.49.1, and R from 9.49.2, using ambient generators of the ideal of Y. Near each point of Y use the ambient representation R_ext=h*Hh in lines 56–62, with H positive definite. Since Y is smooth, choose ambient coordinates flattening Y. The differential of h has kernel exactly TY (the generators generate the smooth submanifold ideal). At Y, R_ext=0, dR_ext=0 and its Levi form is the Gram form dh*H dh, positive definite on the normal space. The restriction of Hf to TY has a positive subspace F of dimension at least max(0,dim Y-q+1). For a sufficiently large scalar t, Hf+t HR is positive on F plus the normal space, by the Schur complement inequality in lines 136–142 (if F=0 use only the normal block). This gives at least N-q+1 positive directions in ambient dimension N; if N<q the condition is vacuous. By continuity each such t works on a smaller neighborhood along Y in the chosen embedding.

Choose a smooth positive function h0 on X locally sufficiently large on Y: cover Y by locally finite smaller relatively compact patches with the above bounds, take a locally finite refinement with compact closures, and choose smooth partition coefficients with constants dominating all bounds of patches meeting each compact support. At any point on Y each active coefficient then has constant at least the bound of a patch valid there. Include patches disjoint from Y with positive constants. Equivalently take a locally bounded positive majorant of the required bounds and dominate it by a smooth partition construction. Because R and dR vanish on Y, the Levi form of f+h0 R there is exactly Hf+h0 HR for the chosen ambient extensions; derivatives of h0 do not contribute. Positivity is open in the relevant ambient extensions. Hence f+h0 R is strongly q-convex on an open neighborhood of Y, while its restriction is exactly psi. This construction avoids the approximate stratum induction for this special smooth case. It does not assume arbitrary sums of strongly q-convex functions are strongly q-convex.

For an exhaustion, use f+h0 R as the initial function varphi in 9.49.8. Its properness-on-a-closed-neighborhood argument, common-positive-subspace barrier and final blow-up function work unchanged; the barrier is identically zero near each point of Y (lines 183–195). After subtracting the initial common constant, the resulting exhaustion restricts to psi, not merely to an approximation. The compactness proof at lines 197–198 needs only the original closed-sublevel compactness and the barrier's divergence at its outer boundary. The printed theorem for general singular Y remains distinct: exact interpolation on arbitrary singular subspaces is not proved here. Downstream 9.49.9–10 may use the exact restriction when their Y is smooth. This is conditional on the stated local ideal coherence, partition and embedding inputs, not a complete transitive certificate.
