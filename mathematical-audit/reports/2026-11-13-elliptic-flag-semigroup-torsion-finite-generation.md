## Sharp finite-generation dichotomy

**Claim.** The graded valuation semigroup (S(A,p)) is finitely generated **if and only if** (B=A(-dp)) has finite order in (operatorname{Pic}^0(E)).

**Nontorsion case.** If (B) is nontorsion, the top valuation (md) never occurs, but ((m,md-1)in S) for all (mge1), so slopes tend to (d). If (S) had finitely many generators ((m_j,k_j)), then every generator would satisfy (k_j/m_j<d). Every additive combination would have slope at most (max_j k_j/m_j<d), a contradiction.

**Torsion case.** If (B) has order (q), let (M=2q). Both (g_0=(M,0)) and (g_1=(M,Md)) lie in (S), since (Mdge2) and (B^M) is trivial. Consider any ((m,k)in S) with (mge2M+1). If (kge Md), subtract (g_1). The remaining degree (m-M) is positive, the deficit (md-k) is unchanged, and (B^{m-M}) has the same triviality status as (B^m); formula (1) shows that the remainder is again in (S). If (k<Md), subtract (g_0). The new deficit is
[
(m-M)d-kge (2M+1)d-Md-(Md-1)=d+1ge2,
]
so the remainder is again in (S) by (1). By induction on degree, every semigroup point is generated by the finitely many points of degrees at most (2M). This proves the converse without a Noetherian semigroup or polyhedral saturation theorem.

The finite-generation behavior therefore changes with the **arithmetic class (B)** even though the complete bodies (Delta=[0,d]), degree-(m) Hilbert functions (md), and leading volumes agree identically. In particular, equality of Okounkov bodies and Hilbert functions does not determine finite generation. A degree-one illustration is (A=mathcal O_E(p)), where (S_1={1}) and (S_2={0,2}), versus (A=mathcal O_E(q)) for a point (q-p) of infinite order, where (S_m={0,ldots,m-1}) for every (mge1).
