Extract a convergent subsequence from any subsequence, w_j→w_* in L1, with w_* in K_phi. Write g_j=exp(lambda_j w_j) and g=exp(lambda w_*), using the zero-exponent convention above. These functions are in [0,1]. For lambda>0, Corollary 17.5.12 gives exp(lambda w_j)→g in L1(nu). On x<=0, the mean-value estimate for exponents bounded below by lambda/2 gives |exp(lambda_j x)-exp(lambda x)| <= 2|lambda_j-lambda|/(e lambda), valid also at x=-infinity with the positive-exponent convention. Thus g_j→g in L1(nu). For lambda=0, for each eta>0 eventually lambda_j<=eta, and 0<=1-g_j<=1-exp(eta w_j) nu-a.e. Corollary 17.5.12 and dominated convergence as eta decreases to zero prove the same L1 convergence to 1. No uniform moment premise is needed.

Since |g_j|<=1, int g_j dnu_j→int g dnu>0 (the pole set is nu-null). Consequently a_j=m/int g_j dnu_j→a_*=m/int g dnu. The signed measure norm estimate

proves TV convergence along this subsequence.

Identification of the MA of the limit requires more than weak convergence of the measures. Decompose nu_j=f_j nu+sigma_j with sigma_j singular positive and f_j>=0. Then int |f_j-1| dnu + sigma_j(X)=||nu_j-nu||_TV, so a_j g_j f_j→a_*g in L1(nu): use boundedness of a_j g_j, the L1 error of f_j, and the previous convergence. Since w_*<=phi, mass monotonicity bounds MA(w_*)(X)<=m, whereas a_*g nu has mass m. Thus equality holds, and w_* has full relative mass.
