**Claim.** Let Omega be a nonempty bounded open subset of C^n, n >= 2, with C^2 boundary in the usual two-sided graph sense. No connectedness of Omega is required. Write E=C^n \ closure(Omega). Then the following are equivalent:
1. E is connected.
2. Every C^1 tangential CR datum on the boundary is the trace of a function holomorphic on Omega and continuous on its closure.

More precisely, for a particular such datum f choose any compactly supported C^1 extension f0 as in the printed proof, and set
\[
v=1_\Omega\bar\partial f_0,\qquad u=Kv.
\]
The function u is the unique compactly supported distributional solution of \(\bar\partial u=v\), is continuous, and is holomorphic on E. The datum extends if and only if \(u|_E=0\). This obstruction is independent of the choice of f0. It vanishes on the unbounded component of E without any hypothesis on the other components.

### Proof

The CR boundary equation gives \(\bar\partial v=0\): use the boundary distribution formula and C^1 approximation in lines 22–34 of the integrated proof. The cited current homotopy (Proposition 5.49.1, tag 7ACDC1AF162C, entry lines 6–22 in docs/tag/7ACDC1AF162C/index.html) provides the convolution solution. Its kernel is bounded by \(C_n|x|^{1-2n}\). Since v is bounded and compactly supported, the integral on a radius r ball around the singularity is O(r), uniformly in the evaluation point. Away from that ball the kernel is continuous. Thus u is continuous and tends to zero at infinity. It is holomorphic on the exterior of a ball containing the support of v. For fixed first n-1 coordinates outside a sufficiently large ball in C^{n-1}, the function in the remaining variable is entire and tends to zero at infinity; Liouville and the identity theorem give zero on the entire exterior of the ball. Hence u has compact support. Two compactly supported distributional solutions differ by a distributionally holomorphic scalar function, hence by an entire function with compact support, which is zero. This uses the scalar CR regularity input already invoked in the printed proof; no transitive certification of that input is claimed.

If \(u|_E=0\), continuity gives zero boundary trace, and \(F=f_0-u\) on Omega is the desired extension. In particular connected E suffices by the identity theorem.

For the converse for individual data, suppose F extends f. Define w=f0-F in Omega and w=0 on the complement, including the boundary. It is continuous and compactly supported. The distributional identity \(\bar\partial w=v\) requires no differentiability of F up to the boundary: let a smooth collar cutoff be zero at interior distance <=epsilon and one at distance >=2epsilon, with first derivative O(1/epsilon). The cut-off functions times w converge to w in distributions. The extra derivative term is bounded against each fixed test form by a constant times the supremum of |w| in the 2epsilon collar, since the collar has Lebesgue volume O(epsilon). That supremum tends to zero by continuity and the zero boundary values. The remaining derivative term converges to v. Thus w=u by compactly supported uniqueness, so u vanishes on E. If f0 is changed to g0, the same cutoff argument for the zero-trace difference gives
\[
K(1_\Omega\bar\partial(f_0-g_0))=1_\Omega(f_0-g_0),
\]
where the right hand side is extended continuously by zero. This proves independence of the exterior obstruction.

For necessity of the topological condition, suppose E is disconnected. As Omega is bounded, E has precisely one unbounded component: the exterior of a sufficiently large ball is connected and contained in E. Choose a bounded component C of E. Each boundary point has a connected exterior half-neighborhood, contained in a unique component of E; the component assignment is locally constant along the boundary by the C^2 graph property. Define f=1 at points assigned to C and f=0 elsewhere. It is locally constant, hence C^1 and CR. The boundary of C is nonempty and contained in the boundary of Omega (it cannot meet the interior of E or Omega). Thus f is 1 somewhere.

Suppose an extension F exists. Glue F on Omega to the constants 1 on C and 0 on all other components of E, and assign f on the boundary. The resulting H is continuous. It is holomorphic off the boundary. Near each boundary point subtract the locally constant boundary value c. The function H-c is zero on the local exterior and continuous with zero boundary trace on the interior. The same collar cutoff argument, now locally against compactly supported test forms and with zero interior derivative, gives \(\bar\partial(H-c)=0\) across the boundary. Thus H is entire. It vanishes on an exterior open set but equals 1 on C, a contradiction.

For example, the spherical shell \(1<|z|<2\) in C^n with boundary data 1 on the inner sphere and 0 on the outer sphere has no extension. This is not a counterexample to the printed theorem, which assumes connected boundary. The stronger topological condition is genuinely weaker for open sets: the union of two disjoint balls with disjoint closures has connected exterior but disconnected boundary. The dimension restriction remains essential; on the unit disc the tangential CR equation is vacuous and the datum \(\bar z\) has no holomorphic continuous extension (multiply the proposed extension by z and apply the maximum principle to the boundary value 1).

Uniqueness of extension follows from the maximum principle on each bounded component. Also the supremum norm on the closure equals that of the datum, by the same principle, so the extension operator when E is connected is linear and isometric on the C^1 CR subspace. It extends isometrically to the uniform closure of that subspace, by uniform convergence on the closure and the local uniform holomorphic limit theorem. No assertion that every continuous distributionally CR datum is in this closure is made.
