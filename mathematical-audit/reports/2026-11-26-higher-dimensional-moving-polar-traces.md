## Statement

Let X = P^n, n >= 2, with normalized Fubini–Study form omega, integral omega^n = 1. Fix a rational 0 < a < 1, any sequence of distinct points x_j, and any positive numbers e_j tending to zero. Let R be a set of smooth modifications as in 18.26.6. There are Gauduchon forms eta_pi on the individual modifications, fixed for the whole sequence, and normalized analytic ideal models p_j with the sole pole x_j of type a log |z|^2 + O(1), such that

    delta_omega^pol(p_j,0) <= e_j,
    d_omega(p_j,0) = a^n,
    L(p_j) = U(p_j) = 1-a^n.

Thus the failure of arbitrary independent trace weights to induce the positive-volume topology holds in every dimension >=2, even on the stratum with lower volume at least (1-a^n)/2. It is not a claim that every choice of weights fails on the analytic locus.

## Proof

1. At [1:0:...:0] use u = a log((sum_{k=1}^n |Z_k|^2)/(sum_{k=0}^n |Z_k|^2)). It is omega-PSH since the residual smooth background is (1-a)omega and the numerator has a PSH logarithm in a local trivialization. Unitary transformations transport u to the prescribed x_j. By 18.4.8, as used in the printed 18.26.13, p_j = P_omega[u_j] is an analytic ideal model of the same ordinary singularity type. In particular it is nonpositive, locally bounded off x_j, and has the required coefficient.

2. Fix one smooth modification pi:Y->X. The polar current R_j of p_j composed with pi is closed and positive by 5.46.7 and carried by the analytic fiber pi^{-1}(x_j). By the same analytic support theorem used in 18.26.13 (DemCADG Chapter III, Corollaries 2.11 and 2.14), it is the finite sum of integration currents over the hypersurface components of that fiber, with nonnegative coefficients. Indeed the dimension principle eliminates the residual closed order-zero (1,1)-current on the codimension >=2 portion. The support theorem is retained as a conditional mathematical input; it is not claimed proved here. Only finitely many x_j can occur as the image of a hypersurface component of the exceptional locus: the latter is a compact analytic subset with finitely many irreducible components, and any hypersurface in it is one of its divisorial components. A component mapping to a positive-dimensional center is not contained in a single point fiber. Since n>=2, every divisor over a point is exceptional. Consequently the set J_pi of j with R_j != 0 is finite.

3. Take any Gauduchon form gamma_pi (existence as used in the printed argument). Put C_pi,j = integral R_j wedge gamma_pi^(n-1), nonnegative and finite. Choose eps_pi>0 so that eps_pi C_pi,j <= e_j for every j in the finite set J_pi; if empty, choose eps_pi=1. Let B_pi = (pi^*omega)^(n-1) + eps_pi gamma_pi^(n-1). This is a smooth strictly positive (n-1,n-1)-form and dd^c B_pi = 0. It is the (n-1)-st power of a unique positive Hermitian form eta_pi. For completeness the pointwise matrix argument is as follows: in a local coframe and the complementary wedge basis, use the conventional fixed (n-1)! factor and transpose to identify the power of a Hermitian matrix H with its cofactor matrix det(H)H^(-1). For a positive Hermitian matrix B of coefficients in that convention, H = det(B)^(1/(n-1)) B^(-1) has det(H)H^(-1)=B. Uniqueness follows from taking determinants. This construction is smooth on the positive cone and transforms intrinsically by uniqueness. Thus eta_pi is Gauduchon, without requiring Y to be Kähler. For n=2 it reduces exactly to the sum used in the printed argument.

4. The trace normalization A_pi = integral pi^*omega wedge B_pi equals 1 + eps_pi integral pi^*omega wedge gamma_pi^(n-1) >=1. The first term equals one by degree-one change of variables outside the exceptional locus (smooth top-degree form). On each divisor contributing to R_j the map pi is constant; the restriction of pi^*omega there vanishes on its smooth locus. Since n-1>=1, R_j wedge (pi^*omega)^(n-1)=0, including for singular components by the definition of integration current. Thus the total variation of the polar trace, which is a positive measure, is eps_pi C_pi,j. The zero model has zero polar trace. Divide by A_pi and take the supremum over all pi: the result is <= e_j. No countability assumption on R is needed.

5. Blow up a point x_j by rho:Z->X with exceptional E. The map F:Z->P^(n-1) resolves the linear projection, so the pulled-back current of u_j equals a[E]+H_a, where H_a=(1-a)rho^*omega+a F^*omega_{n-1}. For every omega-PSH v ordinarily equivalent to u_j, divisorial removal (the same input as printed lines 337–343) gives H_a+dd^c w>=0 with bounded w extending the difference of the pullbacks. Its bounded Bedford–Taylor product does not charge E. Change of variables off E and bounded integration by parts in the closed background therefore give its total non-pluripolar mass equal to integral H_a^n. In the blowup ring H_a represents H-aE, with H=rho^*[omega]. Mixed H^k E^(n-k) for 1<=k<=n-1 vanish because H restricts trivially to E; E^n=(-1)^(n-1) because the normal bundle of E=P^(n-1) is O(-1). Hence integral H_a^n=1-a^n. Both lower and upper volumes equal this number for every representative of the type. The zero model has mass one; since p_j<=0, the deficit definition 13.22.1 gives d_omega(p_j,0)=a^n. This proves the claimed common positive lower bound and topology obstruction.
