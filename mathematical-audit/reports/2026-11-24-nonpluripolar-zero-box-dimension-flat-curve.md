**Claim.** For every n>=2, the graph in Theorem 5.51.1 contains a compact nonpluripolar set K0 of upper box dimension zero (hence Hausdorff dimension zero). At every point of its parameter set D0, all positive-order derivatives of all transverse graph coordinates vanish. Thus even a compact set on a smooth graph which agrees to infinite order at each of its points with its affine real tangent line need not be pluripolar.

**Proof.** Keep Q=2^(n+1)+1, the digit code and functions f_r from the printed proof. Retain only the codes for which all n bits are zero at every level not a perfect square. At level j² all n bits are free. Let D0 be the corresponding closed subset of D, and let K0 be its graph. Both are compact by continuity of the digit map and the graph map. At level m, D0 is covered by at most 2^(n floor(sqrt(m))) intervals of length Q^(-m). The smooth graph map on [0,1] has a finite Lipschitz constant L, so K0 has a cover by that many balls of radius L Q^(-m) (increase L to at least 1). For radii between L Q^(-(m+1)) and L Q^(-m), use the level m+1 cover. The ratio of the logarithm of the covering number to log(1/radius) tends to zero. This proves zero upper box dimension.

At a point x of D, every bump sum psi_(r,k) is constant on its own prefix interval (equal to the corresponding bit), and all other summands have support disjoint from that interval. At endpoints the derivatives also vanish by smoothness and the one-sided constant values. Thus psi_(r,k)^(ell)(x)=0 for every ell>=1. The uniform derivative convergence in the printed proof yields f_r^(ell)(x)=0 on D, in particular on D0. The tangent vector is (1,0,...,0), and every higher derivative of the graph map is zero there.

Fix an arbitrary array of the transverse bits at square levels and vary only b_(j²). The resulting compact fiber C_a^0 carries a probability with independent fair bits on the square levels. Equivalently use the weak limits of finite uniform bit measures exactly as in lines 28–34. For any fixed x in the fiber, first difference at square level j² has probability 2^(-j), and the printed separation gives |x-y|>=Q^(-j²). Singletons have mass zero. Consequently the logarithmic potential on the fiber is bounded above by log(Q) sum_(j>=1) j² 2^(-j), finite. The diameter is <=1, so Tonelli gives finite logarithmic energy. By the same planar energy criterion (permanent tag 5B4ACD7E054D) each fiber is nonpolar.

The set of allowed transverse values is C0'={sum_(j>=1) a_j 10^(-j^4): a_j in {0,1}}. It is compact. The original first-difference separation applies at level j², giving distance at least (1/2)10^(-j^4). The analogous fair-bit measure has logarithmic potential bounded above on C0' by sum_(j>=1) 2^(-j)(log 2+j^4 log 10), finite. Hence C0' is nonpolar by the same criterion. All transverse arrays vary independently, so their attainable tuples form (C0')^(n-1).

If K0 were pluripolar, Josefson's theorem (permanent tag 1822741C4B07) would provide a non-identically-minus-infinity PSH function on C^n with poles on K0. For each allowed tuple of transverse values, its restriction to the first complex coordinate line is subharmonic or identically minus infinity; the corresponding nonpolar C_a^0 forces the latter. Thus it is minus infinity on C x (C0')^(n-1). Repeating the same one-variable argument for each remaining coordinate yields minus infinity everywhere, a contradiction. This proves the claim. No regularization or limit exchange in the pluripolarity argument is needed.

Dependencies: the printed planar logarithmic energy criterion, Josefson globalization, and holomorphic restriction dichotomy. The finite measure weak-limit construction uses compact metric sequential compactness, not compactness of a general net. The complete upstream proof chains of those inputs are not certified here. No affected theorem is refuted; the example can supplement §5.51.1 and illustrates that real Hausdorff dimension alone cannot give a positive universal dimensional threshold for pluripolarity.
