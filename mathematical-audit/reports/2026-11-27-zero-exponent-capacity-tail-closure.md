**Claim.** Retain all hypotheses of Theorem 15.34.2, except allow the nonnegative limiting exponent to be zero (and even allow individual exponents to be zero). At exponent zero interpret the exponential as 1 off the pole set; values on the pole set are immaterial to the non-pluripolar reference measure. Then its entire conclusion holds, including finiteness and total-variation convergence. More generally, if nonpositive theta-PSH potentials u_j converge in capacity to a theta-PSH potential u, and their extended MA measures are bounded above by g_j mu, where mu is a fixed finite non-pluripolar measure and nonnegative g_j are uniformly integrable in L^1(mu), the MA measures converge weakly to the finite extended MA measure of u. Uniform integrability here means bounded L^1 norms and uniform absolute continuity of the integrals over sets of small mu-measure.

**Proof.** By Lemma 5.29.18 the convergence is in mu-measure; the potentials are finite mu-almost everywhere. For every T>1,
```
mu{u_j < -T} <= mu{u < -T/2} + mu{|u_j-u| > T/2}.
```
Given eta>0 choose J so that mu{|u_j-u|>1}<eta for all j>=J. For T>=2 the second term is at most eta. The first term tends to zero by finiteness of u mu-almost everywhere. For the finitely many j<J, the sublevel measures also tend to zero. Thus lim_(T->infty) sup_j mu{u_j<-T}=0. Uniform absolute continuity of the g_j integrals gives lim_(T->infty) sup_j MA_theta^ext(u_j){u_j<-T}=0. Theorem 13.15.13, extended to arbitrary smooth theta by Theorem 13.15.23, proves the general weak-convergence claim and finiteness.

In the exponential problem take g_j=f_j, because u_j<=0 and lambda_j>=0. L^1 convergence gives the required uniform integrability (use the L^1 error for large j and absolute continuity for the finitely many remaining j). For lambda_j->0, on {u_j>=-T} one has 0<=1-exp(lambda_j u_j)<=lambda_j T, and on the complementary set the error is at most 1. Consequently
```
||exp(lambda_j u_j)f_j-f||_1
 <= ||f_j-f||_1 + lambda_j T ||f||_1 + integral_{u_j<-T} f dmu .
```
The last term tends to zero uniformly in j as T tends to infinity, by the same sublevel estimate and absolute continuity of f mu. First take j->infty for fixed T, then T->infty. This proves TV convergence to f mu and comparison with the weak limit identifies MA_theta^ext(u)=f mu. The positive-exponent case follows by the printed uniform exponent comparison and bounded convergence in measure. No uniform L-infinity bound on u_j is used. The proof does not interchange a moving restriction with a weak limit.
