## The precise derived statement

Let `X` be a connected smooth compact complex curve of genus `g>=1`,
let `a in X`, and let `Pic^d(X)` be the line bundles of degree `d`.
By Lemma 10.79.7, tensoring by `O_X(-da)` identifies `Pic^d(X)`
with the Picard torus `J(X)=Pic^0(X)`. Thus the degree-`d`
Abel map is
```text
 A_d : Sym^d(X) -> J(X),
 E |-> O_X(E-da).
```
Here `Sym^d(X)` parametrizes effective divisors of degree `d`.

**(A) Surjectivity.** If `d>=g`, this map is surjective.

**(B) Exact fibers.** If `d>=2g-1`, then for every `L in Pic^d(X)`
its set-theoretic fiber is naturally the complete linear system
`|L| = P(H^0(X,L))`, hence is projective space `P^{d-g}`.
In particular the dimension does not jump anywhere over `J(X)`.
This is a fiberwise assertion; a holomorphic projective-bundle
trivialization or a universal Poincaré bundle is **not** inferred
without additional construction.

**(C) Optimal universal first-jet bounds.** Every line bundle of degree
`d>=2g` is generated by its global sections, and every line bundle
of degree `d>=2g+1` is very ample. Both integer thresholds are
optimal as universal statements for **every** `g>=1`: there exist
a degree-`2g-1` line bundle with a base point and a degree-`2g`
line bundle whose complete linear series fails to embed the curve.

## Proof from the printed internal dependencies

Riemann–Roch and the analytic Serre pairing in (10.78.10) give
```text
 h^0(L) - h^0(K_X tensor L^(-1)) = d + 1 - g,
 deg K_X = 2g-2.
```
In particular `h^0(L)>=d+1-g`. For `d>=g`, every `L`
has a nonzero section. Its zero divisor `E` is effective of
degree `d` and satisfies `O_X(E)=L`, proving (A).

Fix `d>=2g-1`. Since `deg(K_X tensor L^(-1))<0`,
the negative-degree section vanishing from Lemma 10.78.7 gives
`h^1(L)=0`, and
```text
 h^0(L)=d+1-g.
```
A nonzero section determines its effective zero divisor uniquely
up to multiplication by a nonzero constant; conversely any effective
divisor `E` with `O_X(E)=L` determines a section line in
`H^0(X,L)`. This bijection gives (B).

For `d>=2g` and every `p in X`, the line bundle `L(-p)`
has degree at least `2g-1`. The just-proved dimension identity
gives
```text
 h^0(L) - h^0(L(-p)) = 1.
```
Thus evaluation at `p` is onto its one-dimensional fiber:
there is no base point.

For `d>=2g+1` and any points `p,q`, including `p=q`,
the bundle `L(-p-q)` still has degree at least `2g-1`.
Consequently
```text
 h^0(L) - h^0(L(-p-q)) = 2.
```
For `p!=q` the complete series separates the two point evaluations.
For `p=q` it surjects onto the **length-two** local jet space
`L tensor (O_X/m_p^2)` and separates tangent directions.
The resulting map to `P(H^0(X,L)^*)` is an injective immersion,
hence a closed holomorphic embedding by compactness. This proves (C)
without invoking any asymptotic positivity or excluded smoothing
result.

Both cutoffs are genuinely sharp. For any genus `g>=1`, take a point
`p` and `L=K_X(p)`, of degree `2g-1`. Riemann–Roch gives
`h^0(K_X(p))=g=h^0(K_X)`. The natural inclusion
`H^0(K_X) -> H^0(K_X(p))` is therefore an isomorphism, but
the inclusion is multiplication by the divisor section of `O(p)`,
which vanishes at `p`; hence **every** section of `K_X(p)`
vanishes there. (This also works for genus one.)

For each `g>=1`, choose a compact hyperelliptic curve `X`
with a degree-two holomorphic map `pi:X->P^1` branched simply at
`2g+2` finite points. For example, normalize and compactify
`y^2=prod_{i=1}^{2g+2}(z-a_i)` with distinct `a_i`;
the genus is `g` by the integrated Riemann–Hurwitz
Corollary 10.78.16. The bundle `L=pi^*O_{P^1}(g)`
has degree `2g`. Its complete space of sections has dimension
`g+1` by Riemann–Roch, and the `g+1` independent pullbacks of
`H^0(P^1,O(g))` already exhaust it. Hence the complete morphism
factors through the degree-two map `pi`: it does **not** separate
two unramified points in the same fiber and is not very ample.
No claim that every degree-`2g` line bundle fails is made.

## Additional sign/normalization check (independent of the new corollary)

In 10.79.7 and 10.79.13 the transition logarithm is
```text
 c_t(z)=-(2*pi*i)^(-1) log(1-t/z),
 d c_t/dt=(2*pi*i)^(-1)/(z-t), eta_t=bar-del chi * c_t.
```
For `u=U(z) dz`, the printed Serre convention
`S([eta])(u)=int_X u wedge eta` gives
```text
 d/dt S([eta_t])(u)
 =-(2*pi*i)^(-1) int (bar-del chi wedge U(z)dz)/(z-t)
 = U(t),
```
because the inner boundary contributes `-2*pi*i*U(t)`
in the displayed wedge order. Thus the Abel differential has
the **positive** sign and matches the integration definition
of `alpha_a`; this is a selected consistency check, not an
independent proof of the analytic Serre duality or Hodge theory.
