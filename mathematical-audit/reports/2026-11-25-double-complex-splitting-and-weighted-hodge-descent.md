## POME-DOUBLE-COMPLEX-SQUARE-SPLIT-001

Low / verified conditional deduction; not a false printed theorem. Source: docs/sections/general-complex-cohomology-and-modifications--bott-chern-and-aeppli-comparison/index.html, blob d47c823a457ed5daa1b8b0a5cdfae06f65f86980. Lemma 10.85.1 (D823F74394BA; source marker D7034347), lines 17–24; Theorem 10.85.2 (72D73EDC0883; marker 6E4A6CC9), lines 29–40. The hypothesis is a bounded double complex of vector spaces over a field, with a²=b²=ab+ba=0. The printed staircase and lift arguments are correct.

**Strengthening.** If all rows and columns of Q are exact, Q is an algebraic direct sum of elementary four-vertex squares, even when its individual vector spaces are infinite dimensional. More generally, every inclusion of bounded double complexes C→D inducing isomorphisms on all row and column cohomologies admits a bidegree-preserving linear retraction commuting with both differentials. No topology or naturality of the retraction is asserted.

Proof. The printed lemma gives ker(ab)=im(a)+im(b) at every bidegree. Choose, in each bidegree, a linear complement V to ker(ab) in Q. Then ab is injective on V and im(ab)=abV. In any fixed bidegree (all subscripts here are determined by the operators' bidegrees),
Q = V ⊕ aV ⊕ bV ⊕ abV.
For spanning, write x=v+au+bw by the complement and Aeppli vanishing. Write u=v_u+au_1+bu_2 and w=v_w+aw_1+bw_2 by the same rule. Then au+bw=a v_u+b v_w+ab(u_2-w_1), and the last term is in abV. For directness, apply ab to a relation v+a v_1+b v_2+ab v_3=0. This gives v=0 by injectivity on V. Apply b, then a, to get v_1=v_2=0; finally v_3=0. The differentials on these summands are precisely those of a square generated by each element of a basis of V, with ba=-ab at the upper vertex. No bounded-dimension assumption was used.

Now take Q=D/C. Exactness of each quotient row and column follows from the two row/column cohomology comparisons and the corresponding long exact sequence. Lift a basis of each V^{p,q} to D in the same bidegree. Send its three other square vectors to a, b and ab of that lift. The signs and square-zero identities give a double-complex section s:Q→D of the quotient projection π. Thus I-sπ has image C, restricts to the identity on C, and commutes with a and b. This is a genuine algebraic retraction at cochain level, not merely an isomorphism on cohomology.

Conversely, for a bounded double complex, simultaneous BC and Aeppli vanishing implies row and column exactness: if ax=0, then abx=0 and x=au+bv by Aeppli vanishing. Now bv is closed under both maps, so bv=abz by BC vanishing, and x=a(u+bz). Interchanging a and b gives the column statement. This yields an equivalent square criterion.

Application to the smooth/current inclusion: Q^{p,q}=D^{p,q}(X)/A^{p,q}(X) has exact columns by the actual global smooth/current Dolbeault comparison (10.43.11), and exact rows by conjugation as in line 35. Hence the smooth forms are an algebraic double-complex direct summand of the currents. In particular one and the same (noncanonical) algebraic retraction preserves all bidegrees, commutes with both ∂ and ∂̄, and restricts to the identity on smooth forms. This also yields the printed cohomology comparisons. It is not a continuous smoothing operator, not local or natural, and does not preserve positivity or products. It supplies no regularization or analytic approximation result. The global comparison premise is not reproved here.

## POME-HODGE-WEIGHTED-RELATIVE-TRANSFER-001

Low / verified conditional deduction; not a printed error. Source: docs/sections/general-complex-cohomology-and-modifications--strong-hodge-structure-under-modifications/index.html, blob 6414dd82e72d4af59e4706e105c4752501e9829f, Theorem 10.87.1 (C4B9BA82F24B; marker 451F3847), lines 17–28, especially 23–27. The earlier 8 November generically finite degree formula is not counted again.

**Strengthening for positive relative dimension.** Let f:Y→X be proper holomorphic between connected compact complex manifolds, dim Y−dim X=r≥0. Suppose T is a (r,r)-current on Y closed under both ∂ and ∂̄ and that f_*T=c as a degree-zero current, with c nonzero. Then the pullback is split injective simultaneously on de Rham, Dolbeault, BC and Aeppli cohomology, and strong Hodge decomposition descends from Y to X.

Proof. A closed degree-zero current is a constant distribution on a connected manifold: locally all its distributional first derivatives vanish, and the local constants agree on overlaps. Thus the scalar assumption is equivalently nonvanishing of f_*T; it can be checked by testing on any smooth normalized top-degree form. Define, on smooth representatives η,
Rη = c^{-1} f_*(T∧η).
The product is with a smooth form, not a product of two arbitrary currents. Since T has even total degree and is separately ∂- and ∂̄-closed, both differentials commute with R with no extra sign. The bidegree is unchanged by R. Properness permits pushforward, and the projection formula, directly tested against compactly supported smooth forms, gives R f^*η=η as currents. Consequently R descends to each indicated cohomology group, including the Aeppli quotient by im∂+im∂̄. Transport by the actual smooth/current comparison maps (10.43.11, 10.85.2). These maps commute with the natural maps j and k of lines 7–13. If those maps upstairs are isomorphisms, injectivity downstairs follows by pulling a class up and applying R; surjectivity follows by pulling the target up, lifting upstairs and applying R. This is the same diagram argument as the printed modification case but with a different transfer.

Example: for a proper holomorphic submersion with compact Kähler total space, use T=ω^r for a Kähler form ω. The pushforward is closed and equals the strictly positive fiber integral at every point (for r=0 use the generic degree). Thus strong Hodge descends even in positive relative dimension. More generally any separately closed current with nonzero vertical degree suffices; no positivity required. Without the nonzero vertical-degree hypothesis this proof supplies no retraction and makes no unrestricted descent claim.

Suggested addition: a weighted-transfer corollary to 10.87.1. Its dependencies are the existing current calculus and comparison theorems; neither the Kähler Hodge theorem nor Dolbeault lemma is transitively certified here. The earlier generically finite descent is the r=0 special case, not a second new result.
