## POME-RELATIVE-NUCLEAR-FINITE-MATRIX-MODEL-001

Severity low; status **verified conditional deduction**, not a false printed theorem. The argument below is algebraically complete given the continuous factorization and actual-balanced base-change framework in Lemma 9.46.3.

Anchor: docs/sections/analytic-tensor-products-and-proper-direct-images--relative-nuclearity-and-perturbation/index.html, blob 0a17de6159b423086dcfd306ba0734df57ff3bf7, Lemma 9.46.3 (permanent 5C1AB5BDC27C, source B3B1AC10), lines 58–92, particularly the finite-rank splitting at 74–83 and the actual-quotient argument at 85–91.

**Strengthening.** Under exactly its hypotheses (unital Banach algebra B, Fréchet B-module S, B-nuclear endomorphism v), there exists one finite free B-module B^N and one continuous B-linear endomorphism M of it such that the two-term complex (S, I-v, S) is continuously B-linearly chain homotopy equivalent to (B^N, M, B^N). For every continuous unital homomorphism B -> C into a complete Fréchet algebra, the same maps and homotopies descend to the actual balanced quotient. In particular
\[
\ker(I-v_C)\cong\ker M_C,\qquad
\operatorname{coker}(I-v_C)\cong\operatorname{coker}M_C.
\]
The latter module is finitely **presented**, not merely finitely generated. The identifications are natural under further compatible base changes for which the actual-quotient constructions are identified; no general associativity assertion about non-Hausdorff intermediate quotients is needed.

**Proof.** Use the printed factorization v = beta alpha with alpha:S -> L = l^1(B), beta:L -> S, both continuous module maps, and w = alpha beta. Use cochain degrees 0,1 for the two-term complexes. The maps alpha and beta in both degrees intertwine I-v and I-w. Their compositions are v and w, respectively, in both degrees. Since I-v and I-w are the respective differentials, the difference between the identity chain map and each composition is dH+Hd with H: degree 1 -> degree 0 the identity of the corresponding module. This proves the first chain homotopy equivalence, without a closed-image condition.

Split w = w' + w'' exactly as in lines 76–80, with w'=J Lambda through B^N and ||w''||<1. Set T=I-w'', invertible by the Banach Neumann series, U=J:B^N -> L and V=Lambda T^{-1}:L -> B^N. Then
\[
 I-w=(I-UV)T,\qquad M=I_{B^N}-VU .
\]
The complex with differential I-w is isomorphic to that with differential I-UV by T in degree zero and the identity in degree one. The maps U and V in both degrees intertwine I-UV and M. Again their composites differ from the identities precisely by the two differentials, so the identity maps from degree one to degree zero are the required homotopies. Compose the two equivalences and the intervening isomorphism. All maps, including inverses and homotopies, are continuous and B-linear.

For the change of coefficients, tensor each operator identity on the Hausdorff unbalanced completion C completed-projective-tensor S (or the appropriate other module); then pass to the actual relation image. A continuous B-linear map preserves the completed relation image because it commutes with the relation map before taking the quotient. Thus addition, composition, identities, and all the homotopy identities survive, even when the actual balanced quotient is not Hausdorff. The finite free factor is exactly C^N by the unit/multiplication collapse, including the relation formula at lines 85–91. This proves the assertion, with no tensor operation on an existing non-Hausdorff cohomology group. In particular the induced maps give continuous inverse maps on the kernel and quotient topologies as well, if those topologies are retained.

If B and C are commutative, M is an ordinary N by N matrix and for any such base change, I-v_C is bijective precisely when det(M_C) is a unit of C. Indeed surjectivity of M_C gives a right inverse by lifting the standard basis, hence its determinant is a unit; conversely the adjugate gives an inverse. At scalar specializations the kernel and cokernel dimensions coincide and equal N-rank(M_C), so invertibility is detected by one fixed finite determinant. More generally the cokernel has this square finite presentation over arbitrary C, including the noncommutative module convention, but we do not assert a determinant criterion there. Nor do we claim finite generation of the kernel over an arbitrary noncoherent ring.

**Proposed correction/addition:** replace the finite-generation-only conclusion of 9.46.3 by the stronger two-term model or insert it as a corollary, retaining the actual balanced quotient convention. Affected uses: Theorem 9.46.5 (tag 741AFF17E7BD, lines 100–115) uses only finite generation; Theorem 9.46.7 (71936EFE5494, lines 136–161) uses this perturbation result in top degree, and Theorem 9.47.2 uses the resulting free complex. The finite presentation does NOT automatically descend to the cokernel of the general surjective f minus u in 9.46.5 (the quotient by an additional kernel image may introduce infinitely many relations). No replacement of those claims is proposed.
