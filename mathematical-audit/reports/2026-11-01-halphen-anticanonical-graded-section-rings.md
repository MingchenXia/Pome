## Exact strengthening of the printed proposition

Let \(S\) be the surface obtained from \(\mathbb P^2\) by nine successive blowups centered on the successive strict transforms of a fixed smooth cubic, let \(D\) denote its final strict transform, and let
\[
R(S,D)=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}H^0(S,\mathcal O_S(kD)).
\]
The printed Proposition 12.27.7 establishes that \(D\) is a smooth connected elliptic anticanonical divisor with \(D^2=0\), and identifies semiampleness with torsion of \(N:=\mathcal O_D(D)\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(D)\). Its restrictions and the rationality of \(S\) imply the stronger **exact graded algebra classification**:

1. If \(N\) is **nontorsion**, then
\[
R(S,D)\cong\mathbb C[s],\quad\deg s=1,\qquad
h^0(S,\mathcal O_S(kD))=1\ \ (k\ge0),
\quad\sum_{k\ge0}h^0(kD)z^k=\frac1{1-z}.
\]
Here \(s=s_D\) is the canonical section with zero divisor \(D\); the Iitaka dimension of \(D\) is zero.

2. If \(N\) has **exact finite order \(m\ge1\)**, there is a section \(t\in H^0(S,\mathcal O_S(mD))\) whose restriction to \(D\) never vanishes, and
\[
\boxed{R(S,D)\cong\mathbb C[s,t],\qquad \deg s=1,\ \deg t=m.}
\]
Precisely, for every \(k\ge0\), the set
\[
\{s^{k-mj}t^j:0\le j\le\lfloor k/m\rfloor\}
\]
is a **basis** of \(H^0(S,\mathcal O_S(kD))\). Consequently
\[
\boxed{h^0(S,\mathcal O_S(kD))=\lfloor k/m\rfloor+1,\qquad
\sum_{k\ge0}h^0(kD)z^k=\frac1{(1-z)(1-z^m)}.}
\]
The Iitaka dimension is one, and \(|mD|\) is the pencil generated by \(s^m,t\), agreeing with the printed basepoint-free statement.

This cleanly distinguishes the semiampleness/Iitaka jump from the **same rank-one semipositive curvature and order-one variational-volume degeneration** of Proposition 12.27.5 and Corollary 12.27.6. It is an exact extension of the section-growth information in the printed proposition, not a claim that the printed proposition is wrong.

## Complete algebraic proof

The divisor \(D\) is smooth connected elliptic, and \(N=\mathcal O_D(D)\) has degree zero. Multiplication by the defining section \(s\) gives for every integer \(k\ge1\) the **exact** restriction sequence already printed:
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S((k-1)D)
\xrightarrow{\cdot s}\mathcal O_S(kD)
\longrightarrow N^k\longrightarrow0. \tag{1}
\]
Because \(D\) is elliptic and \(N^k\) has degree zero,
\[
h^0(D,N^k)=\begin{cases}1,&N^k\simeq\mathcal O_D,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases} \tag{2}
\]
Indeed any nonzero section of a degree-zero bundle has an effective zero divisor of degree zero, hence is nowhere zero and trivializes the bundle. Taking \(H^0\) in (1) yields the inequality
\[
h^0(kD)\le h^0((k-1)D)+h^0(D,N^k). \tag{3}
\]
Also \(h^0(S,\mathcal O_S)=1\), since \(S\) is connected and compact.

For **nontorsion \(N\)**, the last term of (3) always vanishes. Multiplication by \(s\) is injective and hence an isomorphism on global sections for every \(k\). Induction gives \(H^0(kD)=\mathbb C s^k\). Multiplication respects these identifications, proving \(R(S,D)=\mathbb C[s]\), with no appeal to any analytic metric argument.

For **torsion of exact order \(m\)**, \(H^0(D,N^k)=0\) unless \(m\mid k\), so (3) gives the **upper bound**
\[
h^0(kD)\le1+\lfloor k/m\rfloor. \tag{4}
\]
It remains to produce an independent section of degree \(m\) and prove the upper bound is attained in all degrees. On the elliptic curve, Serre duality and \(K_D\simeq\mathcal O_D\) imply \(H^1(D,N^k)=0\) for \(1\le k<m\). Since \(S\) is a rational surface, \(H^1(S,\mathcal O_S)=0\). Starting at \(k=0\) and applying the long exact sequences of (1) for \(1\le k<m\), induction gives
\[
H^1(S,\mathcal O_S(kD))=0\quad(0\le k<m).
\]
For \(k=m\), the associated connecting map \(H^0(D,N^m)\to H^1(S,\mathcal O_S((m-1)D))\) is therefore zero. Choose \(t\in H^0(S,\mathcal O_S(mD))\) lifting a nonzero constant section of \(N^m\simeq\mathcal O_D\). Its restriction is nowhere zero; in particular,
\[
\operatorname{ord}_D(t)=0,\qquad\operatorname{ord}_D(s)=1. \tag{5}
\]
For each \(0\le j\le\lfloor k/m\rfloor\), the monomial \(s^{k-mj}t^j\) belongs to \(H^0(kD)\) and has vanishing order **exactly** \(k-mj\) along the irreducible divisor \(D\). These orders are pairwise different. In any linear combination, the term of *smallest* vanishing order cannot cancel with terms of higher order, so the monomials are linearly independent. Their number equals the upper bound in (4); hence they are a basis. Every homogeneous element of \(R(S,D)\) is thus a polynomial in \(s,t\), and no nonzero homogeneous polynomial relation is possible. A general polynomial relation decomposes into homogeneous components, so there are no relations at all. This proves the graded polynomial-ring description and the exact Hilbert function. Summing the monomial dimensions gives
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}h^0(kD)z^k
=\sum_{a,b\ge0}z^{a+mb}
=\frac1{(1-z)(1-z^m)}.
\]
For \(m=1\), the same lifting starts immediately from \(H^1(S,\mathcal O_S)=0\), so the proof covers the untwisted elliptic pencil without a special argument.

No sequential compactness, curvature approximation, \(L^2\) method, or passage between noncommuting limits occurs in this calculation.
