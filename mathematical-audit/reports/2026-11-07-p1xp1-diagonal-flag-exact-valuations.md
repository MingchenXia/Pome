## Statement of the verified model

Let $X=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, $D\subset X$ the diagonal (a smooth curve of bidegree $(1,1)$), $p$ any point of $D$, and use the admissible complete flag $X\supset D\supset\{p\}$. Let $L=\mathcal O_X(a,b)$, where $a,b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$; set $m_0=\min(a,b)$ and $A=a+b$. For the complete section algebra $W_k=H^0(X,L^k)$ the valuation semigroup, written as $(s,j,k)$, is **exactly**

$$
S=\{(0,0,0)\}\ \cup\
\bigl\{(s,j,k)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^3:
k\geq1,\ 0\leq s\leq km_0,\ 0\leq j\leq kA-2s\bigr\}.
\tag{1}
$$

Moreover **every element is a sum of degree-one elements**. Consequently the Okounkov body, including non-big edge cases, is precisely

$$
\Delta(L;D,p)=
\bigl\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:
0\leq x\leq m_0,\quad 0\leq y\leq A-2x\bigr\}.
\tag{2}
$$

For every integer $k\geq0$, the valuation count and actual dimension agree **without an error term**:

$$
\#S_k=\sum_{s=0}^{km_0}(kA-2s+1)
=(km_0+1)\bigl(k(A-m_0)+1\bigr)
=(ka+1)(kb+1)=\dim H^0(X,L^k).
\tag{3}
$$

For $a,b>0$ the degree period is $e=1$, the horizontal group is the full $\mathbb Z^2$ and $\operatorname{area}(\Delta)=m_0(A-m_0)=ab$; the leading term of (3) is $abk^2=\frac{c_1(L)^2}{2}k^2$. For exactly one of $a,b$ equal to zero and the other $c>0$, the body is the vertical segment of **intrinsic lattice length $c$**, horizontal lattice $\mathbb Z(0,1)$, growth rank $d=1$, and $\dim W_k=ck+1$. For $a=b=0$ the body is a point, $d=0$ and $\dim W_k=1$. In all cases $e=1$. These cases distinguish intrinsic dimension and intrinsic lattice measure from ambient two-dimensional area.

## Proof (direct, without asymptotic semigroup or Bergman inputs)

Write the product homogeneous coordinates as $([X_0:X_1],[Y_0:Y_1])$ and the diagonal equation as $G=X_0Y_1-X_1Y_0$. The homogeneous sections of $L^k$ are precisely bihomogeneous polynomials $F$ of bidegree $(ka,kb)$. Since the ideal of the irreducible smooth diagonal is generated by $G$, the first flag value is exactly the largest $s$ for which $F=G^sQ$; the residual bidegree is $(u,v)=(ka-s,kb-s)$. Thus $s\leq k\min(a,b)$, and any nonzero residual restriction $Q|_D$ is a homogeneous binary form of degree

$$
u+v=k(a+b)-2s.
$$

Conversely, for every $0\leq s\leq km_0$, both $u,v$ are nonnegative. The restriction map

$$
H^0(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(u,v))
\longrightarrow H^0(D,\mathcal O_D(u+v))
$$

is surjective **elementarily**: after identifying $D\simeq\mathbb P^1$, every binary monomial of degree $u+v$ can be factored into one monomial of degree $u$ and one of degree $v$, and the products span all binary forms of degree $u+v$. For a coordinate $[T_0:T_1]$ with $p=[1:0]$, the form $T_0^{u+v-j}T_1^j$ has vanishing order exactly $j$ at $p$, for each $0\leq j\leq u+v$. Choose a lift $Q$ and put $F=G^sQ$; then $Q|_D\neq0$, so the first value is **exactly** $s$, and the second is $j$. This proves both inclusions in (1). It also shows that no auxiliary divisor or cohomological vanishing input is needed.

For degree-one generation, take $(s,j,k)\in S$ with $k>0$. Because $0\leq s\leq km_0$, choose $s_1,\ldots,s_k\in[0,m_0]\cap\mathbb Z$ with $\sum s_i=s$. Their capacities $c_i=A-2s_i$ are nonnegative integers with sum $kA-2s$. Since $0\leq j\leq\sum c_i$, choose $j_i\in[0,c_i]\cap\mathbb Z$ with $\sum j_i=j$ (greedily filling capacities). Each $(s_i,j_i,1)$ occurs as the valuation of a degree-one section by the preceding paragraph. Multiplicativity of the leading lexicographic term makes the product have valuation $(s,j,k)$, proving degree-one generation. Every integral point in the cone (1) is realized, so the semigroup is saturated in its generated group.

Summing the lengths of the integer intervals in (1) gives (3). Degree-one generation shows that the height-one section of the semigroup cone is the convex hull of all degree-one values; it is the polygon/segment/point (2). If $a,b>0$, $(0,0,1)$, $(0,1,1)$ and $(1,0,1)$ all occur, so differences generate both coordinate unit vectors horizontally and the whole group is $\mathbb Z^3$. If exactly one of $a,b$ is nonzero, first valuations are always zero, and the second values $0,1$ in degree one give the horizontal lattice $\mathbb Z(0,1)$; when both vanish, it is zero. Direct integration of (2), or its intrinsic length/point convention, yields the preceding volumes and agrees with (3). This completes the proof.
