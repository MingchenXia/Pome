## Complete replacement theorem in exactly the project's pointed-cone setting

Fix \(n\geq0\), a closed convex cone
\[
 C\subseteq \mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R_{\geq0},
 \qquad C\cap\{t=0\}=\{0\},
\]
and **any** additive subsemigroup \(S\subseteq C\cap\mathbb Z^{n+1}\). Write \(S_k=\{x\in\mathbb Z^n:(x,k)\in S\}\), \(G=\operatorname{gp}(S)\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n+1}\), and \(d=\operatorname{rank}G\).

1. If \(d\leq n\), then \(k^{-n}|S_k|\to0\) through **all** positive integers.
2. If \(d=n+1\), let \(\operatorname{pr}_t(G)=m\mathbb Z\), \(m\geq1\), and let
   \[
   H=\{x\in\mathbb Z^n:(x,0)\in G\},\qquad
   q=[\mathbb Z^n:H]<\infty,\qquad
   K=\{x:(x,1)\in C(S)\},
   \]
   where \(C(S)\) is the closed convex cone generated by \(S\cup\{0\}\).
   Then \(K\) is a full-dimensional compact convex body, \(S_k=\varnothing\) when \(m\nmid k\), and
   \[
   \boxed{\lim_{\substack{k\to\infty\\m\mid k}}k^{-n}|S_k|
        =\frac{\operatorname{Leb}_n(K)}q.}
   \]
   Consequently, **for every positive integer \(a\) divisible by \(m\)**,
   \[
   \boxed{\lim_{j\to\infty}(ja)^{-n}|S_{ja}|
     =\varlimsup_{k\to\infty}k^{-n}|S_k|
     =\frac{\operatorname{Leb}_n(K)}q.}
   \]
   In particular, when \(G=\mathbb Z^{n+1}\), \(m=q=1\), giving exactly the printed full-lattice formula.

This proves *the entire counting assertion actually quoted in Theorem 19.7.20 under its printed cone hypothesis*, without a separate KK12 input. It is **not** an assertion about arbitrary non-pointed semigroups, or a replacement for every theorem of KK12. The normalization uses the ambient \(\mathbb Z^n\) Lebesgue measure; the factor \(q\) cannot be omitted.

### Step 1: finite-generator conductor for an arbitrary group \(G\)

Suppose \(d=n+1\). For every compact \(E\Subset\operatorname{int}K\), we prove that there exists \(k_E\) such that
\[
 (x,k)\in G,\quad k\geq k_E,\quad x/k\in E
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (x,k)\in S. \tag{*}
\]
Choose finitely many points of \(S\) whose **real positive cone** contains \(E\times\{1\}\) in its interior. Here are the details: each \(v\in E\times\{1\}\) is interior to the closure of the positive cone of \(S\); choose a small simplex around \(v\) whose vertices remain in that interior, approximate its vertices by finite positive combinations of elements of \(S\), and use compactness of \(E\) to select finitely many such simplices. In addition choose finitely many elements of \(S\) generating the abelian group \(G\), possible because every subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^{n+1}\) is finitely generated. Discard any zero generator and let \(F=\{f_1,\ldots,f_r\}\subset S\) contain both finite families. Set \(D=\sum_i\mathbb R_{\geq0}f_i\) and \(M=\sum_i\mathbb N f_i\), the monoid of finite nonnegative sums. All \(f_i\) have positive degree, and **every nonzero element of \(M\) lies in \(S\)**, whether or not the original definition of subsemigroup requires \(0\in S\). Thus \(\operatorname{gp}(F)=G\) and \(E\times\{1\}\Subset\operatorname{int}D\).

For any \(y\in D\cap G\), choose nonnegative real coefficients \(t_i\) with \(y=\sum_i t_if_i\). Then
\[
 y=\sum_i\lfloor t_i\rfloor f_i+r_y,\qquad
 r_y=\sum_i\{t_i\}f_i\in R:=
 G\cap\Bigl\{\sum_i u_if_i:0\leq u_i\leq1\Bigr\}.
\]
The bounded set \(R\) is **finite**, since \(G\) is discrete. Each \(r\in R\) is an integral linear combination \(\sum_i z_{r,i}f_i\), because \(F\) generates \(G\) *as a group*. Select an integer \(N\geq1\) with \(N+z_{r,i}\geq1\) for every \(r,i\), and put \(c=N\sum_i f_i\in S\). Then \(c+r\in M\setminus\{0\}\subseteq S\) for every \(r\in R\), and consequently
\[
 \boxed{c+(D\cap G)\subseteq M\setminus\{0\}\subseteq S.} \tag{conductor}
\]
This is a finite-residue proof; no normality, finite generation of the original \(S\), KK12, or affine semigroup theorem is assumed.

Since \(E\times\{1\}\) is compact inside the *open* cone \(\operatorname{int}D\), there is \(\eta>0\) such that the Euclidean \(\eta\)-balls about its points lie in \(D\). If \(k>\|c\|/\eta\), then for every \(y=(x,k)\in G\) with \(x/k\in E\),
\[
 y-c=k((x/k,1)-c/k)\in D\cap G.
\]
The conductor inclusion implies \(y\in S\). This proves (*), uniformly on \(E\), even when \(G\ne\mathbb Z^{n+1}\).

### Step 2: shifted-lattice asymptotics with the correct index

When \(d=n+1\), the degree projection of \(G\) is \(m\mathbb Z\) and its horizontal kernel \(H\) has rank \(n\). For every \(k\in m\mathbb Z\), the set of first coordinates of \(G\) at height \(k\) is one affine coset \(x_k+H\) of \(H\). Its covolume in \(\mathbb R^n\) is \(q=[\mathbb Z^n:H]\). In particular, \([\mathbb Z^{n+1}:G]=mq\), by the short exact sequence of horizontal kernels and degree quotients.

For a full-dimensional compact convex body \(A\subset\mathbb R^n\), fundamental-parallelepiped tiling of \(x_k+H\) yields **uniformly in the translate \(x_k\)**,
\[
 \#(kA\cap(x_k+H))
    =\frac{\operatorname{Leb}_n(A)}q\,k^n+o(k^n). \tag{lattice}
\]
Indeed, fix a bounded fundamental parallelepiped \(P\) for \(H\), volume \(q\), diameter \(R\). The number of tiles whose anchors lie in \(kA\), times \(q\), lies between the volumes of the inner \(R\)-parallel set and the outer \(R\)-parallel set of \(kA\). After division by \(k^n\) these are volumes of \(A_{R/k}\) and \(A^{R/k}\), which tend to \(\operatorname{Leb}_n(A)\), since the boundary of a full-dimensional convex body has zero Lebesgue measure. The comparison is independent of the affine shift. **If \(A\) has empty interior**, its Lebesgue volume is zero and the *upper* tile bound alone gives \(0\leq k^{-n}\#(kA\cap(x_k+H))\leq q^{-1}\operatorname{Leb}_n(A^{R/k})\to0\). Thus the lattice-count conclusion applies to every nonempty compact convex \(A\), including lower-dimensional bodies. For \(H=\mathbb Z^n\) and \(q=1\), this separately proves the final arbitrary-convex-body lattice statement of printed Theorem 19.7.20. The same argument applies to \(n=0\), with one lattice point per permitted degree and \(\operatorname{Leb}_0\) of a nonempty point equal to \(1\).

For a compact convex \(E\Subset\operatorname{int}K\), Step 1 and the obvious inclusion \(S_k\subseteq kK\cap(x_k+H)\) imply, for all sufficiently large \(k\in m\mathbb Z\),
\[
 \#(kE\cap(x_k+H))\leq |S_k|
 \leq\#(kK\cap(x_k+H)).
\]
Apply (lattice), then exhaust \(\operatorname{int}K\) by compact convex bodies \(E\) of volumes tending to \(\operatorname{Leb}_n(K)\). This proves the boxed equality for multiples of \(m\). Degrees not in \(m\mathbb Z\) are empty, hence the full limsup is exactly the same value.

### Step 3: the rank-deficient case

If \(G=\{0\}\), there are no positive-degree elements. Otherwise the cone hypothesis forces the degree projection of \(G\) to have rank one (all elements of \(C\) of degree zero are zero). The horizontal subgroup \(H=G\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\{0\})\) has rank \(d-1\leq n-1\). For every \(k\) with \(S_k\ne\varnothing\), its elements lie in an affine coset of \(H\) inside \(kP_C\), where the level-one slice \(P_C=\{x:(x,1)\in C\}\) is compact by the pointedness assumption. Relative to a fixed basis of \(H\), every coordinate difference between two points in \(S_k\) is \(O(k)\). Thus
\[
 |S_k|=O(k^{d-1})=o(k^n)
\]
uniformly over all positive integers. This proves part 1, with no case-by-case periodicity argument.

## Sharp model: why “sufficiently divisible” and the index are both necessary

Take \(n=1\), \(S=\mathbb N(0,2)+\mathbb N(2,2)\subset\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\), and any pointed cone \(C\) containing its two rays. Then \(G=2\mathbb Z\times2\mathbb Z\), \(m=2\), \(H=2\mathbb Z\), \(q=2\), and \(K=[0,1]\). At \(k=2j\),
\[
 S_{2j}=\{0,2,\ldots,2j\},\qquad
 \frac{|S_{2j}|}{2j}=\frac{j+1}{2j}\longrightarrow\frac12
 =\frac{\operatorname{Leb}_1(K)}q.
\]
At odd levels \(S_k\) is empty. In particular, the normalized counts along all \(k\) do **not** converge; for \(a=1\) the printed “sufficiently divisible” limit would fail, while for every even \(a\) it exists. Treating the index as \(1\) would also produce the wrong value. This is a test of the *hypotheses*, not a counterexample to the printed theorem.
