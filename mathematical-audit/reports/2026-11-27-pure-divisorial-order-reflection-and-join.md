## Deduction

Let X be connected compact complex and let P be ordinary singularity types of quasi-PSH functions with the printed order a <= b + O(1). Let D be its subposet of pure divisorial types: a representative v has dd^c v = S - beta, beta smooth, S a positive finite-mass countable sum of distinct prime divisor integration currents (allow S=0). The following uses the ordinary-order part of 5.38.5 and existence/uniqueness of 5.38.6; it does not require any approximation or a new proof of the valuative multiplier-ideal criterion.

For a quasi-PSH a choose a Hermitian Lambda with Lambda+dd^c a >= 0. For every finite set of prime divisors, 5.38.1 bounds the corresponding sum of generic Lelong components by this positive current. Integrating against Lambda^(n-1) bounds the sum of their positive masses uniformly; consequently only countably many divisors have positive coefficients (for each positive integer k only finitely many have weighted mass >= 1/k). The increasing sum S_a of these components is positive, closed and of finite mass; convergence follows from summability of the trace norms of its summands. Let R(a) denote its pure type by 5.38.6 (zero gives the smooth type). Ordinary domination implies reverse inequalities of the generic Lelong numbers. Conversely, against a pure target, these inequalities imply ordinary domination by the finite-removal/limit argument of 5.38.5. In particular a <= R(a), and for every pure d,
 
    a <= d  iff  R(a) <= d.

Thus R is the order reflection onto D (a left adjoint to inclusion), and R restricts to the identity there. The formula depends only on the ordinary type. No fixed background or fixed mass bound is asserted for the entire poset.

For two pure types represented by coefficient families c_D,d_D (extend by zero on their countable union), the coefficientwise maximum and minimum both give finite-mass closed positive sums, dominated by the sum of the two currents. By 5.38.5 and 5.38.6 the corresponding types are respectively the meet and join **inside D**, with the order reversed on coefficients. The meet is also a greatest lower bound in the full ordinary poset: a is below both pure types iff its generic coefficients dominate both, iff it is below the pure type of the coefficientwise maximum. The join in the full poset always exists and is the type of max(u,v), a quasi-PSH representative after choosing a common smooth lower curvature bound. Its generic coefficients are the minima, so applying R to the full join gives the pure join. But the full join need not itself be pure.

## Explicit counterexample, even in dimension one

On P^1 take the Fubini–Study form omega of integral 1 and normalized Green kernel G(z,p)=log(chordal_distance(z,p)^2), nonpositive and dd^c_z G=delta_p-omega in the collection's convention. For j>=1 put p_j=exp(-4^j), q_j=-exp(-4^j) in the affine coordinate and w_j=2^(-j). Let

    u = sum_j w_j G(.,p_j),   v = sum_j w_j G(.,q_j).

Partial sums decrease and have dd^c bounded below by -omega. Rotation invariance of the kernel integral gives a finite uniform L^1 bound, so these limits are non-identically-minus-infinity omega-PSH functions, and distributional differentiation gives dd^c u=sum_j w_j delta_pj-omega, and the analogous formula for v. Hence they are pure divisorial representatives. Neither curvature has an atom at 0; the two lists of atoms are disjoint. At 0, G(0,p_j)=log(|p_j|^2/(1+|p_j|^2))<=-2*4^j, and the same holds for q_j. Both series diverge to minus infinity at 0. The full join max(u,v) is therefore unbounded below (upper semicontinuity at 0), while its Lelong numbers are zero at every point: on a curve they are the atoms of the curvature, and the Lelong number of the maximum is the minimum. Its pure reflection is the bounded type. The bounded type is the join inside D but strictly larger than the full join. Thus the inclusion D -> P preserves binary meets, not binary joins, even on P^1. This is not a counterexample to 5.38.5: the ordinary-order test there requires the target to be pure.

For completeness, max(u,v) has trivial multiplier ideals at every positive weight. Locally a subharmonic correction of it has zero atom at the point, so the mass m of its local Riesz measure in a sufficiently small disk is less than 1/t. On a smaller disk its potential equals a bounded harmonic term plus the logarithmic potential of that measure (up to smooth terms). Jensen for the normalized measure bounds exp(-t times the logarithmic potential) by the average of |z-w|^(-2tm), whose disk integral is bounded uniformly in w since tm<1. If m=0 the assertion is immediate. This proves local integrability without the excluded approximation programme. The counterexample thus also exhibits strict ordinary singularity with trivial ideal type, arising specifically as the maximum of two pure atomic types, rather than from an independently imposed diffuse residual.
