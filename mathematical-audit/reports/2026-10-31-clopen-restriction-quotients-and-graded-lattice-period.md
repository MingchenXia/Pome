## POME-LCS-CLOPEN-DENSE-QUOTIENT-001 — low / verified conditional deduction; no printed theorem error

**Production anchor:** `docs/sections/completions-and-locally-convex-quotients/index.html`, blob `e2c6d25d35020ccefa739f04aa893e173f73c17e`, printed Lemma **2.23.3**, permanent tag `5F1410BEA2F7`, source label `demailly-complex-geometry:69616994`, physical lines **51–90** (restriction and openness 71–80; ordinal-plank nonextension and directed-net limit 81–89). **Statement (new exact generalization):** Let X be a locally compact Hausdorff zero-dimensional space with a basis of compact clopen sets, A a closed subset of X, and let C_k(Y) be the continuous complex functions on Y with the compact-open locally convex topology. The restriction map r:C_k(X)→C_k(A) has dense range F. The map r:C_k(X)→F, with F carrying the **subspace topology inherited from C_k(A)**, is a continuous, open, surjective linear map. Both C_k(X) and C_k(A) are complete and Hausdorff, and the completion of F is canonically C_k(A). Consequently F is complete **if and only if** every continuous function on A extends to X. In the ordinal-plank example of Lemma 2.23.3, F is a proper dense subspace and is incomplete; its completion is explicitly C_k(A).

**Proof of completeness:** If (f_i) is a Cauchy net for all compact supremum seminorms, then each scalar f_i(x) converges. The limit f converges uniformly on each compact K, by fixing a Cauchy tail and letting the second index tend to its scalar limit pointwise. Every x lies in a compact neighborhood; the uniform limit on that neighborhood is continuous. Thus f∈C_k(Y) and the original net converges to f. Applied to X and to its closed subspace A (itself locally compact), this proves completeness. Do not replace this arbitrary-net proof with a sequential argument.

**Proof that F is dense:** Fix g∈C(A), a compact K⊂A and ε>0. For each a∈K choose an ambient open U_a such that |g(y)−g(a)|<ε for y∈A∩U_a. Choose a compact clopen neighborhood V_a with a∈V_a⊂U_a. Finitely many V_{a_1},...,V_{a_N} cover K. Put D_i=V_{a_i} minus the union of preceding V_{a_j}; the D_i are pairwise disjoint compact clopen sets. The ambient continuous step function h=Σ_i g(a_i)1_{D_i}, zero outside their union, satisfies |h−g|<ε on K. Because every compact-open neighborhood is controlled by finitely many compact sets (replace them by their union), r(C_k(X)) is dense in C_k(A), without assuming every g extends.

**Proof that r is open onto F:** For any compact K⊂X, consider the basic source neighborhood U={h: sup_K|h|<1}. Take g∈F with sup_{K∩A}|g|<1 and any extension h∈C(X). Choose c strictly between this supremum and 1 (when K∩A is empty, choose any 0<c<1). The closed set D=A∩{|h|≥c} is disjoint from K. Compactness of K and the compact-clopen basis give a compact clopen W⊃K with W∩D=∅. Let T_c(z)=z for |z|≤c and T_c(z)=cz/|z| otherwise; define h'=1_W T_c(h)+(1−1_W)h. This is continuous, agrees with h on A, and obeys sup_K|h'|≤c<1. Therefore r(U) contains {g∈F: sup_{K∩A}|g|<1}, a zero neighborhood in F. The same for scaled K-seminorm balls proves openness. This proof does not rely on a false general open mapping theorem for arbitrary complete locally convex spaces.

**Completion and sharpness:** Restriction is continuous, and the preceding density identifies completion(F) with C_k(A), since the latter is complete. If F were also complete, its inclusion as a Hausdorff locally convex subspace would have closed image, forcing F=C_k(A). Conversely surjective restriction gives F=C_k(A) complete. The ordinal-plank X,A of printed Lemma 2.23.3 provide a *proper* F: the continuous edge function b=1 on H and b=0 on V cannot extend (the uncountable-cofinality argument in printed lines 81–82), whereas the directed family h_β of clopen-step extensions converges to b in C_k(A) (lines 83–89). In particular, the limit is a genuine net indexed by β<ω_1; no countable cofinality or unjustified sequence extraction is asserted.

**Exact model:** Let X=P¹, L=O(1), p=[1:0] the flag point, and use homogeneous coordinates [x:y]. Define the graded subalgebra W_0=C, W_m=0 in odd degree, and
W_{2q}=span_C{x^{2q−2j}y^{2j}:0≤j≤q}.
Products preserve these even exponents, so this is a genuine graded subalgebra of the complete section ring. The valuation at p of the displayed monomial is 2j. Thus the degree-2q valuation set is exactly {0,2,...,2q}, with dim W_{2q}=q+1. The valuation semigroup is generated by (0,2) and (2,2), and its group G has projected degree group 2Z. Its period is e=2, its horizontal difference lattice is Λ=2Z, its intrinsic dimension d=1, and its convex Okounkov body in the ambient real coordinate is Δ=[0,1]. The Euclidean length of Δ is 1 but its **Λ-normalized** length is 1/2.

Consequently
lim_{q→∞} dim W_{2q}/(2q)=(1/2)=vol_Λ(Δ),
whereas dim W_m/m is zero for all odd m and tends to 1/2 on even m. Thus the unrestricted degree-m limit does not exist, and naively substituting ambient Euclidean length 1 gives an incorrect leading coefficient. This verifies separately and concretely the necessity of both the degree-period restriction and intrinsic-lattice normalization in the *correct* printed Theorem 19.9.9. The section-ratio field here is C((y/x)^2), of transcendence degree one as independently predicted by Proposition 19.9.10.
