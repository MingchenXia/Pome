## 1. Precise new statement

Fix \(N\ge1\) and let
\[
\Phi_N:\mathbb C\{x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1}\}\longrightarrow\mathbb C\{t\},
\qquad x_i\longmapsto t^{N+i}.
\]
Write \(I_N=\ker\Phi_N\). For every \(1\le i\le j\le N-1\), set
\[
F_{ij}=
\begin{cases}
x_ix_j-x_0x_{i+j},&i+j<N,\\
x_ix_j-x_0^2x_{i+j-N},&i+j\ge N.
\end{cases}
\]
The second formula includes \(i+j=N\), where the correction is \(x_0^3\). For \(N=1\), there are no such pairs.

Then the **full convergent-power-series defining ideal**, not just its radical or polynomial part, is
\[
\boxed{I_N=(F_{ij}:1\le i\le j\le N-1).}
\]
Its **minimal number of analytic ideal generators at the origin** is exactly
\[
\boxed{\mu(I_N)=\binom N2=\frac{N(N-1)}2.}
\]
Consequently the curve is a local ideal-theoretic complete intersection **if and only if \(N\le2\)**: \(N=1\) is smooth, \(N=2\) is the plane cusp \(x_1^2=x_0^3\), and every \(N\ge3\) requires strictly more than its codimension \(N-1\) generators. In particular, for \(N=3\) the list is precisely
\[
x_1^2-x_0x_2,\quad x_1x_2-x_0^3,\quad x_2^2-x_0^2x_1,
\]
the three equations already printed in Example 9.60.2. That special case is **not recounted as a novel finding**; the all-\(N\) ideal and sharp minimal-generator count are new to this audit.

## 2. Complete kernel proof, including analytic convergence

Each \(F_{ij}\) belongs to \(\ker\Phi_N\), by equality of its two \(t\)-exponents. Let \(J\) be the ideal generated by all \(F_{ij}\).

Start with a monomial \(x^\alpha\) of ordinary total degree \(d\). Whenever it has two factors \(x_i,x_j\) with \(i,j\ge1\), replace their product using \(F_{ij}\). The right side is \(x_0x_{i+j}\), respectively \(x_0^2x_{i+j-N}\). In either case the number of factors whose index is positive drops by at least one, and the ordinary total degree never decreases (it increases by one only in the second case). In at most \(d\) steps, the monomial therefore becomes exactly one of
\[
x_0^a,\qquad x_0^a x_i\quad(1\le i<N),
\]
modulo a finite sum of multiples of the \(F_{ij}\). Throughout this reduction, the degree lies between \(d\) and \(2d\); every coefficient monomial multiplying an \(F_{ij}\) has degree at least \(d-2\).

This reduction is valid for **convergent germs**, not just polynomials. Indeed, let \(f=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha x^\alpha\) converge absolutely on a polydisc of radius \(R>0\). Fix \(0<\rho<\min(R,1)\). Each input monomial of degree \(d\ge2\) produces at most \(d\) relation-coefficient monomials, each of degree at least \(d-2\), and one normal-form monomial of degree at least \(d\). Their \(\rho\)-norms are thus bounded by \(d\rho^{d-2}\) and \(\rho^d\), respectively. After slightly shrinking the original convergence radius if needed,
\[
\sum_\alpha |c_\alpha|(1+|\alpha|)\rho^{|\alpha|}<\infty.
\]
Summing the coefficient and normal-form terms absolutely consequently produces convergent germs \(H_{ij}(x)\) and convergent \(A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1}\in\mathbb C\{x_0\}\) with
\[
f=\sum_{i\le j}F_{ij}H_{ij}
   +A_0(x_0)+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}x_iA_i(x_0).
\]

Under \(\Phi_N\), the exponents of \(A_0(t^N)\) are divisible by \(N\), whereas those of \(t^{N+i}A_i(t^N)\) are congruent to \(i\pmod N\). For \(1\le i<N\), these residue classes are pairwise disjoint and distinct from \(0\). Hence a vanishing image forces **all** the convergent coefficients \(A_i\) to vanish. Thus \(\ker\Phi_N=J\) as ideals of convergent germs. This argument also applies directly at \(N=1\), with an empty relation family and an injective map.

The image algebra is exactly the printed \(\mathbb C+t^N\mathbb C\{t\}\): every exponent \(m\ge N\) is in the numerical semigroup generated by \(N,\ldots,2N-1\), and the same residue-class decomposition with convergent coefficients proves surjectivity onto that subalgebra. **No analytic approximation or completion-only argument is used.**

## 3. Sharp generator count and complete-intersection boundary

The homomorphism has no linear kernel: the \(N\) monomials \(t^N,\ldots,t^{2N-1}\) have distinct orders. Thus \(I_N\subset\mathfrak n^2\), for \(\mathfrak n=(x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1})\), and \(\mathfrak nI_N\subset\mathfrak n^3\).

The degree-two initial form of \(F_{ij}\) is \(x_ix_j-x_0x_{i+j}\) when \(i+j<N\), and \(x_ix_j\) when \(i+j\ge N\). These \(\binom N2\) quadrics are linearly independent: each has its own coefficient \(1\) at the distinct monomial \(x_ix_j\) with both indices positive, while its remaining term, if any, contains \(x_0\). Therefore their classes in \(I_N/\mathfrak n I_N\) are linearly independent. The displayed generators also span that quotient, proving
\[
\dim_\mathbb C I_N/\mathfrak n I_N=\binom N2
\]
by Nakayama's lemma (or the elementary local-minimality criterion). Since the quotient ring has complex dimension one by the explicit parametrization and finite normalization, the ideal's height is \(N-1\). Hence \(\mu(I_N)>N-1\) precisely when \(N\ge3\), proving the claimed complete-intersection classification.

This is an **ideal-theoretic** statement. It does not assert that these curves cannot be set-theoretic complete intersections; the printed distinction between the two notions must be preserved.

## 4. Direct analytic-image crosscheck and dependencies

For \(N\ge2\), the common zero set of the displayed **polynomial** \(F_{ij}\) is also directly seen to equal the parametrized curve, without invoking the proper-image theorem. If \(x_0=0\), every diagonal relation \(F_{ii}=0\) forces \(x_i=0\). If \(x_0\ne0\), let \(t=x_1/x_0\). The relations \(F_{1,i-1}=0\) successively imply \(x_i=x_0t^i\) for \(2\le i<N\); the final relation \(F_{1,N-1}=0\) implies \(x_0=t^N\). This also works at \(N=2\) using \(F_{11}\). Thus every zero is \((t^N,\ldots,t^{2N-1})\). For \(N=1\) the statement is immediate. This is an explicit alternate proof of analyticity for **this special family**, not a proof of general Remmert proper-image theory, and is not counted again as a new repair if a previous audit already supplied such an argument.
