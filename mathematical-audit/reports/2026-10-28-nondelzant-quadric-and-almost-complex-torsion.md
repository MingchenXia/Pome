## POME-TORIC-NONDELZANT-QUADRIC-CONE-001 — low / verified conditional deduction; original smooth-polytope theorem correct

**Exact integrated source:** `docs/sections/convex-analysis-and-toric-potentials--smooth-lattice-polytopes-and-projective-charts/index.html`, main blob `f442a7b663689b97a9b89689b8b7ae8b366a53fe`. Take \(M=\mathbb Z^2\) and the full-dimensional integral triangle
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,1)\}.
\]
Its lattice points are precisely \(A=\{(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(0,1)\}\). At the vertex \(v=(0,1)\), the primitive inward edge directions are \(e_1=(0,-1)\) and \(e_2=(2,-1)\). Their determinant is \(2\), so they **do not** form a basis of \(M\): the Delzant hypothesis of Definition 21.8.1 fails. (The other two vertices do have unimodular primitive edge bases.)

In the character coordinates \(x,y\in\mathbb C^*\), the projective torus map constructed in Theorem 21.8.3 is
\[
  (x,y)\longmapsto[Z_0:Z_1:Z_2:Z_3]=[1:x:x^2:y]\in\mathbb P^3.
\]
Its image satisfies \(Z_0Z_2=Z_1^2\). Conversely, the part of the projective quadric \(Y=\{Z_0Z_2=Z_1^2\}\) with \(Z_0Z_1Z_3\ne0\) is precisely this torus image: on \(Z_0\ne0\), take \(x=Z_1/Z_0\) and \(y=Z_3/Z_0\). The polynomial \(Z_0Z_2-Z_1^2\) is irreducible, so that nonempty Zariski-open subset is dense in \(Y\); hence \(X_P=\overline{\iota(T)}=Y\).

At the torus-fixed point corresponding to \(v\), namely \([0:0:0:1]\), use the affine chart \(Z_3\ne0\) with coordinates \(a=Z_0/Z_3\), \(b=Z_1/Z_3\), \(c=Z_2/Z_3\). The germ of \(X_P\) is
\[
  ac=b^2\quad\text{in }\mathbb C^3.
\]
The gradient of the defining function \(ac-b^2\) is \((c,-2b,a)\), which vanishes at \((a,b,c)=(0,0,0)\). Since the irreducible hypersurface has dimension two, its tangent space at this point has dimension three, so it is singular. Thus the conclusion of Theorem 21.8.3 that \(X_P\) is a complex submanifold with affine \(\mathbb C^n\) vertex charts **cannot** extend to arbitrary full-dimensional integral polytopes after dropping smoothness. The original theorem explicitly assumes smoothness and is not false.

## POME-ALMOSTCOMPLEX-TORSION-BRACKET-001 — low / verified conditional deduction; printed type-decomposition proposition correct

**Exact integrated source:** `docs/sections/formal-integrability-and-smooth-complex-coordinates/index.html`, main blob `218b71104a05350a9bb37b63c64638efecbd1e42`. Proposition 9.53.2: source `demailly-complex-geometry:EF13FDD7`, permanent tag `7D9D38C5DA0E`, physical lines 10–42. Its equation (9.53.3) has source `demailly-complex-geometry:DAF605C6`, tag `A64B66D1EDF0`, physical lines 15–19; formal-integrability equation (9.53.4), source `demailly-complex-geometry:2F4BFF1E`, tag `3BE881B07623`, lines 20–25.

Work on the smooth real manifold underlying \(\mathbb C^2\), with standard real coordinates \(z_1,z_2\), but **do not** use the standard complex structure. Specify a smooth complex rank-two distribution
\[
 T^{1,0}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\left\{
 X=\partial_{z_1},\quad Y=\partial_{z_2}+z_1\partial_{\bar z_1}
 \right\}.
\]
Its complex conjugate is generated by \(\bar X=\partial_{\bar z_1}\), \(\bar Y=\partial_{\bar z_2}+\bar z_1\partial_{z_1}\). The four displayed vector fields are linearly independent at every point (the coefficient matrix is triangular with diagonal entries one), so \(T_{\mathbb C}M=T^{1,0}\oplus T^{0,1}\). Assigning eigenvalues \(+i\) and \(-i\) defines a globally smooth real almost complex structure \(J\) with \(J^2=-1\).

Direct differentiation gives
\[
 [X,Y]=\partial_{\bar z_1}=\bar X,\qquad
 \vartheta(X,Y)=[X,Y]^{0,1}=\bar X\ne0.
\]
The smooth form \(\bar\alpha^1=d\bar z_1-z_1dz_2\) is of type \((0,1)\): it annihilates \(X,Y\), equals \(1\) on \(\bar X\), and equals \(0\) on \(\bar Y\). Consequently
\[
 d\bar\alpha^1=-dz_1\wedge dz_2,\qquad
 (d\bar\alpha^1)^{2,0}(X,Y)=-1
 =-\vartheta'\bar\alpha^1(X,Y),
\]
which independently fixes the **minus sign** of the torsion correction in equation (9.53.3).

There is also a direct check that the formal-integrability assumption is indispensable for \((d'')^2=0\). Here \(\bar\alpha^2=d\bar z_2\) is the other \((0,1)\) coframe member. Because \(\bar Y z_1=\bar z_1\) and \(\bar X z_1=0\),
\[
 d''z_1=\bar z_1\bar\alpha^2,\qquad
 (d'')^2z_1=\bar\alpha^1\wedge\bar\alpha^2\ne0.
\]
This calculation is purely first-order tensor/exterior calculus and uses no analytic solvability, smoothing, weighted \(L^2\), or Newlander–Nirenberg theorem. It shows why the torsion operators in Proposition 9.53.2 cannot be removed for an arbitrary smooth almost complex structure. The printed proposition, which restricts (9.53.4) to \(\vartheta=0\), is correct.
