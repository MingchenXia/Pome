## POME-HODGE-DISTRIBUTIONAL-CHAIN-CONTRACTION-001

Severity low; status **verified conditional deduction**, not a printed error. Under exactly the finite differential-complex hypotheses of 10.46.4, the Green operators extend uniquely to distributions and are bounded G_q:H^s -> H^(s+2) for **every integer s**. For each integer r define a cochain complex with degree-q space C_r^q=H^(r-q)(M,V_q), so the first-order differential D_q:C_r^q -> C_r^(q+1) is bounded. The harmonic projection and inclusion give a bounded deformation retract of this complex onto its finite-dimensional smooth harmonic complex with zero differential, with homotopy K_q=D_(q-1)^*G_q. In particular the ranges of D_q are closed in these Sobolev spaces, and inclusion of smooth sections into distributions induces an isomorphism on cohomology. No regularization theorem for positive currents or singular weights enters.

### Proof

Use the smooth Green identities and formal self-adjointness of G established in 10.46.1, with the stated dual Sobolev pairing. For s<=-2, put t=-s-2>=0. The bounded map G:H^t -> H^(t+2) has transpose H^(-t-2) -> H^(-t), which agrees with G on smooth sections by self-adjointness. These are precisely H^s and H^(s+2). The extensions are consistent on overlaps by density. The missing middle index s=-1 is not a consequence of either of these dual estimates alone. For f in H^-1, first use H^-1 -> H^-2 to obtain Gf in L^2 with a bounded norm. On smooth sections the printed identities imply
     
    G = T (I - Pi + G),     T = (P+1)^(-1).
     
This identity extends to H^-1 as a distributional equality by the already proved H^-2 extension and the shifted inverse in 10.46.1. Its right-hand side belongs to H^1 and has norm bounded by C||f||_(H^-1), using smooth finite-rank Pi and T:H^-1 -> H^1. Thus all integer estimates hold, without interpolation. Every distribution belongs to some negative integer Sobolev space by lines 39–45, so these compatible maps define G on all distributions.

The smooth commutation D_q G_q=G_(q+1) D_q at line 132 extends to each Sobolev space by boundedness, density and the fact that D is first order. Harmonic projection Pi satisfies D Pi=Pi D=0, with the second equality obtained by pairing against smooth harmonic forms. Therefore
     
    D_(q-1) K_q + K_(q+1) D_q = I - Pi_q
     
on every C_r^q, by extending the decomposition at lines 124–130. The degree-lowering map K_q gains one Sobolev derivative and so maps C_r^q boundedly to C_r^(q-1). Pi has smooth image. For closed f, f=Pi f+D K f; an exact harmonic element is zero by Pi D=0. Moreover im D_(q-1)=ker D_q intersect ker Pi_q, a closed subspace. This proves the claimed bounded deformation retract. The same identities hold on arbitrary distributions, since each lies at a finite negative Sobolev order; hence the smooth-to-distribution cohomology isomorphism. The printed analytic Dolbeault and flat de Rham results, 10.46.6 (C2407F7C/AEB699DDD0FF) and 10.46.10 (0400DA90/385FAE3FDC0C), inherit this result with the appropriate differential. This does not assert that distributional wedge products exist or that arbitrary distributional representatives can be integrated against each other.
