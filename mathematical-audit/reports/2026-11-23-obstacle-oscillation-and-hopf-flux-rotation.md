**Statement.** Fix a compact complex manifold \(X\), a smooth real background \(\theta\) admitting \(\theta\)-psh competitors, a smooth positive probability volume form \(dV\), smooth real obstacles \(F,G\), and two ordinary singularity classes \([u],[v]\). For either \(P=P_{\theta,\cdot}[\cdot]\) (ordinary) or \(P=P_{\theta,\cdot}[\cdot]_{\mathcal I}\) (multiplier-ideal envelope), define

\[
D_F^P([u],[v])=\|P_{\theta,F}[u]-P_{\theta,F}[v]\|_{L^1(dV)}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\left|D_G^P([u],[v])-D_F^P([u],[v])\right|
\le \operatorname{osc}_X(G-F)
=\sup_X(G-F)-\inf_X(G-F).}
\]

In particular, **adding a constant to the obstacle does not change these pairwise envelope distances**, and the all-obstacle uniformity may be generated by any countable family dense in \(C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)/\mathbb R\) with respect to the quotient uniform norm. The \(d_\pi\), \(d_{\mathcal I}\), and controlled ideal-relation coordinates do not depend on the obstacle to begin with.

**Proof.** Let \(m=\min_X(G-F)\) and \(M=\max_X(G-F)\). For a fixed class \([h]\), the shift \(P_{\theta,F}[h]+m\) is a \(G\)-admissible competitor, and translating a \(G\)-competitor by \(-M\) makes it \(F\)-admissible. Both operations preserve the ordinary singularity order and all multiplier-ideal inclusions. Taking upper envelopes and using the same argument for the ideal envelope gives
\[
m\le A_h:=P_{\theta,G}[h]-P_{\theta,F}[h]\le M
\quad\text{almost everywhere.}
\]
Consequently \(|A_u-A_v|\le M-m\) almost everywhere. As \(dV(X)=1\), the triangle inequality and its reverse yield
\[
\left|\|P_G[u]-P_G[v]\|_1-\|P_F[u]-P_F[v]\|_1\right|
\le\|A_u-A_v\|_1\le M-m.
\]
All potentials are locally integrable; the argument ignores their pluripolar \(-\infty\) loci, which have \(dV\)-measure zero. If \(G=F+c\), then \(m=M=c\) and, in fact, \(P_G[h]=P_F[h]+c\). This proves both assertions. The proof does not require compactness of the space of modification models, sequential compactness, or an analytic approximation theorem.

**Comparison with the printed estimate.** Proposition 18.12.1 uses \(2\|F-G\|_\infty\) as a uniform bound for the change in the two-class envelope distances. The oscillation bound is never worse, after subtracting an arbitrary constant from \(G-F\); it is strictly stronger when \(G-F\) has a large constant component. It removes the irrelevant additive-constant direction. **No optimality assertion** is made about the coefficient one, and this finding does not negate the printed argument.

**Proposed reviewable patch only:** in the proof of §18.12.1, optionally append the above one-paragraph refinement after the existing bound, or replace that bound with the oscillation estimate. Preserve all permanent tags, citations and historic attribution. **Affected dependents:** Proposition 18.12.1's dense-obstacle generating argument and all-obstacle uniformity in §18.12.3; the latter requires its own analytic-input verification and is **not certified by this lemma**.
