**Statement.** Suppose f:X→Y is continuous and closed, with compact fibers strongly paracompact in X in the precise sense of §10.44 (in particular Hausdorff). For an arbitrary continuous g:Y'→Y, put X'=X×_Y Y', p:X'→X and f':X'→Y'. For every sheaf A of abelian groups on X and q≥0 the natural morphism

    g^{-1} R^q f_* A → R^q f'_* p^{-1} A

is an isomorphism. Neither Y nor Y' needs to be Hausdorff. No derived-category formalism, sequence compactness, or properness of g is required.

**Proof.** First f' is closed. If C⊂X' is closed and y'∉f'(C), every x∈S=f^{-1}(g(y')) has a product neighborhood O_x×V_x of (x,y') whose intersection with X' misses C. Compactness gives finitely many O_i covering S, with a common open V=∩V_i containing y'. Put O=∪O_i. By closedness of f there is an open T containing g(y') with f^{-1}T⊂O. Then f'^{-1}(V∩g^{-1}T) misses C, as each of its points lies in some O_i×V_i. If S is empty use O=∅ and V=Y'. This proves closedness for every g.

Next S'=f'^{-1}(y') is homeomorphic to S via p. For any ambient open cover of S' in X', take finitely many product neighborhoods O_i×V_i covering S', with each relative product contained in an assigned cover member; again use compactness. The O_i cover S. Apply the strong-paracompact refinement of §10.44 to obtain ambient open W_j covering S, locally finite near S, and an open N⊃S with N∩closure_X W_j⊂O_{i(j)}. Set V=∩V_i. In X' use U_j=p^{-1}W_j∩f'^{-1}V and N'=p^{-1}N∩f'^{-1}V. They cover S' and are locally finite near S' by continuity of p. Since closure_{X'} U_j⊂p^{-1}(closure_X W_j), one has N'∩closure_{X'} U_j⊂(O_{i(j)}×V_{i(j)})∩X', in the assigned cover member. Thus S' is strongly paracompact in X'. The empty case is immediate. Notice that no closure of V is required to lie in V.

For the natural morphism, whenever V'⊂Y' and T⊂Y are open and g(V')⊂T, pullback under p|_{f'^{-1}V'} gives H^q(f^{-1}T,A)→H^q(f'^{-1}V',p^{-1}A). These maps commute with restrictions and induce the asserted morphism on the associated sheaves (the stalk description in 10.55.6 identifies those sheaves with the higher images). At y' use the two natural fiber comparison isomorphisms of 10.61.3, applicable by the preceding paragraphs. The induced map is precisely the pullback under the homeomorphism S'→S with the restricted inverse-image coefficients. It is an isomorphism in every degree. A morphism of sheaves is an isomorphism iff it is so on all stalks. This finishes the proof.

**Scope.** Compactness cannot simply be omitted from this argument. For example, X=an infinite discrete set N, Y=a point, Y'=R, the projection N×R→R is not closed: the closed subset {(n,1/n):n≥1} has nonclosed image. This demonstrates the failure of the universal-closedness step, not by itself a failure of the base-change theorem in every noncompact case.

## POME-SHEAF-COMPACT-ZERO-DIM-FIBER-EXACTNESS-001

Severity low; **verified conditional deduction**. Same anchors 10.61.3 and 10.55.6; also Definition 10.61.6 lines 73–74 and Theorem 10.61.8 lines 117–128 in the 10.61 blob.

Under the hypotheses above, suppose every nonempty fiber has sheaf cohomological dimension at most d (d a nonnegative integer). If Y is nonempty and cd Y≤e, then cd X≤d+e. Indeed 10.61.3 annihilates higher-image sheaves in degrees q>d by their stalks; all E2 entries in 10.55.6 vanish for p>e. In total degree >d+e all entries vanish, so the finite filtration gives zero cohomology for every coefficient sheaf. No claim about covering dimension is involved.

In particular, if each fiber has a basis of clopen subsets, each has cd≤0: for a surjection of sheaves B→C on the compact Hausdorff fiber, a section of C lifts locally, giving an open cover. Refine by finitely many clopen sets using compactness, and form the disjoint clopen partition by taking all nonempty Boolean atoms of this finite cover. On each atom choose a local lift and glue them on the disjoint open pieces. Thus the global section functor is exact for every sheaf, which gives vanishing of all positive cohomology using a flabby resolution. The higher images vanish in all positive degrees; the long exact sequence in 10.55.6 shows f_* is exact, and its edge is the cohomology isomorphism H^k(Y,f_*A)≅H^k(X,A). By the first finding all these assertions persist after arbitrary base change. This extends the printed finite discrete fiber case to compact Hausdorff zero-dimensional fibers (e.g. a Cantor set). The stalk in degree zero is the section group on the fiber, not an unrestricted product of individual stalks: even a constant discrete coefficient group on a Cantor fiber gives locally constant functions rather than arbitrary functions.

**Correction/dependents.** Both are optional generalizations; no existing statement needs correction. They strengthen the fiber comparison used in 10.63.3, and provide a reusable base-change lemma and dimension criterion. They rely on 10.44 and 10.60's proved neighborhood-continuity infrastructure and 10.55.6's Leray and ordinary cohomological pullbacks; this report does not certify their entire upstream chains. No new external theorem required beyond these internal inputs.
