## The exact projectively invisible ideal

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed and let
\[
 B=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}B_d
\]
be a commutative graded \(k\)-algebra generated by finite-dimensional \(B_0\) and \(B_1\), exactly as in Theorem 9.59.2. Write \(B_+=\bigoplus_{d>0}B_d=(s_0,\ldots,s_N)\), where \(s_0,\ldots,s_N\) is a \(k\)-basis of \(B_1\). Define the *irrelevant torsion ideal*
\[
 T=H^0_{B_+}(B):=\{b\in B:\ B_+^q b=0\ \text{for some integer }q\ge1\}.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{T=\bigcap_{i=0}^N\ker(B\longrightarrow B_{s_i})}
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \boxed{\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathrm{cl}}B\ \simeq\
        \operatorname{Proj}_{\mathrm{cl}}(B/T)}
\]
canonically as **ringed spaces, not merely as topological spaces**. The first equality concerns localization of the *whole graded ring* \(B_{s_i}\); it is not a claim about a map from all homogeneous degrees into the degree-zero chart rings.

**Proof of the first equality.** If \(B_+^q b=0\), then \(s_i^qb=0\) for every \(i\), so the image of \(b\) in every \(B_{s_i}\) vanishes. Conversely suppose \(b/1=0\) in every \(B_{s_i}\). By the definition of localization there exist positive integers \(q_i\) with \(s_i^{q_i}b=0\). Set
\[
 q=1+\sum_{i=0}^N(q_i-1).
\]
Every monomial of total degree \(q\) in the \(s_i\) is divisible by at least one \(s_i^{q_i}\) (pigeonhole principle). Because \(B_+=(s_0,\ldots,s_N)\), this implies \(B_+^qb=0\). This works with arbitrary zero divisors and arbitrary nilpotents; no reducedness or generic-point argument is used. Since multiplication by homogeneous elements preserves the grading, the annihilation condition is componentwise; \(T\) is a homogeneous ideal.

**Proof of the projective isomorphism.** For each basis element \(s_i\), every \(t\in T\) becomes zero after inverting \(s_i\); in symbols \(T_{s_i}=0\). Exactness of localization therefore gives a graded-ring isomorphism
\[
 B_{s_i}\xrightarrow{\sim}(B/T)_{\bar s_i}.
\]
Taking degree zero gives \(B_{(s_i)}\simeq(B/T)_{(\bar s_i)}\). The identifications are canonical, commute with further localization at \(s_j\), and thus glue on the intersections \(U_{s_i}\cap U_{s_j}=U_{s_is_j}\). Both projective ringed spaces are covered by these degree-one charts by the printed theorem, including the empty-case convention. Their structure sheaves are therefore isomorphic. This **does not** claim that \(T\) is the only way two unrelated graded algebras can have isomorphic Proj: positive Veronese operations already give other identifications.

## Arbitrarily high nilpotence that projective charts erase

For each \(r\ge2\), take the finitely generated graded algebra
\[
 B_r=k[\epsilon,x_0,x_1]\big/(\epsilon^r,\epsilon x_0,\epsilon x_1),
 \qquad \deg\epsilon=0,\quad\deg x_0=\deg x_1=1.
\]
Here \(B_{r,0}=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^r)\) is finite-dimensional; \(B_{r,1}=kx_0\oplus kx_1\); the graded generation hypothesis of 9.59.2 is satisfied. The element \(\epsilon\ne0\) has nilpotence order **exactly \(r\)**, but \((x_0,x_1)\epsilon=0\). Therefore \(T=(\epsilon)\). Indeed every positive-degree component of \(B_r\) equals the corresponding component of \(k[x_0,x_1]\); no nonzero polynomial component is annihilated by a power of \((x_0,x_1)\).

Directly on the two projective charts,
\[
 (B_r)_{(x_0)}=k[x_1/x_0],\qquad
 (B_r)_{(x_1)}=k[x_0/x_1],
\]
because invertibility of \(x_i\) kills \(\epsilon\). The standard \(z\leftrightarrow1/z\) gluing proves
\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathrm{cl}}B_r\simeq \mathbb P^1_k
       \quad (r\ge2).}
\]
By contrast set
\[
 C_r=(k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^r))[x_0,x_1],\qquad\deg\epsilon=0.
\]
Its standard charts are \((C_r)_{(x_0)}=(k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^r))[z]\) and \((C_r)_{(x_1)}=(k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^r))[z^{-1}]\). Thus \(\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathrm{cl}}C_r\) retains a nilradical of exact order \(r\), and is not isomorphic to the reduced \(\mathbb P^1_k\). Both examples share the same reduced graded quotient and the same set of closed points. The distinction is exactly whether nilpotents survive on the localizations.

The printed line-44 construction \(B^+_0=k1\), \(B^+_m=B_m\) for \(m>0\) provides an independent check: for \(B_r\), \(B^+=k[x_0,x_1]\) **because** the positive components are already annihilated by \(\epsilon\); its projective realization discards precisely the irrelevant nilpotence. For \(C_r\), the positive components also contain \(\epsilon x_i\), so the same construction does **not** discard the surviving nilpotent structure. This confirms rather than contradicts the printed projective embedding argument.
