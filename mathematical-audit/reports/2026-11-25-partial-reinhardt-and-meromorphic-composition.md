## POME-REINHARDT-PARTIAL-AXIS-CLASSIFICATION-001

Low / verified conditional deduction, not a false printed theorem. Current integrated file
`docs/sections/reinhardt-domains-and-logarithmic-convexity/index.html` (blob 51d14c9874a7f3ec3a4886644749db8ddb82d923), Proposition 9.62.2 (B7A5F2850576, source tag D34443BE), lines 17–34; Lemma 9.62.3 (9179B3F88EE7, C667FC59), lines 39–82; Theorem 9.62.6 (2A0C2847FC1E, 463F81C1), lines 87–108. The existing complete-Reinhardt results are correct. The following is the partially complete classification, not the earlier bounded gauge or L^p removability result.

**Statement.** For a nonempty connected Reinhardt domain Omega in C^n, let D be its logarithmic image on the nonzero-coordinate locus and I={i: Omega meets z_i=0}. Then Omega is pseudoconvex iff D is convex, D-R_+ e_i is contained in D for each i in I, and Omega is exactly the following partial completion C_I(D): a point z is included iff its zero-index set S is contained in I and log|z_j|, j outside S, belongs to the coordinate projection D_{S^c}. (For S empty the criterion is simply log|z| in D.) Conversely, any nonempty open convex D with those downward directions and any I yields a pseudoconvex Reinhardt domain by this prescription; the axes it meets are exactly those indexed by I.

**Necessity.** The nonzero-coordinate locus of a connected domain is path connected: perturb a piecewise smooth path inside the open domain to avoid the finitely many real codimension-two coordinate hyperplanes (or connect points locally in balls minus those hyperplanes and concatenate). Thus D is connected; Proposition 9.62.2 gives convexity. Average a nonnegative continuous PSH exhaustion rho over the compact rotation torus. The average is a continuous PSH exhaustion: if its value is at most c, some point of the orbit has rho at most c, so the sublevel is a closed subset of the compact torus orbit of that original sublevel. On the torus locus the average is chi(log|z|), where chi is finite continuous convex on D, by the tube criterion (the local one-dimensional distributional second derivative along every real line is nonnegative, since its pullback is subharmonic and independent of imaginary coordinate).

For each i in I choose an interior point on z_i=0 with all other coordinates nonzero, possible by openness. There is x^0 in D such that x^0-s e_i belongs to D for all s>=0, and chi on that ray is bounded above by continuity of the averaged exhaustion on the corresponding closed radial segment in Omega. For arbitrary x in D and t>0 first prove x-te_i in D: for large s, write it as (1-t/s)y_s+(t/s)(x^0-s e_i), where y_s=(x-(t/s)x^0)/(1-t/s) tends to x, hence lies in D. Convexity gives membership. Convexity of chi in the same identity, continuity at x, and the ray bound give chi(x-te_i)<=chi(x) on letting s tend to infinity. This is a downward direction for D and a monotonicity direction for chi, at every x.

Every point already in Omega satisfies the partial completion criterion by openness, taking nearby points with all coordinates nonzero while holding the nonzero coordinates fixed. Conversely, for z in C_I(D), take a lift x in D of its nonzero-coordinate logarithms, and approach z by points with all coordinates nonzero whose S-coordinate logarithms are x_i-s. Repeated use of the downward property gives membership in the torus locus of Omega and chi at most chi(x). This sequence lies in a single compact exhaustion sublevel and converges to z in C^n. Thus z is in Omega, including simultaneous zeros. This proves equality, not merely separate axis filling.

**Sufficiency.** C_I(D) is open: a lift at a given zero stratum can be increased slightly in all coordinates by openness; nearby nonzero log coordinates can be chosen in a small neighborhood of the lift, and small newly nonzero coordinates in I can be decreased as far as needed by downward closure. Points on the same or a smaller zero stratum then satisfy the defining criterion; coordinates nonzero at the point stay nonzero. The torus locus is connected (product D times the rotation torus) and dense, so the whole open set is connected. Each i in I meets its hyperplane by projecting a point of D, while forbidden zeros are excluded by definition.

On D use the convex nonnegative function
\[
\chi(x)=\sum_j e^{2x_j}+\sum_{j\notin I} e^{-2x_j}
+\max(0,-\log d_D(x)).
\]
Omit the last term when D is all of R^n. The distance d_D to the complement of the open convex D is concave and nonincreasing along +e_i for i in I: a ball in D translates into D in each downward direction. Hence chi is nondecreasing in coordinates I. The proof of 9.62.3 applies with only the coordinates S contained in I sent to minus infinity: compact boxes in the projection have a common sufficiently negative lift by a finite subcover, the decreasing finite convex functions have a finite continuous convex limit, and convergence is uniform on compact boxes by the open-cover monotonicity argument. The lower bound is zero. The same sandwich proves continuity between all included strata. PSH follows by replacing log|z_i| in the vanishing coordinates by (1/2)log(|z_i|^2+epsilon^2), retaining the actual smooth pluriharmonic logarithms of the other nonzero coordinates, then applying convex Jensen to the vector of circle averages. Monotonicity is needed only in the replaced coordinates; the others satisfy equality in the submean relation. All vectors and averages lie in D on sufficiently small neighborhoods. Pass the submean inequality to the continuous limit as in 9.62.3. This is an elementary coordinate calculation, not the deferred regularization programme.

The extension is an exhaustion. Its first sum bounds |z|^2. The second prevents approaching any forbidden coordinate hyperplane along a bounded sublevel. If a bounded sequence approaches a boundary point with nonzero set J and allowed zero set S, the projected logarithms approach a finite point of the boundary of the open convex D_J: they are in the closure, but not in D_J by the exact membership criterion. The case J empty is impossible since then I is all indices and the origin is interior. The inequality d_D(x)<=d_{D_J}(x_J) (projection of a ball), including after continuous extension, forces the last term to diverge. If D_J is all space there is no such boundary point. Thus bounded sublevels are compact in the domain. This proves pseudoconvexity.

## POME-REDUCED-GRAPH-COMPOSITION-FAILURE-001

Low / verified deduction, not a false printed theorem. Current integrated
`docs/sections/meromorphic-maps-and-analytic-graphs/index.html` (blob f7dfea961fe6ec171c08c1532a8d0ab8e11b8a8c), Definition 9.67.4 (97D17B3FB015, E391EEEA), lines 47–51. The definition deliberately requires analytic reduced graph closure but not properness. Its representatives are not closed under composition, even on smooth reduced one-dimensional spaces and with the second map everywhere holomorphic.

Let f:C dashrightarrow C be 1/z, defined away from 0. Its graph closure is the closed analytic hypersurface zw=1; it has empty fiber over 0 and nonproper projection. Let g:C->C be exp(w), with closed analytic graph. The composite on C* is exp(1/z). Suppose its ordinary graph closure were analytic near (0,1). In a bidisc about that point, each holomorphic local defining function H(z,w) would vanish at (1/(log w+2 pi i k),w) for every w sufficiently near 1 and every sufficiently large positive integer k, choosing a local branch of log. For fixed w, these distinct z tend to zero, so the one-variable identity theorem gives H(z,w)=0 on the entire z-disc. Since this holds for all w near 1, every local defining function vanishes identically on the bidisc. The closure would contain an open bidisc, contradicting its restriction to z!=0, where it is the graph of an ordinary holomorphic function. Removing further nowhere dense analytic sets from the domain does not repair the closure, by the density argument of Definition 9.67.4 itself. Thus no representative of the composite in this sense exists.

This is distinct from the earlier essential nilpotent example: all spaces here are reduced, and the obstruction is composition, not full graph closure or extension of a nilpotent coefficient. Suggested clarification immediately after Definition 9.67.4: this broader class need not be composition-closed; do not infer a category or a proper graph convention from the name.
