## New precise classification

Let \(U=\mathbb C^2\setminus\{0\}\), and let \(X_F=U/\langle F\rangle\) be any one of the compact Hopf surfaces in Theorem 23.3.3:
\[
 F(z,w)=(\lambda_1z,\lambda_2w),\quad
 0<|\lambda_1|\leq|\lambda_2|<1,
\]
or
\[
 F_p(z,w)=(\lambda z,\lambda w+z^p),\quad
 0<|\lambda|<1,\quad p\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1}.
\]
Write \(\operatorname{Cent}(F)\) for the group of **entire biholomorphic automorphisms** \(G\) of \(\mathbb C^2\) satisfying \(GF=FG\). Then every automorphism of \(X_F\) has an entire, indeed polynomial, lift and
\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Aut}_{\rm hol}(X_F)\simeq
       \operatorname{Cent}(F)/\langle F\rangle.} \tag{A}
\]
The centralizers are **exactly**:

1. **Scalar diagonal:** if \(\lambda_1=\lambda_2=\lambda\), then \(\operatorname{Cent}(F)=GL_2(\mathbb C)\). Consequently \(\operatorname{Aut}(X_F)=GL_2(\mathbb C)/\langle\lambda I\rangle\).

2. **Distinct diagonal without resonance:** if \(\lambda_1\ne\lambda_2\) and there is **no** integer \(q\geq2\) with \(\lambda_1=\lambda_2^q\), then
\[
 \operatorname{Cent}(F)=\{(z,w)\mapsto(az,bw):a,b\in\mathbb C^*\}
       \simeq(\mathbb C^*)^2.
\]
The automorphism group is this torus modulo the cyclic subgroup generated by \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\).

3. **Distinct diagonal with resonance:** if \(\lambda_1=\lambda_2^q\) for an integer \(q\geq2\), then \(q\) is unique, and
\[
 \operatorname{Cent}(F)=
 \{(z,w)\mapsto(az+c w^q,bw):
                      a,b\in\mathbb C^*,\ c\in\mathbb C\}. \tag{B}
\]
The quotient by the cyclic subgroup generated by \((\lambda_2^q,\lambda_2,0)\) is the full automorphism group of \(X_F\). For each \(c\ne0\), the shear \(S_c(z,w)=(z+c w^q,w)\) gives a **genuinely nonlinear** automorphism lift. Thus the *linear*-centralizer formula alone is incomplete precisely on the resonant diagonal locus. In the parameters of (B), composition is
\[
 (a,b,c)\circ(a',b',c')=(aa',bb',a c'+c(b')^q).
\]

4. **Jordan triangular, \(p=1\):** \(F_1=\lambda I+N\), where \(N(z,w)=(0,z)\), \(N^2=0\). Then
\[
 \operatorname{Cent}(F_1)=
 \{(z,w)\mapsto(az,aw+bz):a\in\mathbb C^*,\ b\in\mathbb C\},
\]
and \(\operatorname{Aut}(X_{F_1})\) is this group modulo \(\langle(\lambda,1)\rangle\) in the displayed \((a,b)\)-parameters. Equivalently \(\operatorname{Cent}(F_1)\simeq\mathbb C^*\times(\mathbb C,+)\) via \((a,b)\mapsto(a,b/a)\), with deck generator \((\lambda,1/\lambda)\). **No nonlinear entire lift exists** in these coordinates.

5. **Nonlinear triangular, \(p\geq2\):** the polynomial shear
\[
 H_p(z,w)=\left(z,w+\frac{z^p}{\lambda-\lambda^p}\right)
\]
satisfies \(H_pF_pH_p^{-1}=\lambda I\). Every entire automorphism commuting with \(F_p\) is
\[
 H_p^{-1}BH_p,\qquad B\in GL_2(\mathbb C),
\]
so \(\operatorname{Aut}(X_{F_p})\simeq GL_2(\mathbb C)/\langle\lambda I\rangle\). Unlike the \(p=1\) case, nonlinear polynomial lifts occur, but none beyond the explicitly conjugated linear group.

This classifies the **full holomorphic automorphism groups of the specific surfaces in 23.3.3**, not only the subgroup induced by complex-linear transformations.

## Proof

### I. Every quotient automorphism has a polynomial commuting lift

The published proof (lines 43–50) establishes that \(p:U\to X_F\) is a holomorphic regular universal covering with deck group \(\langle F\rangle\simeq\mathbb Z\): \(U\) retracts onto the simply connected three-sphere. For \(\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(X_F)\), the map \(\phi\circ p:U\to X_F\) has a holomorphic lift \(G:U\to U\). A lift of \(\phi^{-1}\) gives a two-sided inverse of \(G\) after composition with one deck transformation, so \(G\) is biholomorphic. Uniqueness of lifts on the connected domain shows
\[
 GFG^{-1}=F^\epsilon,\qquad\epsilon=\pm1,
\]
because the induced automorphism of the deck group \(\mathbb Z\) is \(\pm1\).

Each coordinate of \(G\) extends holomorphically over the origin by an elementary two-variable puncture argument. Indeed, for a holomorphic scalar \(g\) on a punctured bidisk and sufficiently small \(r>0\), set
\[
 \widetilde g(z,w)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=r}
                       \frac{g(z,\zeta)}{\zeta-w}\,d\zeta,
 \qquad |z|<r,\ |w|<r.
\]
The integrand is defined even for \(z=0\). This is holomorphic in both variables; for \(z\ne0\), Cauchy's formula recovers \(g\), so it agrees with \(g\) on the punctured bidisk. Apply this to each coordinate of \(G\) and its holomorphic inverse. Their compositions equal the identity off the origin and hence everywhere by continuity, so \(G\) extends to an entire automorphism of \(\mathbb C^2\).

All maps \(F\) under consideration, as well as their inverses, have the origin as **unique fixed point**. The conjugacy relation forces \(G(0)=0\). Differentiating at zero gives the similarity
\[
 DG_0\,DF_0 = (DF_0)^{\epsilon}\,DG_0.
\]
Every eigenvalue of \(DF_0\) has modulus less than one; every eigenvalue of its inverse has modulus greater than one. Thus the two cannot be similar when \(\epsilon=-1\), so \(\epsilon=1\). In particular *every* quotient automorphism has a commuting lift.

Conversely each commuting entire automorphism preserves \(U\) and the orbits, so descends to \(X_F\). Two such lifts induce the same quotient map precisely when they differ by a deck transformation (uniqueness of lifts on connected \(U\)). This proves (A), with no unstated claim about arbitrary holomorphic maps that fail to be invertible.

### II. Diagonal centralizers and the exhaustive resonance list

Let \(F=A=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\) and \(G=(g_1,g_2)\) be an entire commuting automorphism. Since \(G(0)=0\), the Taylor expansion around zero gives
\[
 g_j(z,w)=\sum_{a,b\geq0}c_{j,a,b}z^aw^b,\qquad
 c_{j,a,b}\ne0\Longrightarrow
 \lambda_1^a\lambda_2^b=\lambda_j. \tag{C}
\]
The condition follows by comparing coefficients in \(G(A(z,w))=AG(z,w)\). Because \(0<|\lambda_i|<1\), for fixed \(j\) the resonance equation allows only **finitely** many exponents \((a,b)\): if \(r=\max|\lambda_i|<1\), then
\(|\lambda_1|^a|\lambda_2|^b\leq r^{a+b}\), which cannot equal the fixed positive \(|\lambda_j|\) for arbitrarily large total degree. Therefore the local Taylor polynomial is finite and the entire map equals that polynomial by the identity principle.

If \(\lambda_1=\lambda_2=\lambda\), (C) forces \(a+b=1\), hence \(G\) is a linear invertible matrix.

Suppose \(\lambda_1\ne\lambda_2\) and \(|\lambda_1|\leq|\lambda_2|\). In \(g_2\), any monomial with \(a\geq1\) and total degree at least two has modulus strictly less than \(|\lambda_2|\), as does \(w^b\) with \(b\geq2\). The only degree-one alternatives are \(z,w\), but the \(z\)-term requires \(\lambda_1=\lambda_2\) and is excluded. Therefore \(g_2=bw\). In \(g_1\), a monomial with \(a\geq1\) and total degree at least two has modulus strictly less than \(|\lambda_1|\), and the \(w\)-term would again require equal eigenvalues. The only options are \(az\) and \(c w^q\) where \(q\geq2\) and \(\lambda_2^q=\lambda_1\). Since \(|\lambda_2|<1\), such \(q\), if it exists, is unique. Since \(G\) is an automorphism, its differential at the origin is invertible, so \(a,b\ne0\). Conversely these maps are explicit polynomial automorphisms and satisfy (C); their inverses are triangular and polynomial. Thus cases 1–3 are exhaustive, including the potentially equal-modulus but unequal-phase case.

### III. Jordan centralizer contains no nonlinear entire maps

For \(p=1\), \(A=\lambda I+N\) with \(N^2=0\). Write the entire \(G\) as a sum of homogeneous Taylor polynomials \(G_d\) at zero. The equation \(G(Az)=AG(z)\) preserves each homogeneous degree. On the vector space of homogeneous degree-\(d\) polynomial maps, composition with \(A\) is triangularizable with **all** eigenvalues \(\lambda^d\), while postcomposition by \(A\) has eigenvalues \(\lambda\); these operators commute. Consequently their difference is invertible for every \(d\ne1\), since \(\lambda^d\ne\lambda\) for \(d\ne1\) (this also excludes \(d=0\)). Thus \(G_d=0\) except at degree one and \(G\) is linear. A direct \(2\times2\) commutator calculation \(BN=NB\) gives
\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\b&a\end{pmatrix},\qquad a\ne0.
\]
Every such \(B\) commutes and is invertible. This proves case 4. To avoid relying on simultaneous triangularization, one may instead identify the degree-\(d\) space as \(V\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^d(V^*)\): the induced intertwiner has the single eigenvalue \(\lambda^{d-1}\); hence it cannot fix a nonzero vector unless \(d=1\). This is the same finite-dimensional algebra.

### IV. Polynomial conjugacy of the \(p\ge2\) triangular models

For \(H_p(z,w)=(z,w+c z^p)\) one calculates
\[
 H_pF_p(z,w)
 =(\lambda z,\lambda w+[1+c\lambda^p]z^p),\qquad
 (\lambda I)H_p(z,w)
 =(\lambda z,\lambda w+\lambda c z^p).
\]
They coincide for \(c=(\lambda-\lambda^p)^{-1}\), which is well defined since \(0<|\lambda|<1\) and \(p\ge2\). Both \(H_p,H_p^{-1}\) are polynomial. Conjugacy carries entire commuting automorphisms bijectively to entire commuting automorphisms of \(\lambda I\), already shown to be exactly \(GL_2(\mathbb C)\). This proves case 5 and completes the classification.
