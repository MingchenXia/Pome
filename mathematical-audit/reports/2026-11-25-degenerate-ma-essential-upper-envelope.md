Let X be the connected compact complex manifold of the global convention, omega a smooth Hermitian form, and F a bounded nonnegative measurable density, not zero a.e. Write dV=omega^n, mu=F dV. Assume bounded omega-psh u,v solve the same equation with density F. Define the essential upper envelope
\[
F^+(x)=\inf_{r>0}\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B(x,r)} F,
\]
where balls are for any fixed smooth metric and essential suprema use dV. Let U be the union of the open neighborhoods on which F has a strictly positive essential lower bound.

**Claim A.** If F^+=F almost everywhere, both global extremal sets of w=u-v have strictly positive mu-mass. This conclusion does not require connectedness or density of U.

**Claim B.** Suppose, in addition, U is connected and dense and F=0 a.e. on X\U. Then u-v is constant. Without connectedness, if U is dense and F=0 a.e. outside U, any nonconstant pair must have two distinct connected components of U on which w is identically its global minimum and maximum, respectively.

All conclusions are conditional on the already printed bounded-solution continuity, modified comparison, mixed-density and energy lemmas, and concentrating test-potential construction; no new existence theorem for F is asserted. Dimension one follows directly from dd^c w=0. For n>=2:

### Proof of Claim A

The envelope is bounded, upper semicontinuous, and independent of changing the smooth metric or volume density. Indeed if its value at x is below c, some ball has essential supremum below c and the same bound holds in a neighborhood of x. At Lebesgue points of F one has F<=F^+. Thus the hypothesis F=F^+ a.e. holds, in particular, if F has a bounded upper-semicontinuous representative (the representative bounds F^+ from above at every point) or a bounded representative continuous a.e. In the latter case use continuity at each such point and the Lebesgue-point inequality.

By 14.19.29 the solutions are continuous. Put a=min w and K={w=a}, a nonempty compact set. Suppose mu(K)=0. Fix B0>||1||_2 and take epsilon>0 from 14.24.13. The set K0=K intersect {F^+>=epsilon/2} is compact and dV-null: F^+=F a.e. and F vanishes a.e. on K by the assumption mu(K)=0. Choose A>1 such that epsilon A>||F^+||_infty. Choose an open neighborhood O of K0 of sufficiently small dV-volume that dV(X)+(A^2-1)dV(O)<B0^2. A smooth cutoff chi supported in O, equal to one near K0 and between zero and one, gives g=1+(A-1)chi and ||g||_2<B0; if K0 is empty use g=1. The test potential from 14.24.13 has smooth positive density G>=epsilon g.

At K0, G>=epsilon A>F^+; at K\K0, G>=epsilon>F^+. The ratio F^+/G is upper semicontinuous and attains its maximum on K, strictly below one. Choose kappa in (0,1) greater than this maximum. W={F^+<kappa G} is an open neighborhood of K. Compactness and continuity of w imply {w<a+eta} subset W for some eta>0. Consequently F<=kappa G a.e. in this sublevel, contradicting 14.24.2, which gives strictly positive mu-mass to its intersection with {F>kappa G}. Thus mu(K)>0. Apply the argument to -w by interchanging u and v.

Note the distinction between the ordinary pointwise density and its essential upper envelope: the set of high values of a general bounded measurable function need not have a null closure even when its intersection with K has measure zero. No unsupported extension to arbitrary measurable densities is being claimed.

### Proof of Claim B and disconnected-component obstruction

The pushforward density k(t) in 14.24.5 is nonnegative and Lipschitz, supported on [a,b] with k(a)=k(b)=0. Assume a<b. Take any h_j decreasing to zero with h_j<b-a. For delta>0 let J_j(delta)=integral_a^{a+h_j} 1/(k(t)+delta) dt. Since k(t)<=L(t-a), J_j(delta) tends to infinity as delta decreases to zero (including L=0). Choose delta_j with J_j>=j. Define p_j equal to zero up to a, equal to one beyond a+h_j, and with derivative 1/[J_j(k+delta_j)] in between. These are Lipschitz, piecewise C^1. The energy identity bounds integral |p'_j(w)|^2 dE by 1/J_j. At each point of U, a relatively compact chart V with a positive essential lower bound on a neighborhood of its closure exists by definition of U. Equation 14.24.8 thus implies convergence of the distributional gradients of p_j(w) to zero in L^1_loc(U). The functions converge pointwise and locally in L^1 to the indicator of {w>a}. Its distributional gradient vanishes on U. It is therefore a.e. constant, equal to zero or one on each connected component of U (local convolution proves constancy on balls and overlap propagates it).

On a component where the indicator equals zero a.e., continuity implies w=a everywhere, since {w>a} is open. On any other component the intersection with K is dV-null. Repeat for the upper extremal set. Components are countable because the manifold has a countable basis and components of an open subset are open. Since F=0 a.e. outside U, Claim A forces a minimum-plateau component and a maximum-plateau component. They are distinct if a<b. If U is connected, this is impossible. In fact connectedness and the other hypotheses suffice without the extra density assumption: both plateau components must exist regardless of density. In the printed continuous case U={F>0} and F=0 off U, so this proof also removes the printed density assumption from 14.24.1, conditional on exactly the same inputs. Normalizing suprema fixes the additive constant.

Example of new scope (not a solution-existence claim): multiply a continuous nonnegative function vanishing at precisely one point by a function equal to two on a closed coordinate ball and one outside. The resulting upper-semicontinuous density need not be continuous and has no uniform positive lower bound; its local positive-lower-bound locus is the punctured manifold, connected. Another new scope is a continuous density supported on a proper closed ball, positive in its interior and zero outside; the positive set is connected but not dense. Both satisfy the strengthened uniqueness hypotheses if bounded solutions exist.
