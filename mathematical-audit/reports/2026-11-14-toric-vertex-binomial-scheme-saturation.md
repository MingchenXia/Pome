## Precise statement

Let `M` be a free abelian group of rank `n`, `P ⊂ M_R` a full-dimensional smooth (Delzant) lattice polytope, `A=P∩M`, and `V` its finite vertex set. Set `S=C[Z_m : m∈A]` with each variable of degree one. The projective torus is parametrized by `[χ^m(t)]_(m∈A)`. Consider the homogeneous prime toric ideal

```text
I_A = ker( S -> C[M][T], Z_m |-> χ^m T ),
```

where `T` has degree one and the group ring `C[M]` has degree zero.

For each nonvertex `m∈A\V`, choose (once) a rational convex combination of vertices, clear denominators, and write
`d_m m = Σ_(v∈V) c_(m,v)v`, with `d_m≥1`, `c_(m,v)≥0` integers and `Σ_v c_(m,v)=d_m`. Put

```text
R_m = Z_m^(d_m) - product_(v∈V) Z_v^(c_(m,v)).
```

For every vertex `v`, let `e_(v,1),...,e_(v,n)` be its primitive edge basis. Lemma 21.8.2(4) guarantees `v+e_(v,i)∈A`, and for `m∈A` it gives `m-v=Σ_i b_(v,i)(m)e_(v,i)` with `b_(v,i)(m)∈Z_(≥0)`. If `m≠v`, put `s_v(m)=Σ_i b_(v,i)(m)≥1` and

```text
B_(v,m) = Z_m Z_v^(s_v(m)-1)
          - product_i Z_(v+e_(v,i))^(b_(v,i)(m)).
```

Let `J` be the homogeneous ideal generated by all these finitely many `R_m` and `B_(v,m)`, and let `m_irr=(Z_m:m∈A)`.

**Claim (scheme-theoretic strengthening of the printed set-theoretic argument).**
1. For every vertex `v`, the dehomogenized coordinate ring of `Proj(S/J)` on `Z_v≠0` is exactly `C[w_1,...,w_n]`, with `w_i=Z_(v+e_(v,i))/Z_v`, and all other ratios the indicated monomials. There are no embedded, nonreduced, or extra components on these charts.
2. These vertex opens cover `Proj(S/J)`; hence it is a reduced smooth irreducible projective scheme whose underlying projective variety is `X_P` in Theorem 21.8.3.
3. The exact saturated homogeneous ideal is **`J:m_irr^∞=I_A`**. This proves the entire homogeneous defining ideal, rather than merely the set of common zeros of the displayed equations. No claim that the unsaturated ideal `J` always equals `I_A` is required.

No assumption of normal generation or projective normality of `P` is used.

## Proof

**(i) Toric kernel and local equations.** The map `S→C[M][T]` is well defined; its target is an integral domain, so `I_A` is prime. All `R_m` vanish under this map because their degree-one exponents sum to `d_m` and their lattice weights sum to `d_m m`. All `B_(v,m)` vanish because `m-v=Σ_i b_i e_i` and the total homogeneous degree of either term is `s_v(m)`. Thus `J⊆I_A`.

Fix `v`. Dehomogenize by writing `x_m=Z_m/Z_v` for `m≠v` and `w_i=x_(v+e_i)`. The `B_(v,m)` become exactly
`x_m=∏_i w_i^(b_i(m))` for all remaining `m`. They generate the kernel of the surjective substitution map from the free affine coordinate ring `C[x_m:m≠v]` onto `C[w_1,...,w_n]`: elimination of all non-`w` variables produces the polynomial ring with no additional relations. Every other generator of `J` vanishes after this substitution, by the toric-character identities proved above. Consequently

```text
(S/J)[Z_v^(-1)]_0  ≅  C[w_1,...,w_n].
```

The very same substitution describes `I_A` on this chart: `e_1,...,e_n` is a basis of `M`, so the restriction of the character map is the inclusion of the polynomial monomial graph in the Laurent torus coordinates, and its kernel is the graph ideal. In particular `J S[Z_v^(-1)] = I_A S[Z_v^(-1)]` as homogeneous localized ideals. The local scheme is regular and reduced, not merely set-theoretically a copy of affine space.

**(ii) Vertex-cover argument.** If a homogeneous prime `q⊇J` contains every vertex coordinate `Z_v`, each relation `R_m` implies `Z_m^(d_m)∈q`, hence `Z_m∈q` for every nonvertex `m`. Thus `q` contains the entire irrelevant ideal and does not define a point of `Proj(S/J)`. Therefore the vertex charts cover it. On each, the polynomial coordinate ring is an integral domain of dimension `n`; these charts share the dense torus parametrized by `χ^{e_i}∈C^*` and so glue to a reduced smooth irreducible scheme. The ordinary projective set is precisely the `X_P` constructed in the printed proof.

**(iii) Explicit saturation.** Because `J⊆I_A`, and `I_A` is prime and contains no coordinate variable, `J:m_irr^∞⊆I_A:m_irr^∞=I_A`. Conversely, let `F∈I_A` be homogeneous. Local equality from (i) gives, for each vertex `v`, an integer `a_v≥0` with `Z_v^(a_v)F∈J`. For each nonvertex `m`, choose a vertex `v` for which `c_(m,v)>0`. The relation `R_m` implies modulo `J` that
`Z_m^(k d_m) F = (∏_u Z_u^(k c_(m,u)))F`. For sufficiently large `k` the latter expression is divisible by `Z_v^(a_v)F`, so it belongs to `J`. Thus, for **every** variable `Z_m`, some positive power kills `F` modulo `J`. With finitely many variables, a sufficiently high power of `m_irr` then kills `F` (pigeonhole principle for total degrees). Hence `F∈J:m_irr^∞`. By homogeneity, this holds for all `F∈I_A`, establishing the claimed equality.

For `n=0`, `A=V` has one element, `I_A=J=0`, and `Proj S` is a reduced point; the same statement holds with the empty edge basis.

**(iv) Small independent check, without a project build.** For the smooth unit square `P=[0,1]^2`, `A=V`, and the vertex equations include
`Z_(0,0)Z_(1,1)-Z_(1,0)Z_(0,1)`. All others are repetitions or zero. The toric kernel is this principal quadric ideal, and its `Z_(0,0)≠0` chart is `C[w_1,w_2]` with `Z_(1,1)/Z_(0,0)=w_1w_2`. This checks signs, degrees, and local coordinates in a non-simplex example.
