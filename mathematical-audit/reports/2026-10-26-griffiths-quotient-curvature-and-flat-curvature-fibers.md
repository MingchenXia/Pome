## POME-TAUT-GRIFFITHS-POSITIVITY-001

**Status:** verified conditional deduction; **severity:** low; **printed proposition:** correct, no confirmed error. **Hypotheses:** X is a complex manifold, E→X a holomorphic vector bundle of positive rank with a smooth Hermitian metric h; the quotient metric is used on O_E(1) over P(E*). The curvature convention is the repository's unscaled Chern curvature Θ and normalized iΘ/(2π).

**Precise assertion.** The quotient metric on O_E(1) has semipositive curvature at every point if and only if (E,h) has **Griffiths-semipositive** Chern curvature. It has strictly positive curvature at every point if and only if (E,h) has **Griffiths-positive** curvature. These statements also include rank one, when there is no vertical summand.

**Proof, including the mixed terms.** At a fixed (x,[a]) choose the Chern-normal frame of Proposition 10.37.3, and identify the tangent space with a horizontal copy of T_xX and a vertical copy of T_[a]P(E_x*). At the chosen point, the Chern curvature of the quotient line is block diagonal:
\[
 \Theta_{O_E(1)}((v,w),\overline{(v,w)})
  =\langle R^E(v,\bar v)a^\sharp,a^\sharp\rangle_h
     +\|w\|_{\rm FS}^2 ,
\]
where a is a unit covector and \(a^\sharp\) is its metric dual; the contraction is exactly the component expression \(\sum c_{jk\mu\lambda}a_\lambda\bar a_\mu v_j\bar v_k\) in the printed proposition. The vertical expression is the positive Fubini–Study form and the mixed block vanishes because the base metric is Chern-normal to first order. The normalization i/(2π) multiplies both blocks by the same positive factor. If E is Griffiths (semi)positive, both blocks yield (semi)positivity; strict positivity holds because either v≠0 supplies a strictly positive horizontal value or w≠0 supplies a strictly positive vertical value. Conversely, evaluate quotient curvature on the horizontal vectors (v,0), for every nonzero v and unit a. The corresponding inequality is exactly the Griffiths condition. This is pointwise; no compactness or global frame is needed. The same argument handles rank one by omitting w.

**Sharpness/limitation.** For E=O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)⊕O_{\mathbb P^1} with the direct-sum Fubini–Study/flat metrics, E is Griffiths-semipositive but not positive. Its quotient metric on O_E(1) has a zero horizontal direction over a covector projecting to the flat summand, although every projective fiber is strictly positive vertically. Fiberwise Fubini–Study positivity alone is not sufficient.

**Proposed patch and dependents.** Optionally add the characterization as a corollary immediately after Proposition 10.37.3. It provides a convenient metric-level input for projectivized-bundle curvature discussions and §11.11; it does *not* establish an equivalence for numerical ampleness, nor replace the analytic projective-embedding proofs. Keep all permanent tags and source variants unchanged.

## POME-INTEGRAL-CURVATURE-UNITARY-FLAT-TORSOR-001

**Status:** verified conditional deduction; **severity:** low; **printed theorems:** correct, not in need of repair. **Hypotheses:** X is a complex manifold and ω is a smooth, real, d-closed (1,1) form whose de Rham class is integral, in the precise sense of Theorem 10.40.6. Consider pairs (L,h) where L is holomorphic, h is a smooth Hermitian metric, and c1(L,h)=ω, modulo **unitary holomorphic bundle isomorphisms**.

**Precise assertion.** The collection of these isomorphism classes is a nonempty torsor for \(H^1(X,\underline{U(1)})\), the group of flat unitary line-bundle classes (locally constant unitary transition cocycles). When X is connected it is equivalently the group of unitary characters of π1(X). There is **no uniqueness of the holomorphic line bundle or of the metric** without quotienting by this flat ambiguity.

**Proof.** Theorem 10.40.6 supplies one pair (L0,h0). Let (L1,h1) be another. The quotient line Q=L1⊗L0^{-1}, with the induced quotient metric hQ, has zero Chern curvature by the tensor curvature formula and exact equality of the specified forms. Its Chern connection is a flat unitary connection. On a simply connected small coordinate neighborhood, flat parallel transport constructs a nowhere-vanishing unit-length parallel section. Because the (0,1) part of the Chern connection is the holomorphic structure operator, this frame is also holomorphic. On overlaps the frames differ by locally constant elements of U(1), giving a Čech class in H1(X,U(1)_loc.const). Conversely, a locally constant U(1) cocycle glues a holomorphic line Q with a flat Hermitian metric, so (L0⊗Q,h0⊗hQ) again has curvature exactly ω. Tensor product acts transitively; if it fixes (L0,h0) up to a unitary holomorphic isomorphism, then Q is unitary-holomorphically trivial and its cocycle is a coboundary, proving freeness. On a connected complex manifold the equivalence with Hom(π1(X),U(1)) follows by parallel transport and its inverse local-system construction. No Hodge theorem or \(\partial\bar\partial\)-lemma is used in this classification.

**Concrete noncompact example.** On X=C* and its trivial holomorphic line, choose \(h_c(e,e)=|z|^{2c}\) for c∈R. Each has Chern curvature zero, but (O,h_c) and (O,h_d) are unitary-holomorphically isomorphic **exactly when** c−d∈Z. Indeed any such isomorphism is multiplication by a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic f with \(|f|=|z|^{c-d}\). On the universal covering write log z=w; the holomorphic f(e^w) has constant-modulus quotient by \(e^{(c-d)w}\), hence is a unit constant times this exponential; single-valuedness forces c−d∈Z. Conversely multiplication by z^{c-d} gives the isomorphism. The monodromy character is \(e^{-2\pi i c}\) with the indicated Chern connection convention. Thus even one fixed holomorphic line on a noncompact X admits a circle of distinct flat metric-line **isometry classes**; this does not contradict the compact same-bundle metric rigidity recorded earlier, whose equivalence relation and hypotheses differ.

**Proposed patch and dependents.** Add an optional clarification after Theorem 10.40.6 or in the discussion of Pic^0 and flat metrics. It explains the exact ambiguity in integral-curvature realization, is compatible with Theorem 10.40.8 including integral torsion, and helps distinguish curvature, Picard and divisor invariants in §20.7. No change to the theorem, Chern normalization or local-system classification is necessary.

## §9.34 verification, without a new finding ID

Proposition 9.34.1 has the **correct sign and factor four**. With \(\beta=i(\bar\partial\rho-\partial\rho)\) and \(\alpha=\beta/|\nabla\rho|\), one has \(\alpha(v)=g(v,J\nu)\) on real tangent vectors and \(d\beta=2i\partial\bar\partial\rho\). For real complex-tangent ξ,η, \(d\beta(\xi,\eta)=-4\operatorname{Im}\sum\rho_{z_j\bar z_k}\xi_j\bar\eta_k\), so
\[
 g([\xi,\eta],J\nu)=-d\alpha(\xi,\eta)
   =4\operatorname{Im}L(\xi,\eta).
\]
The normalized defining function is assumed negative inside; reversing that convention changes orientation, so it must be retained.
