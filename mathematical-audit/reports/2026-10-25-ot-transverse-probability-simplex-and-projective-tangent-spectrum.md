## POME-OT-TRANSVERSE-PROBABILITY-SIMPLEX-001

**Severity:** low. **Status:** verified conditional deduction, NOT a false printed theorem. **Source:** main 'docs/sections/bott-chern-classes-and-singular-loci--oeljeklaus-toma-manifolds/index.html', blob '8846df97111fa26289cd5e0cb4fd8f8a1fc5a035'; Theorem 10.29.4, permanent tag '244BCBFB750A', lines 69–94; its automorphy Eq. 10.29.5, tag '8DB42C059FB9', lines 73–78; Corollary 10.29.6 tag '98BD3A283B8E', lines 100–107; atomic example lines 111–125. **Precise statement.** Let X be an Inoue/Oeljeklaus–Toma surface of type (s,t)=(1,1), with admissible unit group generated by a totally positive unit whose real embedding is lambda>1, and let L=log(lambda). Fix the closed semipositive form theta of Eq. 10.29.1. The theta-psh functions modulo additive constants, and equivalently positive (1,1)-currents in the class of theta, are affinely homeomorphic to the space of Borel probability measures on the circle T_L=R/LZ with its weak-star topology. On the potential side use the uniform topology. In particular, the mean-zero normalized theta-psh potentials form a compact convex subset of C^0(X) in the UNIFORM norm, not merely in L1. There are continuous theta-psh potentials with purely singular-continuous transverse curvature, in addition to the printed atomic-hypersurface example.

**Proof, including the exact measure normalization.**

By Theorem 10.29.4, every theta-psh potential has the form

\[
u(w,z)=f(y)+\log y,\qquad f:(0,\infty)\longrightarrow\mathbb R\ {\rm convex},\quad f(\lambda y)=f(y)-L.
\]

Put s=log y and h(s)=f(e^s)+s. Then h is continuous, L-periodic and u=h(s). For smooth h, ordinary differentiation gives

\[
f''(y)=y^{-2}\{h''(s)-h'(s)+1\}.
\]

For continuous convex f the same is an identity of distributions after the smooth change of variable y=e^s: the nonnegative Radon measure

\[
\mu=ds+(D_s^2-D_s)h
\]

on T_L is the pushforward of y\,f''(dy). In particular mu(T_L)=L: the integrals of the first and second periodic distributional derivatives are zero. This supplies a probability rho=mu/L, invariant under adding constants to h.

Conversely, let rho be ANY probability measure on T_L and put mu=L rho. Set omega_k=2 pi k/L and Fourier coefficients m_k=int_{T_L}exp(-i omega_k s)d mu(s). There is a unique periodic distribution of zero average solving

\[
(D_s^2-D_s)h=\mu-ds.
\]

Indeed it is explicitly

\[
h(s)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}}
 \frac{m_k}{L(-\omega_k^2-i\omega_k)}e^{i\omega_k s}.
\]

Because |m_k|<=L and the denominators grow quadratically, the series converges absolutely and uniformly, giving a real continuous periodic h (the negative-frequency coefficients are conjugate to the positive-frequency coefficients). Distributional differentiation recovers mu-ds, hence the associated f(y)=h(log y)-log y has positive distributional second derivative: the above change-of-variable identity says y f''(dy) pushes forward to mu>=0. A continuous function with nonnegative distributional second derivative is convex. Its automorphy holds by periodicity. Theorem 10.29.4 therefore gives a theta-psh potential. The correspondence is bijective after the zero-mean normalization and affine by construction.

If rho_i converges weak-star to rho along ANY directed net, each Fourier coefficient m_{k,i} tends to m_k. Every Fourier series has the same summable coefficient majorant 1/|\omega_k^2+i\omega_k|. A finite-frequency approximation followed by this uniform tail estimate proves ||h_i-h||_{C^0(T_L)}->0 without choosing subsequences. Conversely the distributional operator ds+D^2-D maps uniform limits of potentials to distributional limits of their transverse probability measures; on the compact circle, convergence of positive measures in distributions with fixed mass L implies weak-star convergence (smooth functions are uniformly dense in continuous functions). Thus the correspondence is a homeomorphism. Compactness also follows from weak-star compactness of probability measures on T_L, without invoking sequential compactness for an arbitrary net.

For rho=delta_{[s_0]}, the transverse measure is L delta_{[s_0]}. Its lifts to the positive y-axis have atoms at y_j=exp(s_0+jL), and the jump in f' at y_j is exactly L/y_j. This recovers the printed slopes in lines 111–125, and checks the factor L. For an atomless singular probability rho (e.g. a Cantor probability), f'' is nonzero, singular continuous and non-atomic on each fundamental interval; the resulting curvature dd^c f(y) is supported over the corresponding fractal transverse set, while u remains continuous. No analytic or Demailly regularization input is used: the only construction is a one-dimensional Fourier solution to a constant-coefficient distribution equation.

**Precise statement and derivation.** For n>=2, put on T P^n the quotient/twist metric specified in Example 10.34.10, and on O(k) the kth tensor-power metric induced from Fubini–Study, k any integer (dual metric when k<0). At a unitary point and with u=(u_{ij}) in T^{1,0}P^n tensor (T P^n tensor O(k)), write u_sym=(u+u^T)/2 and u_skew=(u-u^T)/2. Proposition 10.34.6 and Example 10.34.10 give the EXACT Nakano form

\[
R_{T\mathbb P^n\otimes\mathcal O(k)}(u)
 =\frac12\sum_{i,j}|u_{ij}+u_{ji}|^2+k\sum_{i,j}|u_{ij}|^2
 =(k+2)\|u_{\rm sym}\|^2+k\|u_{\rm skew}\|^2.
\]

Thus its two eigenvalues are exactly k+2 on symmetric tensors (multiplicity n(n+1)/2) and k on skew tensors (multiplicity n(n-1)/2). The induced metric is Nakano semipositive iff k>=0, and Nakano strictly positive iff k>=1. At k=0 its Nakano nullspace has dimension n(n-1)/2 at every point. At k=-1 the form is indefinite, despite the quotient bundle interpretation T P^n tensor O(-1)=H.

On decomposable u=xi tensor v the same formula becomes

\[
R(u)=(k+1)|xi|^2|v|^2+|\langle xi,v\rangle|^2.
\]

For n>=2 there are nonzero orthogonal xi,v and parallel xi,v, so Griffiths semipositivity holds exactly for k>=-1 and strict Griffiths positivity exactly for k>=0 (integer k). This exhibits the SHARP one-twist gap between strict Griffiths and strict Nakano positivity for this precise induced metric. For n=1 the skew summand is absent: T P^1 tensor O(k)=O(k+2) has the single eigenvalue k+2, and the n>=2 thresholds must not be asserted there.
