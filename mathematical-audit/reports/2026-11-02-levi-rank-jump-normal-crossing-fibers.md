## General local identity

Fix the collection's normalization `dd^c=(i/2π)∂∂̄`. For any holomorphic map `f:Ω⊂C^n→C`, put `u=|f|²`. Then

```math
(dd^c u)_{j\bar k}=\frac{i}{2\pi} f_{z_j}\overline{f_{z_k}},
\qquad
dd^c u=\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial f\wedge\overline{\partial f}.
```

This is a semipositive form everywhere, and its complex Levi rank is **one where `df≠0`, zero where `df=0`**. At regular points the complex nullspace is precisely `ker(df)`. Every connected component of a regular fiber `f^{-1}(c)` is therefore a maximal null leaf locally; along it `u=|c|²` is constant and the ambient derivative `u_{z_j}=\bar c f_{z_j}` restricts holomorphically. This checks directly, without a PDE or regularization input, the potential and gradient clauses of Theorem 7.15.3 wherever its rank hypothesis holds.

## Normal-crossing critical point: global failure of continuous null-line extension

Take `n=2`, `f(z,w)=zw`, and `u(z,w)=|zw|²`. Its Hermitian Levi coefficient matrix, after the harmless common positive factor `1/(2π)`, is

```math
H(z,w)=\begin{pmatrix}|w|^2&w\bar z\\z\bar w&|z|^2\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}w\\z\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}\bar w&\bar z\end{pmatrix}.
```

Thus `H²` is irrelevant; the exterior square `(dd^c u)^2` vanishes **identically**, while `dd^c u` is nonzero at every `(z,w)≠(0,0)`. In the punctured space the null line is generated by the holomorphic vector field

```math
Z=z\,\partial_z-w\,\partial_w,
\qquad df(Z)=wz+z(-w)=0.
```

For `c≠0`, the fiber `zw=c` is a connected immersed (indeed embedded) curve biholomorphic to `C^*`; for `c=0`, the punctured coordinate axes are two distinct null leaves. At the origin `df=0`, so the pointwise kernel is all of `C²`, not a complex line. Worse, the projective null line has incompatible approach limits:

```math
\lim_{z\to0,\,z\ne0}\ker(dd^c u)_{(z,0)}
 =\mathbb C\partial_z,
\qquad
\lim_{w\to0,\,w\ne0}\ker(dd^c u)_{(0,w)}
 =\mathbb C\partial_w.
```

The two limits differ. Hence **no continuous complex line distribution can extend the regular-null distribution across the origin**. In particular it is impossible to drop the constant-nullity hypothesis of Theorem 7.15.3 and demand an ordinary (constant-dimensional) null foliation through that critical point. This does not exclude a singular-leaf stratification; it rules out the specifically printed regular foliation conclusion without its stated rank hypothesis.

For Proposition 7.15.4, set `α=dd^c u`, `β=0`, `p=1`, `q=0`. Then `α` is reducible (Hermitian, positive semidefinite), `β` is semipositive, and `α²=0` everywhere; however `α^p∧β^q=α` vanishes at the origin. Thus the **everywhere nonvanishing** requirement in the wedge criterion cannot be inferred from its vanishing higher wedge, even for a smooth polynomial PSH potential. It is a genuine, logically independent rank hypothesis.
