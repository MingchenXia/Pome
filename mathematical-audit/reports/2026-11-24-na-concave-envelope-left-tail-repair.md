### Counterexample

The definition allows arbitrary finitely many theta-psh nodes A_i at strictly decreasing real times tau_i; it does not require A_N to dominate the other nodes. Take any connected compact Kähler X, theta Kähler, N=1, tau_0=0, tau_1=-1, A_0=0 and A_1=-1 (constant potentials). The printed clause 3 gives A'_tau=0 on [-1,0); clause 2 gives 0 at 0; clause 4 gives -1 for tau<-1. At -1 the value 0 is larger than -1 just to its left. More explicitly, concavity at -3/2 would require A'_{-3/2} >= (A'_{-2}+A'_{-1})/2=-1/2, whereas it is -1. This violates concavity and the decreasing requirement for a test curve. The true scalar least concave majorant is identically 0 on (-infinity,0]: a finite concave majorant of a function constant on a left half-line must be nonincreasing (a positive chord slope would force values tending to -infinity to its left, contradicting the finite lower bound there). This counterexample respects the global connected compact convention.

The issue is not just additive normalization. Take X=P^1, theta the probability Fubini–Study form in the stated dd^c convention, A_0=0, A_1=g=log(|Z_1|^2/(|Z_0|^2+|Z_1|^2)). Then theta+dd^c g is the Dirac measure at p=[1:0]; g<=0 and g has Lelong number 1 at p. The same printed recursion gives A'_tau=0 on [-1,0], but g for tau<-1. For omega=theta, I-projection retains divisorial valuations (the property invoked at line 1357 of the original lemma). Consequently the projected levels at -2 and -1/2 have different Lelong numbers, respectively 1 and 0. The earlier level cannot dominate the later one: domination would imply the reverse Lelong inequality. Hence the literal printed algorithm can fail to produce even a decreasing family after projection. This is a counterexample to the construction, not to the intended Lemma 22.1.89 with the actual monotone concave envelope.

### Corrected finite envelope

For tau<=tau_0 put
\[
 K_\tau=\{\lambda\in[0,1]^{N+1}:\ \sum_i\lambda_i=1,\quad \sum_i\lambda_i\tau_i\ge\tau\},
 \qquad B_\tau=\max_{\lambda\in K_\tau}\sum_i\lambda_i A_i,
\]
where the maximum is pointwise and a zero weight contributes zero, even at a pole. Put B_tau=-infinity for tau>tau_0. For fixed tau the maximum is over finitely many vertices: the simplex vertices with tau_i>=tau, and intersections of an edge with the hyperplane of weighted time tau. At a point where some A_i=-infinity, restrict to the face spanned by the finite-valued nodes; the same vertex argument applies, or all feasible values are -infinity. Therefore B_tau is a finite maximum of theta-psh convex combinations. It is theta-psh for tau<=tau_0, since the feasible node 0 gives a nonidentically-infinite lower bound. Here and below inequalities are pointwise including the extended values.

It is nonincreasing in tau by nesting of K_tau and concave in tau by mixing two feasible weight vectors and their objective values. It majorizes the original interpolation (two adjacent nodes, or the tail node). For tau<=tau_N, K_tau is the full simplex, hence
\[
 B_\tau=\max_{0\le i\le N} A_i.
\]
At tau_0 the only feasible vertex is node 0. For fixed x the scalar function is piecewise affine on the interval where finite, with upper-semicontinuous values at its finite endpoint (linear programming on the finite-valued face, with a finite fixed set of vertices there). If that face is empty it is identically -infinity. This proves upper semicontinuity in tau, including an endpoint where A_0(x)=-infinity; immediately to the right of the largest time of a finite node it is -infinity.

For any nonincreasing concave majorant H of A, concavity at the nodes gives H(sum lambda_i tau_i)>=sum lambda_i A_i, and monotonicity gives H(tau)>=H(sum lambda_i tau_i) for feasible lambda. Hence H>=B. B is exactly the least nonincreasing concave majorant. At points where all nodes are finite it also equals the ordinary least concave majorant, by the left-tail argument above. At common or individual poles, explicitly specifying the nonincreasing majorant avoids an unwarranted claim about ordinary pointwise concavification of extended-valued functions; this is the version needed by the NA application. The replacement is a finite maximum construction, not a regularization argument.

### Conditional repair of Lemma 22.1.89

For any divisorial valuation v let a_i=A_i^An(v). Use precisely the two identities already invoked in the original proof: analytification of a finite maximum is the maximum, and that of a positive affine combination is the affine combination. Thus B_tau^An(v)=max_{lambda in K_tau} sum lambda_i a_i. Every feasible vector satisfies
\[
 \sum_i\lambda_i a_i+\tau
 \le \sum_i\lambda_i(a_i+\tau_i)
 \le \max_i(a_i+\tau_i).
\]
Conversely at tau=tau_i the feasible node i yields the reverse inequality after taking the supremum over tau. Hence the Legendre-type supremum of B equals that of A exactly. No passage through an infinite supremum or a choice of convergent sequence is needed. I-projection in the augmented backgrounds preserves the same divisorial values, so the equality in (22.1.90) survives.

For the membership assertion, work in theta+omega. The constant left endpoint max A_i has positive mass in that background: theta+omega+dd^c max A_i >= omega, so its non-pluripolar mass is at least the integral of omega^n (the potentials are finite off a pluripolar set). Project the endpoint to a model potential and all levels to I-model potentials. Monotonicity and affine-combination concavity of this projection, preservation of the left endpoint, and compatibility of the transition maps are the inputs of 17.14.6 (tag BF40FB71C62C, blob e058100104283d27de55bc64b4ce784f66ace28b, lines 91–131) and the proof of 22.1.89. If necessary the terminal level is set by the decreasing left limit as in 17.14.1; it does not increase the Legendre supremum, since divisorial values of the left limit agree by the existing endpoint-continuity input, which is not re-certified here. The projected levels therefore give the intended construction conditional on those existing projection, mass, test-curve endpoint and cocycle results. This report does not certify them transitively.
