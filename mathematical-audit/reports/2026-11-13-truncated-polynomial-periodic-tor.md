## Exact example and complete elementary resolution

For every integer m >= 2 take the finite-dimensional commutative nuclear Fréchet algebra

\[
 A_m=\mathbb C[t]/(t^m),\qquad k=A_m/(t)\cong\mathbb C.
\]

Give the residue module k its natural jointly continuous action. All underlying Fréchet spaces and all finitely generated modules in the following resolution are finite dimensional, hence nuclear, and every linear image is closed.

Consider the augmented complex P_0=A_m -> k, P_q=A_m for each q >= 1, with
\[
 d_{2j+1}=\times t\quad(j\ge0),\qquad d_{2j}=\times t^{m-1}\quad(j\ge1).
\]
Successive composites are multiplication by \(t^m=0\). More precisely,
\[
 \ker(\times t)=t^{m-1}A_m=\operatorname{im}(\times t^{m-1}),\qquad
 \ker(\times t^{m-1})=tA_m=\operatorname{im}(\times t),
\]
while the augmentation has kernel tA_m. Thus this is an **exact** nuclearly free resolution of k in every positive degree, and it is a strict Fréchet resolution: the finite-dimensional images are closed, and every quotient map has its usual quotient topology. No implicit Hausdorff completion is involved.

The free-factor formula of Proposition 9.44.2 gives \(k\widehat\otimes_{A_m}P_q\cong k\) topologically in every degree, including zero. Since t and \(t^{m-1}\) act as zero on k, **every** differential after balanced tensoring is zero. Therefore
\[
 H_q(k\widehat\otimes_{A_m}P_\bullet)\cong k
 \qquad\hbox{for each integer }q\ge0.
\]
By the resolution-comparison assertion in Theorem 9.44.3 this gives the precise conditional conclusion
\[
 \widehat{\operatorname{Tor}}^{A_m}_q(k,k)\cong\mathbb C
 \quad\hbox{as a Hausdorff one-dimensional topological vector space, for every }q\ge0.
\]
The independent free-resolution calculation itself is unconditional; identifying its homology with the theorem's **specified completed bar complex** invokes the printed comparison theorem. This does **not** certify the theorem for arbitrary non-Hausdorff Fréchet complexes.

## Extra independent bar and long-sequence crosschecks when m = 2

For \(A_2=\mathbb C[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\), the usual normalized augmented bar complex has in degree q the line generated by \([\varepsilon|\cdots|\varepsilon]\) for q >= 1 and k in degree zero. It is the quotient of the ordinary algebraic bar complex by the standard degenerate subcomplex generated by an inserted unit; the degeneracy subcomplex is contractible by the usual unit-insertion simplicial homotopy. On these normalized generators the end terms vanish because \(\varepsilon k=0\) and every middle multiplication vanishes because \(\varepsilon^2=0\). Hence the **normalized bar differential is identically zero in every positive degree**, directly corroborating one surviving homology class per degree without truncating the bar complex. As every chain group here is finite dimensional in each fixed degree, ordinary and completed projective tensor products agree degree by degree.

The module short exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow k\xrightarrow{\;\iota\;}A_2
   \xrightarrow{\;\pi\;}k\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \iota(1)=\varepsilon,\quad \pi(a+b\varepsilon)=a,
\]
is exact and strict. Since \(A_2\) is free, \(\widehat{\mathrm{Tor}}_q^{A_2}(k,A_2)=0\) for q >= 1. In the long sequence printed in Theorem 9.44.3 this makes
\[
 \partial:\widehat{\mathrm{Tor}}^{A_2}_q(k,k)
   \stackrel{\sim}{\longrightarrow}
 \widehat{\mathrm{Tor}}^{A_2}_{q-1}(k,k)\qquad(q\ge2).
\]
For q=1 the boundary maps isomorphically to \(k\widehat\otimes_{A_2}k\): the next map to \(k\widehat\otimes_{A_2}A_2\) vanishes, because \(1\otimes\varepsilon=\varepsilon\cdot1\otimes1=0\). Hence the boundary is again an isomorphism. These are finite-dimensional topological isomorphisms, not merely algebraic bijections. They check the all-degree exactness claim and emphasize why non-flat residue modules cannot be treated as nuclearly free.
