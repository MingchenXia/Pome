Let U be the tube in Lemma 12.28.1, with radius R, D a smooth compact connected divisor of a complex manifold S of dimension n >= 2, and L = O_S(D) unitary flat on U. Put r=|s_D|_h and t=log r² on U minus D.

**Infinite holonomy.** Every PSH function on the punctured tube, apart from the identically minus-infinity function, is exactly g(t), with g a finite convex function on (-infinity, log R²). In particular it is continuous and locally bounded there. It extends as a PSH function to U if and only if g is nondecreasing; its value on D is the limit of g at minus infinity (possibly minus infinity).

Proof: the restriction of a PSH function u to any complex leaf of M_r is PSH or identically minus infinity. If its maximum on the compact M_r is finite, a maximum point exists by upper semicontinuity. On the connected leaf through that point the restriction is constant by the maximum principle. The leaf is dense by Theorem 12.28.3. Upper semicontinuity at each limit point gives u >= that constant, while the maximum gives the reverse inequality. If the maximum is minus infinity, u is identically minus infinity on the level. Thus u depends only on r, without any regularity or boundedness assumption. In a holomorphic submersion chart (z,W_j), restriction to a transverse complex disk is a radial subharmonic function of W_j (or identically minus infinity). On any annulus its logarithmic radial profile is convex: on two circles with finite boundary values, harmonic interpolation in log |W_j| bounds the function by the maximum principle; equivalently, distributionally the Laplacian of the radial function on an annulus is a positive multiple of the second distributional derivative in t. The latter argument applies also to extended-valued PSH representatives, which are locally integrable on transverse annuli where not identically minus infinity. A radial subharmonic function not identically minus infinity has a finite convex representative throughout its interval, and the subharmonic representative agrees pointwise with that continuous function. A profile identically minus infinity on any open subinterval would make u identically minus infinity on an open tube and then everywhere by connectedness. Local convexity therefore patches on the whole interval. Conversely any finite convex profile gives a PSH function, by composition in holomorphic submersion charts, or local one-variable radial subharmonicity.

A PSH extension must be bounded above near the compact D. A finite convex function on (-infinity,b) is bounded above near minus infinity iff it is nondecreasing: a negative secant slope forces growth to plus infinity on the left by convexity; a nondecreasing function is bounded above on every left ray. In the latter case the usual bounded-above removable-singularity principle for subharmonic functions in one variable extends g(log |W|²) over W=0, assigning its left-end limit. Pullback via W gives the PSH extension and the local expressions agree. Uniqueness is the canonical PSH representative, determined off the divisor (a Lebesgue-null set).

**Finite holonomy, exact order m.** Let phi be the proper map in Proposition 12.28.2. Every PSH function on U minus D is exactly v composed with phi, for a subharmonic v on the punctured disk of radius R^m (including the identically minus-infinity case). Indeed every nonzero fiber is a compact connected smooth complex manifold, so the restriction is constant or identically minus infinity. The induced v is subharmonic, since phi is a holomorphic submersion off D and locally admits holomorphic sections. Conversely pullback preserves PSH. Extension over D is equivalent to v being bounded above near zero, using the compactness of D and the tubular properness, then the one-variable removable-singularity theorem. The same factorization for holomorphic functions follows by the maximum modulus principle and local sections, and does not require boundedness.

**Consequence common to both cases.** All locally bounded PSH functions on the punctured tube have (dd^c u)^p=0 for p>=2. In each chart u is a pullback from one complex variable. For bounded subharmonic profiles, local decreasing smooth subharmonic approximations in that variable yield rank-at-most-one Hessians; Bedford–Taylor local monotone continuity proves the claim. For arbitrary PSH functions the non-pluripolar powers of degree >=2 likewise vanish: apply the bounded case to max(u,-j) on relatively compact charts (these truncations are locally bounded) and then use the defining non-pluripolar truncation limit. The same vanishing on the entire tube holds for any PSH extension, because non-pluripolar products charge no part of D. This is a local statement, not a claim for nonzero background forms. It strengthens the printed smooth strict-PSH obstruction to arbitrary PSH functions and a rank statement in the Bedford–Taylor sense.
