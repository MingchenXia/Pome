## Precise question and conclusion

Section 9.74 invokes the non-pseudoeffectivity of the conormal bundle to force equality between the dimension of the smooth local leaf-relation germ (S) and its analytic closure (Msubset X\times X). Its next step uses an external analytic-graph/cycle-space theorem to produce a meromorphic quotient. We **do not reprove** that external theorem. We test the necessity of the hypothesis for the dimension equality and for meromorphic quotient existence using a completely explicit compact Kähler family.

Let
[
 E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+i\mathbb Z),\qquad X=E\times E
]
and, for real (\alpha), let (\mathcal F_\alpha\subset T_X) be the rank-one foliation generated by
[
 V_\alpha=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_1}
              +\alpha\frac{\partial}{\partial z_2}.
]
This is a nowhere-vanishing constant vector field, hence a saturated involutive subbundle. In all cases
[
 \mathcal F_\alpha\simeq\mathcal O_X,qquad
 \mathcal F_\alpha^*\simeq\mathcal O_X.
]
In particular its conormal line is numerically flat and **pseudoeffective**, not non-pseudoeffective. The local leaf-relation germ (S_\alpha) near the diagonal has complex dimension (\dim X+1=3).

**Exact dichotomy:**
[
 \dim \overline{S_\alpha}^{\rm an} =
 \begin{cases}
 3,&\alpha\in\mathbb Q,\\
 4,&\alpha\in\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q.
 \end{cases}
]
For rational (\alpha), there is an explicit holomorphic elliptic quotient inducing (\mathcal F_\alpha); for irrational (\alpha), every leaf is dense in (X), and there is **no nonconstant meromorphic first integral**.

Thus the printed sufficient non-pseudoeffectivity assumption cannot simply be removed from the dimension-equality inference, while rational examples show that it is not necessary for the conclusion in individual cases. Nothing here refutes §9.74, or supplies its external general analytic-graph/cycle-space input.

## Proof: irrational case

Fix (z=(z_1,z_2)\in X). The leaf through (z) is
[
 H_{\alpha,z}=\{(z_1+[t],z_2+[\alpha t]):t\in\mathbb C\}.
]
For fixed first-coordinate target (z_1+[t_0]), every (t=t_0+m+in), (m,n\in\mathbb Z), gives that same first coordinate; the second coordinate ranges over
[
 z_2+[\alpha t_0]+[\alpha m+i\alpha n].
]
For irrational real (\alpha), ({\alpha m\bmod1:m\in\mathbb Z}) is dense in (\mathbb R/\mathbb Z) (the standard elementary irrational-rotation lemma). Independently varying (m,n) makes the displayed second coordinates dense in (E). Since the first-coordinate target was arbitrary, (H_{\alpha,z}) is dense in all of (X).

Let (M\subset X\times X) be **any** closed analytic subset containing the germ of the local leaf relation near the diagonal. For every (z), the fiber
[
 M_z=\{w\in X:(z,w)\in M\}
]
is a closed analytic subset of (X). The inverse image of (M_z) under the entire holomorphic map
[
 \mathbb C\longrightarrow X,quad
 t\longmapsto(z_1+[t],z_2+[\alpha t])
]
contains a neighborhood of (t=0), by the germ inclusion. This inverse image is an analytic subset of the connected complex line, hence is the whole line by the identity theorem. Consequently (M_z) contains (H_{\alpha,z}), and therefore, by closedness and density, (M_z=X). As this holds for all (z), necessarily (M=X\times X). The smallest analytic closure is the whole product and has complex dimension (4).

Suppose a meromorphic map (f:X\dashrightarrow Y) were constant along every leaf and nonconstant. On a dense open set where it is holomorphic, the restriction of (f) to one dense leaf takes a single value. Continuity on that open set then makes (f) constant there, and hence meromorphically constant on (X), contradiction. Thus no nonconstant meromorphic first integral exists.

## Proof: rational case

Write (\alpha=p/q) with relatively prime integers (p,q), (q>0). The group homomorphism
[
 h:X\to E,qquad h(z_1,z_2)=qz_2-pz_1
]
is well defined because (p,q\in\mathbb Z), is holomorphic, and is a submersion. Its kernel is a connected elliptic subtorus (coprimality makes the induced map on integral lattices primitive). The fibers are precisely the leaves of (\mathcal F_\alpha). Hence the global leaf relation
[
 M=\{(z,w)\in X\times X:h(z)=h(w)\}
]
is a closed analytic submanifold of dimension (4-1=3). For each (z) its connected fiber is generated by the germ of the local leaf, so it is the minimal analytic closure. The map (h) is the desired holomorphic quotient.
