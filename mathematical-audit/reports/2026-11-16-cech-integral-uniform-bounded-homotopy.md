## Precise additional theorem

Let \(I\) be any linearly ordered index set and let \(\mathcal N\) be a simplicial nerve, in particular the nerve of an arbitrary indexed open cover. Let \(C_q\) be the free abelian group generated by *ordered* \((q+1)\)-tuples of vertices spanning a simplex, **including repetitions**, with the usual alternating omission boundary, and let \(C_{-1}=0\). Let \(A_q\) be the free subgroup on *strictly increasing* tuples. The map \(\pi:C_q\to A_q\) sorts with the permutation sign and sends a repeated-index tuple to zero; write \(P=i\pi\) for the projection back into \(C_q\).

The explicit integral homotopy \(H_0=0\) and
\[
e_\sigma=\sigma-P\sigma-H_{q-1}\partial\sigma,\qquad
H_q\sigma=(m(\sigma))*e_\sigma,\quad m(\sigma)=\min(\sigma),\ q\ge1,
\]
where the star prefixes the indicated vertex, satisfies
\[
\partial H+H\partial=\mathrm{id}-P,\qquad
\partial P=P\partial,\qquad \pi i=\mathrm{id}.
\]
**Quantitative refinement:** for the basis \(\ell^1\) chain norm,
\[
\boxed{\|H_q\|_{1\to1}\le B_q,\quad B_0=0,\quad
 B_q=2+(q+1)B_{q-1}
 =2(q+1)!\sum_{j=2}^{q+1}\frac1{j!}.}
\]
In particular \(B_1=2,B_2=8,B_3=34,B_4=172,B_5=1034\).
The first two bounds are attained by this specific recursive construction:
\(\|H_1(1,0)\|_1=2\) and \(\|H_2(2,1,0)\|_1=8\). This is not an optimality claim across all homotopies.

## Complete justification of the selected assertion

The sorting map is a chain map **over the integers**. For a tuple with distinct entries, deleting an entry before sorting has the same orientation sign as deleting it after sorting. With exactly one repeated pair, the only potentially surviving deleted terms occur with opposite signs and cancel. With more repetitions each image vanishes. Thus \(\partial\pi=\pi\partial\), and \(\partial P=P\partial\); in degree zero the unaugmented boundary is zero.

Inductively assume \(\partial H+H\partial=\mathrm{id}-P\) in degree \(q-1\). Using \(\partial^2=0\) gives
\[
\partial e_\sigma=(\mathrm{id}-P)\partial\sigma-
\partial H_{q-1}\partial\sigma=0.
\]
Since \(q\ge1\), the elementary cone boundary formula on the \(q\)-cycle \(e_\sigma\) says
\(\partial((m(\sigma))*e_\sigma)=e_\sigma\).
Hence
\(\partial H_q\sigma+H_{q-1}\partial\sigma=\sigma-P\sigma\).
All terms of \(H_q\sigma\) use **only** vertices already in \(\sigma\); no new index is introduced by deletion, sorting, or the prefix minimum. Thus in Čech cochains the formula restricts sections from larger intersections to the original common intersection, and works on infinite covers as well (each image of a basis tuple is a finite chain).

For any basis tuple of dimension \(q\), the projection has \(\|P\sigma\|_1\le1\) and the signed boundary satisfies \(\|\partial\sigma\|_1\le q+1\). Prefixing a vertex does not increase \(\ell^1\) mass. Consequently
\[
\|H_q\sigma\|_1\le1+1+\|H_{q-1}\partial\sigma\|_1
\le2+(q+1)B_{q-1}=B_q.
\]
Extend linearly and then continuously to the chain \(\ell^1\) completion. Dividing by \((q+1)!\) solves the recurrence. In degree one,
\(H_1(1,0)=(0,1,0)+(0,0,1)\) has exactly two unit terms. In degree two, a direct expansion of \(H_2(2,1,0)\) has eight **distinct** signed unit four-tuples, so the upper bound is attained there also.

For a **normed coefficient presheaf whose restriction maps are all nonexpansive**, the dual Čech operator \(K^q=(H_{q-1})^*\) is well defined on bounded ordered cochains and obeys
\[
\boxed{\|K^q c\|_\infty\le B_{q-1}\|c\|_\infty,\qquad
\delta K+K\delta=\mathrm{id}-\mathrm{alt}\circ\mathrm{res}.}
\]
Each cochain component is an integer combination of \(\le B_{q-1}\) terms counted with multiplicity, restricted to the relevant original intersection; the nonexpansive assumption controls each term. The constants depend on cochain degree but **not** on the number of cover sets, repeated indices, or nerve topology. Ordinary constant normed coefficients are an example. For an arbitrary abelian sheaf without norms, the *algebraic* integral homotopy is still valid but this numerical operator inequality has **no asserted meaning**. Arbitrary order-changing refinements are not claimed to commute strictly with this chosen homotopy.
