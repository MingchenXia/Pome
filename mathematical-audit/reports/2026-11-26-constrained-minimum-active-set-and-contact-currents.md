## POME-CONSTRAINED-MINIMUM-ACTIVE-SET-001

Under exactly the hypotheses there, write F(z,s)=H(z,s)-cs, s_*(z)=argmin_{s<=b(z)}F(z,s), v=F(z,s_*), and a=H_ss. Then:

1. s_* is locally Lipschitz and v is C^{1,1}_loc in real coordinates.
2. On the free region H_s(z,b)>c, the complex Hessian of v is P-yy*/a, where P=H_{z bar z}, y=H_{zs}, evaluated at s_*.
3. On the strict active region H_s(z,b)<c, the Hessian is the matrix with entries
   H_{j bar k}+H_{js} b_{bar k}+b_j H_{s bar k}+a b_j b_{bar k}+(H_s-c)b_{j bar k},
   evaluated at b. This same formula holds a.e. on the tie set H_s(z,b)=c.
4. In particular the printed distributional bound improves to the a.e. bound
   i partial barpartial v >= -Lambda - [c-H_s(z,b(z))]_+ C_b widehat omega.
   The coefficient c C_b in the original uniform version cannot be reduced universally.

Proof. For fixed z, the increasing nonnegative H_s tends to zero as s goes to -infinity: any positive limiting slope would force H to tend to -infinity. F is strictly convex and coercive on the left, so the constrained minimizer is unique. If H_s(b)>c there is a unique interior root H_s=c and the implicit function theorem makes it smooth locally. If H_s(b)<c then s_*=b in a neighborhood. At a tie, H is smooth in a neighborhood of the endpoint and a>0, so an unconstrained root r(z) exists locally, possibly beyond b; the minimizer is min(r,b). Thus s_* is locally Lipschitz, including at ties. The value equals F(z,r) or F(z,b) on the two sides. Their real first derivatives agree where r=b, since F_s=0 there. Equivalently the first derivatives everywhere are F_z(z,s_*)+(H_s(z,b)-c)_- b_z, where x_- means min(x,0) here (and the conjugate formula holds). These are locally Lipschitz, proving C^{1,1}. This also follows by Taylor expansion at a tie, since the first-order s contribution vanishes. No singular Hessian measure is left at the interface.

In the free region differentiate H_s=c to get the printed Schur complement P-yy*/a. The joint complex Hessian has blocks (P,y/2;y*/2,a/4) because s=Re zeta. At active points apply the chain rule to F(z,b(z)) to get the displayed expression. Its first four terms are exactly the joint Hermitian form evaluated on a vector with base component xi and complex vertical component 2 partial b(xi); the additional term is (H_s-c) times the base Hessian of b. At the tie, the value agrees with the smooth boundary branch on that measurable set, so their weak second derivatives agree almost everywhere there (locality of weak derivatives, cf. 7.13.3). The coefficient of the last term is zero on the tie. Apply the assumed joint lower bound at the minimizer, where H_s<=c, and multiply the upper Hessian bound of b by the nonpositive coefficient H_s-c. This gives the stated improvement, including on the free region where the positive-part coefficient vanishes. Since v is C^{1,1}, the a.e. bound is the distributional bound. The argument does not require a smooth interface.

Sharpness: on a disk in C take widehat omega=i dz wedge dbar z, H=e^s, Lambda=0, b=B|z|^2-L with B=C_b>0. For L large, e^b<c throughout a fixed smaller disk. At zero the Hessian coefficient of v=e^b-cb is B(e^{-L}-c), tending to -Bc as L tends to infinity. Thus no universal improvement of c C_b by a fixed smaller multiple, even for a jointly psh smooth H. The refined variable-coefficient formula is attained at zero in this example.

The printed barrier argument is also valid. A quantitative improvement to its convergence (lines 52–54) is available without any joint curvature estimate: on any compact base subset, for sufficiently small 0<epsilon<1, with uniform L>=1 bounding b-s_epsilon at the penalized minima,
   -epsilon log L <= v_epsilon-v <= c epsilon + epsilon log(1/epsilon).
Indeed the left bound follows from F(z,s_epsilon)>=v and the uniform compact range proved in the original coercivity argument. For the right bound evaluate the barrier at s_*-epsilon, where b-s>=epsilon and 0<=H_s<=c along [s_*-epsilon,s_*]. Integration yields F(z,s_*-epsilon)-v<=c epsilon. On a strict active point set d=c-H_s(z,b)>0. Local uniform convergence of the minima and uniqueness imply s_epsilon->b (the lower bound on the penalty ensures F(z,s_epsilon)->v). The critical equation then yields b-s_epsilon=epsilon/(d+o(1)), and Taylor expansion gives
   v_epsilon-v=epsilon log(1/epsilon)+epsilon(1+log d)+o(epsilon).
Thus the logarithmic rate is genuinely sharp at strict active points. At a fixed free point the penalized root is smooth in epsilon near zero by IFT, so v_epsilon-v=O(epsilon). No uniform strict-active expansion across the tie is asserted.

Assume the printed hypotheses for p ordered pairs (u_j,f_j), 1<=p<=n, with closed theta_j, on the compact Kähler manifold. Put E=intersection {u_j=f_j}, T_j=theta_j+dd^c u_j, A_j=theta_j+dd^c f_j (weak coefficients). Then the top-degree theorem implies the full coefficient-current identity
   1_E <T_1 wedge ... wedge T_p> = 1_E A_1 wedge ... wedge A_p
in bidegree (p,p). On E, each A_j is positive semidefinite a.e., with bounded coefficients there. The right side is a well-defined coefficient measure: each f_j is W^{2,q} for all finite q by 16.14.9, so coefficients of a p-fold product are L^1 by Holder. The left side has order zero by positivity.

Proof. Fill the remaining n-p slots in 16.20.2 with background arbitrary global Kähler forms kappa_l and potentials and obstacles both zero. The full contact set is still E. Smooth bounded-factor compatibility of the non-pluripolar product gives equality after wedging both sides with every such product of Kähler forms. By multilinearity, the same holds for arbitrary smooth closed real (1,1)-forms beta_l, because each beta_l is a difference of two Kähler forms, beta_l+M omega and M omega for M sufficiently large. For p=n no additional testing is needed. For p<n, fix a relatively compact coordinate ball and a smaller one. Each constant-coefficient real (1,1)-form on the smaller ball is the restriction of a global smooth exact real (1,1)-form dd^c(chi q), using a real quadratic potential q and a cutoff chi equal to one on the smaller ball. Products of constant (1,1)-forms linearly span the real (n-p,n-p)-forms: after complexification every basis monomial dz_I wedge dbar z_J is such a product up to sign and scalar, and real and imaginary parts recover the real span. Test the wedge equalities with compactly supported scalar functions in the smaller ball. This separates every coefficient measure of the difference, hence the two restricted currents agree there and globally. For p=1 the positive current on the left forces the weak matrix A_1 to be positive semidefinite Lebesgue-a.e. on {u_1=f_1}: test against a countable dense family of constant vectors and nonnegative functions in each coordinate chart. Applying this separately to each pair proves the positivity assertion on E. Bounded coefficients on each contact set follow from exactly the v_j envelope/weak Hessian locality argument in Step 5 (line 106). No top-degree mass convergence for unbounded potentials is assumed.

This strengthens the mixed top-degree statement to all degrees without a new regularization argument. It does not assert that lower-degree products of envelopes are carried by the contact set.
