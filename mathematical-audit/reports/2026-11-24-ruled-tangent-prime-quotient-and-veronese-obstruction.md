## Deduction

Retain the printed hypotheses: E is an elliptic curve, A a line bundle of positive degree d, M the specified split ruled surface, U the nonsplit rank-two extension of trivial line bundles with invariant line generated by h. Write R for the graded symmetric tangent section algebra in Theorem 12.31.4, and R_{r,k} for degree r and weight -k (k>=0). Let B=direct sum_{k>=0} H^0(E,A^k), with its usual multiplication, and B_+ its positive-degree ideal. Introduce a formal variable t of tensor degree one and weight index zero; it is NOT the ruling coordinate of the printed local chart.

**Claim 1.** There is a bigraded algebra isomorphism
```
R/hR = C + B_+[t]  (subalgebra of B[t]).
```
In this formula an element of H^0(E,A^k)t^s has bidegree (k+s,k). In particular hR is a prime ideal; the quotient is reduced and is not Noetherian.

Proof. In weight zero the quotient is C, by Lemma 12.31.2. For k>=1 and s=r-k>=1 the leading u coefficient in a homogeneous polynomial in h,u gives the short exact bundle sequence
```
0 -> A^k tensor Sym^{s-1} U --h--> A^k tensor Sym^s U -> A^k -> 0.
```
The argument in lines 123–129 works for every k, not only k=1. The final coefficient is invariant under translation of u by a multiple of h; the kernel consists precisely of the polynomials divisible by h. By the H^1 vanishing in lines 85–87, global sections of the last term lift. For s=0 the quotient is already H^0(E,A^k). Thus the leading coefficient times t^s identifies each block of R/hR. For negative weights multiplication of the coefficient polynomials is ordinary multiplication, by Lemma 12.31.1. The highest-degree coefficients therefore multiply (also when either vanishes); this proves the algebra isomorphism, not just equality of Hilbert series. The section ring B is a domain: nonzero sections multiply to a nonzero section on the integral curve, and the highest nonzero degree of a product of two inhomogeneous elements cannot cancel. Hence its polynomial ring and the displayed subalgebra are domains.

The ideal J=B_+[t] of this subalgebra is not finitely generated. Its quotient J/J^2 contains the entire weight-index-one part, H^0(E,A)[t], since every term in J^2 has weight index at least two. The latter space is infinite-dimensional as d>0. A finite generating set for J would span J/J^2 over the residue field (C+B_+[t])/J=C, contradiction. This also proves the non-Noetherian assertion for R itself via its quotient.

**Claim 2.** Every positive Veronese R^{(m)} = direct sum_{j>=0} R_{mj} (m>=1, regraded by j) fails to be finitely generated, even though its degree-j, weight-index-one indecomposable space has the exact dimension md for every j>=1.

Proof. Let Q_m be its positive-degree ideal modulo the square of that ideal. All weights have nonnegative index. The zero-weight part is C[h^m]. A product of two positive-degree terms of total weight index one must have one factor of index zero and one factor of index one. Therefore
```
(Q_m)_{j,1} = R_{mj,1}/h^m R_{m(j-1),1},
```
where R_{0,1}=0. The dimensions of the numerator and denominator are respectively dmj and dm(j-1), by the formula in Lemma 12.31.2; multiplication by h^m is injective since it is multiplication of polynomials in the local chart. This also gives the formula for j=1. Consequently these infinitely many disjoint blocks each have dimension md. Any finite set of algebra generators would give a finite spanning set of the indecomposable quotient after removing the constant terms. This contradiction proves the claim. More precisely, to generate all components in Veronese degrees <=N requires at least mdN+1 independent generator classes (including h^m), in the algebra truncated by the ideal of degrees >N.

For V=W^* under the hypotheses of Theorem 12.32.2, its section algebra is R[w] by the printed degree-preserving isomorphism, with deg w=1. Evaluation w=0 induces a surjection on every positive Veronese onto R^{(m)}. It follows that none of these Veronese section algebras is finitely generated or Noetherian (a nonnegative graded connected C-algebra that is Noetherian has finitely generated augmentation ideal, hence finitely generated algebra by taking homogeneous parts and induction on degree). Under the projectivization convention of Theorem 12.30.4 these are precisely the section rings of all positive tensor powers of the tautological line bundles on P(T_M) and P(V). The latter includes every canonical extension of a Kähler class by Corollary 12.32.3. No claim about existence of a Zariski decomposition is needed.
