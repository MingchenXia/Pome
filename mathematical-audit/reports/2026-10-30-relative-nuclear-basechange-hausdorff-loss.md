## Finding POME-RELNUC-BASECHANGE-HS-LOSS-001

**Severity:** low. **Status:** verified conditional deduction (indeed an elementary, fully explicit example under the printed hypotheses), **not** a false theorem, proof gap, or verified general repair.

**Claim.** All assumptions of Lemma 9.46.3 can hold with a **Hausdorff original cokernel of complex dimension one**, but after extension of scalars to a complete Fréchet algebra the induced cokernel can be **non-Hausdorff and infinite-dimensional over the complex numbers**, while remaining generated by **one element over the new algebra**. Thus the lemma's refusal to assert Hausdorffness cannot be strengthened even by assuming Hausdorffness of the source cokernel.

### Explicit algebras and nuclear map

Let \(B=A(\overline{\mathbb D})\) be the disk algebra (continuous on the closed unit disk, holomorphic in its interior) with its supremum Banach algebra norm, and let \(z\in B\) be the coordinate. Set \(S=B\) as a Fréchet \(B\)-module and
\[
v:S\longrightarrow S,\qquad v(b)=(1-z)b.
\]
In the printed definition of relative \(B\)-nuclearity (§9.46 lines 6–13), \(v\) has the **one-term nuclear representation**
\[
v(b)=1\cdot\operatorname{id}_B(b)\cdot(1-z).
\]
The coefficient map is \(B\)-linear and equicontinuity and boundedness are immediate. Moreover \(I-v=m_z\). If \(f\in B\) satisfies \(f(0)=0\), the quotient \(f(z)/z\), filled holomorphically at zero, is still in \(B\). Consequently
\[
zB=\ker(\operatorname{ev}_0),\qquad \operatorname{coker}(I-v)=B/zB\simeq\mathbb C
\]
as a **Hausdorff** one-dimensional complex topological vector space.

Let \(C=C^\infty([-1,1],\mathbb C)\) with its usual seminorms \(\|f\|_{C^k}\), a complete unital Fréchet algebra. Define
\[
h(x)=\begin{cases}\exp(-1/x^2),&x\ne0,\\0,&x=0.\end{cases}
\]
Here \(0\le h\le e^{-1}<1\). The substitution
\[
\rho:B\longrightarrow C,\qquad \rho(f)=f\circ h
\]
is a continuous unital algebra homomorphism: for any fixed \(k\), the \(k\) derivatives of \(f\circ h\) are uniformly bounded by a constant times \(\|f\|_B\), by the chain rule and Cauchy inequalities for \(f\) on a closed disk strictly between \(e^{-1}\) and \(1\). In particular \(\rho(z)=h\).

Since \(S=B\) is free of rank one, multiplication gives an isomorphism of **actual** balanced completed Fréchet modules
\[
C\widehat\otimes_B B\simeq C,\qquad c\otimes b\longmapsto c\rho(b),
\]
with continuous inverse \(c\mapsto c\otimes1\). The induced \(v_C\) is multiplication by \(1-h\), so
\[
\operatorname{coker}(I-v_C)=C/hC. \tag{1}
\]
There is no passage to a Hausdorff quotient in (1).

### Proof that the new range is not closed

Let
\[
g(x)=\begin{cases}\exp(-1/(2x^2)),&x\ne0,\\0,&x=0.\end{cases}
\]
Both \(g\) and \(h\) are smooth and flat at \(x=0\). But \(g/h=\exp(1/(2x^2))\) is unbounded near zero, hence \(g\notin hC\).

Choose smooth cutoffs \(\chi_\varepsilon\) that vanish on \(|x|\le\varepsilon\), equal \(1\) on \(|x|\ge2\varepsilon\), and satisfy
\(\|\chi_\varepsilon^{(j)}\|_\infty=O_j(\varepsilon^{-j})\).
Then
\[
f_\varepsilon=\chi_\varepsilon\,g/h\in C,\qquad
hf_\varepsilon=\chi_\varepsilon g.
\]
For every fixed \(k\), Leibniz's rule together with the flatness estimate
\(\sup_{|x|\le2\varepsilon}|g^{(j)}(x)|=O_{j,N}(\varepsilon^N)\)
for every \(N\) shows
\[
\|\chi_\varepsilon g-g\|_{C^k}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus \(g\in\overline{hC}\setminus hC\) and \(C/hC\) is **not Hausdorff**.

In fact the closure is exactly the ideal
\[
\overline{hC}
=\{f\in C:f^{(k)}(0)=0\text{ for every }k\ge0\}.
\]
The inclusion from left to right follows because \(h\) is flat and differentiation is continuous. Conversely if \(f\) is flat, \(\chi_\varepsilon f\to f\) in every \(C^k\), while \(\chi_\varepsilon f=h(\chi_\varepsilon f/h)\in hC\), since the quotient is smooth and identically zero near zero. Finally no nonzero polynomial is flat, so the distinct classes of \(1,x,x^2,\dots\) are complex-linearly independent in \(C/hC\): it is infinite-dimensional over \(\mathbb C\), **though cyclic as a \(C\)-module**.
