## POME-PSH-REMOVABLE-CRITICAL-HAUSDORFF-NULL-001

**Low / verified conditional deduction (strengthening, NOT a false printed theorem).** Theorem 5.11.3 (permanent tag 76D63A606619, physical lines 103–122) and Lemma 5.11.2 (B88B826B1EDC, lines 51–98) in `docs/sections/plurisubharmonic-functions--extension-across-closed-exceptional-sets/index.html` (blob fdcb827646cee9efcdeae37dfc8d6daf1c2a225b) use locally H^{2n-3}-null sets. Their entire argument works for locally H^{2n-2}-null sets. The dimension exponent 2n-2 is optimal for this null-measure formulation.

**Statement.** Let n>=2, let Omega be a domain in C^n, and S relatively closed in Omega with locally zero (2n-2)-dimensional Hausdorff measure. Every PSH function u on Omega\S extends uniquely to a PSH function on Omega without assuming a local upper bound; the extension on S is the limsup from the complement.

**Proof.** Write d=2n, k=d-2. Every compact K contained in S has H^k(K)=0 by finite localization. We check each of the three inputs of Lemma 5.11.2 with this new exponent.

1. Real polarity. If K is nonpolar, the finite Newtonian energy criterion (Theorem 4.1.17, as used in lines 69–81) supplies a probability mu on K of finite energy for c_d|x-y|^{-k}, c_d>0. The lower semicontinuous potential U_mu is finite mu-a.e.; for some M<infinity the compact E=K intersect {U_mu<=M} has positive mu-mass. Put nu=mu restricted to E. For each a in E and r>0, c_d r^{-k}nu(B(a,r))<=U_mu(a)<=M. Given any eta>0, cover E by balls with sum r_l^k<eta; discard balls missing E, and recenter the others at a_l in E with doubled radii. Then 0<nu(E)<=2^k M eta/c_d, impossible as eta tends to zero. Thus K is real polar. Unlike the printed estimate, no use of an extra power of the radius is necessary.
2. Directions. For each fixed x, exhaust K\{x} by the compact sets K_m=K intersect {|y-x|>=1/m}. The map y↦[y-x] to CP^{n-1} is Lipschitz on each K_m by the normalization and Hopf-map estimate of lines 83–95. Its image has zero H^k measure, and therefore zero invariant probability sigma, using the ball-volume upper bound sigma(B)<=C r^k of Lemma 2.9.19. Countable union preserves sigma-nullity; the direction set is Borel. This uses equality of the exponent with the real dimension of CP^{n-1}, not a strict inequality.
3. Projections. Orthogonal projection onto any complex hyperplane is 1-Lipschitz. The image of K has zero H^k measure in real dimension k, hence zero Lebesgue measure. Again equality of dimensions is allowed.

Apply these three properties to S intersect each closed ball compactly contained in Omega. For p in S, choose a complex line through p whose direction avoids all other points of S in that ball. Its boundary circle is disjoint from S. By upper semicontinuity, u is bounded above by a finite C on a neighborhood of the circle. For any sequence x_j outside S tending to p choose complex lines through x_j avoiding S in the closed ball, with directions tending to the first line (the complement of a sigma-null set is dense, since open balls have positive sigma-measure). Their boundary circles converge uniformly to the original circle by the explicit projection/radius computation of lines 113–115. The maximum principle on the corresponding closed disks gives u(x_j)<=C for all sufficiently large j. This rules out any sequence of unbounded values, hence establishes the local upper bound near p.

A countable compact exhaustion of the relatively closed S shows S real polar. The subharmonic removable-set theorem 3.4.27 now gives the canonical subharmonic extension U. The extension is locally integrable since S is Lebesgue null and the complement carries finite-a.e. u. For each unit complex direction a and nonnegative smooth compactly supported test chi, the projection of S intersect supp chi to (Ca)^perp is Lebesgue null by step 3 (that intersection is compact). For almost every parallel complex line, the open subset obtained by deleting S contains the support of its restricted test, and U there is u. The precise hypothesis of Lemma 5.8.2 (tag 3C70A4ABB14A, lines 19–41 of `docs/sections/plurisubharmonic-functions--the-line-definition-and-upper-semicontinuity/index.html`, blob 7a23771b2b16ff8610174712a72ceb42fcf011ff) is therefore satisfied for this direction/test. Its Fubini argument gives nonnegative directional distributional Hessian. Since the direction and test are arbitrary, dd^c U>=0. Theorem 5.9.1 identifies its canonical subharmonic representative as PSH. Any two extensions coincide a.e., hence everywhere. This proves the assertion.

**Sharpness of the Hausdorff-null exponent, not a classification of all removable sets.** In the unit ball in C^n set S={z_1=0}. It is relatively closed, of locally finite positive H^k measure, and H^q-null for every q>k. On its complement u=-log|z_1| is smooth pluriharmonic, hence PSH, but tends to +infinity approaching S. A PSH function taking values in [-infinity,+infinity) is locally bounded above, so no extension exists. In particular neither replacing the zero H^k assumption by locally finite H^k nor replacing k by any larger null exponent suffices in general. The original stricter hypothesis is sufficient, not false.
