## POME-RELATIVE-OBSTACLE-DELETION-CAPACITY-SHARP-001

Severity low; status **verified conditional deduction**, not a false printed theorem. Target: Proposition 16.35.2, permanent tag 884332AF4253, source anchor klimek-pluripotential-theory-40886378, docs/sections/polynomial-families-and-equilibrium-null-obstacles/index.html, blob 568257f81e9f31491e0cdde36ba8efb357423cd7, lines 38–46.

Let D be a bounded hyperconvex domain, K compact in D, N a Borel subset of K, h=H_{K,D}, v=H_{K\N,D} (H denotes the regularized relative extremal). Write nu=(dd^c h)^n. Then -1<=h<=v<=0 and both have zero boundary limits. In the exact capacity normalization of Definition 7.4.2, for every t>0,
    
    t^n Cap_D({v-h>t}) <= nu({v>h}) <= nu(N).

In particular the estimate gives uniform convergence in global capacity whenever the deleted equilibrium mass tends to zero, even if N varies over a net; it is an individual inequality, not a measure-theoretic dominated convergence theorem for nets. The coefficient 1 is sharp for every 0<t<1 and every n>=1. Moreover h=v if and only if nu({v>h})=0; the stronger sufficient condition nu(N)=0 in the printed statement is not necessary.

### Proof
If K is pluripolar the envelopes vanish and the assertion is immediate. Otherwise the support and contact statements in Theorem 7.16.5 give nu carried by K intersect {h=-1}; on K\N, v=-1 outside the pluripolar discrepancy set of the raw envelope. Bounded Bedford–Taylor products do not charge pluripolar sets, so nu({v>h})<=nu(N).

Given any psh capacity test 0<=a<=1, put w=v+t(a-1). This is bounded psh and v-t<=w<=v. At every boundary point liminf(h-w)>=0, since h tends to zero and w<=0. Thus Theorem 7.3.11 applies to h,w. The mixed product expansion with positive coefficients implies (dd^c w)^n >= t^n(dd^c a)^n. The Borel inclusions {v-h>t} subset {h<w} subset {v>h} give

    t^n int_{v-h>t}(dd^c a)^n
    <= int_{h<w}(dd^c w)^n
    <= int_{h<w}(dd^c h)^n
    <= nu({v>h}).

Taking the supremum over a proves the estimate. If nu({v>h})=0, every positive superlevel of v-h has capacity zero, hence Lebesgue measure zero by the fixed quadratic capacity test (or 7.4.3). The countable union of levels 1/j has Lebesgue measure zero. Equality almost everywhere of the two psh canonical representatives implies equality everywhere. The converse is immediate. This also gives an alternative comparison-only proof of the null-deletion proposition. Completed-null subsets are treated by sandwiching between the Borel null deletion and K, exactly as in the original proof.

### Sharpness, and why the converse with nu(N) fails
Take D the Euclidean unit ball in C^n, K the closed ball of radius r in (0,1), N=K. The ball formula in Proposition 7.16.1 yields h=max(-1,log|z|/log(1/r)), v=0. For 0<t<1 the strict superlevel is the open ball of radius r^t. Set g_s=max(log|z|,log s), 0<s<1. Its top measure is supported on the sphere of radius s: inside it is constant, and outside the sphere dd^c log|z| has rank n-1 (zero in dimension one). For any s,s' the potentials agree outside a compact subball, so the telescoping bounded-product identity in the proof of 7.3.11, tested by a cutoff equal to one on the compact difference support, gives equal total masses A_n for g_s and g_s'. A_n>0 by the positive quadratic capacity test and 7.16.5. Hence Cap_D(closed B_s)=A_n/(log(1/s))^n. Borel continuity from below gives exactly the same formula for open B_s. Consequently t^n Cap_D(B_{r^t})=A_n/(log(1/r))^n=nu(K)=nu(N). No numerical value of A_n or change of dd^c convention is necessary.

For the converse counterexample use the same K, but take N=its boundary sphere. Now nu(N)=nu(K)>0. The remaining open ball contains every concentric closed ball of smaller radius. The explicit formula and monotonicity squeeze its relative extremal to h as the radii increase to r. Thus v=h although all the original equilibrium mass was deleted. This is about obstacle deletion, not a claim that the support of the printed global equilibrium measure lies inside an open obstacle.
