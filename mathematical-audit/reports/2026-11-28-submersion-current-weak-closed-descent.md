## Statement and proof

Let F:X→Y be a surjective holomorphic submersion of second-countable complex manifolds, relative dimension r. For every bidegree (p,q) on Y the map F* on currents is a continuous linear injection with weakly closed image and continuous inverse on that image. It admits a weakly continuous linear left inverse P. In diagonal bidegree P preserves positivity. If fibers are connected, the image consists precisely of the currents S of that bidegree on X satisfying i_V S=0 and L_V S=0 for every smooth real vertical vector field V. Here contractions and Lie derivatives are distributional; L_V=d i_V+i_V d. No properness or Kähler hypothesis is required.

### A. A properly supported fiber averaging form

For each y choose a product coordinate chart in X through a point of F^{-1}(y), over a base neighborhood U. Shrink to a product U×W and choose a smooth nonnegative compactly supported top-degree form eta_W on W of integral 1, in the complex orientation. Choose a locally finite smooth partition of unity rho_i on Y subordinate to such U_i, with supports contained in U_i. On each product chart extend rho_i(z) eta_{W_i}(w) by zero. It is smooth: the base support is closed and contained in U_i, and the fiber support is compactly contained in W_i. The locally finite sum eta of these forms is a smooth strongly positive (r,r)-form with support proper over Y. Indeed a compact K in Y meets only finitely many supports of rho_i, and for each of these the product of K intersected with supp rho_i and supp eta_{W_i} is compact in the chart. The same construction works at r=0 with integral-one weight on a one-point fiber chart.

Define P(S)=F_*(S wedge eta). Proposition 5.6.1 makes this well-defined; for any compactly supported test phi on Y the expression is exactly S(eta wedge F*phi), a compactly supported smooth test on X. This gives weak continuity for arbitrary nets. Positivity follows from strong positivity of eta, with positive pullback tests, and the sign is positive because eta has even total degree. Proposition 5.6.2, tested in each product chart, gives P(F*T)=T since fiber integration of eta is identically 1; the partition sum on the base is 1. No differential commutation for P is claimed since eta need not be closed.

Thus F* is injective and a weak homeomorphism onto its image. The image equals the kernel of I−F*P, a continuous linear map into a Hausdorff weak current space. This proves closedness, without sequential compactness. In particular if F*T_i tends weakly to S along a net then S=F*P(S) and T_i tends weakly to P(S). Positivity is reflected by P; closedness is reflected because F* commutes with d and is injective in the relevant degrees (or by the local coefficient formula), not because P commutes with d. The same applies to partial derivatives.

### B. Intrinsic characterization for connected fibers

Necessity follows locally from the coefficient expression of 5.6.2: there are no vertical differential factors and coefficients are independent of fiber coordinates in the distributional sense. Cartan's identity, and the same fact for d(F*T), then give the two conditions for arbitrary vertical V.

Conversely, work in a product coordinate chart U×W with W a real rectangular box (shrink the complex chart to a polydisc of rectangular real coordinates if necessary). Testing the contractions against vertical coordinate vector fields, locally extended with cutoff, shows all coefficients with any vertical differential vanish. Testing Lie derivatives against constant vertical coordinate fields shows all vertical distributional derivatives of the remaining coefficients vanish.

For completeness, a distribution a on U×W with these derivatives zero has the form b(z) tensor Lebesgue measure in w. Write W as a product of intervals and fix an integral-one test bump h_j in each interval. Given a compactly supported smooth test psi, replace its dependence on the first coordinate w_1 by h_1(w_1) times its integral in w_1. The difference is a w_1-derivative of a compactly supported smooth function: integrate the difference from the left endpoint; its integral over the interval is zero, so the primitive vanishes near both endpoints. Iterate through all coordinates. The sum of these derivatives is annihilated by a, leaving only the test (product h_j) times the total fiber integral of psi. Define b by testing a against this fixed product times a base test. The definition is continuous in the base test topology, and proves the asserted factorization. This also proves uniqueness. Thus S locally has the form F*T_i for a unique current T_i on the product chart's base.

For each y, any two points of F^{-1}(y) can be joined by a path in the connected smooth fiber. Cover the path by finitely many product charts with successive overlaps meeting the fiber. On each overlap shrink to another product chart through a point over y. Local pullback injectivity (testing with an integral-one fiber bump) shows the base germs at y agree. Hence the locally constructed base current germ is independent of the point of the fiber. As this holds at every base point, the T_i agree locally on overlaps of their base domains, so the current gluing lemma gives a global T with S=F*T. Without connected fibers the assertion about the image fails: on two disjoint copies of Y mapping identically to Y, different constants on the two sheets satisfy the vertical conditions but do not descend. The split embedding in A nevertheless holds.

### C. Distinction from proper pushforward

There is no analogous unconditional weak continuity for pushforward merely assuming each support is proper separately. For F:C→{point}, delta_j at the integer j is a compactly supported (1,1)-current, converging weakly to zero against compactly supported tests on C; F_*delta_j is the constant mass 1 on the point. This does not contradict 5.6.1, which does not assert that continuity. On a fixed closed support proper over the base the cutoff in its proof is uniform for every current in the family, and continuity then follows. For the averaging construction above this uniform support is supp eta.
