## POME-NULL-INDEFINITE-COMPRESSION-EXACT-DEFECT-001
Low / verified conditional deduction; not a false theorem. Proposition 7.15.4, source klimek-pluripotential-theory:3B18B0CF, permanent 992E9692EF3B, lines 84–107; Example 7.15.5, source klimek-pluripotential-theory:D21B8572, permanent 849194DD5398, lines 115–120. Retain reducibility of A and the three wedge premises of 7.15.4, but allow B Hermitian indefinite. Put K=ker A=ker A*, E=K-perp; write B in blocks (H,D;D*,C) for E⊕K. Then dim E=p, rank C=q, and the exact common complex nullity is

    dim(ker A ∩ ker B)=n-p-q-rank(D restricted to ker C).

Proof: the exterior-power expansion in line 97 gives rank A=p. Since A is invertible on E and zero on K, its p-th power fills E, and the other wedge conditions reduce to powers of the compression C, giving rank C=q. By the real-nullspace calculation at lines 62–68 and reducibility, the common nullspace is the complex kernel intersection. For v∈K, B(0,v)=(Dv,Cv), so the intersection is the kernel of D restricted to ker C. Rank-nullity proves the formula. Every integer defect r between zero and min(p,n-p-q) is realized by constant closed forms: take A=diag(I_p,0), C=diag(I_q,0) on K, H=0, and let D send r independent basis vectors of ker C to r distinct basis vectors of E and vanish on the others. Then the wedge conditions hold and the common nullity is n-p-q-r. If B is semipositive, a zero quadratic value implies Bv=0, so D vanishes on ker C; this recovers the original proposition. The original indefinite example corresponds to n=3,p=q=r=1. This identifies the exact weaker pointwise substitute for semipositivity. For variable C^1 forms constant defect yields constant rank; the differential nullspace hypothesis of Theorem 7.15.3 (source klimek-pluripotential-theory:FB2152E8, permanent 8FE75D79B9CA, lines 51–79) is still needed for a foliation. ## POME-RAY-LAPLACE-SHARP-SLOPE-001
Low / verified conditional deduction with local proof repair, not a false theorem. Proposition 17.13.35 (He–Testorf–Wang), source sgpt:5698E0B2, permanent E6915C7339CD, lines 698–760. Retain its positive-mass model base and ray hypotheses. Set C=ell^*_max, a=C-tau>0 and J_tau(x)=integral_0^infty exp(-ell_t(x)+t tau) dt. Almost everywhere,

    exp(-ell^*_tau(x))/(C-tau) <= J_tau(x)
        <= exp(-ell^*_(tau+epsilon)(x))/epsilon, 0<epsilon<C-tau.

Proof: the normalized ray ell_t-Ct decreases by 17.13.31. Write g(t)=ell_t(x)-t tau. For every s>0 and t>=s, g(t)<=g(s)+a(t-s). Integrating over [s,infty) gives J_tau>=exp(-g(s))/a. Supremize over s>0. This also handles an infimum of -infty with finite slices by taking arbitrary finite approximations. No minimizing time, measurable selection, or exchange of infimum and integral is required. The upper bound follows from g(t)>=ell^*_(tau+epsilon)(x)+epsilon t, by 17.13.14; this upper bound was already implicit in lines 745–759. Sharpness of the lower bound: the constant-speed ray ell_t=phi+Ct has conjugate phi at every tau<C, and J_tau=exp(-phi)/(C-tau) wherever phi is finite.

Line 712 of Step 1 asserts existence of a near-infimum time for any y; this is literally false when ell^*_tau(y)=-infty and every ell_t(y) is finite. On P^1 with probability Fubini–Study, the smooth geodesic ray ell_t(z)=log((1+exp(-t)|z|^2)/(1+|z|^2)) has C=0, ell_t(infty)=-t, and ell^*_{-1/2}(infty)=-infty. The displayed near-infimum inequality cannot hold at infinity. This is not a counterexample to the multiplier ideal equality: conjugates for tau<C are theta-psh and finite almost everywhere, and the integrability argument only needs that full-measure set.
