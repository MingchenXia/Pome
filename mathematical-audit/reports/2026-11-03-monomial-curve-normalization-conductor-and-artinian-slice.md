## Independent complete algebra calculation

**(1) Fraction field and normalization.** For (N\ge2), (t=t^{N+1}/t^N\in\operatorname{Frac}(R_N)). Every convergent germ (g(t)) has a convergent residue-class expansion
[
g(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}t^r g_r(t^N),
qquad g_r\in\mathbb C\{x\},
]
so (A\subset\operatorname{Frac}(R_N)). Thus (operatorname{Frac}(R_N)=operatorname{Frac}(A)) (also trivially for (N=1)). This decomposition shows that (A) is a finite (R_N)-module generated by (1,t,\ldots,t^{N-1}); therefore every element of (A) is integral over (R_N). Conversely every element of the common fraction field integral over (R_N) is integral over (A), hence belongs to the integrally closed discrete valuation ring (A). Consequently
[
\boxed{\overline{R_N}=\mathbb C\{t\}.}
]
This calculation distinguishes the one-branch normalization from a purely topological reduction.

**(2) Exact defect and conductor.** Modulo (R_N), the germs (t,\ldots,t^{N-1}) form a (mathbb C)-basis of (A/R_N). The maximal ideal (mathfrak m_N=t^NA) of (R_N) kills that quotient, so its **module length** is
[
\boxed{\delta(R_N)=\ell_{R_N}(\overline{R_N}/R_N)=N-1.}
]
The conductor in the normalization is
[
(R_N:A)=\{f\in A:fA\subset R_N\}.
]
Every element of (t^NA) works. If a proposed conductor element has a nonzero term of order (1,\ldots,N-1), it is not even in (R_N); if it has a nonzero constant term, multiplication by (t) produces a term of order (1<N) and fails membership in (R_N) for (N\ge2). Thus (and trivially at (N=1))
[
\boxed{(R_N:A)=t^N\mathbb C\{t\}=\mathfrak m_N.}
]
In particular the conductor exponent and the delta invariant are **different**: (N) versus (N-1).

**(3) One-dimensional parameter, multiplicity, and exact Artinian slice.** Put (x=t^N). Grouping exponents modulo (N) more carefully yields a **free**, rank-(N) (mathbb C\{x\})-module decomposition
[
R_N=\mathbb C\{x\}\oplus
\bigoplus_{r=1}^{N-1}t^{N+r}\mathbb C\{x\}.
]
Hence (R_N) is a one-dimensional local domain finite flat over the smooth parameter ring (mathbb C\{x\}); (x) is a non-zero-divisor and a parameter. Explicitly
[
xR_N=\mathbb C t^N+t^{2N}\mathbb C\{t\},\qquad
R_N/xR_N=\mathbb C\langle1,t^{N+1},\ldots,t^{2N-1}\rangle.
]
For (N\ge2), the images of (t^{N+1},\ldots,t^{2N-1}) form a maximal ideal whose square vanishes. Thus
[
\boxed{R_N/(t^N)\cong
\mathbb C[\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_{N-1}]
/(\epsilon_i\epsilon_j:1\le i,j\le N-1),}
]
and its (mathbb C)-dimension is exactly (N). The parameter length, equivalently the Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity of this one-dimensional Cohen–Macaulay local ring, is
[
\boxed{e(R_N)=N.}
]
Its Artinian parameter quotient has socle dimension (N-1) for (N\ge2); for (N=1), the quotient is (mathbb C) and its socle dimension is (1). This is an **explicit calculation**, not an unsupported assertion that all these curves are complete intersections.

**(4) Independent small finite check.** The exponent-basis test for (N=1,\ldots,8) returned parameter lengths (1,2,\ldots,8), delta dimensions (0,1,\ldots,7), and socle dimensions (1,1,2,3,4,5,6,7). This arithmetic check is supplementary to (1)–(3), not a software test of the Pome repository or a formal proof.
