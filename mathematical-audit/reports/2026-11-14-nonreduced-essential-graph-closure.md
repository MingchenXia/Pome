## Exact hypothesis and statement

Let `D` be a disk about 0 and let `X` be the (ordinary) doubled analytic disk with structure sheaf
\[
\mathcal O_X=\mathcal O_D[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Its reduced space is `D`. Set `A={0}`, `U=X\setminus A` (with the full nonreduced structure), and let `Y=\mathbb C_w` be the reduced affine line. There is a holomorphic morphism
\[
F:U\longrightarrow Y,\qquad F^\#(w)=\varepsilon e^{1/z}.
\]
It is well defined: the target coordinate can be assigned any section of `\mathcal O_U`, including a square-zero section. This particular assignment has an essential singularity only in the nilpotent direction.

**Claims (all proved below):**
1. `F` is an admissible meromorphic-map **representative exactly as defined** by the printed Definition 9.67.4: `A` is nowhere dense and the reduced closure of its graph is the analytic set `{w=0}` in `(X\times Y)_{red}=D\times\mathbb C`.
2. The scalar coordinate `\varepsilon e^{1/z}` has **no extension as a meromorphic section of `\mathcal M_X` at 0**. Nor is `F` equivalent to the zero morphism on any smaller analytic dense open.
3. More strongly, the graph of `F` over `U` admits **no closed analytic subspace extension near `(0,0)` whose underlying support is exactly its reduced topological closure `{w=0}`**. Thus the reduced-graph condition cannot secretly guarantee a full nonreduced analytic graph closure.

This is a **scope separation**, not a false assertion: the printed Definition expressly asks only for an analytic *reduced* graph closure and expressly disclaims a canonical full nilpotent graph. The reduced theorem 9.67.2 is unaffected.

## Proof

**(1) Reduced closure.** Since the residue of `\varepsilon e^{1/z}` is zero, the underlying continuous map of `F` is `z\mapsto0`. By Proposition 9.67.3 the full graph is a closed analytic subspace of `U\times Y`, locally defined by `w-\varepsilon e^{1/z}`, but its support is simply `\{z\ne0,w=0\}`. Its ordinary closure in `D\times\mathbb C` is the analytic hypersurface `w=0`. Thus Definition 9.67.4 is satisfied.

**(2) Failure of a meromorphic coordinate.** At the origin put `R=\mathbb C\{z\}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)`. An element `a(z)+\varepsilon b(z)` is a nonzerodivisor exactly when `a\not\equiv0`. Indeed multiplication by such an element is injective on the two components after comparing the constant and epsilon coefficients; if `a=0`, it annihilates the nonzero element epsilon. Every element of the total fraction ring is therefore of the form
\[
\frac{a+\varepsilon b}{c+\varepsilon d}
=\frac a c+\varepsilon\left(\frac bc-\frac{ad}{c^2}\right),\qquad c\not\equiv0,
\]
where the coefficient functions belong to the field of one-variable *meromorphic germs* at zero. If such an element restricted to `\varepsilon e^{1/z}` on the punctured disk, its constant component would vanish and its epsilon coefficient would equal `e^{1/z}` there. This is impossible: a meromorphic germ has a Laurent expansion with finitely many negative powers, whereas `e^{1/z}` has infinitely many. For any nonempty smaller open of the punctured disk the epsilon component is still nonzero, so the full morphism is not the zero morphism on any such open; the equivalence relation in Definition 9.67.4 cannot identify them.

**(3) No full analytic closure with that support.** Suppose a closed analytic subspace `G\subset X\times\mathbb C_w` near `(0,0)` restricted to `\Gamma_F` off `z=0` and had support exactly `\{w=0\}`. Let `B=\mathcal O_{G,(0,0)}` and `R=\mathcal O_{X,0}`. Because `w` vanishes on the reduced support of the analytic germ, the analytic local Nullstellensatz gives `w^N=0` in `B` for some `N\ge1`. Thus `B` is a finite `R`-module (generated by `1,w,\ldots,w^{N-1}`), by convergent division modulo `w^N`. The structure map `R\to B` is injective: any element of its kernel is zero on the punctured doubled disk, hence both its holomorphic coefficient germs vanish by the identity theorem. The finite module `B/R` restricts to zero off `z=0`, because `G` agrees there with the full graph and its first projection is an isomorphism. Its support is therefore contained in `\{z=0\}`; finiteness implies `z^M(B/R)=0` for some `M\ge1`. In particular,
\[
z^M w=a(z)+\varepsilon b(z)\quad\text{in }B
\]
for holomorphic germs `a,b` at 0. Restricting this equality to the punctured graph yields `a=0` and `b=z^M e^{1/z}`, impossible because multiplying `e^{1/z}` by any finite power of `z` does not eliminate its essential singularity. Contradiction.

The analytic Nullstellensatz / coherence in this short local argument is a genuine local analytic-algebra input (not the excluded regularization programme). The proof otherwise uses only elementary Laurent expansions, the identity theorem and finite modules.

## Classification, correction, dependents

- **Verified conclusion:** the printed definition is deliberately strictly more permissive, on nonreduced domains, than demanding meromorphic affine coordinate pullbacks or analytic closure of the *full* graph. Its reduced-graph predicate is verified but cannot be silently canonicalized to either stronger condition.
- **Not a theorem counterexample:** neither Theorem 9.67.2 (explicitly reduced spaces) nor Proposition 9.67.3 (graphs on their holomorphic domains) fails. They have different hypotheses.
- **Suggested editorial clarification, not a production patch:** append a note near Definition 9.67.4 explicitly distinguishing this permissive notion from the total-quotient-sheaf notion of a meromorphic scalar map on a nonreduced space. If later developments *need* their equivalence, strengthen the definition or mark that downstream step unresolved; do not silently alter the meaning of `\mathcal M_X`.
- **Affected interface:** §9.67.4 and any downstream meromorphic-map operations assuming meromorphic coordinate extendibility or a full analytic graph closure; §9.67.2 is unaffected. No specific downstream theorem was found false in this selected review.
