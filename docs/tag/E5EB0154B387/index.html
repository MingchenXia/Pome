Theorem 9.39.7 (Bochner's tube theorem)E5EB0154B387

Theorem 9.39.7 (Bochner's tube theorem).E5EB0154B387 Let $\omega \subset \mathbb R^ n$ be nonempty, open and connected. Every $f\in \mathcal O(T_\omega )$ has a unique holomorphic extension to

\[ T_{\operatorname {conv}\omega } =\operatorname {conv}(T_\omega ). \]

Proof. Fix $a\in \omega $. We construct functions on convex tubes containing a neighborhood of $a$, prove that they agree with $f$ there, and then glue them. The connectedness of $\omega $ provides the paths in the construction.

Choose finitely many points $b_0=a,b_1,\ldots ,b_ N$ in $\omega $ whose convex hull $C$ has nonempty interior and contains $a$ in its interior. Such choices exist by taking a small real coordinate cross about $a$. For each $j$ choose a smooth path $\beta _ j:[0,1]\to \omega $ from $a$ to $b_ j$, with $\beta _0=a$. An open connected subset of Euclidean space is polygonally connected: the points reachable by polygonal paths form a nonempty subset that is both open and relatively closed, using small balls. Rounding each of the finitely many corners inside such a ball gives the required smooth paths. Reparametrize their outward and return traversals by a smooth increasing function $\chi :[0,1]\to [0,1]$ that is flat at both endpoints.

Use circle length normalized to one for the following travel times; its variable is $t/(2\pi )$. Give the outward and return trips of each path relative duration $1/(2N+2)$, so that a total travel time $\delta $ uses these same fixed proportions. In addition, dwell at $b_ j$ for time $\mu _ j$. Place the dwell at $b_0=a$ last. The parameter space is

\[ P=\{ (\delta ,\mu _0,\ldots ,\mu _ N): 0<\delta <1/2,\ \mu _ j>0,\ \delta +\textstyle \sum _ j\mu _ j=1\} . \]

It is a convex relatively open subset of an affine hyperplane. The resulting real loop $\gamma _ p$ starts at $a$, makes each outward trip, dwell and return, and then dwells at $a$. All joins are smooth because the trips are flat at the endpoints. The loops depend smoothly on $p\in P$: interval endpoints depend affinely on the times, and every parameter derivative matches at the flat joins. On compact subsets of $P$ the durations stay positive, so this assertion holds in every smooth norm on the circle.

There is a fixed travel mean $c\in \operatorname {conv}\omega $ such that

F09A8AEAD47E 9.39.8
\begin{equation} A_{\gamma _ p}(0)=\delta c+\sum _{j=0}^ N\mu _ j b_ j=:m(p). \end{equation}

To see $c\in \operatorname {conv}\omega $ without a closure assumption, partition the finitely many paths into sufficiently short intervals on which their images lie in balls contained in $\omega $. Each interval's mean lies in its ball; their finite convex combination is $c$.

For every $p$, this loop contracts smoothly to $a$ through loops in $\omega $: replace each outward path $\beta _ j(\chi (u))$ by $\beta _ j(s\chi (u))$, use its reverse on the return trip, and dwell at $\beta _ j(s)$, for $0\leq s\leq 1$. The time intervals remain unchanged. The Schwarz discs of these loops, also after any fixed imaginary translation $\mathrm iy$, have their boundary in $T_\omega $ and start at the constant disc $a+\mathrm iy$. The boundary images of each such compact parameter family are compact in $T_\omega $; smoothness bounds their imaginary parts and their real parts lie in the fixed compact union of the paths. By lemma 9.39.2 each $A_{\gamma _ p}+\mathrm iy$ therefore defines a holomorphic center germ at $m(p)+\mathrm iy$ by (9.39.3).

Put $D=m(P)$. It is convex, and it is open in $\mathbb R^ n$: the vectors $b_ j-b_0$ span $\mathbb R^ n$, so variations of the $\mu _ j$ with zero sum give a surjective linear mean map while $\delta $ is held fixed. A linear right inverse gives a local affine section at every parameter, remaining in $P$ for small variations. Also

00458B646C9D 9.39.9
\begin{equation} \operatorname {int}C\subset D\subset \operatorname {conv}\omega . \end{equation}

For the first inclusion, positive convex coefficients on all $b_ j$ map onto $\operatorname {int}C$. One direction follows from the same local surjectivity. Conversely the average $\bar b$ of all the $b_ j$ is an interior point; for $x\in \operatorname {int}C$ and small $e>0$ the point $(x-e\bar b)/(1-e)$ lies in $C$. Its nonnegative coefficients, mixed with the positive equal coefficients of $e\bar b$, give positive coefficients for $x$. For sufficiently small $\delta >0$ apply this to $(x-\delta c)/(1-\delta )$ and set $\mu _ j=(1-\delta )\lambda _ j$. The second inclusion in (9.39.9) follows from (9.39.8).

We now check that the germs define one holomorphic function $F_ D$ on $D+\mathrm i\mathbb R^ n$. If $m(p)=m(p')$, the fiber $P\cap m^{-1}(m(p))$ is convex. Along its line segment the boundary discs vary continuously in every smooth norm and their centers are fixed. Local consistency (9.39.4) therefore makes their center germs equal; a finite chain of overlapping parameter neighborhoods along the segment gives equality of the endpoint germs. Thus the germ does not depend on the representative of its center. This is the single-valuedness step.

To check holomorphy rather than only well-defined values, fix $z_0=m(p_0)+\mathrm iy_0$ and choose the local affine section of $m$ above. For $z=x+\mathrm iy$ close to $z_0$, the corresponding discs are close to $A_{\gamma _{p_0}}+\mathrm iy_0$. Formula (9.39.4) says that their center values equal the fixed holomorphic germ at $z_0$ evaluated at $z-z_0$. Hence these values form a holomorphic function $F_ D$, and its germ at each center is exactly the Cauchy germ constructed there.

It remains to identify that germ with the original function. For small $\delta >0$ choose $\mu _ j=\delta $ for $1\leq j\leq N$ and $\mu _0=1-(N+1)\delta $. The entire nonconstant part of $\gamma _ p$ is then compressed into an arc of length $O(\delta )$, with a fixed smooth profile based at $a$; the remaining arc is constant $a$. In the angular variable it has the form in lemma 9.39.5, after absorbing a fixed factor into its profile and scale. The center $m(p)$ tends to $a$. Because $a\in \operatorname {int}C\subset D$, a fixed complex polydisc about $a+\mathrm iy$ lies in $D+\mathrm i\mathbb R^ n$. On a sufficiently small fixed translation polydisc the boundary Cauchy integrals tend locally uniformly to $f(a+\mathrm iy+w)$ by (9.39.6). They equal $F_ D(m(p)+\mathrm iy+w)$ there: equality first holds as germs, and then on this fixed polydisc by the identity theorem, after making it small enough that all its centers and translates remain in $D$ and its boundary translates remain in $T_\omega $. Continuity of $F_ D$ now gives $F_ D(a+\mathrm iy+w)=f(a+\mathrm iy+w)$ near zero. In particular $F_ D=f$ on a neighborhood of $a$.

The domains $D$ obtained from finite choices above cover $\operatorname {conv}\omega $. Indeed, for any $x$ in that open convex set, choose a small real coordinate cross about $x$ still in it. Each of its finitely many vertices is a finite convex combination of points of $\omega $. Collect those points together with $a$ and a small coordinate cross in $\omega $ about $a$. Their convex hull contains both $x$ and $a$ in its interior, so (9.39.9) places $x$ in the corresponding $D$. The convex hull of an open set is open: in any finite convex representation, varying a point with positive coefficient supplies a ball about the represented point.

Any two resulting convex sets $D,D'$ contain $a$ in their interiors. Their tube intersection $(D\cap D')+\mathrm i\mathbb R^ n$ is connected, and $F_ D,F_{D'}$ agree near $a$ with $f$. The identity theorem makes them agree throughout that intersection. They consequently glue to a single holomorphic function on $T_{\operatorname {conv}\omega }$. It agrees with $f$ near $a$ and hence on all of the connected original tube. The same identity theorem proves uniqueness. Finally the real part of any convex combination from $T_\omega $ lies in $\operatorname {conv}\omega $. Conversely, for $x=\sum t_ jx_ j$ and any $y\in \mathbb R^ n$, the equality $x+\mathrm iy=\sum t_ j(x_ j+\mathrm iy)$ proves the asserted equality of convex hulls. $\square$
