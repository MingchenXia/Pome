Lemma 2.20.1 (Positive fourth-order modes)CB1875F07408

Lemma 2.20.1 (Positive fourth-order modes).CB1875F07408 Let $z\geq 2$, $0<a<1$, and $\lambda _ z(t)=z^3-z^4t^4\geq 0$ for $0\leq t\leq a$. There is a positive nondecreasing solution $w$ of

\[ (\partial _ t-z)^4w=\lambda _ z(t)w \]

with any prescribed positive value at any $s\in [0,a]$. For every integer $m\geq 0$ there are constants $C_ m,A_ m$ independent of $z,a,s$ and that prescribed value such that

9F264158A115 2.20.2
\begin{equation} |w^{(m)}(t)|\leq C_ m z^{A_ m}w(t)\qquad (0\leq t\leq a). \end{equation}

There are absolute constants $c,C>0$ such that, whenever $0\leq T\leq t\leq a$ and $0\leq l\leq \lambda _ z\leq L$ on $[T,t]$,

F83983317719 2.20.3
\begin{equation} c z^{-3}e^{(z+l^{1/4})(t-T)} \leq \frac{w(t)}{w(T)} \leq C z^3e^{(z+L^{1/4})(t-T)}. \end{equation}

Proof. Solve $y^{(4)}=\lambda _ z y$ with $y^{(j)}(0)=1$ for $0\leq j\leq 3$. Here existence and the needed comparison principle have an elementary integral proof. The first-order state $Y=(y,y',y'',y''')$ satisfies $Y'=M(t)Y$, where $M$ has three superdiagonal entries equal to one and its bottom-left entry equal to $\lambda _ z(t)$, all other entries being zero. Successive substitution in $Y=Y(0)+\int _0^ t M(v)Y(v)\, dv$ converges uniformly on $[0,a]$: its $j$th iterated-integral term is bounded by $\| Y(0)\| (a\sup \| M\| )^ j/j!$. Differentiating the integral equation gives a smooth solution. The same series on $[T,t]$, including a nonnegative inhomogeneous term, proves componentwise comparison, since every matrix entry is nonnegative. In particular all four state components are at least one and are nondecreasing.

For $0\leq j\leq 2$, integration gives $y^{(j)}\leq 1+a y^{(j+1)}\leq 2y^{(j+1)}$. Also $y'''\leq 1+a z^3y\leq 2z^3y$. Thus

\[ 2^{-j}y\leq y^{(j)}\leq 8z^3y\qquad (0\leq j\leq 3). \]

All derivatives of $\lambda _ z$ are bounded by a fixed power of $z$ on this interval, with constants depending only on their order. Differentiating $y^{(4)}=\lambda _ z y$ and inducting therefore bounds every $|y^{(m)}|$ by $C_ m z^{A_ m}y$. Multiplication by $e^{zt}$, and a positive constant to set $w(s)$, proves the equation, positivity, monotonicity and (2.20.2).

For the upper growth bound compare $y$ on $[T,t]$ with

\[ q(v)=8z^3y(T)e^{(L^{1/4}+1)(v-T)}. \]

Its first four state values dominate those of $y$ at $T$, and $q^{(4)}\geq \lambda _ z q$. For the lower bound use

\[ p(v)=\frac{y(T)}{8(1+l)}e^{l^{1/4}(v-T)}. \]

Its state at $T$ is dominated by that of $y$, because $l^{j/4}/(1+l)\leq 1$ for $0\leq j\leq 3$; also $p^{(4)}\leq \lambda _ zp$. The componentwise comparison just proved applies to both. Since $t-T<1$ and $l\leq z^3$, their prefactors give the stated bounds after multiplication by $e^{z(t-T)}$. $\square$
