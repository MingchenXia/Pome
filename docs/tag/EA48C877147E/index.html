Lemma 9.21.1EA48C877147E

Lemma 9.21.1.EA48C877147E Let $D\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be bounded and hyperconvex. Put $H=A^2(D)$, with its usual Lebesgue $L^2$ norm, and let $B_ H$ be its closed unit ball. If $p$ is the $L^2$ norm on any relatively compact open subset of $D$, there are another compact-open continuous norm $q$ and $C>0$ such that, for every $r>0$,

\[ \{ f\in \mathcal O(D):q(f)\leq 1\} \subset CrB_ H+r^{-1}\{ f\in \mathcal O(D):p(f)\leq 1\} . \]

In particular $H$ is dense in $\mathcal O(D)$.

Proof. Take a continuous negative PSH exhaustion $\rho $. The function $-\log (-\rho )$ is a PSH exhaustion, so $D$ is pseudoconvex. Choose $a<0$ so that the compact set used for $p$ lies in $\{ \rho <a\} $. Choose a smooth cutoff $\chi $, equal to one on $\{ \rho \leq a/2\} $ and supported in $\{ \rho <a/4\} $. These sets are compactly nested, so the ordinary cutoff construction applies. Take a relatively compact open set $D_ m$ containing its support and the compact set for $p$, and set $q(f)=\| f\| _{L^2(D_ m)}$.

For $L\geq 0$ put $P_ L=L\max \{ 0,\rho -a\} $. It is PSH, $0\leq P_ L\leq -aL$, vanishes on $\{ \rho \leq a\} $ and is at least $-aL/2$ on $\operatorname {supp}\bar\partial \chi $. Apply the weighted $L^2$ existence theorem with weight $P_ L+|z|^2$ to the closed form $f\bar\partial \chi $. Boundedness of $D$ and the fixed cutoff give a solution $v$ with

\[ \bar\partial v=f\bar\partial \chi ,\qquad \int _ D|v|^2e^{-P_ L}\, dV\leq C_0e^{aL/2}q(f)^2. \]

The constant is independent of $L$ and $f$; the smooth part $|z|^2$ has Hessian equal to the identity and its exponential is bounded above and below on $D$. Thus

\[ p(v)\leq C_1e^{aL/4}q(f),\qquad \| v\| _{L^2(D)}\leq C_1e^{-aL/4}q(f). \]

The distribution $g=\chi f-v$ is holomorphic on $D$ and belongs to $H$. On the set for $p$ the residual $f-g$ equals $v$. With $R=e^{-aL/4}\geq 1$ this gives $\| g\| _ H\leq C_2Rq(f)$ and $p(f-g)\leq C_1R^{-1}q(f)$. Replace $R$ by a fixed multiple of $r$ to make the second coefficient $r^{-1}$; enlarge the first constant. For the remaining bounded range of $r$, use $g=0$ and $p(f)\leq q(f)$, or the estimate with $R=1$, and enlarge the constant once more. This proves the inclusion for all $r>0$. These $L^2$ norms generate the compact-open topology by local submean estimates. Letting $r\to \infty $ proves density. $\square$
