Example 3.8.3FADCC209A003

Example 3.8.3.FADCC209A003 Quasi-everywhere zero boundary limits alone do not specify a Green function uniquely. In the unit disc, for every $t>0$ the functions

\[ -\log |z|+t\operatorname {Re}\frac{1+z}{1-z} \]

are positive harmonic off zero, have the required logarithmic pole, and tend to zero at every boundary point except $1$. In the exterior of the closed unit disc, the corresponding functions

\[ \log |z|+t\operatorname {Re}\frac{z+1}{z-1} \]

are positive harmonic, have the required finite logarithmic limit at infinity, and again have zero boundary limits except at $1$. The exceptional singleton is polar. The added Poisson term is positive in each indicated domain, diverges at $1$, and is harmonic across the finite pole in the first formula. Thus the weaker boundary condition must be supplemented by minimality, or by the bounded boundary condition above, when it is used as a characterization.

For a finite pole on a bounded domain, the canonical Green function is likewise the least function meeting the weaker conditions. If $v$ is another such function, $v-G$ has a removable pole and is bounded below near the boundary, since $v>0$ and $G$ is bounded there. The polar boundary minimum principle gives $v\geq G$. If $v$ is also bounded near the boundary, apply that principle to $G-v$ to get equality. Here boundedness of the canonical $G$ near the boundary follows from $G(z,a)\leq \log (C/|z-a|)$ with $C>\operatorname {diam}D$.
