Theorem 7.29.58FBB0E4DE397

Theorem 7.29.5.8FBB0E4DE397 Let $T$ be a closed positive current of bidimension $(p,p)$ on a complex manifold $X$. Suppose a finite family of plurisubharmonic functions has the following property: $X$ is covered by coordinate balls $B$ with compact closure in their charts, such that every function of the family is locally bounded on a neighborhood of $\partial B$. For $0\leq r\leq p$, their recursive products

\[ P_ T(u_1,\ldots ,u_ r) =dd^ c\bigl (u_1P_ T(u_2,\ldots ,u_ r)\bigr ),\qquad P_ T()=T, \]

are well defined closed positive currents. They are symmetric, additive and positively homogeneous in the potentials, unchanged by addition of constants, and local in the ordinary topology. For $0\leq r\leq p-1$, an additional function $u_0$ of the family is locally integrable for the coefficient variations of $P_ T(u_1,\ldots ,u_ r)$.

If $u_{l,j}\downarrow u_ l$ are any decreasing plurisubharmonic approximations, then

\begin{align*} P_ T(u_{1,j},\ldots ,u_{r,j})& \longrightarrow P_ T(u_1,\ldots ,u_ r) & & (0\leq r\leq p),\\ u_{0,j}P_ T(u_{1,j},\ldots ,u_{r,j}) & \longrightarrow u_0P_ T(u_1,\ldots ,u_ r) & & (0\leq r\leq p-1) \end{align*}

weakly. The limits need not be locally bounded in the interior of the balls. All listed potentials, including the limit of a weight, are subject to the common-boundary hypothesis.

Proof. We prove the assertions simultaneously for arbitrary positive closed base currents and all their bidimensions. No assertion of integrability of an unbounded weight against a top-degree current is included.

1. A collar without cutting off interior singularities. Fix a ball $B$, a compact set $K\Subset B$ and the finitely many sequences involved. On a fixed annulus around $\partial B$, their limits are bounded below, and $u_ l\leq u_{l,j}\leq u_{l,1}$ gives bounds uniform in $j$. After subtracting constants, arrange that all these functions are at most $-1$ on a neighborhood of $\overline B$. Choose two concentric inner spheres, outside $K$ and all their unbounded loci in $B$, such that the closed annulus between and outside them lies where the limits are bounded below by $-M$. Let $\psi =|z-a|^2-R^2$ and take $A$ so large that $A\psi <-M-2$ on a neighborhood of the inner sphere. Leave each function unchanged inside that sphere, and on the outer annulus replace it by $\max \{ u,A\psi \} $. The formulas agree near the inner gluing sphere, so this is plurisubharmonic by maximum and boundary gluing of plurisubharmonic functions. Near $\partial B$ it is $A\psi $, since $u\leq -1$. The same choices work for every $j$, preserve inequalities and decreasing limits, and leave the functions unchanged near $K$. They are nonpositive on $B$. We henceforth use the modified functions and the same symbols.

Each fixed modified function has smooth decreasing plurisubharmonic approximations with this same collar. Indeed, convolve the original function on a slightly larger ball and on the annulus use the smooth maximum $M_\varepsilon (u*\rho _\varepsilon ,A\psi )$ of smooth convex approximations of the maximum, with the decreasing regularization of the Levi criterion and decreasing convolution. On the inner gluing annulus the two arguments have a gap greater than $2\varepsilon $ for small $\varepsilon $, so this equals $u*\rho _\varepsilon $ there. Near the outer sphere it equals $A\psi $. Thus it glues to the convolution in the interior. The convolution and smooth maximum both decrease as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, so these smooth functions decrease to the modification. They remain nonpositive on $B$ for sufficiently small $\varepsilon $: wherever the maximum is not an exact branch, both arguments are bounded away from zero; near the sphere the exact branch is $A\psi $. Their boundedness for fixed $\varepsilon $ follows from smoothness near $\overline B$.

It suffices to prove the theorem for functions with this fixed collar, on $B$. On the collar all products are ordinary bounded products with factors $A\omega $, where $\omega =dd^ c\psi $. Consequently, whenever weak convergence is established in $B$, integrals over the whole ball converge as well if the currents in question have this fixed collar: split the integral with a compact cutoff equal to one away from the collar. The complementary integrals are fixed and finite.

2. Starting the induction. Suppose $p\geq 1$ and $u\leq 0$ has the fixed collar. Its bounded truncations $u_ N=\max \{ u,-N\} $ have the same collar for all large $N$. The $r=0$ case of (7.29.3) gives

\[ \int _ B(-u_ N)T\wedge \omega ^ p \leq \| \psi \| _\infty A M_ T. \]

Monotone convergence, with the measure fixed, proves $u\in L^1(T\wedge \omega ^ p)$ and hence coefficient integrability. For $u_ j\downarrow u$, the normalized functions are nonpositive and $|u_ j|\leq |u|$; dominated convergence gives $u_ jT\to uT$. This is the weighted assertion for zero Hessian factors. The unweighted zero-factor assertion is $T\to T$. If $p=0$, only that unweighted assertion is required.

3. Constructing the next product and its unweighted limit. Assume the weighted assertions have been proved for fewer than $r$ Hessian factors, for all base currents, and let $1\leq r\leq p$. By induction the product with $r-1$ factors is closed positive, and the first potential is integrable for it. Therefore the displayed recursion defines $P_ T$ with $r$ factors. With this fixed base product, bounded truncations of the first potential converge in coefficient $L^1$. Applying $dd^ c$ shows that the new product is a weak limit of closed positive bounded products, hence closed positive. For simultaneous decreasing approximations, the weighted induction hypothesis with weight $u_{1,j}$ and factors $u_{2,j},\ldots ,u_{r,j}$, followed by $dd^ c$, proves the unweighted convergence for $r$ factors. Smooth decreasing approximations then show symmetry, because the smooth exterior products are symmetric. The recursion gives locality, linearity, invariance under constants and compatibility with multiplication of the base current by a smooth closed positive form; these follow exactly by coefficient integration and the smooth-factor Leibniz rule. In particular the construction agrees in overlapping charts and is independent of this local collar modification wherever that modification leaves the functions unchanged.

For bounded simultaneous truncations, the mass identity (7.29.2) holds. Their just-proved unweighted convergence and the fixed collar pass that identity to the possibly unbounded limits. Thus $P_ T$ with $r$ factors has finite total trace mass on $B$, equal to $A^ rM_ T$ in the $\omega $ normalization.

4. Integrability of the next weight. Let $r\leq p-1$. Put $S=P_ T(u_1,\ldots ,u_ r)$ and $S_ N=P_ T(u_{1,N},\ldots ,u_{r,N})$, with simultaneous bounded truncations of all the functions, including $u_0$. We know $S_ N\to S$. The uniform bounded-collar estimate gives

\[ \int _ B(-u_{0,N})S_ N\wedge \omega ^{p-r}\leq C, \qquad C=\| \psi \| _\infty A^{r+1}M_ T. \]

For fixed $m$ and $N\geq m$, one has $-u_{0,m}\leq -u_{0,N}$. For a nonnegative compact test cutoff $\chi \leq 1$, lower semicontinuity of $-u_{0,m}$ and weak convergence of the positive trace measures imply

\[ \int _ B\chi (-u_{0,m})S\wedge \omega ^{p-r}\leq C. \]

The measure inequality used here follows from the bounded continuous majorants of upper semicontinuous functions in decreasing continuous majorants of upper semicontinuous functions, applied to the negative of the bounded lower semicontinuous test function. Let $m\to \infty $ by monotone convergence for the fixed measure $S\wedge \omega ^{p-r}$, then exhaust $B$ by such cutoffs. This proves integrability of $u_0$ and the same bound $C$. Coefficient domination in coefficient measures and compactness of positive currents gives all coefficient integrability. Applying this argument to each vector of functions shows that the weighted products for an arbitrary decreasing family have mass uniformly bounded by $C$.

5. An upper bound for every weighted weak limit. Continue with $r\leq p-1$ and put $S_ j=P_ T(u_{1,j},\ldots ,u_{r,j})$. Their unweighted convergence is already known. Uniform weighted trace bounds and positive-current compactness give subsequential weak limits $W$ of $u_{0,j}S_ j$. For a nonnegative strongly positive test form $\alpha $, write $\mu _ j=S_ j\wedge \alpha $ and $\mu =S\wedge \alpha $. For fixed $l,m$ and all $j\geq l$,

\[ \int u_{0,j}\, d\mu _ j \leq \int \max \{ u_{0,l},-m\} \, d\mu _ j. \]

The bounded upper semicontinuous function on the right has decreasing bounded continuous majorants on $B$, by decreasing continuous majorants of upper semicontinuous functions. Passing first to the weak limit in $j$, with a fixed compact cutoff near the test support, and then through those majorants gives $W(\alpha )\leq \int \max \{ u_{0,l},-m\} \, d\mu $. Let $m\to \infty $ and then $l\to \infty $ for the fixed measure, using the integrability from Step4. It follows that $u_0S-W$ is positive. On the common collar it is zero.

6. Reverse inequality for total masses. Set $\gamma =\omega ^{p-r}$ and $I=\int _ Bu_0S\wedge \gamma $, $I_ j=\int _ Bu_{0,j}S_ j\wedge \gamma $, all finite by Step4. We prove $I\leq I_ j$. Fix $j$ and choose smooth decreasing approximations $w_ l=u_{l,j}^{\varepsilon _ l}$ of Step1, for $0\leq l\leq r$. They satisfy $u_ l\leq w_ l$ and have the same collar. For $0\leq k\leq r$, define

\[ J_ k=\int _ Bu_ k\, dd^ cw_0\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cw_{k-1} \wedge dd^ cu_{k+1}\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cu_ r \wedge T\wedge \gamma . \]

Replacing $u_ k$ by its majorant $w_ k$ cannot decrease this integral. If $k<r$, use symmetry from Step3 and (7.29.4) to interchange the smooth weight $w_ k$ and the potential $u_{k+1}$. The base in that exchange is

\[ R_ k=dd^ cw_0\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cw_{k-1} \wedge dd^ cu_{k+2}\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cu_ r \wedge T\wedge \gamma , \]

of bidimension $(1,1)$. Its product with $u_{k+1}$ is integrable by the weighted induction hypothesis for fewer factors, and its $dd^ c$ is the already-constructed product with $r$ factors. Thus the exchange satisfies every hypothesis of the lemma, and gives $J_ k\leq J_{k+1}$. Therefore

\[ I=J_0\leq \cdots \leq J_ r \leq \int _ Bw_ r\, dd^ cw_0\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cw_{r-1} \wedge T\wedge \gamma . \]

Let $\varepsilon _ r\downarrow 0$ first. The measure is fixed and smooth factors times $T$, and $u_{r,j}$ is integrable for it by the weighted zero-factor case with that base current, whose bidimension is $p-r\geq 1$. Dominated convergence applies. For $r\geq 2$, next let $\varepsilon _{r-1}\downarrow 0$, then the other parameters down to $\varepsilon _1\downarrow 0$, keeping $\varepsilon _0$ fixed. At the step for $\varepsilon _ k$, the smooth factors already fixed next to $T$ give a fixed positive closed base of bidimension $p-k$. There are $r-k\leq r-1$ remaining Hessian factors and fixed weight $u_{r,j}$. The weighted induction hypothesis applies, since $r-k\leq (p-k)-1$. Hence these sequential limits are justified without assuming the weighted assertion currently under proof. Every integral limit over $B$ is valid by the fixed-collar splitting from Step1. The resulting integral is

\[ \int _ Bu_{r,j}\, dd^ cw_0\wedge dd^ cu_{1,j}\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cu_{r-1,j}\wedge T\wedge \gamma . \]

Interchange its weight with the smooth $w_0$ by the same $L^1$ collar exchange. This becomes $\int _ Bw_0S_ j\wedge \gamma $. Finally $w_0\downarrow u_{0,j}$, which is integrable for $S_ j$ by Step4; dominated convergence gives $I\leq I_ j$.

Along a subsequence defining $W$, the fixed collar gives $I_ j\to \int _ BW\wedge \gamma $. The positive compactly supported current $u_0S-W$ therefore has trace mass $I-\lim I_ j\leq 0$. Its trace vanishes and coefficient domination implies $W=u_0S$. Every subsequence has this same further weak limit, so the whole weighted sequence converges. This completes the weighted induction, and consequently the construction through $r=p$.

The original functions agree with their modifications near $K$; locality transfers every conclusion there. Compact sets in each covering ball and then a partition of unity give the conclusions on $X$. The coefficient recursion makes these local constructions agree on overlaps, so no choice of spheres or coordinates enters the result. $\square$
