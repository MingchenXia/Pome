Lemma 20.5.3323D5DA3E240

Lemma 20.5.3.323D5DA3E240 Let $D$ be an irreducible hypersurface of a smooth complex manifold $M$ of dimension $r+1$, and let $F:M\to X$ be holomorphic, proper on $D$. Write $B=F(D)$. If $\dim B<r$, then $F_*[D]=0$. If $\dim B=r$, there is an integer $d\geq 1$ such that

\[ F_*[D]=d[B]. \]

Here $d$ is the number of sheets of $F|_ D$ over the complement of a proper analytic subset of $B$. No integrality assertion about an arbitrary weak limit of cycles is used.

Proof. The image is analytic by Remmert's proper mapping theorem and has dimension at most $r$ by dimension bounds for holomorphic images. It is irreducible: a decomposition into two proper analytic subsets would pull back to such a decomposition of $D$. These assertions also give purity of $B$ by purity and dimension of irreducible analytic sets. The current $[D]$ and its proper pushforward are well defined by local equations and currents of irreducible hypersurfaces, pushforward of currents proper on their support.

If $\dim B<r$, the derivative of $F|_{D_{\mathrm{reg}}}$ has rank less than $r$ everywhere. Otherwise the constant rank theorem near a point of maximal rank would put an $r$-dimensional submanifold in $B$, contradicting its dimension. Every pulled-back $(r,r)$-form therefore vanishes on $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$, proving the first assertion. For $r=0$, $D$ is one point and its pushforward is the unit point mass at its image, so the second assertion holds with $d=1$. Assume henceforth $r\geq 1$ and $\dim B=r$.

We construct the entire critical exceptional set analytically, including at singular points of $D$. On a coordinate neighborhood in $M$, choose a reduced local equation $e$ of $D$ by local equations and currents of irreducible hypersurfaces. In target coordinates $F_1,\ldots ,F_ n$, put

\begin{equation} Q=\left\{ e=0:\ de\wedge dF_{i_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge dF_{i_ r}=0 \text{ for every }i_1<\cdots <i_ r\right\} . \end{equation}

These equations include $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$, where $de=0$, and on $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ express exactly rank less than $r$. Multiplying $e$ by a unit changes its differential on $D$ by that unit; changing target coordinates takes invertible linear combinations of the displayed minors. Thus these local analytic sets agree and define a closed analytic subset $Q\subset D$. It is proper. If it were all of $D$, the image of $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ would be $\mathcal H^{2r}$-null by dimension bounds for holomorphic images; so would the image of $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$, by its smaller dimension and analytic singular strata and Hausdorff dimension. Countably many charts suffice. This cannot cover the regular locus of the $r$-dimensional analytic set $B$. Hence $\dim Q\leq r-1$.

The set

\[ Z=B_{\mathrm{sing}}\cup F(Q) \]

is analytic, closed in $B$, and has dimension at most $r-1$; properness and the same image dimension bound justify this for $F(Q)$. The manifold $B_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is connected by global irreducible analytic components. Removing its proper analytic subset $Z\cap B_{\mathrm{reg}}$ leaves it connected, by bounded holomorphic removal and connected complements. Over $B\setminus Z$ every source point is regular with derivative rank $r$. The inverse function theorem on these two $r$-manifolds makes $F$ a local biholomorphism there. Every fiber is compact by properness and discrete by this local description, hence finite. For any such fiber take finitely many disjoint inverse neighborhoods. Properness supplies a smaller target neighborhood with its whole inverse image in their union: the image of their closed complement in $D$ is closed and misses the target point. Intersect the inverse neighborhood images as well. This is an evenly covered neighborhood. The sheet number is a positive locally constant integer and therefore a fixed $d\geq 1$ on the connected $B\setminus Z$. Holomorphic coordinate change preserves complex orientation, so integration over these sheets gives $F_*[D]=d[B]$ off $Z$.

There is no unexamined current supported on $Z$ in this equality. Indeed $F^{-1}(Z)\cap D$ is a proper analytic subset of $D$. Its intersection with $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ has smooth volume zero: locally it lies in a nontrivial holomorphic zero set, and the connectedness and density of $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ exclude an open component. The integration defining $[D]$ assigns its singular locus zero coefficient mass as well. For a compact target set, properness puts its inverse image in a compact source set; there the coefficients of $F^*$ of a bounded test form are bounded by a constant times the induced volume on $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$. Consequently every coefficient measure of $F_*[D]$ gives $Z$ zero mass. This follows first for positive trace tests from the integral formula and then for all coefficients by coefficient measures and compactness of positive currents. The same argument on $B_{\mathrm{reg}}$ shows that $[B]$ gives $Z$ zero mass. Their equality off $Z$ is therefore their equality everywhere. $\square$
