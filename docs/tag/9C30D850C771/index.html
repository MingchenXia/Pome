Theorem 11.48.3.9C30D850C771 In every bidegree and every bundle rank,

8C926C4A6B4A
\begin{align} [\delta '',L]& =\mathrm i(D'+\tau ),& [\delta ',L]& =-\mathrm i(D''+\bar\tau ), \tag{11.48.4}\\ [\Lambda ,D'']& =-\mathrm i(\delta '+\tau ^*),& [\Lambda ,D']& =\mathrm i(\delta ”+\bar\tau ^*). \tag{11.48.5} \end{align}

Proof. We first prove the scalar identities, retaining the first jet of the metric. At a given point choose holomorphic coordinates in which $\omega (0)=\omega _0=\mathrm i\sum _ jdz_ j\wedge d\bar z_ j$ and write $\gamma =\omega -\omega _0$, retaining its linear term. Put $L_0=\omega _0\wedge $ and $\Lambda _0=L_0^*$. On forms of each degree let $M$ be the smooth positive matrix defined by

\[ \langle u,v\rangle _\omega \, \mathrm dV_\omega =\langle Mu,v\rangle _0\, \mathrm dV_0, \qquad \mathrm dV_0=\omega _0^ n/n!. \]

Its first variation is

34BA424819E3 11.48.6
\begin{equation} M=I-[\gamma ,\Lambda _0]+O(|z|^2). \end{equation}

Indeed, diagonalize $\gamma $ at a point with respect to $\omega _0$, with eigenvalues $\gamma _ j$, so the eigenvalues of $\omega $ are $1+\gamma _ j$. On the coefficient $dz_ I\wedge d\bar z_ J$ the metric-volume multiplier is

\[ \frac{\prod _ j(1+\gamma _ j)}{\prod _{i\in I}(1+\gamma _ i)\prod _{j\in J}(1+\gamma _ j)} =1+\sum _ j\gamma _ j-\sum _{i\in I}\gamma _ i-\sum _{j\in J}\gamma _ j +O(|\gamma |^2). \]

The creation/contraction formula in the curvature operator on $(n,1)$-forms identifies its linear term with $-[\gamma ,\Lambda _0]$. This is an identity for the first derivative of the smooth matrix-valued function of the metric; it requires no smooth choice of diagonalizing frames. Its remainder and first derivatives vanish at zero.

Write $\bar\delta _0$ for the flat scalar adjoint of $\bar\partial $. On the source and target form bundles, the exact adjoint formula is $\bar\partial ^*=M_{q-1}^{-1}\bar\delta _0M_ q$ for fixed $p$. Thus, as an operator on first jets at zero,

FA92E1C99B83 11.48.7
\begin{equation} \bar\partial ^* =\bar\delta _0-[\bar\delta _0,[\gamma ,\Lambda _0]] =\bar\delta _0+[\Lambda _0,C],\qquad C=[\bar\delta _0,\gamma ]. \end{equation}

For the last equality use $[\bar\delta _0,\Lambda _0]=0$ and expand the three products. The flat scalar Kähler identities in the line-bundle Bochner–Kodaira identity give $[L_0,\bar\delta _0]=-\mathrm i\partial $. The operator $C$ has degree one. Since $[L_0,\gamma ]=0$, the graded Jacobi identity gives

\[ [L_0,C]=[-\mathrm i\partial ,\gamma ] =-\mathrm i\partial \gamma \wedge , \qquad [C,L_0]=\mathrm i\partial \gamma \wedge . \]

Also $[L_0,\Lambda _0]$ acts on degree $k$ by $k-n$, so $[[L_0,\Lambda _0],C]=C$. Therefore

\[ [L_0,[\Lambda _0,C]] =C-\mathrm i[\Lambda _0,\partial \gamma \wedge ]. \]

Combining this with (11.48.7) and $L=L_0+\gamma $ gives, at zero,

\[ [L,\bar\partial ^*] =-\mathrm i\partial +C-\mathrm i[\Lambda _0,\partial \gamma \wedge ]-C =-\mathrm i(\partial +\tau ). \]

Terms containing two factors of $\gamma $, with at most one derivative, vanish at zero. This accounts also for $[\gamma ,[\Lambda _0,C]]$ in this expansion. The point is arbitrary, proving the first scalar identity. Complex conjugation, for which $L,\Lambda $ and the metric-volume pairing are real, gives the second. Taking formal adjoints gives the last two.

To pass to $E$, choose a holomorphic Chern-normal frame at the point. Its metric matrix is $H=I+O(|z|^2)$ and its connection matrix $H^{-1}\partial H$ vanishes there. This frame is obtained by the constant and holomorphic linear changes proved in theorem 10.33.2; that frame construction uses no derivative of the base metric. The bundle operators and their adjoints at the point are the componentwise scalar operators above, plus zeroth-order terms vanishing at that point. Their commutators with $L$ and $\Lambda $ consequently have exactly the same values. This proves all four identities in arbitrary rank. $\square$
