9.53.1 Formal complex structures and complex coordinates9F96757DAC7B

Let $M$ be a smooth real manifold of dimension $2n$, $n\geq 1$, and let $J^2=-1$ be a smooth endomorphism of its tangent bundle. In dimension zero the manifold is discrete and already has its unique complex atlas, so that case requires no construction. All manifolds are Hausdorff and second countable. Complex differential forms below are smooth unless a distributional domain is specified. Write $\mathcal H(f)=\mathrm i d'd''f$; this is the unscaled Levi form. No complex atlas is assumed in the definitions or arguments below.

Proposition 9.53.2.7D9D38C5DA0E The projections $(1-\mathrm iJ)/2$ and $(1+\mathrm iJ)/2$ split $T_{\mathbb C}M=T^{1,0}\oplus T^{0,1}$ into conjugate rank-$n$ bundles and induce the type decomposition of exterior forms. For $(1,0)$ vector fields define $\vartheta (X,Y)=[X,Y]^{0,1}$. This is a tensor in $\Lambda ^{2,0}T^*M\otimes T^{0,1}M$. If $\vartheta =\sum _ j\alpha _ j\otimes \bar\xi _ j$ in a local frame, put

\[ \vartheta ' u=\sum _ j\alpha _ j\wedge \iota _{\bar\xi _ j}u, \qquad \vartheta ''u=\sum _ j\bar\alpha _ j\wedge \iota _{\xi _ j}u. \]

These intrinsic derivations have types $(2,-1)$ and $(-1,2)$. Let $d'$ and $d''$ be the components of $d$ of bidegrees $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$. Then

A64B66D1EDF0 9.53.3
\begin{equation} d=d'+d''-\vartheta '-\vartheta ''. \end{equation}

In particular $\vartheta =0$ if and only if $d=d'+d''$. In this case

3BE881B07623 9.53.4
\begin{equation} (d')^2=(d'')^2=0,\qquad d'd''+d''d'=0. \end{equation}

We call this condition formal integrability.

Proof. The displayed projections are complementary idempotents, with the asserted images and conjugation. Their ranks are $n$: a real basis $(e_1,Je_1,\ldots ,e_ n,Je_ n)$ gives the corresponding eigenbases. The bracket formula $[fX,gY]=fg[X,Y]+f(Xg)Y-g(Yf)X$ proves tensoriality after projecting to $T^{0,1}$. Tensor contraction makes the two ensuing operators frame-independent. Since contraction is an odd derivation and each $\alpha _ j$ has degree two, their signed product rule is that of $d$. The type projections of the product rule for $d$ give the same rule for $d'$ and $d''$.

It is enough to check (9.53.3) on functions and one-forms, which locally generate the exterior algebra. For a function it is its type decomposition. For a $(0,1)$ one-form $u$ and $(1,0)$ fields $X,Y$,

\[ du(X,Y)=X(u(Y))-Y(u(X))-u([X,Y])=-u(\vartheta (X,Y)). \]

Thus $(du)^{2,0}=-\vartheta 'u$; its other components are the two specified type derivatives. Conjugation handles $(1,0)$ one-forms. If $d=d'+d''$, the same formula detects every component of $\vartheta $ by pairing with $(0,1)$ covectors. Finally the distinct types in $d^2=0$ give (9.53.4). $\square$

Lemma 9.53.5.30126C7FDE2C Suppose $J$ is formally integrable. At every $a\in M$, for every integer $s\geq 1$, there are smooth complex-valued real coordinates $z=(z_1,\ldots ,z_ n)$ centered at $a$ with

1F71301249BA 9.53.6
\begin{equation} d''z_ j=O(|z|^ s),\qquad 1\leq j\leq n. \end{equation}

Here an estimate for a smooth coefficient also says that all its Taylor terms of smaller degree vanish.

Proof. Choose real linear coordinates so that $dz_ j(a)$ are a basis of $(T^{1,0}_ a)^*$. This gives $s=1$, with invertible real differential. Assume the assertion at order $s$. The coframe $d''\bar z_ k$ is invertible near $a$, and Taylor expansion gives

\[ d''z_ j=\sum _ k P_{jk}(z,\bar z)d''\bar z_ k+O(|z|^{s+1}), \]

where each $P_{jk}$ is homogeneous of total degree $s$. The identity $(d'')^2z_ j=0$ implies $\partial _{\bar z_ l}P_{jk}=\partial _{\bar z_ k}P_{jl}$. Indeed these are the coefficients of degree $s-1$ of its two-form: terms involving $d''z_ l$ have order at least $2s-1\geq s$, and $d''d''\bar z_ k=0$; differentiating the remainder gives order $s$. Set

\[ Q_ j(z,\bar z)=\int _0^1\sum _ l\bar z_ lP_{jl}(z,t\bar z)\, dt. \]

The preceding symmetry and the fundamental theorem of calculus give

\[ \partial _{\bar z_ k}Q_ j =\int _0^1\left(P_{jk}(z,t\bar z) +t\sum _ l\bar z_ l\partial _{\bar z_ l}P_{jk}(z,t\bar z)\right)dt =P_{jk}(z,\bar z). \]

The polynomial $Q_ j$ has degree $s+1$. The chain rule now gives $d''(z_ j-Q_ j)=O(|z|^{s+1})$: its uncancelled term $\sum _ k(\partial _{z_ k}Q_ j)d''z_ k$ has order $2s\geq s+1$. The differential of this change at $a$ is the identity, so it is a real coordinate change. Norms and orders are preserved under that change, completing the induction. $\square$

Lemma 9.53.7.53F867AC87E1 On a formally integrable almost complex manifold, let $z$ be real coordinates satisfying (9.53.6) with $s\geq 3$. Put $r=|z|$ and $\psi =r^2$. On one sufficiently small coordinate ball, independent of $0<\epsilon \leq 1$,

\[ \mathcal H(\psi )>0,\qquad \mathcal H\bigl (\psi +\log (\psi +\epsilon ^2)\bigr )>0. \]

These forms are closed. On that ball $\mathcal H(\log (\psi +\epsilon ^2))\geq -\mathcal H(\psi )$.

Proof. Use the coframe $\zeta _ j=d'z_ j$, whose conjugate is $d''\bar z_ j$. Differentiating smooth Taylor remainders lowers their order by at most one. The product rule and $d'd''z_ j=-d''d'z_ j$ therefore give

\[ \mathcal H(\psi )=\mathrm i\sum _ j\zeta _ j\wedge \bar\zeta _ j +O(r^ s),\qquad d'\psi =\sum _ j\bar z_ j\zeta _ j+O(r^{s+1}). \]

For example the error $z_ jd'd''\bar z_ j$ has order $s$, and the term $d'\bar z_ j\wedge d''z_ j$ has order $2s$. With $t=r^2+\epsilon ^2$ the chain rule yields

\begin{align*} \mathcal H(\psi +\log t) & =\mathcal H(\psi )+\mathrm i \frac{t\sum _ j\zeta _ j\wedge \bar\zeta _ j -(\sum _ j\bar z_ j\zeta _ j)\wedge (\sum _ k z_ k\bar\zeta _ k)}{t^2}\\ & \hspace{8mm}+O(r^ s/t)+O(r^{s+2}/t^2). \end{align*}

The fraction is semipositive by Cauchy–Schwarz. For $r>0$ the error norm is at most $Cr^{s-2}$, uniformly in $\epsilon $; at $r=0$ the errors vanish. The first Levi form has a fixed positive lower bound after shrinking, so the assertions follow. Closedness is a consequence of (9.53.4) and $d=d'+d''$. Subtracting $\mathcal H(\psi )$ from the positive second form proves the last inequality. $\square$

We next justify the analytic identity needed before constructing a complex atlas. A positive real $(1,1)$ form $\gamma $ with $d\gamma =0$ will be called a formally Kähler metric. It induces the usual pointwise form norms, real metric and volume $dV_\gamma =\gamma ^ n/n!$. The notation in the next lemma describes smooth bundles and differential operators, not presumed holomorphic trivializations.

Lemma 9.53.8.7B10208C6D56 Let $J$ be formally integrable and let $\gamma $ be formally Kähler. Let $K=\Lambda ^{n,0}T^*M$. The operator $d''$ on sections of $K$ defines a partial connection with square zero. Its dual on $K^{-1}$ and the induced metric define a smooth Hermitian connection whose curvature is of type $(1,1)$. Denote its real unscaled curvature by $\operatorname {Ric}(\gamma )$. For a smooth real weight $\phi $, if

68B288E2D6BF 9.53.9
\begin{equation} \mathcal H(\phi )+\operatorname {Ric}(\gamma )\geq \gamma , \end{equation}

then every compactly supported smooth $(0,1)$ form $v$ satisfies

B23883666796 9.53.10
\begin{equation} \int _ M|v|_\gamma ^2e^{-\phi }\, dV_\gamma \leq \| d''v\| _\phi ^2+\| \delta _\phi ''v\| _\phi ^2. \end{equation}

Here $\delta _\phi ''$ is the formal adjoint with the displayed smooth weight. If $\gamma $ is complete, every distributionally closed $g\in L^2_{0,1}(e^{-\phi },\gamma )$ has a solution

1BE0D874AB82 9.53.11
\begin{equation} d''u=g,\qquad \int _ M|u|^2e^{-\phi }\, dV_\gamma \leq \int _ M|g|_\gamma ^2e^{-\phi }\, dV_\gamma . \end{equation}

Proof. The product rule on $(n,0)$ forms gives the partial connection on $K$, and $(d'')^2=0$ gives its square zero. For clarity, a smooth line frame with squared norm $H$ and partial-connection form $a''$ has unique metric connection with $a'=H^{-1}d'H-\overline{a''}$ and $(0,1)$ part $a''$. This formula transforms as a connection and verifies compatibility by differentiating $H$. Its $(0,2)$ curvature is zero by the partial connection equation, and its $(2,0)$ part is zero by compatibility and conjugation. For $E=K^{-1}$ equipped with its induced metric times $e^{-\phi }$, its real curvature is consequently $\operatorname {Ric}(\gamma )+\mathcal H(\phi )$.

Here is a pointwise justification of the Kähler identity for this connection, including the jets which are needed. At any point use lemma 9.53.5 with $s=5$. In these real coordinates $J-J_{\rm std}=O(r^5)$: the $(0,1)$ projection of each $dz_ j$ is $O(r^5)$ and its conjugate has the same property, which determines the matrix of $J$. The degree-two real Taylor polynomial $\gamma _2$ of $\gamma $ is a standard type $(1,1)$ form, and is closed. Indeed all its non-$(1,1)$ coefficients have zero Taylor polynomial through degree two, and $d\gamma _2$ is the degree-one Taylor polynomial of $d\gamma =0$. It is positive on a smaller ball. Thus $\gamma _2$ is an actual standard Kähler metric there and has exactly the same two-jet as $\gamma $ at the point.

The smooth frame $\eta =d'z_1\wedge \cdots \wedge d'z_ n$ of $K$ satisfies $d''\eta =O(r^4)$, since $d''d'z_ j=-d'd''z_ j$. Hence its partial-connection coefficients and their derivatives through order two vanish at the point. The metric of $\eta ^{-1}$ is the reciprocal of the squared norm of $\eta $, so it agrees through order two with the metric of the standard inverse canonical frame for $\gamma _2$. Use also the degree-two Taylor polynomial of $\phi $. The connection just defined, the exterior operators, their adjoints and Laplacians, and their curvature operators therefore have exactly the same pointwise coefficients as in that genuine Kähler model. Exterior operators use first jets, adjoints use first metric jets, and their second-order compositions and curvature use at most second jets; the type projections also have matching jets. Coefficients of a test form can be given the same two-jet in the two frames.

Apply the already proved local identity theorem 10.33.2 in the genuine model. Equality of these coefficients proves the same identity for $E$ at the chosen point, hence everywhere. This uses the proof of that local differential-operator identity, without presuming the existence of any holomorphic coordinates for $J$. There is a global isometry from scalar $(0,q)$ forms to $E$-valued $(n,q)$ forms, given in a local frame by $v\mapsto (-1)^{nq}\eta \wedge v\otimes \eta ^{-1}$. The connection terms on $\eta $ and its dual cancel, and the indicated sign makes this commute with $d''$ in consecutive degrees. Adjoints commute as well. On $(n,1)$ forms the curvature operator is precisely the Hermitian form of $\mathcal H(\phi )+\operatorname {Ric}(\gamma )$, by the coefficient formula in proposition 10.33.6. Its lower bound is the identity under (9.53.9). The integrated Kähler identity now gives (9.53.10).

The complete-domain argument requires no complex coordinate charts. For precision, take maximal distributional operators $T=d'':L^2_{0,0}\to L^2_{0,1}$ and $S=d'':L^2_{0,1}\to L^2_{0,2}$; if $n=1$ the last space is zero. They are closed and densely defined, and $ST=0$. The smooth complete gradient cutoffs of lemma 6.26.1 have the product rules

\[ d''(\chi v)=\chi d''v+d''\chi \wedge v, \quad \delta _\phi ''(\chi v)=\chi \delta _\phi ''v -(d''\chi \wedge )^*v. \]

Their errors are bounded by $C\| v\| _\phi /R$. On each compact support the operators are smooth first-order matrices in real coordinates. The joint cutoff and mollifier proof of lemma 6.26.4, using graph-norm density by cutoffs and mollification, applies verbatim to these matrices and the smooth positive weight. It identifies $T^*$ with the maximal $\delta _\phi ''$ and proves joint graph density. Thus (9.53.10) extends to $\operatorname {Dom}T^*\cap \operatorname {Dom}S$. By Cauchy–Schwarz, $|\langle g,v\rangle |^2\leq \| g\| _\phi ^2 (\| T^*v\| _\phi ^2+\| Sv\| _\phi ^2)$. The exact Hilbert-complex theorem Hilbert-complex solvability from the dual energy estimate, applied to this closed complex and $Sg=0$, proves (9.53.11). No boundary condition or global boundedness of weight derivatives is imposed beyond these maximal domains. $\square$

Lemma 9.53.12.515854F40204 For a smooth almost complex structure, if $u\in L^2_{\rm loc}$ and $d''u$ is smooth distributionally, then $u$ is smooth. Formal integrability is not needed for this assertion.

Proof. Choose any smooth positive metric and its unweighted local formal adjoint. The operator $P=\delta ''d''$ on functions has principal symbol $|\xi ^{0,1}|^2=|\xi |^2/2$ for real covectors. Thus it is uniformly elliptic on compact coordinate balls, and $Pu=\delta ''(d''u)$ is smooth. We specify the initial regularity step: after a compact cutoff, the right-hand side of the localized equation is in $H^{-1}$, since its commutator with $P$ is first order applied to $u\in L^2$. Conjugate by the smooth square root of the density and coefficient metric. The symmetric principal coefficient matrix is real and uniformly positive; antisymmetric coefficients contribute only first-order terms. In divergence form, ordinary mollification $J_ t$ satisfies $\| [a^{ij}\partial _ j,J_ t]u\| _2\leq C\| u\| _2$ on the fixed compact chart, by graph-norm density by cutoffs and mollification. Consequently $PJ_ tu$ is bounded in $H^{-1}$. The coercive integration-by-parts inequality $\| w\| _{H^1}^2\leq C(|\langle Pw,w\rangle |+\| w\| _2^2)$, followed by duality and Young's inequality, bounds $J_ tu$ in $H^1$. Weak compactness gives $u\in H^1_{\rm loc}$. This is the same local commutator argument as in lemma 6.28.1, with a smooth source added. The checked divergence-form interior theorem interior regularity for elliptic systems in divergence form now bootstraps on nested balls to every Sobolev order. The local Sobolev embedding Sobolev spaces, duality and local embedding gives smoothness. $\square$

Theorem 9.53.13.58DA08F550FB A smooth almost complex structure is induced by a complex analytic atlas if and only if it is formally integrable. That atlas is unique up to compatibility. More precisely, about every point of a formally integrable structure there are smooth complex-valued real coordinates $w_1,\ldots ,w_ n$ satisfying $d''w_ j=0$.

Proof. An existing complex atlas gives $\vartheta =0$ by commuting its coordinate fields and tensoriality. For the converse choose the coordinates of lemma 9.53.5 with $s\geq 5$ and shrink to a ball $\Omega =\{ \psi =|z|^2<r_0^2\} $ on which lemma 9.53.7 holds. Here $\psi $ is strictly PSH in the defined formal sense. The function $\rho =(r_0^2-\psi )^{-1}$ is a smooth positive strict-PSH exhaustion. The form $\gamma =\mathcal H(e^\rho )$ is positive and closed, and complete: its rank-one summand gives $|d\rho |_\gamma ^2\leq 2e^{-\rho }\leq 2$. A length-Cauchy sequence has bounded $\rho $ and so lies in a compact sublevel, where the smooth metric and coordinate topologies agree. It therefore converges. This repeats the real-metric proof of lemma 6.26.8, which uses only the displayed formal product and closedness identities.

Choose a smooth convex increasing function $b$ on the range of $\rho $ so fast that

AC2A3454F009 9.53.14
\begin{equation} \mathcal H(b\circ \rho )\geq (n+1)\mathcal H(\psi )-\operatorname {Ric}(\gamma )+\gamma . \end{equation}

Here is an explicit existence check. Relative to the positive $\mathcal H(\rho )$, the right side has a finite upper eigenvalue bound on each compact sublevel. Take a nondecreasing positive sequence dominating those bounds on successive sublevels; a locally finite sum of nondecreasing smooth step functions gives a smooth nondecreasing derivative $b'$ dominating the bound on each band. Its integral is convex and increasing. The chain rule $\mathcal H(b\circ \rho )=b'(\rho )\mathcal H(\rho )+ \mathrm i b''(\rho )d'\rho \wedge d''\rho $ proves the inequality. No boundedness of the Ricci form near the boundary is assumed. Put

\[ \phi _\epsilon =b(\rho )+(n+1)\log (\psi +\epsilon ^2), \qquad 0<\epsilon \leq 1. \]

The uniform logarithmic-weight lemma and (9.53.14) give $\mathcal H(\phi _\epsilon )+\operatorname {Ric}(\gamma )\geq \gamma $ for every $\epsilon $. All these weights are smooth on $\Omega $.

Take $h\in C_ c^\infty (\Omega )$ equal to one near zero and set $g_ j=d''(hz_ j)$. These are smooth closed $(0,1)$ forms by (9.53.4). Near zero $g_ j=O(r^ s)$; away from zero their support is compact. Consequently

\[ I_ j=\int _\Omega |g_ j|_\gamma ^2 e^{-b(\rho )} \psi ^{-n-1}\, dV_\gamma <\infty : \]

near zero the radial bound is $C r^{2s-3}\, dr$, and $s\geq 2$ is enough. By lemma 9.53.8 there are solutions $u_{j,\epsilon }$ with $d''u_{j,\epsilon }=g_ j$ and $\| u_{j,\epsilon }\| _{\phi _\epsilon }^2\leq I_ j$.

For a sequence $\epsilon _ k\downarrow 0$, these bounds give weak compactness in every fixed space $L^2(e^{-\phi _{\epsilon _ l}})$ after discarding $k<l$. A diagonal subsequence has one distributional limit $u_ j$, because the weights and metric have fixed positive lower bounds on compact sets. Weak lower semicontinuity in each fixed space followed by monotone convergence gives

1594A5CCAFB4 9.53.15
\begin{equation} d''u_ j=g_ j,\qquad \int _\Omega |u_ j|^2 e^{-b(\rho )}\psi ^{-n-1}\, dV_\gamma \leq I_ j. \end{equation}

The function $u_ j$ is locally $L^2$, and $g_ j$ is smooth. Hence lemma 9.53.12 proves smoothness using the unweighted smooth elliptic operator. No regularity theorem for a singular weighted adjoint is being invoked. The last integral forces $u_ j(0)=du_ j(0)=0$. Indeed a nonzero constant Taylor term gives radial divergence $\int r^{-3}dr$; if the constant term vanishes and the real linear term is nonzero, it is bounded below by $cr$ on a fixed cone of directions and gives divergence $\int r^{-1}dr$. Smooth positive metric and weight factors near zero do not change either conclusion.

Thus $w_ j=hz_ j-u_ j$ satisfies $d''w_ j=0$ and $dw_ j(0)=dz_ j(0)$. The real inverse function theorem makes $w$ a coordinate system on a smaller neighborhood. Its differential intertwines $J$ with the standard structure, since $d''w_ j=0$. On two such neighborhoods the transition has complex-linear differential, so satisfies the ordinary Cauchy–Riemann equations. The canonical local Cauchy theorem makes it holomorphic, with the convergent local expansion holomorphic power series and Cauchy bounds. Conversely any compatible complex atlas has these same local $J$-holomorphic functions, proving uniqueness. $\square$
