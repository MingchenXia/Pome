Theorem 6.28.4FD2B7A8ED856

Theorem 6.28.4.FD2B7A8ED856 Let $(X,\omega )$ possess a complete Kähler metric. Let $G:E\to Q$ be a surjective holomorphic bundle map, with the induced quotient metric on $Q$. Put $r=\operatorname {rank}E$, $a=\operatorname {rank}Q$, $s=r-a$, and fix $0\leq k\leq n$. Set $m=\min (n-k,s)$. Suppose $E$ is $m$-semipositive and a Hermitian line bundle $L$ satisfies

\[ \mathrm i\Theta (L)\geq (m+\epsilon )\mathrm i\Theta (\det Q), \qquad \epsilon >0. \]

Every closed $L^2$ form $f$ of type $(n,k)$ with values in $Q\otimes L$ has a closed lift $h$ with values in $E\otimes L$ such that

1FDBE18C9CE0 6.28.5
\begin{equation} Gh=f,\qquad \| h\| _2^2\leq (1+m/\epsilon )\| f\| _2^2. \end{equation}

Smooth data admit a smooth lift. When $m=0$, no curvature hypothesis is needed and the constant is one.

Proof. If $Q=0$ take $h=0$. If $s=0$, the inverse of $G$ is holomorphic and isometric for the quotient metric. If $k=n$, the orthogonal lift $G^*f$ is automatically closed by degree and isometric. These cases give the stated $m=0$ clause. Assume henceforth $s\geq 1$, $k<n$, so $m\geq 1$.

Let $S=\ker G$ and use the orthogonal splitting $E=S\oplus Q$. The complete connection and curvature calculation of proposition 10.34.9 writes the second fundamental form as $\beta =\sum _ j\mathrm dz_ j B_ j$ and gives

\[ R_ S=R_ E|_ S-C_\beta ,\qquad R_ Q=R_ E|_ Q+(B_ jB_ l^*)_{j\bar l},\qquad C_\beta (v)=\left|\sum _ jB_ jv_ j\right|^2. \]

Put $\alpha =\operatorname {tr}_ S C_\beta $. The rank-one tests contained in $m$-semipositivity give $\operatorname {tr}_ Q R_ E|_ Q\geq 0$, hence $R_{\det Q}\geq \alpha $. Moreover $m\alpha \otimes I_ S-C_\beta \geq _ m0$ by theorem 10.34.2, including $s=m=1$. The curvature assumption therefore gives

8CCE15D3C2B6 6.28.6
\begin{equation} R_{S\otimes L}\geq _ m(m+\epsilon )\alpha \otimes I_ S-C_\beta \geq _ m(\epsilon /m)C_\beta . \end{equation}

The last difference is $(1+\epsilon /m)(m\alpha \otimes I_ S-C_\beta )$.

In degree $(n,k+1)$ the coefficient tensors $(v_{jJ})_ j$ have rank at most $\min (n-k,s)=m$, by proposition 10.33.6. If $B=[\mathrm i\Theta (S\otimes L),\Lambda _\omega ]$, then

B33FBDD0925E 6.28.7
\begin{equation} \langle Bv,v\rangle \geq (\epsilon /m) \sum _{|J|=k}\left|\sum _ j B_ jv_{jJ}\right|^2, \qquad |\beta ^*\wedge f|_{B^{-1}}^2\leq (m/\epsilon )|f|^2. \end{equation}

Indeed the adjoint of $f\mapsto \beta ^*\wedge f$ is contraction with coefficients $\sum _ jB_ jv_{jJ}$, with the common top-form sign cancelled in its squared norm. Pairing with $f$ and applying finite-dimensional Cauchy–Schwarz proves the second inequality, also on a kernel of $B$.

Write $j:S\to E$ for inclusion. The $(0,1)$ connection block gives $D''(G^*f)=-j(\beta ^*\wedge f)$. Thus $F=\beta ^*\wedge f$ is locally $L^2$ and closed distributionally: apply $(D'')^2=0$ and use that $j$ is holomorphic and injective. It need not be globally $L^2$. Its finite inverse energy is bounded by $(m/\epsilon )\| f\| _2^2$ by (6.28.7). The original-metric part of theorem 6.28.2 supplies $D''u=F$ with that squared norm bound. Set $h=G^*f+ju$. Then $Gh=f$, $D''h=0$, and orthogonality gives pointwise $|h|^2=|f|^2+|u|^2$. This proves (6.28.5). If $f$ is smooth, choose the smooth primitive supplied by the same theorem. $\square$
