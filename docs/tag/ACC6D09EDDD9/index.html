Proposition 20.7.3ACC6D09EDDD9

Proposition 20.7.3.ACC6D09EDDD9 Let $L$ be a holomorphic line bundle, $h$ a smooth quadratic metric, and $s$ a meromorphic section, nonzero on every component. Meromorphic coefficients are taken in holomorphic frames. Then

\[ dd^ c\log |s|_ h^2=[\operatorname {div}s]-c_1(L,h), \qquad dd^ c=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial . \]

In particular the divisor current represents the real image of the integer first Chern class. Every locally finite integral hypersurface divisor $D$ determines a holomorphic line bundle $\mathcal O(D)$, with a canonical meromorphic section of divisor $D$. If $D$ is effective, that section is holomorphic and its zero set is the support of $D$. Moreover $\mathcal O(D_1+D_2)\simeq \mathcal O(D_1)\otimes \mathcal O(D_2)$, and the map $D\mapsto \mathcal O(D)$ has kernel the principal divisors and image the line bundles admitting a componentwise nonzero meromorphic section. Thus $\mathcal O(D_1)\simeq \mathcal O(D_2)$ exactly when $D_2-D_1$ is the divisor of a global meromorphic function, nonzero on every component; these divisors are called linearly equivalent. It is the divisor connecting map in the frame-cocycle convention of theorem 10.40.2.

Proof. In a holomorphic frame $e$, write $s=(a/b)e$, with holomorphic numerator and nonzero denominator germs. Apply the normalized Poincaré–Lelong formula separately to $a,b$, subtract, and use $c_1(L,h)=-dd^ c\log |e|_ h^2$. The local logarithms are integrable and their difference is the global locally integrable norm logarithm. This proves the displayed current identity. The current/smooth de Rham comparison and theorem 10.40.5 give the stated cohomology class.

The local equations of local equations and currents of irreducible hypersurfaces give meromorphic local equations $u_ i$ for $D$, by multiplying their integral powers. Their ratios are holomorphic units. Here is the local cancellation argument. If a nonzero holomorphic germ vanishes on an irreducible hypersurface germ, it is divisible by that hypersurface's local equation, since the equation generates its vanishing ideal. Repeatedly divide while a zero hypersurface remains. This terminates: the order of the first nonzero Taylor term at the base point drops by at least one at each division by a nonunit. A remaining nonunit would still have a zero hypersurface by purity of a nontrivial holomorphic zero set, so the final remainder is a unit. At a regular point of each factor outside the others, the equation vanishes to order one; hence its exponent in this factorization is exactly the divisor multiplicity. Applying this factorization to the numerator and denominator cancels equal factors. A quotient of divisor zero is therefore a holomorphic unit, and a meromorphic germ with nonnegative divisor is holomorphic. Thus the meromorphic functions $f$ with $\operatorname {div}f+D\geq 0$ form the locally free sheaf $u_ i^{-1}\mathcal O$ on each such neighborhood. Its local frames $e_ i=1/u_ i$ obey $e_ i=(u_ j/u_ i)e_ j$. Their canonical section one has coefficient $u_ i$, hence divisor $D$. These frame transitions are the reciprocals of the corresponding coefficient transitions. If $D\geq 0$, the local coefficients $u_ i$ are holomorphic by the same cancellation argument and vanish exactly on its support. Multiplying the local equations for two divisors multiplies their frame transitions, proving the tensor-product formula.

For the exact sheaf sequence $1\to \mathcal O^*\to \mathcal M^*\xrightarrow {\operatorname {div}} \operatorname {Div}\to 0$, local equations $u_ i$ are local lifts of $D$. The connecting cocycle is $u_ j/u_ i$, by lemma 10.40.1, precisely the frame cocycle above. Exactness gives the kernel assertion, and applying it to $D_2-D_1$ together with the tensor formula gives linear equivalence. Finally any nonzero meromorphic section $s$ yields an isomorphism $\mathcal O(\operatorname {div}s)\to \mathcal O(L)$ by $f\mapsto fs$: in a local factorization its source generator maps to a holomorphic unit times a frame. Conversely the canonical section of $\mathcal O(D)$ gives such a section for any line bundle in the image. No existence of meromorphic sections for all higher-dimensional line bundles is asserted. $\square$
