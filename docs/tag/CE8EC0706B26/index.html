Proposition 2.20.11 (An ordinary second-order prolongation)CE8EC0706B26

Proposition 2.20.11 (An ordinary second-order prolongation).CE8EC0706B26 Let $\Omega $ be an open subset of $\{ (t,x)\in \mathbb R^2:|t|<1\} $, and let $f\in C^\infty (\Omega ,\mathbb C)$. Put

\[ D=\partial _ t-i\partial _ x,\qquad A=D^2+it^2\partial _ x^2,\qquad B=D^2-it^2\partial _ x^2, \qquad P_ f=D^4+t^4\partial _ x^4+i\partial _ x^3-f. \]

There is a smooth second-order elliptic differential operator $\mathcal P_ f$ on the trivial complex bundle $\Omega \times \mathbb C^3$ such that every smooth solution $u$ of $P_ fu=0$ gives a solution

\[ U=(u,\partial _ xu,Bu),\qquad \mathcal P_ fU=0. \]

The section $U$ is zero on every open set on which $u$ is zero, and $U$ is nonzero whenever $u$ is nonzero. The realification of $\mathcal P_ f$ is also an ordinary second-order elliptic operator on a trivial real bundle of rank six.

Proof. The coefficients $t^2$ commute with $\partial _ x$, and $D(t^2)=2t$, $D^2(t^2)=2$. Applying the product rule to a test function gives the exact differential-operator identity

\[ AB=D^4+t^4\partial _ x^4-4it\partial _ x^2D-2i\partial _ x^2. \]

Consequently

\[ P_ f=AB+Q\partial _ x-f,\qquad Q=4it\partial _ t\partial _ x+(4t+i)\partial _ x^2+2i\partial _ x. \]

Here the term $4t\partial _ x^3$ comes from $4it\partial _ x^2(-i\partial _ x)$; in particular it has positive sign. Define, for arbitrary smooth triples $(u,w,v)$,

\[ \mathcal P_ f \begin{pmatrix} u \\ w \\ v \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} Bu-v \\ Bw-\partial _ xv \\ Av+Qw-fu \end{pmatrix}. \]

This is a differential operator of order two in every component. Since $B$ commutes with $\partial _ x$, substitution of $w=\partial _ xu$, $v=Bu$ makes its first two components zero, and the third component is exactly $P_ fu$. This proves the asserted solution map. Derivatives of a function that is identically zero on an open set are identically zero there; the first component also proves the nontriviality assertion.

For ellipticity, take the principal-symbol convention that replaces $\partial _ t,\partial _ x$ by real variables $\tau ,\xi $; the alternative convention with $i\tau ,i\xi $ changes the entire second-order matrix by a nonzero scalar and gives the same invertibility test. The principal matrix is

\[ \sigma _2(\mathcal P_ f)(t,x;\tau ,\xi )= \begin{pmatrix} b & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & b & 0 \\ 0 & q & a \end{pmatrix}, \quad \begin{aligned} a& =(\tau -i\xi )^2+it^2\xi ^2,\\ b& =(\tau -i\xi )^2-it^2\xi ^2,\\ q& =4it\tau \xi +(4t+i)\xi ^2. \end{aligned} \]

Its determinant is $b^2a$. If $\xi =0$ and $(\tau ,\xi )\ne 0$, then $a=b=\tau ^2\ne 0$. If $\xi \ne 0$, the vanishing of either $a$ or $b$ would require, on putting $r=\tau /\xi \in \mathbb R$,

\[ r^2-1=0,\qquad -2r\pm t^2=0. \]

The first equation gives $r=1$ or $r=-1$, whereas the second is impossible for $|t|<1$. Thus the symbol is invertible at every nonzero real covector. This proves ordinary ellipticity, without a Douglis–Nirenberg weighting or an unasserted positivity of the symbol.

Write complex coefficients and components in real and imaginary parts. The real principal matrix is the realification of the displayed complex matrix. Its determinant is $|b^2a|^2$, so it is invertible as a real matrix at the same covectors. The real and imaginary parts of $U$ give the corresponding real solution and preserve its zero set and nontriviality. $\square$
