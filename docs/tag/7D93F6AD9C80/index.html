Theorem 5.54.27D93F6AD9C80

Theorem 5.54.2.7D93F6AD9C80 On every open $\Omega \subset \mathbb R^ m$ there are linear operators $R_\varepsilon $ and $K_\varepsilon $, $0<\varepsilon <1$, on currents, of respective degree $0$ and $-1$, such that $R_\varepsilon T$ is smooth and

\[ R_\varepsilon T-T=\mathrm dK_\varepsilon T +K_\varepsilon \mathrm dT, \qquad \mathrm dR_\varepsilon T=R_\varepsilon \mathrm dT. \]

As $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, $R_\varepsilon T\to T$ and $K_\varepsilon T\to 0$ weakly. If $T$ has order at most the nonnegative integer $s$, then $K_\varepsilon T$ has order at most $s$. In particular every closed current is cohomologous to a smooth closed form.

Proof. Choose $\psi ,F_ v$ as in the lemma and a nonnegative smooth probability kernel $\rho _\varepsilon $ supported in $B(0,\varepsilon )$. Put $H_ v(t,x)=F_{tv}(x)$ and define

\[ R_\varepsilon T=\int \rho _\varepsilon (v)(F_ v)_*T\, \mathrm dv, \qquad K_\varepsilon T= \int \rho _\varepsilon (v)(H_ v)_*([0,1]\otimes T)\, \mathrm dv. \]

These are weak integrals, defined by pairing with a test form. For tests supported in a fixed compact $K$, the lemma gives one compact subset of $\Omega $ containing every relevant inverse image. Pullback and its coefficient derivatives are uniformly bounded on this compact set for $t\in [0,1]$ and $v$ in the kernel support. Thus current continuity defines both integrals. More precisely, when $T$ has order at most $s$, the resulting bound uses only test derivatives of order at most $s$; integrating the parameters does not raise that order. This proves the order assertion for $K_\varepsilon T$.

The real dimensions of the source and target of $F_ v$ are equal, so its pushforward commutes with $\mathrm d$. The sign-corrected homotopy in theorem 5.53.1, with endpoints the identity and $F_ v$, gives

\[ (F_ v)_*T-T=\mathrm d(H_ v)_*([0,1]\otimes T) +(H_ v)_*([0,1]\otimes \mathrm dT). \]

Averaging proves both displayed identities. This argument uses properness only on the tensor support; the lemma supplies it uniformly.

We verify smoothness, including the test-space assertion needed for distribution coefficients. Write a test form of degree $m-q$ as $\phi =\sum _ I\phi _ I(y)\, \mathrm dy_ I$. The coefficients of $F_ v^*\phi $ are finite sums of

\[ P_{JI}(x,v)\phi _ I(x+\psi (x)v), \]

where $P_{JI}$ is smooth in $x$ and polynomial in $v$, being a minor of $D F_ v$. For $x\in \Omega $, the change of variables $y=x+\psi (x)v$ rewrites their averages as

\[ \int _\Omega L_{JI}(x,y)\phi _ I(y)\, \mathrm dy, \quad L_{JI}(x,y)=\psi (x)^{-m} \rho _\varepsilon \! \left(\frac{y-x}{\psi (x)}\right) P_{JI}\! \left(x,\frac{y-x}{\psi (x)}\right). \]

Each kernel is smooth on $\Omega \times \Omega $. If $y$ ranges in a compact neighborhood, its $x$-support lies in one compact subset of $\Omega $, by the lemma. Pairing the $x$-variable with each coefficient distribution of $T$ therefore gives a smooth function of $y$: every $y$-derivative can be passed through the pairing, because the kernel and its differentiated difference quotients converge in the compact test-space seminorms. Pairing commutes with the compact $y$-integral by the same seminorm estimates, or by its Riemann sums. Consequently $\langle R_\varepsilon T,\phi \rangle $ is the pairing of $\phi $ with a smooth $q$-form. The constant coordinate orientation signs are exactly those in the current's coefficient representation. This proves smoothness of $R_\varepsilon T$ on the entire $\Omega $.

For completeness, as $v\to 0$, $F_ v^*\phi \to \phi $ in all test-space seminorms with a common compact support. This proves $R_\varepsilon T\to T$. In $H_ v^*\eta $, the coefficient containing $\mathrm dt$ has a factor $\partial _ tH_ v=\psi (x)v$. All its $x$-derivatives tend to zero uniformly on the common compact set. The tensor convention of section 5.53 therefore gives $K_\varepsilon T\to 0$. If $\mathrm dT=0$, the displayed identity expresses the difference from its smooth closed representative as an exact current, as required. $\square$
