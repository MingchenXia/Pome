Lemma 10.47.199F7CD84D262

Lemma 10.47.1.99F7CD84D262 Let $V=\bigoplus _{k=0}^{2n}V^ k$ be a finite-dimensional graded Hermitian space with orthogonal degrees. Suppose that $L$ has degree two, $\Lambda =L^*$, and

\[ [\Lambda ,L]|_{V^ k}=(n-k)I. \]

Set $P^ k=\ker \Lambda \cap V^ k$. Then $P^ k=0$ for $k>n$, and, for $u\in P^\ell $, the lowering formula holds for $1\leq j\leq n-\ell $ and the norm formula for $0\leq j\leq n-\ell $:

29B066DE4DF2
\begin{align} \Lambda L^ j u& =j(n-\ell -j+1)L^{j-1}u, \tag{10.47.2}\\ \| L^ ju\| ^2& =\frac{j!(n-\ell )!}{(n-\ell -j)!}\| u\| ^2, \qquad L^{n-\ell +1}u=0. \tag{10.47.3} \end{align}

Every $v\in V^ k$ has a unique orthogonal decomposition

FCD784AF69AF 10.47.4
\begin{equation} v=\sum _{r=\max (0,k-n)}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor }L^ ru_ r, \qquad u_ r\in P^{k-2r}. \end{equation}

Each $u_ r$ is a polynomial in $L,\Lambda $ applied to $v$, with rational coefficients depending only on $n,k,r$. For $k\leq n$, $L^{n-k}:V^ k\to V^{2n-k}$ is an isomorphism. On the summand $L^ rP^{k-2r}$, writing $s=n-k$, its inverse and norm ratio are given by the following formulas, with the inverse acting on the image of that summand:

A1C2A0FF7C70 10.47.5
\begin{equation} (L^ s)^{-1}=\left(\frac{r!}{(r+s)!}\right)^2\Lambda ^ s, \qquad \| L^ s v\| ^2=\left(\frac{(r+s)!}{r!}\right)^2\| v\| ^2. \end{equation}

These assertions apply pointwise to the complex exterior algebra of a Hermitian vector space. In its bigrading the summands are $L^ rP^{p-r,q-r}$, with $\max (0,p+q-n)\leq r\leq \min (p,q)$.

Proof. Expanding a commutator of $\Lambda $ with $L^ j$ and applying the degree formula to $L^ a u$ gives

\[ [\Lambda ,L^ j]u =\sum _{a=0}^{j-1}(n-\ell -2a)L^{j-1}u =j(n-\ell -j+1)L^{j-1}u. \]

For primitive $u$ this is (10.47.2). Taking its inner product with $L^{j-1}u$ proves the norm formula by induction. If $\ell >n$, the case $j=1$ gives $\| Lu\| ^2=(n-\ell )\| u\| ^2$, forcing $u=0$. For $\ell \leq n$ the norms are positive through $j=n-\ell $ and zero at $j=n-\ell +1$, proving termination.

Here is a complete decomposition argument. In any nonzero graded subspace invariant under $L$ and $\Lambda $, choose a nonzero vector $u$ of its lowest nonzero degree. It is primitive. The chain $u,Lu,\ldots ,L^{n-\ell }u$ has nonzero, mutually orthogonal vectors and is invariant under both operators. Its orthogonal complement in the subspace is also invariant: this follows from $L^*=\Lambda $. It is graded since the chain is graded. Repeating on that complement terminates after finitely many steps and gives an orthogonal sum of such chains. If $V$ is bigraded and the degrees are orthogonal, with $L$ of type $(1,1)$ and $\Lambda $ of type $(-1,-1)$, choose the lowest-degree vector of pure type at each step; the complements still respect type. On each chain the kernel of $\Lambda $ is its first line, since all the lowering coefficients at the later vectors are positive. Thus the initial vectors of the chains of degree $\ell $ span $P^\ell $.

In a chain of primitive degree $\ell $, the vector at degree $k$ is $L^ ru$, where $\ell =k-2r$ and $r\leq n-\ell $. These conditions give exactly the range in (10.47.4). The resulting summands are mutually orthogonal and account for all of $V^ k$. To see uniqueness independently of the chosen chains, put $T=L\Lambda $ on $V^ k$. On the displayed summand its eigenvalue is

\[ t_ r=r(n-k+r+1). \]

These numbers are distinct: $t_{r+1}-t_ r=n-k+2r+2>0$ in the indicated range. Thus its component projection and primitive coefficient are

69B921CFAC64 10.47.6
\begin{equation} Q_{k,r}=\prod _{a\ne r}\frac{T-t_ aI}{t_ r-t_ a},\qquad u_ r=\frac{(n-k+r)!}{r!(n-k+2r)!}\Lambda ^ rQ_{k,r}v, \end{equation}

where the product runs over the same finite range and an empty product is $I$. All factorials are of nonnegative integers. The coefficient follows by applying the lowering formula $r$ times to $L^ ru_ r$. This also proves the claimed rational-polynomial expression.

Each primitive chain has one vector in degree $k$ precisely when it has one in degree $2n-k$. Multiplication by $L^{n-k}$ takes the first to the second with nonzero coefficient. It is therefore an isomorphism. Applying (10.47.2) repeatedly, and then (10.47.3), gives both formulas in (10.47.5).

For the exterior algebra, an $\omega $-unitary coframe gives $\omega =\mathrm i\sum \theta _ j\wedge \bar\theta _ j$. The creation/contraction calculation of the curvature operator on $(n,1)$-forms, with every curvature eigenvalue equal to one, gives $[L,\Lambda ]=(k-n)I$, or the commutator used here with the opposite order. The bigraded argument proves the last assertion. $\square$
