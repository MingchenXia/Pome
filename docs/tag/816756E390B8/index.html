7.32.1 Collar estimates for plurisubharmonic potentials816756E390B8

Corollary 7.32.2.EAB247B80433 Under the preceding geometric hypotheses, fix $\delta _0>0$ and set $\Omega _\delta =\{ \psi <-\delta \} $, $0<\delta \leq \delta _0$. If $L(u)\cap \operatorname {Supp}T\subset \Omega _\delta $, put

\[ M=\sup _{(\overline\Omega \setminus \Omega _\delta ) \cap \operatorname {Supp}T}|u|. \]

Then $M<\infty $, $uT$ is coefficient-integrable, and

\[ \| uT\| _{\overline\Omega ,\omega } \leq \frac{C}{\delta }M\| T\| _{\overline\Omega ,\omega }, \qquad C=4\delta _0+8\| \psi \| _{L^\infty (\Omega )}. \]

Here $\| uT\| _{A,\omega }=\int _ A|u|T\wedge \omega ^ p$. For any fixed smooth Hermitian norm the same assertion holds with a constant depending only on the fixed geometric data and $p$. It has no dependence on $u$, $T$ or $\delta $.

Proof. The closed collar support is compact and misses $L(u)$, so $u$ is bounded on a neighborhood of each of its points; a finite subcover makes $M$ finite. Empty collar support forces $T=0$ inside by the preceding theorem. We may otherwise use the same decreasing smooth majorants $u_ j$. For each $\eta >0$, eventually $-M\leq u_ j\leq M+\eta $ on that compact collar support. The lower bound follows from $u_ j\geq u$, and the upper bound from compactness and decreasing convergence, as above. The support maximum theorem gives $u_ j\leq M+\eta $ on the whole closed domain support. Set

\[ v_ j=u_ j-M-2\eta ,\qquad N=2M+3\eta ,\qquad A=4N/\delta . \]

Thus $v_ j\leq -\eta $ on the closed domain support, and $v_ j>-N$ on the collar support. Continuity makes these strict inequalities valid on suitable neighborhoods.

Modify $v_ j$ only near that support: leave it unchanged for $\psi <-\delta /2$, and use $\max \{ v_ j,A\psi \} $ on the outer collar. These formulas glue on a support neighborhood. Indeed, choose the outer neighborhood with $\psi >-3\delta /4$; on the overlap $-3\delta /4<\psi <-\delta /2$ one has $A\psi \leq -2N<v_ j$. Near the boundary the maximum is exactly $A\psi $. Call the resulting locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $w_ j$. It is defined near $\overline\Omega \cap \operatorname {Supp}T$, is nonpositive on that support and equals $A\psi $ on its boundary collar. Its currents extend unambiguously across the complementary region where $T=0$; no values of $w_ j$ away from the support are needed.

Write $m_ T=\int _\Omega T\wedge \omega ^ p$ and $B=\| \psi \| _{L^\infty (\Omega )}$. The two common-collar identities in lemma 7.29.1 apply here with precisely the same compact-difference proof, which does not use the spherical shape of its original ball. Namely $(w_ j-A\psi )T$ has compact support in $\Omega $; integration by parts with a cutoff equal to one near that support gives

\[ \int _\Omega dd^ cw_ j\wedge T\wedge \omega ^{p-1}=A m_ T, \quad \int _\Omega (-w_ j)T\wedge \omega ^ p =\int _\Omega (-\psi )dd^ cw_ j\wedge T\wedge \omega ^{p-1} \leq BA m_ T. \]

The functions $w_ j$ and $v_ j$ agree on the inner region, and $-v_ j\leq N$ on the rest of the support. Therefore

\[ \int _\Omega |u_ j|T\wedge \omega ^ p \leq (N+M+2\eta +4BN/\delta )m_ T. \]

Fatou as $j\to \infty $, followed by $\eta \downarrow 0$, bounds the open-domain integral by $(3+8B/\delta )Mm_ T$. The boundary integral is at most $M\int _{\partial \Omega }T\wedge \omega ^ p$ because the boundary belongs to the closed collar. This proves the claimed closed-domain estimate, also when $M=0$. Coefficient integrability follows by the existing trace domination theorem. On the fixed compact closed domain any fixed Hermitian metric is uniformly comparable with $\omega $, giving the final assertion. $\square$

The strictly plurisubharmonic defining-function convention entails no loss for a smoothly strongly pseudoconvex Stein domain. For completeness, first take any smooth defining function $r$ on a boundary collar. Replacing it by $r+Cr^2$ makes it strictly plurisubharmonic on a smaller collar: on the compact boundary its Levi form is already positive on the complex tangent space, and $2C\, \partial r\otimes \bar\partial r$ makes the remaining normal direction positive for one sufficiently large $C$, by the Schur complement. Continuity preserves positivity on a smaller collar; the sign and zero set remain unchanged. Now use this strict defining function and the strict exhaustion given by theorem 9.32.12. Choose two negative levels inside the collar. A sufficiently small positive multiple of the exhaustion plus a negative constant lies above the defining function near the inner level and below it near the outer level; all relevant inner sets are compact, so both strict inequalities can be imposed at once. The normalized smooth maximum Euclidean Richberg smoothing glues these two functions, with exact branches near the two levels. It stays negative inside, agrees with the original defining function near the boundary, and is strictly plurisubharmonic throughout a neighborhood of the closed domain. Thus the support maximum and collar bound hold throughout the stated smooth strongly pseudoconvex Stein scope, including discontinuous potentials and boundary mass. The case $p=0$ is excluded here: a point mass and a logarithmic pole already violate integrability.
