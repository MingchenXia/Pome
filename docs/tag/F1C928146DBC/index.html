Theorem 20.5.5F1C928146DBC

Theorem 20.5.5.F1C928146DBC Let $A$ be a reduced pure $p$-dimensional analytic subset of a complex manifold $X$, and let $f$ be holomorphic near $A$, not identically zero on any irreducible component of $A$. Then $\log |f|^2$ is coefficient-integrable for $[A]$. If $p\geq 1$ and $C_ k$ are the irreducible components of $A\cap \{ f=0\} $, there are positive integers $m_ k$ such that

14ADBD4BFF7F 20.5.6
\begin{equation} dd^ c\bigl (\log |f|^2[A]\bigr )=\sum _ k m_ k[C_ k]. \end{equation}

The sum is locally finite. The same assertion applies linearly to effective integral analytic cycles whose components are not contained in $\{ f=0\} $.

If $f_ j\to f$ locally uniformly holomorphically, the logarithms converge to $\log |f|^2$ in local coefficient $L^1([A])$, whenever the local logarithms are defined for large $j$. In particular

85DED4E0947F 20.5.7
\begin{equation} dd^ c\bigl (\log |f-\varepsilon |^2[A]\bigr ) \longrightarrow dd^ c\bigl (\log |f|^2[A]\bigr ) \quad (\varepsilon \to 0). \end{equation}

For $p=0$ the hypotheses force $f$ to be nonzero at every point of $A$; the Hessian current is zero and the local logarithm convergence is ordinary convergence against finitely many point masses.

Proof. All assertions are local on $X$. Local finiteness of components reduces the proof to one irreducible component $A$; all sums below are finite over each compact target neighborhood. The identity principle on the connected $A_{\mathrm{reg}}$ shows that $f$ is nontrivial on every local branch: vanishing on one branch would force vanishing on a nonempty regular open subset of $A$, hence on $A$. In particular its zero set is pure of dimension $p-1$ when nonempty, by purity of a nontrivial holomorphic zero set. We may assume $p\geq 1$, the point-mass case being as stated.

Take the proper bimeromorphic resolution $\pi :M\to A\subset X$ of the stated resolution-of-singularities input. The composition to $X$ is proper because $A$ is closed. Each component of $M$ has dimension $p$ and meets the isomorphism locus; the exceptional analytic set $E=\pi ^{-1}(A_{\mathrm{sing}})$ is proper on every such component by nowhere-dense inverse images under bimeromorphic maps. It has smooth volume zero by the holomorphic zero-set lemma. Off $E$ the map is a biholomorphism onto $A_{\mathrm{reg}}$. For a compact target set $K$, $\pi ^{-1}(K)$ is compact, and $\pi ^*\beta ^ p$ is bounded by a smooth volume form times a constant there, where $\beta $ is any smooth positive ambient Hermitian form. Thus the pushforward of the integration current of $M$ has locally finite mass, equals $[A]$ off $A_{\mathrm{sing}}$, and gives that singular set zero mass because $E$ is null. Hence

\[ \pi _*[M]=[A]. \]

This also directly checks the coefficient convention at singularities.

Put $g=f\circ \pi $. It is nontrivial on every component of $M$. By the distributional Hessian of a holomorphic logarithm, $\log |g|^2$ is locally integrable for smooth volume. The preceding compact pullback bound therefore proves

\[ \int _ K|\log |f|^2|\, [A]\wedge \beta ^ p =\int _{\pi ^{-1}(K)}|\log |g|^2|\, \pi ^*\beta ^ p<\infty . \]

The equality uses change of variables off the respective null sets; there is no product of an infinite value with a positive atom there. Trace domination supplies coefficient integrability, and the same argument for all test forms gives the projection identity

E212244D4A1F 20.5.8
\begin{equation} \log |f|^2[A]=\pi _*\bigl (\log |g|^2[M]\bigr ). \end{equation}

The ordinary manifold identity of the normalized Poincaré–Lelong formula and commutation of proper pushforward with $dd^ c$ now give

74331B0B45F1 20.5.9
\begin{equation} dd^ c\bigl (\log |f|^2[A]\bigr ) =\pi _*[\operatorname {Div}(g)] =\sum _ D e_ D\, \pi _*[D]. \end{equation}

Here the $D$ are the irreducible prime hypersurfaces of the divisor on the smooth $M$ and $e_ D\geq 1$ are their transverse vanishing orders. Only finitely many $D$ meet the compact preimage of a compact target neighborhood, by local finiteness of analytic components. Apply lemma 20.5.3 to each of them. A component with lower-dimensional image contributes zero; every other contributes a positive integer sheet number times its image. Those images are irreducible analytic subsets of dimension $p-1$ in $A\cap \{ f=0\} $, so are its irreducible components.

Every component $C_ k$ of that intersection receives a contribution. The resolution is surjective, since its proper image contains the dense regular locus. Therefore $\pi (\{ g=0\} )=\{ f=0\} \cap A$. Near a compact neighborhood of any point of $C_ k$, the finitely many images of the relevant $D$ cover that zero set. An irreducible local branch of $C_ k$ cannot be covered by finitely many analytic sets of smaller dimension, by purity and dimension of irreducible analytic sets and the local branch decomposition. At least one $D$ consequently has image of dimension $p-1$ containing an open subset of that branch, hence is the whole global component containing it. This proves $m_ k>0$; the formula gives explicitly

\[ m_ k=\sum _{D:\, \pi (D)=C_ k}e_ D\deg (\pi |_ D)\in \mathbb Z_{>0}. \]

The current on the left defines these coefficients uniquely by restriction to regular points of $C_ k$ outside the other components, so the local formulas agree on overlaps and do not depend on the chosen resolution.

Finally put $g_ j=f_ j\circ \pi $. Properness gives local uniform convergence on $M$, and lemma 20.5.2 gives smooth-volume $L^1$ convergence on every compact preimage. The compact pullback bound gives

\[ \int _ K\bigl |\log |f_ j|^2-\log |f|^2\bigr |\, [A]\wedge \beta ^ p \leq C_ K\int _ L\bigl |\log |g_ j|^2-\log |g|^2\bigr |\, dV_ M \longrightarrow 0, \]

where $L$ is a fixed compact neighborhood of $\pi ^{-1}(K)$. This proves coefficient $L^1$ convergence and therefore convergence of the Hessian currents. For shifts, the local logarithms are defined for all small $\varepsilon $: on each of the finitely many relevant components choose one point where $f\ne 0$ and exclude their positive minimum absolute value. No monotone-limit theorem is being used. The integer conclusion was already proved in (20.5.9), independently of this limit. $\square$
