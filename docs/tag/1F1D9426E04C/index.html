9.51.7 Acyclic patches and cohomology topologies1F1D9426E04C

We use the analytic ambient-neighborhood theorem 9.49.8 in its $q=1$ case, with the strict-PSH extension and embedding invariance proved with it. If $U$ is a strongly $1$-complete open subset of a distinguished patch $A\subset \Omega $, first replace $\Omega $ by an ambient open set meeting $A$ precisely in $U$. The set $U$ is then closed analytic in that ambient space. The theorem supplies a strongly $1$-complete ambient open $V$ meeting $A$ precisely in $U$. The distinguished free resolution restricts to $V$, so lemma 9.51.3 gives $H^ k(U,\mathcal F)=0$ for $k\geq 1$ and an open free section surjection. The same holds if $U$ is only strongly $1$-convex in an embedded patch: add $C|z|^2$ to its exhaustion. On the exceptional compact set finitely many ambient smooth extensions have bounded negative Hessian, which the Euclidean term dominates for one sufficiently large $C$. Outside that set it preserves strict positivity. Its nonnegativity preserves the exhaustion property.

Small distinguished polydisc patches are strongly $1$-complete, with the sum of the usual reciprocal boundary-distance exhaustions. Finite intersections of strongly $1$-complete opens are strongly $1$-complete: make each exhaustion nonnegative and add them on the intersection. A bounded sublevel lies in the compact sublevel of each summand, hence is compact in the intersection. Positivity follows by local ambient extension in a common embedding. An intersection of distinguished patches lies in the first such patch, so the preceding ambient argument makes it acyclic for $\mathcal F$. We can consequently choose countable acyclic covers and a countable acyclic basis, with common acyclic refinements.

Give $H^ k(U,\mathcal F)$ the quotient topology of its cycle space in the Fréchet Čech complex of any such cover. The canonical acyclic-cover comparison theorem 10.43.16 identifies this with sheaf cohomology. Refinement maps are continuous cochain maps and induce cohomology bijections. By theorem 2.12.5, their induced maps are open, even if these quotient spaces are not Hausdorff. The topology is therefore independent of the cover. Restrictions and coherent morphisms act continuously: refine by an acyclic basis on which their cochain maps are the already continuous section restrictions or morphisms.

The same topology is obtained from any countable acyclic cover: refine it by the acyclic basis, use the algebraic acyclic-cover comparison, and apply the same cohomological openness. In particular the closed-embedding identification $H^ k(V,i_*\mathcal F)=H^ k(A\cap V,\mathcal F)$ is topological. Choose an acyclic cover for $i_*\mathcal F$ on $V$. Its intersections with $A$ are acyclic by the flabby direct-image comparison above. The two Čech complexes have identical section spaces and differentials; their section topologies coincide by the intrinsic germ construction. Thus their cohomology identification preserves the specified quotient topologies.

For a holomorphic vector bundle $E$ on a complex manifold, this topology agrees with the smooth Dolbeault quotient topology. Use the double complex of smooth $E$-valued $(0,j)$-forms on the intersections of an acyclic basis. Its terms, with their compact $C^\infty $ seminorms and countable products, are Fréchet. The augmented horizontal rows are exact by the checked partition-of-unity Čech contraction; that contraction is continuous because only finitely many partition supports meet each fixed compact set. The augmented vertical columns are exact by the full bundle Dolbeault comparison and the just proved bundle vanishing on the strongly $1$-complete intersections. The finite-diagonal comparison lemma therefore makes the two augmentations, from the global Dolbeault complex and from the holomorphic Čech complex, cohomology bijections. They are continuous: restrictions are continuous, and holomorphic-to-smooth inclusion is continuous by local Cauchy estimates. Cohomological openness makes both maps topological isomorphisms on quotient cohomology, including non-Hausdorff cases.

Lemma 9.51.8.403B48857D12 In a distinguished patch $A\subset \Omega $, let $U'\subset U$ be $q$-Runge complete: each compact $L\subset U'$ lies in $\{ \psi <b\} \Subset U'$ for a smooth strongly $q$-convex exhaustion $\psi $ of $U$. Then $H^{q-1}(U,\mathcal F)\to H^{q-1}(U',\mathcal F)$ has dense image, with the quotient cohomology topologies.

Proof. Apply corollary 9.49.10, including its Runge clause, to $U$ after restricting the ambient open set to meet $A$ exactly in $U$. For each compact $L\subset U'$ it supplies a strongly $q$-complete ambient $V$, with $A\cap V=U$, and an exhaustion sublevel $V_ b$ such that $L\subset U_ b=A\cap V_ b\Subset U'$. Free presentation and dimension shifting give $H^ q(V,Z_0)=H^ q(V_ b,Z_0)=0$. Thus the free-to-coherent maps in degree $q-1$ are surjective and continuous. The bundle approximation theorem and topological Dolbeault comparison make restriction $H^{q-1}(V,\mathcal O^{r_0})\to H^{q-1}(V_ b,\mathcal O^{r_0})$ dense. Its commuting square with the two presentation maps proves density from $U$ to $U_ b$.

Choose such sets $U_{b_\nu }$ increasingly, with compact closures nested in the next one and exhausting $U'$: at each step include the previous compact closure and the next compact of a fixed exhaustion in $L$. Use one countable acyclic basis $\mathcal W$ of relatively compact patches on $U$. Its subfamilies with closures in $U_{b_\nu }$ give Fréchet Čech complexes $E_\nu $ with surjective coordinate projections. Their inverse limit is the complex on the subfamily with closures in $U'$. The map from $F=\check C^\bullet (\mathcal W,\mathcal F)$ to this limit is degreewise surjective: it forgets components, and every remaining cochain extends by zero on omitted tuples. The just proved cohomology density holds at every stage. Apply theorem 2.14.6(4). This proves density on $U'$ itself, without assuming closed boundary spaces or commuting completion with cohomology. $\square$

Lemma 9.51.9.600DE1CB7CE6 For arbitrary open $G,U$ in an analytic space, their ordinary sheaf Mayer–Vietoris sequence has continuous maps for these cohomology topologies. Whenever its cohomology difference map is surjective, that map is open, including on non-Hausdorff quotient cohomology.

Proof. Take a countable distinguished acyclic basis $\mathcal W$ on $G\cup U$, retaining members contained in $G$ or $U$. Let $\mathcal W_ G,\mathcal W_ U$ be the subfamilies contained in $G,U$, and $\mathcal W_0=\mathcal W_ G\cap \mathcal W_ U$. They are acyclic covers of $G,U,G\cap U$. Put $A=\check C^\bullet (\mathcal W_ G,\mathcal F)\oplus \check C^\bullet (\mathcal W_ U,\mathcal F)$, $B=\check C^\bullet (\mathcal W_0,\mathcal F)$, and let $r:A\to B$ be restriction difference. Form the complex

\[ K^ n=A^ n\oplus B^{n-1},\qquad D(a,b)=(da,ra-db),\qquad B^{-1}=0. \]

The map $\check C^\bullet (\mathcal W,\mathcal F)\to K$ given by the two restrictions and zero in $B^{n-1}$ is continuous and induces the ordinary Mayer–Vietoris identification $H^*(K)=H^*(G\cup U,\mathcal F)$. Here is an explicit verification of this identification. Resolve $\mathcal F$ by its checked Godement resolution $\mathcal I^\bullet $. On each of the three acyclic covers, the augmented resolution columns are exact, as in the acyclic-cover comparison. Thus the double total with entries $K^ n(\mathcal I^ j)$ computes $H^*(K(\mathcal F))$. For fixed $j$, flabby Cech acyclicity computes the row's cohomology as that of

\[ \mathcal I^ j(G)\oplus \mathcal I^ j(U) \longrightarrow \mathcal I^ j(G\cap U). \]

The last map is onto by flabbiness and its kernel is $\mathcal I^ j(G\cup U)$ by gluing. Hence these rows are exact after that augmentation. The actual finite-diagonal comparison lemma identifies the same total with $\mathcal I^\bullet (G\cup U)$. The continuous map above, used also on Godement terms, agrees with the identity on that augmented resolution. It therefore induces this canonical cohomology bijection. Both source and target are Fréchet complexes, so cohomological openness makes it a topological identification.

The degreewise strict sequence $0\to B[-1]\to K\to A\to 0$ gives the continuous cohomology connecting maps: pull back the last complex's closed cycle space, use the open surjection from that Fréchet preimage, and descend the lifted differential. This is the continuous long-exact construction already proved in section 9.41. The lift formula and its Godement comparison give the usual sheaf Mayer–Vietoris maps, up to the common choice of difference sign. The difference map is induced by the continuous cochain map $r:A\to B$; if it is onto on cohomology, theorem 2.12.5 makes it open. $\square$

Theorem 9.51.10 (Compact coherent finiteness).7C6C814BF9A5 For a coherent sheaf $\mathcal F$ on a compact analytic space, every $H^ k(X,\mathcal F)$ is Hausdorff and finite dimensional.

Proof. Choose finitely many distinguished polydisc patches $U_ a$ and smaller concentric patches $U'_ a\Subset U_ a$ still covering $X$. Both covers and their finite intersections are acyclic, as proved above. For every tuple $I$, $U'_ I\Subset U_ I$: its closure is contained in the compact intersection of the individual smaller closures inside $U_ I$. The restriction $\mathcal F(U_ I)\to \mathcal F(U'_ I)$ is compact by corollary 9.51.6. In each fixed degree there are only finitely many tuples, so the Čech cochain restriction is a compact map on one zero neighborhood. It induces a cohomology bijection by the acyclic-cover comparison. The full Fréchet-complex compact cohomology criterion theorem 2.12.5 gives the claim in each degree. Nilpotents were retained throughout the section constructions and the sheaf exactness arguments. $\square$

Lemma 9.51.11.7305161C3E98 Let $q\geq 1$, let $X$ have a smooth exhaustion $\psi $ strongly $q$-convex outside a compact set $K$, and choose $c<d$ with $K\subset X_ c$. There is a finite chain

\[ X_ c=G_0\subset G_1\subset \cdots \subset G_ s=X_ d \]

of sublevel sets of smooth exhaustions strongly $q$-convex outside $K$, where $G_{j+1}=G_ j\cup U_ j$. The sets $U_ j$ and $G_ j\cap U_ j$ lie in one distinguished patch, are strongly $q$-complete, and the intersection is $q$-Runge complete in $U_ j$. They have zero cohomology in degrees $k\geq q$, and restriction

\[ H^{q-1}(U_ j,\mathcal F)\longrightarrow H^{q-1}(G_ j\cap U_ j,\mathcal F) \]

has dense image for their quotient cohomology topologies.

Proof. If the compact band $\{ c\leq \psi \leq d\} $ is empty, $X_ c=X_ d$ and no bump is needed. Otherwise cover it by finitely many distinguished embedded ball patches $A_ j$ disjoint from $K$, on which $\psi $ has a strongly $q$-convex ambient extension. Take smooth $\theta _ j\geq 0$ supported compactly there, with $\sum \theta _ j\leq 1$ everywhere and sum one on the band. Choose these by the nonnegative ambient partition construction in lemma 9.49.1. For small $\epsilon _0>0$ all the functions

\[ \psi _ j=\psi -\epsilon \sum _{i<j}\theta _ i, \qquad 0<\epsilon \leq \epsilon _0, \]

remain strongly $q$-convex outside $K$ and exhaustive. This is a finite compact $C^2$ perturbation. More explicitly, cover the compact supports in their ambient charts by finitely many smaller pieces where the original Hessian is bounded below positively on a fixed subspace of codimension at most $q-1$. Bounded second derivatives of all the finitely many bump extensions allow one $\epsilon _0$ preserving positivity on these same subspaces for every $\psi _ j$, and hence every convex combination. Nonnegative ambient extensions of the bumps are supplied by the same smooth-tools lemma. On each ball use these same extensions for every $\widetilde\psi _ j$, so $\widetilde\psi _{j+1}\leq \widetilde\psi _ j$. Choose ball closures inside their extension neighborhoods; the functions are unchanged off a fixed compact set.

For $0<\epsilon \leq \min (\epsilon _0,d-c)$ set $G_ j=\{ \psi _ j<c\} $. Then $G_ s=X_{c+\epsilon }$: the sum is one on the band and at most one elsewhere. The change is supported in $A_ j$, so $U_ j=G_{j+1}\cap A_ j$ works. In its ambient ball the two analytic intersections are those with

\[ V_ j=\{ \widetilde\psi _{j+1}<c\} \cap B(z_ j,r_ j), \qquad V'_ j=\{ \widetilde\psi _ j<c\} \cap B(z_ j,r_ j). \]

Their strongly $q$-convex exhaustions are respectively

\[ \varphi _ j=\frac1{c-\widetilde\psi _{j+1}} +\frac1{r_ j^2-|z-z_ j|^2},\qquad \varphi '_ j=\frac1{c-\widetilde\psi _ j} +\frac1{r_ j^2-|z-z_ j|^2}. \]

Their positive Hessian subspaces are the preserved ones above; the extra squared-gradient terms and ball term are semipositive. They tend to infinity at every domain boundary. The distinguished free resolution is defined on a larger ambient neighborhood, so the preceding coherent vanishing corollary applies to both intersections.

The ambient inclusion $V'_ j\subset V_ j$ is $q$-Runge complete. For $L\Subset V'_ j$ choose $\max _ L\widetilde\psi _ j<b<c$. For small $\eta >0$, $\widetilde\psi _ j+\eta \varphi _ j$ is an exhaustion of $V_ j$ with

\[ L\subset \{ \widetilde\psi _ j+\eta \varphi _ j<b\} \Subset V'_ j. \]

The function $\widetilde\psi _ j$ is bounded below on the ball, and $\varphi _ j\geq 0$. Its Hessian is positive on the same preserved subspace, so it is strongly $q$-convex. Restriction of these ambient exhaustions to the closed analytic intersections proves the corresponding strong $q$-completeness and $q$-Runge completeness there. The full bundle approximation theorem 9.50.8 shows that $H^{q-1}(V_ j,\mathcal O^{r_0})\to H^{q-1}(V'_ j,\mathcal O^{r_0})$ has dense image, using the topological Dolbeault comparison proved above. For $q=1$ the direct weighted scalar proof in lemma 9.51.2 already gives this density. In the free presentations, $H^ q(V_ j,Z_0)=H^ q(V'_ j,Z_0)=0$ by the same finite resolution/dimension-shift argument. Hence the horizontal maps from these free cohomology groups onto $H^{q-1}(U_ j,\mathcal F)$ and $H^{q-1}(G_ j\cap U_ j,\mathcal F)$ are surjective and continuous. Their commuting restriction square transfers the density to the stated coherent cohomology map.

The same finite patches and perturbation bounds work for every intermediate level between $c$ and $d$. Choose a finite subdivision of increments at most $\epsilon _0$ and repeat to reach $d$. $\square$

Proposition 9.51.12.C3662AF30135 In the preceding setting, all $H^ k(X_ c,\mathcal F)$ are Hausdorff and finite dimensional for $k\geq q$ when $K\subset X_ c$. For $K\subset X_ c\subset X_ d$, restriction is a topological isomorphism in degrees $k\geq q$ and has dense image in degree $q-1$.

Proof. Mayer–Vietoris for one bump and local vanishing first give surjectivity in degrees $k\geq q$, and bijectivity for $k>q$. Thus restriction from a larger exhaustion sublevel onto a smaller one is surjective in those degrees.

We prove Hausdorffness before using density in the connecting map. Fix $c<d$ with $K\subset X_ c$. Take an acyclic cover $\mathcal V$ of $X_ d$ and finitely many smaller opens $V'_ a\Subset V_ a$ covering $\overline{X_ c}$. Choose an acyclic cover $\mathcal W$ of $X_ c$ refining $(V'_ a\cap X_ c)$. Its continuous cochain restriction map

\[ \check C^\bullet (\mathcal V,\mathcal F) \longrightarrow \check C^\bullet (\mathcal W,\mathcal F) \]

factors, in each degree, through finitely many compact maps $\mathcal F(V_ I)\to \mathcal F(V'_ I)$, followed by continuous restrictions to the possibly countable $\mathcal W$-product. Here $V'_ I\Subset V_ I$ because the individual smaller closures are compact inside their larger opens. This map is compact on one source neighborhood in each degree and induces the already surjective restriction in degrees $k\geq q$. The Fréchet-complex compact criterion proves the claimed Hausdorff finite-dimensional sublevel cohomology. The same reasoning applies to every compactly perturbed exhaustion $\psi _ j$ used in the bumps.

For one bump the connecting map $H^{q-1}(G_ j\cap U_ j,\mathcal F)\to H^ q(G_{j+1},\mathcal F)$ is continuous by lemma 9.51.9. It vanishes on the dense image of $H^{q-1}(U_ j,\mathcal F)$ and its target has just been proved Hausdorff. Thus it is zero. This gives injectivity in degree $q$, and a surjective cohomology difference map

\[ H^{q-1}(G_ j,\mathcal F)\oplus H^{q-1}(U_ j,\mathcal F) \longrightarrow H^{q-1}(G_ j\cap U_ j,\mathcal F). \]

It is open by the topological Mayer–Vietoris lemma. Given a class $g$ in the first factor, approximate its restriction by restrictions of classes $u_\nu $ from $U_ j$. Their differences $w_\nu \to 0$ have lifts $(g'_\nu ,u'_\nu )\to 0$ under that open map. Indeed quotient cohomologies have a countable neighborhood basis, inherited from their Fréchet cycle spaces through the open quotient; choose successively smaller source neighborhoods and successively later target terms in their images. The pair $(g-g'_\nu ,u_\nu -u'_\nu )$ is in the difference map's kernel. Mayer–Vietoris exactness gives a class on $G_{j+1}$ restricting to this pair, whose first restriction approaches $g$. This proves density in degree $q-1$. Finite composition proves density for $X_ d\to X_ c$; the positive-degree bijective maps are topological isomorphisms by cohomological openness. $\square$

Theorem 9.51.13.5F4DB7E4B26B Let $q\geq 1$ and let $X$ be a countable-basis analytic space, possibly nonreduced, with a smooth strongly $q$-convex exhaustion outside a compact set $K$. Then $H^ k(X,\mathcal F)$ is Hausdorff and finite dimensional for every coherent $\mathcal F$ and $k\geq q$. If $K=\varnothing $, these groups are zero. If $U\subset X$ is $q$-Runge, meaning that every compact $L\subset U$ lies in a sublevel $X_ b\Subset U$ of some smooth exhaustion strongly $q$-convex on $X\setminus \overline{X_ b}$, then restriction is a topological isomorphism for $k\geq q$ and has dense image for $k=q-1$. For $q=1$ and $K=\varnothing $, finitely many global sections generate $\mathcal F$ near any prescribed point.

Proof. Fix $c$ with $K\subset X_ c$, and choose a countable acyclic basis $\mathcal W$ of relatively compact distinguished patches. Let $\mathcal W_\nu $ consist of members whose closures lie in $X_{c+\nu }$. These cover that sublevel. Their Čech complexes $E_\nu $ have degreewise surjective transition projections and their inverse limit is the Čech complex of $\mathcal W$: every finite tuple of relatively compact patches occurs at some stage. Their cohomology restrictions are bijective in degrees $k\geq q$ and dense in degree $q-1$, by the sublevel proposition. The complete abstract Mittag–Leffler theorem 2.14.6(1),(3) makes $H^ k(X,\mathcal F)\to H^ k(X_ c,\mathcal F)$ bijective for $k\geq q$. It is topological by cohomological openness, so gives the first assertion.

The same inverse-limit argument, now using part (2) of the abstract theorem, shows that restriction $H^{q-1}(X,\mathcal F)\to H^{q-1}(X_ c,\mathcal F)$ has dense image whenever $K\subset X_ c$.

Let $U$ be $q$-Runge and choose a compact $L\subset U$. By its definition choose an exhaustion $\psi $ and $b$ with $L\subset X_ b\Subset U$ and strong $q$-convexity outside $\overline{X_ b}$. Compactness and properness allow $c>b$ with $X_ c\Subset U$. The preceding conclusion applies with $K=\overline{X_ b}$ and gives an isomorphism $H^ k(X,\mathcal F)\to H^ k(X_ c,\mathcal F)$ for $k\geq q$, and density in degree $q-1$. Recursively include each previous compact closure and the next compact of an exhaustion of $U$ in $L$. This produces nested $V_\nu \Subset V_{\nu +1}$ of the form $X_ c$, for possibly different exhaustions, with union $U$. Restrictions $H^ k(V_{\nu +1},\mathcal F)\to H^ k(V_\nu ,\mathcal F)$ are isomorphisms for $k\geq q$: the two maps from $H^ k(X,\mathcal F)$ are isomorphisms and commute. In degree $q-1$ these restrictions are dense, because their images contain the dense image from $X$. Use again subfamilies of one countable relatively compact acyclic basis of $X$, this time with closures in $V_\nu $. Their complexes have surjective coordinate transitions and inverse limit computing cohomology on $U$. Parts (1),(3) of the abstract theorem identify $H^ k(U,\mathcal F)$ with $H^ k(V_0,\mathcal F)$ for $k\geq q$. The restriction from $X$ is therefore an isomorphism, and it is topological by openness. For degree $q-1$, the complex on the full basis of $X$ projects degreewise surjectively onto that on $U$; all stage cohomology maps have dense image. Part (4) proves the asserted density on $U$.

If $K=\varnothing $, shift the exhaustion to be nonnegative. Finite bumping from the empty sublevel $X_{-1}$ shows that every $H^ k(X_ c,\mathcal F)$ with $k\geq q$ is zero. The preceding limit isomorphism therefore proves full strongly $q$-complete vanishing. There is no degree-zero vanishing assertion.

For generation take $q=1$ and $K=\varnothing $. The ideal $\mathcal I_ x$ of a point is coherent: locally it is generated by the ambient coordinate differences, acting on $\mathcal O_\Omega /\mathcal J$; off the point it is the whole structure sheaf. Its product $\mathcal I_ x\mathcal F$ is a coherent image of a finite local presentation. Its quotient is the skyscraper with finite stalk $\mathcal F_ x/\mathfrak m_ x\mathcal F_ x$. The vanishing already proved gives a surjection

\[ \mathcal F(X)\longrightarrow \mathcal F_ x/\mathfrak m_ x\mathcal F_ x. \]

Lift a finite vector-space basis by global sections. They generate $\mathcal F_ x$ by Nakayama's finite-module argument: the remaining finite quotient $Q$ satisfies $Q=\mathfrak m_ xQ$; write a generator vector $v=Av$ with entries of $A$ in $\mathfrak m_ x$, then $\det (I-A)v=0$. The determinant is a unit, so $Q=0$. The coherent cokernel has zero stalk at $x$, hence vanishes near $x$: finitely many local generators have zero germs and vanish on a common neighborhood. This proves generation without having assumed it in any local resolution or vanishing step. $\square$

Corollary 9.51.14.19FC1BA2D71A Let $A\subset \Omega \subset \mathbb C^ N$ be any analytic patch, $\mathcal F$ coherent on $A$, and $U\subset A$ strongly $q$-convex. Then $H^ k(U,\mathcal F)=0$ for $k\geq q$. Moreover, for any coherent presentation $\mathcal O_ V^ r\to i_*\mathcal F\to 0$ on a strongly $1$-complete ambient $V$, the section map $\mathcal O(V)^ r\to \mathcal F(A\cap V)$ is an open surjection, and its quotient topology is the intrinsic one.

Proof. The Euclidean compact-set upgrade already proved makes $U$ strongly $q$-complete. Apply the full coherent vanishing theorem, whose proof used sufficiently long resolutions only on smaller local patches available at every point. It places no resolution-length condition on $U$ or its given patch. For the second assertion the presentation kernel is coherent; its first cohomology on $V$ is zero by that theorem with $q=1$. Sheaf exactness gives section surjectivity. The section map is continuous between Fréchet spaces, so is open and has the asserted quotient topology. Thus the shorter local resolutions in a previously fixed distinguished patch are covered as well; the deliberately larger finite bound used in the foundational argument causes no loss of cases. $\square$
