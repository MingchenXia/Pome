5.62.2 Currents on CR submanifolds4E08EE0E7CED

For a real $C^1$ submanifold $M$ of a complex manifold put $H_ x=T_ xM\cap JT_ xM$ and $h(x)=\dim _\mathbb C H_ x$. It is a CR submanifold of CR dimension $p$ when $h(x)=p$ everywhere. A normal current and its exterior derivative have measure coefficients; we use their action on continuous forms and the $C^1$ calculus of lemma 5.56.1. A current of bidimension $(p,p)$ has bidegree $(n-p,n-p)$.

Theorem 5.62.3.88C601DA5D62 Let $T$ be normal of bidimension $(p,p)$ and let $\operatorname {supp}T\subset M$, where $M$ is a real $C^1$ submanifold. Then

\[ \operatorname {supp}T\subset \{ x\in M:h(x)\geq p\} . \]

In particular $T=0$ when every $h(x)<p$; no constant-rank or positivity assumption is needed. On any open part of $M$ where $h(x)=p$, the dimensional coefficient vectors of $T$ are tangent to $H$, in its real dimension $2p$.

Proof. Locally let $g_1,\ldots ,g_ m$ be real $C^1$ defining functions with independent real differentials. Multiplication of their values with the coefficient measures gives $g_ jT=g_ jdT=0$, since both supports lie in $M$. The $C^1$ normal product rule gives $dg_ j\wedge T=0$. As $T$ has a pure bidegree, its distinct type components yield separately

\[ \partial g_ j\wedge T=\bar\partial g_ j\wedge T=0. \]

These are products of continuous forms with measures, not derivatives of a merely continuous moving frame.

The complex rank of the forms $\partial g_ j$ at $x$ is $n-h(x)$. Indeed their common complex kernel identifies with $H_ x$: a real vector $v$ gives $(v-iJv)/2$ in that kernel exactly when $dg_ j(v)=dg_ j(Jv)=0$ for all $j$. If $h(x)<p$, choose $n-p+1$ independent such forms at $x$. Their minor stays nonzero near $x$, so complete them to a continuous complex coframe there. In the coefficient expansion of a current of bidegree $(n-p,n-p)$, wedging with each of these $n-p+1$ forms forces every coefficient to vanish: an index of length $n-p$ cannot contain all of them. Continuous invertible changes of coframe are legitimate for order-zero measures. Thus $T$ vanishes near $x$, proving the support assertion also for varying $h$.

If $h=p$ in the neighborhood, choose $n-p$ independent annihilating forms $\zeta _1,\ldots ,\zeta _{n-p}$. The same argument in both types leaves only a scalar measure coefficient times $\zeta _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \zeta _{n-p}\wedge \bar\zeta _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \bar\zeta _{n-p}$. Equivalently the current evaluates only the restriction of a $2p$-test form to $H$; its dimensional coefficient vector is in $\Lambda ^{2p}_{\mathbb R}H\otimes \mathbb C$. This is the claimed tangential assertion. The full conjugate coframe factor is essential for the degrees to match. $\square$

Lemma 5.62.4.403785A5B419 Let $D=I_1\times \cdots \times I_ a$ be a real open box and $B\subset \mathbb R^ b$ open. If a complex Radon measure $\nu $ on $D\times B$ satisfies $\partial _{s_ j}\nu =0$ for every $1\leq j\leq a$, there is a unique complex Radon measure $\mu $ on $B$ such that $\nu =\mathcal L^ a|_ D\otimes \mu $. For $a=0$ this means $\mu =\nu $ on $B$. All complex measures here have locally finite total variation.

Proof. Take nonnegative $\kappa _ j\in C_ c^\infty (I_ j)$ with integral one and put $\kappa =\prod _ j\kappa _ j$. Define

\[ \mu (\psi )=\int \kappa (s)\psi (t)\, d\nu (s,t). \]

On each compact subset of $B$ this is a finite-variation measure, namely the pushforward of $\kappa \nu $. These local measures agree and define the asserted complex Radon measure.

For a smooth compactly supported $f(s,t)$, subtract $\kappa (s)\int _ D f(u,t)du$, so its integral in $s$ is zero. Such a remainder $f_0$ is a sum $\sum _ j\partial _{s_ j}F_ j$ with all $F_ j$ smooth compactly supported in $D\times B$. Explicitly put

\[ f_ j(s_{j+1},\ldots ,s_ a,t) =\int _{I_ j}f_{j-1}(u,s_{j+1},\ldots ,s_ a,t)du \]

and

\[ F_ j(s,t)=\Bigl (\prod _{l<j}\kappa _ l(s_ l)\Bigr ) \int _{a_ j}^{s_ j} \bigl (f_{j-1}(u,s_{j+1},\ldots ,s_ a,t) -\kappa _ j(u)f_ j(s_{j+1},\ldots ,s_ a,t)\bigr )du. \]

Choose $a_ j\in I_ j$ to the left of the compact supports involved. Each integrand has zero full integral in $u$, so its antiderivative vanishes near both ends of $I_ j$. Telescoping the derivatives gives $f_0-\kappa f_ a=\sum _ j\partial _{s_ j}F_ j$, and $f_ a=0$ by the zero-integral condition. This proves the compact divergence decomposition, with smooth dependence on $t$. The derivative hypotheses annihilate that remainder. Therefore

\[ \nu (f)=\int _ B\left(\int _ D f(s,t)ds\right)d\mu (t). \]

Equality on smooth tests gives equality of the locally finite measures by uniform smooth approximation on buffered compact sets. The normalization by $\kappa $ also proves uniqueness. $\square$

Theorem 5.62.5.2CCAC11D6524 Let $M$ be a closed embedded real $C^1$ CR submanifold of a complex manifold $X$, of CR dimension $p$. Suppose $\sigma :M\to Y$ is a surjective $C^1$ submersion onto a Hausdorff countable-basis real $C^1$ manifold, with connected fibers $F_ y$ satisfying $TF_ y=H$. Then each $F_ y$ is a closed complex $p$-dimensional submanifold of $X$ and every closed current $T$ of order zero, bidimension $(p,p)$ and support in $M$ has a unique representation

41AB7C8419D3 5.62.6
\begin{equation} T=\int _ Y[F_ y]\, d\mu (y) \end{equation}

by a complex Radon measure $\mu $ on $Y$. Conversely every such measure defines a closed order-zero current by this formula. Its support is $\sigma ^{-1}(\operatorname {supp}\mu )$ as a closed subset of $X$. It is positive if and only if $\mu $ is positive; in that case it is strongly positive as well.

Proof. The fibers are embedded $C^1$ submanifolds with complex tangent spaces, hence complex by holomorphic regularity of complex tangent submanifolds. They are closed in $X$ because $M$ is closed and $\sigma $ is continuous. Choose submersion product charts $F:D\times B\to M$, with real leaf coordinates $s\in D$ and base coordinates $t\in B\subset Y$. Orient each leaf-coordinate box by the complex orientation; this is a consistent choice after shrinking a connected box.

First check that the proposed integral defines a genuine current. For a compactly supported continuous $2p$-form $\alpha $ on $X$, the compact set $M\cap \operatorname {supp}\alpha $ is covered by finitely many such buffered charts. Insert a finite $C^1$ partition of unity there. In each chart the integrand is a continuous coefficient with compact support in $D\times B$, and integration in $s$ gives a continuous function of $t$ with compact support. The chart's first derivatives are bounded on this compact set, so its absolute fiber integral is at most a fixed constant times $\| \alpha \| _\infty $. Thus $y\mapsto \int _{F_ y}\alpha $ is continuous and compactly supported in $Y$, with this uniform bound. Integration against a locally finite $\mu $ is well defined and satisfies a local order-zero estimate. Each fiber current is closed by proposition 5.55.1; the same finite bounds allow this identity to pass through the measure integral. The integral has pure bidimension $(p,p)$ because the fibers are complex; hence it has all the asserted current properties.

For the converse let $j:M\hookrightarrow X$. By theorem 5.56.3, write $T=j_*Q$ for a unique normal dimensional $2p$-current on $M$. Boundary commutation and injectivity of inclusion pushforward in that theorem show that $Q$ is closed as well. The rank theorem above makes its coefficient vector tangent to $H$. In a foliation chart only the purely vertical coefficient remains, so

\[ Q\left(f(s,t)\, ds_1\wedge \cdots \wedge ds_{2p} +\hbox{terms with a }dt\right)=\int f\, d\nu \]

for a complex coefficient measure $\nu $ on $D\times B$. The intrinsic $C^1$ normal-current calculus permits computation of its boundary against smooth Euclidean tests in these coordinates. Testing with forms missing the factor $ds_ j$ shows $\partial _{s_ j}\nu =0$. The preceding measure lemma gives $\nu =ds\otimes \mu _ B$. Consequently

\[ T|_\Omega =\int _ B[F_ y\cap \Omega ]\, d\mu _ B(y) \]

in this box, for an actual transverse complex Radon measure. No distributional derivative of a continuous coframe is taken.

We justify global gluing, including boxes that meet the same fiber in different plaques. If two boxes overlap at a point, a smaller product box in their overlap projects onto a neighborhood of its base point. A continuous leaf top form, supported in that smaller box and with fiber integral one over a still smaller buffered base neighborhood, tests both formulas and shows that their transverse measures agree there. The normalized form can be obtained in its product coordinates from a compact leaf bump of integral one times a base cutoff equal to one on that buffered neighborhood; its coefficients extend continuously to an ambient chart, which is enough for order-zero currents. A change of leaf coordinates preserves this normalization by ordinary oriented $C^1$ change of variables.

Now take any two boxes over a point $y$. Choose points in their plaques. Connectedness of $F_ y$, a manifold, makes it path connected. A compact connecting path is covered by finitely many product boxes with successive overlaps along that path. Every overlap projects onto a neighborhood of $y$; shrink to their finite intersection. The preceding local agreement propagates through this chain. Thus the two transverse measures agree near $y$, even when the original boxes are disjoint. They therefore agree on their entire common base domain and glue to a unique complex Radon measure on $Y$. Surjectivity ensures that these domains cover $Y$. The finite partition argument used to define the integral then proves (5.62.6) globally. The same normalized tests prove its uniqueness.

For positivity choose a Hermitian form $\omega $ on $X$. In a product box its restriction to a leaf satisfies $F_ t^*(\omega ^ p/p!)=J(s,t)ds$, with continuous $J>0$. For a nonnegative compactly supported base function $\psi $, let $\kappa \geq 0$ be a compact leaf bump of integral one. The nonnegative continuous scalar $\kappa (s)\psi (t)/J(s,t)$ extends, after a local $C^1$ graph retraction and cutoff, to a nonnegative compactly supported ambient function. Multiply it by $\omega ^ p/p!$. Positivity of $T$, extended from smooth to these continuous tests by nonnegative smooth approximation, gives $\int \psi \, d\mu _ B\geq 0$. Thus $\mu $ is positive. Conversely, if $\mu $ is positive, every weakly positive $(p,p)$-form restricts nonnegatively to each complex fiber by strong and weak positivity of differential forms. Its integral is nonnegative, which is precisely strong positivity of the current, and implies ordinary positivity.

Finally, if $\mu $ vanishes near a base point, the local formula vanishes on all its plaques. If a base point is in its support, the normalized tests in any arbitrarily small product box over that point detect a nonzero measure pairing. Hence every point of that fiber is in the support of $T$. This proves the exact support formula. For $p=0$ the leaf boxes are points and the same argument is simply transport of a measure; for $p=n$ the base is discrete and it says that the coefficient on each connected complex component is a constant. No rank endpoint is omitted. $\square$

Corollary 5.62.7.F39ED1E5BACF A normal current of bidimension $(p,p)$ supported on an analytic set of dimension less than $p$ is zero, even if it is not closed or positive.

Proof. On the regular locus of the analytic set its CR dimension is less than $p$, so the rank theorem annihilates the current there. Its support is consequently contained in the singular set, whose local dimension is smaller by analytic singular strata and Hausdorff dimension. Repeat on its regular locus. Near each point at most $\dim X+1$ repetitions exhaust the possible dimensions. Normality survives restriction to each open complement, and the support of the derivative is always contained in the support of the current, so every step has the same hypotheses. $\square$

Corollary 5.62.1.D73EBFFE10D2 Let $A$ be an analytic subset of $X$ of dimension at most $p$, and let $A_ j$ be its locally finite $p$-dimensional irreducible components. Every closed complex order-zero current $T$ of bidimension $(p,p)$ with support in $A$ has a unique expression

\[ T=\sum _ j\lambda _ j[A_ j],\qquad \lambda _ j\in \mathbb C. \]

It is positive if and only if all $\lambda _ j\geq 0$, and then it is strongly positive.

Proof. In $X\setminus A_{\rm sing}$ the regular locus is closed. On its components of dimension less than $p$, corollary 5.62.7 makes the current zero. The $p$-dimensional connected components are $A_ j\cap A_{\rm reg}$, by global irreducible analytic components. Apply theorem 5.62.5 with the discrete base of those components. It gives one complex constant $\lambda _ j$ per $p$-dimensional component. The currents $[A_ j]$ are closed positive of order zero by theorem 5.61.2. Their sum is locally finite, so the difference from $T$ is a closed normal current supported on $A_{\rm sing}$, of dimension less than $p$. Corollary 5.62.7 makes it zero. The same local tests on regular points give uniqueness and the coefficient positivity criterion; nonnegative sums of complex integration currents are strongly positive. $\square$
