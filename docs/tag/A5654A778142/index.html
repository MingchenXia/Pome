2.24.1 Magnetic models and Dirichlet spectral localizationA5654A778142

We use the Riemannian volume of a Hermitian metric $\omega $, normalized so that $\omega =(\mathrm i/2)\sum dz_ j\wedge d\bar z_ j$ has the standard Euclidean metric. The curvature of a Hermitian holomorphic line bundle $L$ is its Chern curvature $\Theta _ L$; thus its real first Chern form is $\Omega _ L=\mathrm i\Theta _ L/(2\pi )$. Curvature eigenvalues below refer to $\mathrm i\Theta _ L$ relative to $\omega $.

Lemma 2.24.2.3E8E852DB903 A densely defined, nonnegative closed quadratic form $Q$ on a Hilbert space, whose form-domain unit ball for $Q+\| \cdot \| ^2$ is compact in that space, has an orthonormal eigenbasis with eigenvalues $0\leq e_1\leq e_2\leq \cdots \to \infty $, counted with multiplicity. In finite dimension the list terminates; the zero-dimensional case has an empty list. Its counting function and first Riesz mean are

\[ N_ Q(t)=\# \{ j:e_ j\leq t\} ,\qquad R_ Q(t)=\sum _ j(t-e_ j)_+. \]

The count is the largest dimension of a subspace on which $Q\leq t\| \cdot \| ^2$. Moreover, for a positive trace-class contraction $0\leq \gamma \leq I$ with finite form trace,

89116945DA7D 2.24.3
\begin{equation} \operatorname {Tr}\gamma (t-Q)\leq R_ Q(t), \end{equation}

where the left side means $t\operatorname {Tr}\gamma $ minus its form trace. Equality is obtained by the spectral projection onto $e_ j<t$.

Proof. Hilbert representation for the form inner product gives the bounded inverse $T=(Q+I)^{-1}$; its image is form-norm bounded on bounded sets, so it is compact, positive and self-adjoint. A compact positive self-adjoint operator has the required eigenbasis as follows. Let $\mu =\sup _{\| v\| =1}\langle Tv,v\rangle $. Positivity and Cauchy–Schwarz for the form $\langle T\cdot ,\cdot \rangle $ give $\| Tv\| ^2\leq \mu \langle Tv,v\rangle $, hence $\| T\| =\mu $. For a maximizing unit sequence, $\| (T-\mu I)v_ j\| ^2\leq \mu (\mu -\langle Tv_ j,v_ j\rangle )\to 0$. Compactness of $T$ therefore gives a convergent subsequence of $v_ j$ and a unit eigenvector with eigenvalue $\mu >0$. Repeat on its orthogonal complement. If the resulting positive eigenvalues did not tend to zero, their orthogonal unit eigenvectors would contradict compactness. On the orthogonal complement of all of them the operator has norm zero, by the same maximizing argument. Its kernel is zero, because its range is dense and it is an inverse. Hence these vectors are complete. The eigenvalues of the form are $e_ j=\mu _ j^{-1}-1$. Their expansions follow by the form inner product and completion. The dimension assertion follows by intersecting a subspace with the orthogonal complement of the first $N_ Q(t)$ eigenvectors; conversely those eigenvectors themselves span an admissible space. Finally $w_ j=\langle \gamma e_ j,e_ j\rangle $ lies in $[0,1]$, so $\sum _ j(t-e_ j)w_ j\leq \sum _ j(t-e_ j)_+$. Finite form trace justifies the summation and proves (2.24.3). $\square$

For $b\geq 0$ define a measure on $[0,\infty )$ by

6919A4A3FD8F 2.24.4
\begin{equation} d\mu _ b=\frac b{2\pi }\sum _{p\geq 0}\delta _{(2p+1)b}\quad (b>0), \qquad d\mu _0(t)=\frac{dt}{4\pi }. \end{equation}

For $b=(b_1,\ldots ,b_ n)$ let $\mu _ b$ be the convolution of these $n$ measures, and put

72E6618A3A3C 2.24.5
\begin{equation} \nu _ b(E)=\mu _ b([0,E)),\qquad r_ b(E)=\int (E-t)_+\, d\mu _ b(t). \end{equation}

Both vanish for $E\leq 0$. The open interval in $\nu _ b$ specifies its value at a Landau-level jump; the Riesz mean has no such ambiguity.

Lemma 2.24.6.0F14CB3D46F3 On $\mathbb R^{2n}$ let $H_ b$ be the magnetic connection Laplacian with constant field having pair magnitudes $b_ j$, including zero pairs. Its spectral measure per unit volume is $\mu _ b$. In particular the projection $P_ E=\mathbf1_{(-\infty ,E)}(H_ b)$ and the positive operator $(E-H_ b)_+$ have constant diagonal densities $\nu _ b(E)$ and $r_ b(E)$. Uniformly for all $b_ j\geq 0$,

DD3B970F19C7 2.24.7
\begin{equation} \nu _ b(E)\leq C_ n(E_+)^ n,\qquad r_ b(E)\leq C_ n(E_+)^{n+1}. \end{equation}

The function $(b,E)\mapsto r_ b(E)$ is continuous on $[0,\infty )^ n\times \mathbb R$.

Proof. Here are the full model calculation and its completeness. The one-dimensional oscillator $-\partial _ x^2+b^2x^2$, $b>0$, has the normalized functions

\[ \phi _0=(b/\pi )^{1/4}e^{-bx^2/2},\qquad \phi _ p=(p!)^{-1/2}(a^*)^ p\phi _0, \quad a=\frac{\partial _ x+bx}{\sqrt{2b}},\quad a^*=\frac{-\partial _ x+bx}{\sqrt{2b}}. \]

The identities $[a,a^*]=I$ and $a\phi _0=0$ give orthonormality and eigenvalues $(2p+1)b$. They are complete. Indeed their polynomial parts span all polynomials. If $f\in L^2$ is orthogonal to them, $F(\zeta )=\int f(x)e^{-bx^2/2}e^{\zeta x}\, dx$ is entire by Cauchy–Schwarz and Gaussian domination on compact $\zeta $-sets. Every derivative at zero vanishes. Thus $F=0$, and Fourier uniqueness from Fourier inversion and the Plancherel theorem, applied on the imaginary axis, gives $f e^{-bx^2/2}=0$. Integration by parts gives the oscillator form expansion with these eigenvalues: expand $af$ in the same basis and use $\| f'\| ^2+b^2\| xf\| ^2=2b\| af\| ^2+b\| f\| ^2$. Cutoff and convolution density extend it from smooth rapidly decreasing functions to the closed form domain.

For one magnetic pair use the gauge $H_ b=-\partial _ x^2+(-\mathrm i\partial _ y+bx)^2$. The unitary Fourier transform in $y$, with kernel $(2\pi )^{-1/2}e^{-\mathrm iy\xi }$, turns it into the oscillator centered at $x+\xi /b$. Its complete generalized modes are $(2\pi )^{-1/2}e^{\mathrm iy\xi }\phi _ p(x+\xi /b)$. Integrating their squared value in $\xi $ gives $b/(2\pi )$ for every $p$ and every $(x,y)$. A zero pair is the free two-dimensional Laplacian, with Fourier modes $(2\pi )^{-1}e^{\mathrm i x\cdot \xi }$; polar coordinates give spectral density $dt/(4\pi )$. Tensor products of these complete transforms give the stated convolution and constant diagonals. Reversing a field sign is a coordinate reflection and gives the same density.

For $b>0$, the mass of (2.24.4) below $E$ is zero if $b\geq E$, and otherwise is at most $E/(2\pi )$: the number of allowed $p$ is at most $(E/b+1)/2\leq E/b$. The zero-pair bound is immediate. The convolution mass below $E$ is at most the product of these one-pair masses below $E$, proving the first uniform bound; integration gives the second. As $b\downarrow 0$ the one-pair measures converge on compact continuous tests by midpoint Riemann sums, with mesh $2b$. For $b>0$ varying to a positive value the same assertion follows by continuity of the finitely many levels meeting a compact support. Uniform local mass bounds allow tensor products and convolution: approximate a continuous test on a compact product by finite sums of separated continuous functions, using a rectangular partition and uniform continuity. Apply this to $(E-\sum t_ j)_+$ with a compact cutoff beyond its support. This proves joint continuity of $r_ b(E)$, also when ranks change. The uniform mass bound makes variation in $E$ locally uniformly Lipschitz. $\square$

Proposition 2.24.8.C554004EA447 Let $U_ R=R U$, where $U$ is a parallelepiped in $\mathbb R^{2n}$ whose shape and inverse shape matrices are bounded. Let $H_{b,U_ R}$ be the Dirichlet realization of $H_ b$. Uniformly for $E$ in bounded intervals, all $b_ j\geq 0$, and such shapes,

F7EE2C73C836 2.24.9
\begin{equation} \frac{R_{H_{b,U_ R}}(E)}{\operatorname {Vol}(U_ R)} =r_ b(E)+o(1)\qquad (R\to \infty ). \end{equation}

Proof. On each fixed bounded domain the magnetic form plus a sufficiently large multiple of $\| u\| ^2$ is equivalent to the $H^1_0$ norm. Its zero extension has the compact local Sobolev inclusion proved in lemma 10.46.1. Hence the preceding form lemma applies, by the same Hilbert representation construction. For its Dirichlet eigenvectors, extended by zero, the full-space spectral expansion and $(E-t)\leq (E-t)_+$ give

\[ R_{H_{b,U_ R}}(E) \leq \operatorname {Tr}\mathbf1_{U_ R}(E-H_ b)_+\mathbf1_{U_ R} =\operatorname {Vol}(U_ R)r_ b(E). \]

More explicitly, sum the expansion over the eigenvectors with energy below $E$. Bessel's inequality for their restrictions to $U_ R$ bounds the squared coefficients of each full-space generalized mode by its squared norm on $U_ R$. Positive integration gives the displayed bound. No condition on the boundary values of separated modes is used.

For the lower bound take a real $0\leq \eta \leq 1$ compactly supported in $U_ R$. The operator $\gamma =\eta P_ E\eta $ is a positive contraction. It is trace class: $P_ E\eta $ is Hilbert–Schmidt, with squared norm $\nu _ b(E)\int \eta ^2$, by the explicit mode transform above. Its form trace is finite. For each generalized mode $v$ of energy $t$, compactly supported integration by parts gives the exact identity

\[ \int |\nabla _ b(\eta v)|^2 =t\int \eta ^2|v|^2+\int |d\eta |^2|v|^2. \]

Integrate over modes with $t<E$. Parseval and nonnegative Fubini compute the trace and form trace, and (2.24.3) yields

B6FBF3F18EA3 2.24.10
\begin{equation} R_{H_{b,U_ R}}(E) \geq r_ b(E)\int \eta ^2-\nu _ b(E)\int |d\eta |^2. \end{equation}

This trace computation can equally be obtained by finite-rank approximations in the mode transform and monotone convergence; thus it does not presume a trace formula for an unproved heat kernel.

Choose $\eta =1$ away from a boundary layer of width $R^{1/2}$, with $|d\eta |\leq C R^{-1/2}$ and support compactly inside $U_ R$. The uniformly bounded shape matrices give $\int \eta ^2=\operatorname {Vol}(U_ R)(1-O(R^{-1/2}))$ and $\int |d\eta |^2=O(\operatorname {Vol}(U_ R)R^{-3/2})$. The uniform bounds (2.24.7) now prove (2.24.9). For $E\leq 0$ both sides are zero. This proves uniformity even as positive field magnitudes tend to zero. $\square$
