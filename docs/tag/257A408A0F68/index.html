Proposition 2.10.3257A408A0F68

Proposition 2.10.3.257A408A0F68 The topology just defined induces the usual smooth topology on every $\mathcal D^ p_{K,\infty }$. A family of test forms is bounded for this topology if and only if its supports are contained in one compact set and all coefficient derivatives are uniformly bounded on compact coordinate sets. A sequence converges in this topology if and only if its terms and limit have support in one compact set and converge there in every smooth coefficient seminorm.

If $M$ is noncompact, $\mathcal D^ p(M)$ is dense and proper in $\mathcal E^ p_\infty (M)$ and is incomplete for the topology induced from that space. Its locally convex test-form topology is strictly finer and is not Fréchet. If $M$ is compact, the two spaces and their topologies coincide and are Fréchet.

Proof. Every smooth coefficient seminorm restricts continuously to every fixed-support space, so the inclusion $\mathcal D^ p(M)\longrightarrow \mathcal E^ p_\infty (M)$ is continuous. Conversely, each allowed seminorm is continuous on a fixed-support space by definition. These two observations prove the first assertion.

Choose compact sets $K_ j\subset \operatorname {Int}K_{j+1}$ whose interiors exhaust $M$. Such an exhaustion follows from a countable cover by relatively compact coordinate balls: at each step include the first $j$ closed balls and finitely many more whose interiors cover the preceding compact set. Suppose a bounded family $B$ of test forms has no common compact support. For every $j$ choose $u_ j\in B$ and $x_ j\notin K_ j$ with $u_ j(x_ j)\neq 0$. The points $x_ j$ leave every compact set. Choose a complex-linear functional $\ell _ j$ on the finite-dimensional fiber at $x_ j$ with $\ell _ j(u_ j(x_ j))\neq 0$, and choose $c_ j>0$ so that $c_ j|\ell _ j(u_ j(x_ j))|\geq j$. Then

\[ q(u)=\sup _ j c_ j|\ell _ j(u(x_ j))| \]

is finite for every compactly supported $u$. On every fixed-support space only finitely many terms can be nonzero; they are continuous point-evaluation seminorms. Hence $q$ is an allowed continuous seminorm, whereas $\sup _{u\in B}q(u)=\infty $, a contradiction. This proves the common-support assertion. The remaining boundedness characterization follows from the first assertion: on a fixed-support space a continuous seminorm is bounded by a constant times finitely many coefficient seminorms. Here boundedness in a locally convex space is equivalently boundedness for every continuous seminorm.

A convergent sequence together with its limit is bounded, because each of its continuous seminorms is bounded. It therefore has common compact support, and convergence is smooth by the continuous inclusion into $\mathcal E^ p_\infty (M)$. Conversely, convergence in a fixed-support space implies convergence in $\mathcal D^ p(M)$ by continuity of its inclusion. This proves the sequence assertion; no corresponding common-support assertion for arbitrary convergent nets is made.

Smooth compact cutoffs $\chi _ j=1$ near $K_ j$ give $\chi _ j u\longrightarrow u$ in $\mathcal E^ p_\infty (M)$ for every smooth form $u$, proving density. The real-coordinate cutoff construction preceding Stokes' formula on compact domains with corners supplies these cutoffs. If $M$ is noncompact, choose points escaping every compact set and pairwise disjoint, locally finite, relatively compact coordinate neighborhoods of them. They can be chosen recursively outside an exhaustion enlarged to contain the previously chosen closures. Nonzero smooth $p$-forms supported in these neighborhoods have a locally finite sum with noncompact support. Call this form $u$. The sequence $\chi _ j u$ is Cauchy in the induced smooth topology and has no limit there in $\mathcal D^ p(M)$, since its unique smooth limit is $u$. This proves properness and incompleteness. Choose instead one nonzero test form $v_ j$ supported outside $K_ j$ and normalize it to have value $1$ under a fiber functional at a point outside $K_ j$. Then $v_ j\to 0$ in the induced smooth topology, whereas the seminorm constructed above with weights $c_ j=j$ prevents convergence in the test-form topology. Thus that topology is strictly finer.

Each $\mathcal D^ p_{K_ j,\infty }$ is a closed proper linear subspace of $\mathcal D^ p(M)$: it is the common kernel of the point evaluations outside $K_ j$, and a nonzero bump form outside $K_ j$ proves properness. A proper linear subspace has empty interior, since a neighborhood of zero absorbs every vector. These spaces cover $\mathcal D^ p(M)$. It therefore cannot be Fréchet, by the Baire property of a complete metric space. For completeness, if a complete metric space were a countable union of closed sets with empty interior, one could choose nested closed balls of positive radii at most $2^{-j}$, each contained in the interior of its predecessor and disjoint from the $j$th closed set. Their centers are Cauchy and their common limit belongs to none of the sets, a contradiction. Finally, if $M$ is compact then $\mathcal D^ p(M)=\mathcal D^ p_{M,\infty }=\mathcal E^ p_\infty (M)$; the fixed-support assertion proves equality of topologies. $\square$
