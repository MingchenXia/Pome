Lemma 6.48.1D0453276E370

Lemma 6.48.1.D0453276E370 Let $1\leq q\leq n$ and let $u$ be extended-continuous plurisubharmonic on $D\times W$, with pole set exactly $Z=\{ 0\} \times W$. Put

\[ Q=(dd^ cu)^ q,\qquad \mu _ w=(dd^ c_ z u(\cdot ,w))^ q. \]

These classical products exist. There is a nonnegative constant $\lambda $ with $\mathbf1_ ZQ=\lambda [Z]$, and

\[ \lambda =\mu _ w(\{ 0\} )\quad \text{for Lebesgue almost every }w\in W. \]

More precisely, for every continuous compactly supported function $f$ on $D\times W$,

C03DD469B3DD 6.48.2
\begin{equation} \int f\, Q\wedge dV_ w =\int _ W\left(\int _ D f(z,w)\, d\mu _ w(z)\right)dV_ w. \end{equation}

Proof. The unbounded locus of $u$ is $Z$, of codimension $q$. Every intersection of $m\leq q$ repeated unbounded loci therefore has codimension at least $m$. Thus corollary 7.31.4 gives $Q$ and its decreasing continuity. On each normal fiber the pole set is the single point zero, so the same theorem gives $\mu _ w$ and the corresponding decreasing continuity there. The coefficient restriction formula proposition 6.40.2 gives $\mathbf1_ ZQ=\lambda [Z]$ with a constant $\lambda \geq 0$, since $Z$ is connected and smooth of dimension $n-q$. For $q=n$, $Q$ is the single fiber measure and all assertions follow immediately from extraction of its atom. Suppose $q<n$.

Work first over a compact parameter set $L\Subset W$ and a normal ball $B(0,r)\Subset D$ containing the normal projection of the test support. Choose $r<R$ with $\overline{B(0,R)}\subset D$. Take a closed collar of the sphere $|z|=r$, still disjoint from zero, and enlarge $L$ slightly within $W$. On the product of that collar with the enlarged compact parameter set, $u$ is finite continuous and hence bounded below. Set

\[ u_ m=\max \{ u,-m\} ,\qquad Q_ m=(dd^ cu_ m)^ q, \qquad \mu _{m,w}=(dd^ c_ z u_ m(\cdot ,w))^ q. \]

For every integer $m\geq m_0$ with $m_0$ sufficiently large, $u_ m=u$ on this common collar. For fixed $w\in L$, the bounded collar identity lemma 6.34.1 gives

\[ \mu _{m,w}(B(0,r))=\mu _{m_0,w}(B(0,r)). \]

There is no regular-value or zero-boundary-mass requirement: the two products agree on an open collar and their distributional difference has compactly supported primitive in the ball. The functions $u_{m_0}(\cdot ,w)$ are uniformly bounded on $\overline{B(0,R)}\times L$. The bounded CLN estimate the bounded Chern–Levine–Nirenberg estimate consequently gives a constant $C_ L$ such that

D82DEC261845 6.48.3
\begin{equation} \mu _{m,w}(B(0,r))\leq C_ L \quad (w\in L,\ m\geq m_0). \end{equation}

It also bounds the limiting $\mu _ w$ on every smaller compact normal set, by weak convergence. All masses used below have one fixed such buffered bound.

We first prove (6.48.2) for each fixed $m$, with $Q_ m$ and $\mu _{m,w}$. Locally convolve the continuous bounded PSH function $u_ m$ in the joint $(z,w)$ coordinates. Its smooth PSH regularizations converge locally uniformly. For each regularization, wedging with the full parameter volume kills every Hessian factor containing a parameter differential. Ordinary Fubini therefore gives exactly the fiber identity with the normal Hessian power. Bounded-product uniform continuity uniform convergence of bounded Bedford–Taylor products passes the left side to $Q_ m$. On each fixed fiber it passes the right-side measures to $\mu _{m,w}$. For this fixed $m$, the regularized potentials have one common bound on buffered compact product sets, so the bounded CLN estimate bounds all their normal-fiber masses uniformly in the smoothing parameter and $w\in L$. Dominated convergence therefore passes the integrated right side to its limit as well. This proves the fixed-$m$ identity.

For clarity, its parameter integrands are measurable. For the smooth approximations they are continuous in $w$ for continuous $f$, by ordinary integration on a fixed compact normal support. Their pointwise limits, supplied by the fiber uniform-continuity theorem, are measurable. The same is true for the pointwise limit as $m\to \infty $. The local test support can be covered by finitely many buffered product sets; a continuous partition of unity gives the identity for every continuous compactly supported $f$.

Now let $m\to \infty $. The full classical decreasing-continuity theorem gives $Q_ m\to Q$ and $\mu _{m,w}\to \mu _ w$ on every fiber. The common estimate (6.48.3) permits dominated convergence in $w$. It proves (6.48.2) for the actual unbounded products, retaining their pole mass. Neither positivity of a weak slice limit alone nor monotonicity of the measures $Q_ m$ is being assumed.

Finally take $\psi \in C_ c(W)$ nonnegative and normal radial cutoffs $0\leq \chi _ j\leq 1$ with $\chi _ j(0)=1$, decreasing pointwise to $\mathbf1_{\{ 0\} }$ and supported in a fixed buffered normal ball. Apply the formula to $f(z,w)=\chi _ j(z)\psi (w)$. Continuity from above for the fixed locally finite measures, and the same uniform bound for the parameter integral, give

\[ \lambda \int _ W\psi \, dV_ w =\int _ W\psi (w)\mu _ w(\{ 0\} )\, dV_ w. \]

In particular $w\mapsto \mu _ w(\{ 0\} )$ is measurable, as a decreasing limit of the already measurable cutoff integrals. Uniqueness of locally finite measures tested against continuous compactly supported functions proves the almost-everywhere identity. This extracts the coefficient from an actual measure restriction, rather than interchanging a point mass with weak convergence. $\square$
