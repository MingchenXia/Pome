Corollary 6.49.2A9F7692E230F

Corollary 6.49.2.A9F7692E230F Let $\varepsilon _1,\ldots ,\varepsilon _ j\in \mathbb C$ and put $H_ j=F_{\varepsilon _ j}\circ \cdots \circ F_{\varepsilon _1}$. Then $H_ j$ is proper and surjective, its fibers are finite, its degree and local degree at zero are $4^ j$, and its zero fiber is $\{ 0\} $. Its classical logarithmic measure is

\[ \bigl (dd^ c\log \| H_ j\| _\infty ^2\bigr )^2=4^ j\delta _0. \]

Proof. Compositions of proper maps are proper: inverse images of compact sets are obtained by successive compact inverse images. The same reasoning gives surjectivity, finite fibers and the asserted zero fiber.

The sheet count also follows directly. Put $B_1=B_{\varepsilon _1}$ and inductively

\[ B_ j=B_{\varepsilon _ j}\cup F_{\varepsilon _ j}(B_{j-1}). \]

These sets are closed, by properness, and null. Indeed, a polynomial map is Lipschitz on each compact box in $\mathbb R^4$, and cube covers of a null set show that a Lipschitz map in the same real dimension has null image. A countable box cover proves the claim. Every target outside $B_ j$ has four regular preimages under the last map, all outside $B_{j-1}$. Induction gives $4^ j$ distinct regular preimages under $H_ j$. Its Jacobian is nonzero there, and its degree is $4^ j$.

For local degrees, choose disjoint neighborhoods of the finitely many intermediate fiber points. Properness excludes other points over a sufficiently small target neighborhood. Counting the nearby regular preimages at each step multiplies the local degrees just proved. In particular the unique zero fiber has local degree $4\cdots 4=4^ j$. Equivalently, for every neighborhood $V$ of zero, all preimages of sufficiently small target points lie in $V$: otherwise properness would give a nonzero limit point in the zero fiber. Thus all $4^ j$ nearby sheets belong to the local degree there. Apply the proper sheet-degree formula the proper hypersurface degree formula to the prime hypersurface $D=\mathbb C^2\times \{ 0\} $ in $\mathbb C^2\times \mathbb C$ and the map $(\zeta ,t)\mapsto H_ j(\zeta )$. Its restriction to $D$ is proper, and the sheet count just proved gives $(H_ j)_*1=4^ j1$. For $T>0$ set $L_ T=\max \{ L,-2T\} $. The bounded continuous projection identity in the weighted projection formula therefore gives

\[ (H_ j)_*\bigl (dd^ c(L_ T\circ H_ j)\bigr )^2 =4^ j(dd^ cL_ T)^2. \]

Both decreasing limits have only the isolated pole zero. The classical decreasing continuity of the theorem on classical products with compact unbounded loci applies: coordinate balls around the pole have boundaries on which these weights are bounded, and smaller balls suffice elsewhere. If $\chi \in C_ c(\mathbb C^2)$, properness makes $\chi \circ H_ j$ compactly supported as well. Consequently weak convergence can be passed through this particular pushforward, giving

\[ (H_ j)_*\bigl (dd^ c(L\circ H_ j)\bigr )^2=4^ j\delta _0. \]

No mass can be lost at infinity in this step. Positivity forces the upstairs measure to be supported on $H_ j^{-1}(0)=\{ 0\} $: for a compact set disjoint from zero choose a target cutoff supported away from zero and equal to one on its image. A target cutoff equal to one near zero identifies its atom as $4^ j$. This proves the claimed identity on all of $\mathbb C^2$. $\square$
