20.5.1 Divisors on analytic cyclesC07FF239CD6A

We use $dd^ c=\frac{i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial $ and therefore $dd^ c\log |f|^2=[\operatorname {Div}(f)]$ on a manifold. For a reduced pure-dimensional analytic subset $A$, $[A]$ denotes integration over $A_{\mathrm{reg}}$, with coefficient measures extended by zero on $A_{\mathrm{sing}}$, as in theorem 5.61.2. An effective integral analytic $p$-cycle is a locally finite sum $\sum a_ j[A_ j]$, where the $A_ j$ are distinct irreducible $p$-dimensional analytic subsets and the $a_ j$ are positive integers.

Lemma 20.5.2.551A781E3A1C Let $g_ j,g$ be holomorphic on a complex manifold, with $g_ j\to g$ locally uniformly. Suppose $g$ is not identically zero on any connected component. Then, locally for all sufficiently large $j$, $\log |g_ j|^2$ is integrable, and

\[ \log |g_ j|^2\longrightarrow \log |g|^2 \quad \hbox{in }L^1_{\mathrm{loc}} \]

with respect to smooth volume. In particular this holds for $g_ j=g-\varepsilon _ j$, $\varepsilon _ j\to 0$; no monotonicity is required.

Proof. Work on a connected coordinate ball. The identity theorem shows that $g$ is not identically zero there. Choose a point $b$ with $g(b)\ne 0$; then $g_ j(b)\ne 0$ for all large $j$. By the distributional Hessian of a holomorphic logarithm, the logarithms are plurisubharmonic and integrable, and they are locally uniformly bounded above by local uniform boundedness of $g_ j$. Apply plurisubharmonic compactness and Hartogs' lemma to any subsequence. Its alternative of locally uniform convergence to $-\infty $ is excluded by convergence at $b$. It therefore has a further subsequence converging in local $L^1$. A further extraction converges almost everywhere: on each compact set choose summable $L^1$ errors and use the integrability of their sum, then diagonalize. Outside $\{ g=0\} $ the original sequence converges pointwise to $\log |g|^2$. That zero set is null by measure-zero holomorphic zero sets, so every $L^1$ cluster limit is $\log |g|^2$. If full convergence failed on a compact set, a subsequence with its $L^1$ distance bounded below would contradict this conclusion. Finite coordinate covers prove the assertion for any smooth volume. On a zero-dimensional manifold it is ordinary convergence at each of the finitely many points in a compact set. $\square$

Lemma 20.5.3.323D5DA3E240 Let $D$ be an irreducible hypersurface of a smooth complex manifold $M$ of dimension $r+1$, and let $F:M\to X$ be holomorphic, proper on $D$. Write $B=F(D)$. If $\dim B<r$, then $F_*[D]=0$. If $\dim B=r$, there is an integer $d\geq 1$ such that

\[ F_*[D]=d[B]. \]

Here $d$ is the number of sheets of $F|_ D$ over the complement of a proper analytic subset of $B$. No integrality assertion about an arbitrary weak limit of cycles is used.

Proof. The image is analytic by Remmert's proper mapping theorem and has dimension at most $r$ by dimension bounds for holomorphic images. It is irreducible: a decomposition into two proper analytic subsets would pull back to such a decomposition of $D$. These assertions also give purity of $B$ by purity and dimension of irreducible analytic sets. The current $[D]$ and its proper pushforward are well defined by local equations and currents of irreducible hypersurfaces, pushforward of currents proper on their support.

If $\dim B<r$, the derivative of $F|_{D_{\mathrm{reg}}}$ has rank less than $r$ everywhere. Otherwise the constant rank theorem near a point of maximal rank would put an $r$-dimensional submanifold in $B$, contradicting its dimension. Every pulled-back $(r,r)$-form therefore vanishes on $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$, proving the first assertion. For $r=0$, $D$ is one point and its pushforward is the unit point mass at its image, so the second assertion holds with $d=1$. Assume henceforth $r\geq 1$ and $\dim B=r$.

We construct the entire critical exceptional set analytically, including at singular points of $D$. On a coordinate neighborhood in $M$, choose a reduced local equation $e$ of $D$ by local equations and currents of irreducible hypersurfaces. In target coordinates $F_1,\ldots ,F_ n$, put

C02C6380EF73 20.5.4
\begin{equation} Q=\left\{ e=0:\ de\wedge dF_{i_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge dF_{i_ r}=0 \text{ for every }i_1<\cdots <i_ r\right\} . \end{equation}

These equations include $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$, where $de=0$, and on $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ express exactly rank less than $r$. Multiplying $e$ by a unit changes its differential on $D$ by that unit; changing target coordinates takes invertible linear combinations of the displayed minors. Thus these local analytic sets agree and define a closed analytic subset $Q\subset D$. It is proper. If it were all of $D$, the image of $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ would be $\mathcal H^{2r}$-null by dimension bounds for holomorphic images; so would the image of $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$, by its smaller dimension and analytic singular strata and Hausdorff dimension. Countably many charts suffice. This cannot cover the regular locus of the $r$-dimensional analytic set $B$. Hence $\dim Q\leq r-1$.

The set

\[ Z=B_{\mathrm{sing}}\cup F(Q) \]

is analytic, closed in $B$, and has dimension at most $r-1$; properness and the same image dimension bound justify this for $F(Q)$. The manifold $B_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is connected by global irreducible analytic components. Removing its proper analytic subset $Z\cap B_{\mathrm{reg}}$ leaves it connected, by bounded holomorphic removal and connected complements. Over $B\setminus Z$ every source point is regular with derivative rank $r$. The inverse function theorem on these two $r$-manifolds makes $F$ a local biholomorphism there. Every fiber is compact by properness and discrete by this local description, hence finite. For any such fiber take finitely many disjoint inverse neighborhoods. Properness supplies a smaller target neighborhood with its whole inverse image in their union: the image of their closed complement in $D$ is closed and misses the target point. Intersect the inverse neighborhood images as well. This is an evenly covered neighborhood. The sheet number is a positive locally constant integer and therefore a fixed $d\geq 1$ on the connected $B\setminus Z$. Holomorphic coordinate change preserves complex orientation, so integration over these sheets gives $F_*[D]=d[B]$ off $Z$.

There is no unexamined current supported on $Z$ in this equality. Indeed $F^{-1}(Z)\cap D$ is a proper analytic subset of $D$. Its intersection with $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ has smooth volume zero: locally it lies in a nontrivial holomorphic zero set, and the connectedness and density of $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ exclude an open component. The integration defining $[D]$ assigns its singular locus zero coefficient mass as well. For a compact target set, properness puts its inverse image in a compact source set; there the coefficients of $F^*$ of a bounded test form are bounded by a constant times the induced volume on $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$. Consequently every coefficient measure of $F_*[D]$ gives $Z$ zero mass. This follows first for positive trace tests from the integral formula and then for all coefficients by coefficient measures and compactness of positive currents. The same argument on $B_{\mathrm{reg}}$ shows that $[B]$ gives $Z$ zero mass. Their equality off $Z$ is therefore their equality everywhere. $\square$

Theorem 20.5.5.F1C928146DBC Let $A$ be a reduced pure $p$-dimensional analytic subset of a complex manifold $X$, and let $f$ be holomorphic near $A$, not identically zero on any irreducible component of $A$. Then $\log |f|^2$ is coefficient-integrable for $[A]$. If $p\geq 1$ and $C_ k$ are the irreducible components of $A\cap \{ f=0\} $, there are positive integers $m_ k$ such that

14ADBD4BFF7F 20.5.6
\begin{equation} dd^ c\bigl (\log |f|^2[A]\bigr )=\sum _ k m_ k[C_ k]. \end{equation}

The sum is locally finite. The same assertion applies linearly to effective integral analytic cycles whose components are not contained in $\{ f=0\} $.

If $f_ j\to f$ locally uniformly holomorphically, the logarithms converge to $\log |f|^2$ in local coefficient $L^1([A])$, whenever the local logarithms are defined for large $j$. In particular

85DED4E0947F 20.5.7
\begin{equation} dd^ c\bigl (\log |f-\varepsilon |^2[A]\bigr ) \longrightarrow dd^ c\bigl (\log |f|^2[A]\bigr ) \quad (\varepsilon \to 0). \end{equation}

For $p=0$ the hypotheses force $f$ to be nonzero at every point of $A$; the Hessian current is zero and the local logarithm convergence is ordinary convergence against finitely many point masses.

Proof. All assertions are local on $X$. Local finiteness of components reduces the proof to one irreducible component $A$; all sums below are finite over each compact target neighborhood. The identity principle on the connected $A_{\mathrm{reg}}$ shows that $f$ is nontrivial on every local branch: vanishing on one branch would force vanishing on a nonempty regular open subset of $A$, hence on $A$. In particular its zero set is pure of dimension $p-1$ when nonempty, by purity of a nontrivial holomorphic zero set. We may assume $p\geq 1$, the point-mass case being as stated.

Take the proper bimeromorphic resolution $\pi :M\to A\subset X$ of the stated resolution-of-singularities input. The composition to $X$ is proper because $A$ is closed. Each component of $M$ has dimension $p$ and meets the isomorphism locus; the exceptional analytic set $E=\pi ^{-1}(A_{\mathrm{sing}})$ is proper on every such component by nowhere-dense inverse images under bimeromorphic maps. It has smooth volume zero by the holomorphic zero-set lemma. Off $E$ the map is a biholomorphism onto $A_{\mathrm{reg}}$. For a compact target set $K$, $\pi ^{-1}(K)$ is compact, and $\pi ^*\beta ^ p$ is bounded by a smooth volume form times a constant there, where $\beta $ is any smooth positive ambient Hermitian form. Thus the pushforward of the integration current of $M$ has locally finite mass, equals $[A]$ off $A_{\mathrm{sing}}$, and gives that singular set zero mass because $E$ is null. Hence

\[ \pi _*[M]=[A]. \]

This also directly checks the coefficient convention at singularities.

Put $g=f\circ \pi $. It is nontrivial on every component of $M$. By the distributional Hessian of a holomorphic logarithm, $\log |g|^2$ is locally integrable for smooth volume. The preceding compact pullback bound therefore proves

\[ \int _ K|\log |f|^2|\, [A]\wedge \beta ^ p =\int _{\pi ^{-1}(K)}|\log |g|^2|\, \pi ^*\beta ^ p<\infty . \]

The equality uses change of variables off the respective null sets; there is no product of an infinite value with a positive atom there. Trace domination supplies coefficient integrability, and the same argument for all test forms gives the projection identity

E212244D4A1F 20.5.8
\begin{equation} \log |f|^2[A]=\pi _*\bigl (\log |g|^2[M]\bigr ). \end{equation}

The ordinary manifold identity of the normalized Poincaré–Lelong formula and commutation of proper pushforward with $dd^ c$ now give

74331B0B45F1 20.5.9
\begin{equation} dd^ c\bigl (\log |f|^2[A]\bigr ) =\pi _*[\operatorname {Div}(g)] =\sum _ D e_ D\, \pi _*[D]. \end{equation}

Here the $D$ are the irreducible prime hypersurfaces of the divisor on the smooth $M$ and $e_ D\geq 1$ are their transverse vanishing orders. Only finitely many $D$ meet the compact preimage of a compact target neighborhood, by local finiteness of analytic components. Apply lemma 20.5.3 to each of them. A component with lower-dimensional image contributes zero; every other contributes a positive integer sheet number times its image. Those images are irreducible analytic subsets of dimension $p-1$ in $A\cap \{ f=0\} $, so are its irreducible components.

Every component $C_ k$ of that intersection receives a contribution. The resolution is surjective, since its proper image contains the dense regular locus. Therefore $\pi (\{ g=0\} )=\{ f=0\} \cap A$. Near a compact neighborhood of any point of $C_ k$, the finitely many images of the relevant $D$ cover that zero set. An irreducible local branch of $C_ k$ cannot be covered by finitely many analytic sets of smaller dimension, by purity and dimension of irreducible analytic sets and the local branch decomposition. At least one $D$ consequently has image of dimension $p-1$ containing an open subset of that branch, hence is the whole global component containing it. This proves $m_ k>0$; the formula gives explicitly

\[ m_ k=\sum _{D:\, \pi (D)=C_ k}e_ D\deg (\pi |_ D)\in \mathbb Z_{>0}. \]

The current on the left defines these coefficients uniquely by restriction to regular points of $C_ k$ outside the other components, so the local formulas agree on overlaps and do not depend on the chosen resolution.

Finally put $g_ j=f_ j\circ \pi $. Properness gives local uniform convergence on $M$, and lemma 20.5.2 gives smooth-volume $L^1$ convergence on every compact preimage. The compact pullback bound gives

\[ \int _ K\bigl |\log |f_ j|^2-\log |f|^2\bigr |\, [A]\wedge \beta ^ p \leq C_ K\int _ L\bigl |\log |g_ j|^2-\log |g|^2\bigr |\, dV_ M \longrightarrow 0, \]

where $L$ is a fixed compact neighborhood of $\pi ^{-1}(K)$. This proves coefficient $L^1$ convergence and therefore convergence of the Hessian currents. For shifts, the local logarithms are defined for all small $\varepsilon $: on each of the finitely many relevant components choose one point where $f\ne 0$ and exclude their positive minimum absolute value. No monotone-limit theorem is being used. The integer conclusion was already proved in (20.5.9), independently of this limit. $\square$

Theorem 20.5.10.DDB973C0C1DD Let $Z_1,\ldots ,Z_ q$ be effective divisors on an $n$-dimensional complex manifold, where $1\leq q\leq n$. Suppose every intersection of $m$ distinct supports is empty or has codimension at least $m$. If $C_ k$ are the irreducible components of their common support intersection, then the classical product satisfies

\[ [Z_1]\wedge \cdots \wedge [Z_ q]=\sum _ k m_ k[C_ k], \qquad m_ k\in \mathbb Z_{>0}. \]

The sum is locally finite, and the product is zero when that intersection is empty. Its coefficients are independent of the order of the divisors.

Proof. Locally write $Z_ j=\operatorname {Div}(f_ j)$, with $f_ j$ holomorphic and nontrivial on every connected ambient component. Each nonempty $m$-fold intersection is in fact pure of codimension $m$. Induct on $m$: no component of the preceding pure intersection can be contained in the next divisor, by the assumed codimension bound; the identity principle on its connected regular locus rules out vanishing on any local branch. Then purity of a nontrivial holomorphic zero set gives exactly one dimension drop on every branch. This applies to every subfamily. The classical products and their symmetry exist by theorem 7.28.1, corollary 7.31.4. Here $L(\log |f_ j|^2)=\{ f_ j=0\} $ and the analytic codimension hypotheses give the required Hausdorff nullness. The case $q=1$ is the normalized Poincaré–Lelong formula. Inductively suppose the first $q-1$ factors form an effective integral cycle of pure codimension $q-1$. No component $A$ of this cycle is contained in $\{ f_ q=0\} $; otherwise their full intersection would have dimension at least $n-q+1$, contrary to the hypothesis. The recursive definition and local finiteness give

\[ [Z_ q]\wedge \sum _ A a_ A[A] =\sum _ A a_ A\, dd^ c\bigl (\log |f_ q|^2[A]\bigr ). \]

Each summand is the effective integral cycle proved in theorem 20.5.5. Every final irreducible component is a component of $A\cap \{ f_ q=0\} $ for at least one such $A$: locally the full intersection is their finite union, and all these nonempty intersections are pure of dimension $n-q$. Irreducibility and equal dimensions give the assertion. Thus its coefficient is a nonempty sum of positive integers. The formulas agree on overlaps by their equality with the intrinsically defined current. Symmetry of that classical product gives order independence. No conclusion about a varying-base family of cycles is needed. $\square$

Example 20.5.11.FF4839E28893 For the cusp $A=\{ y^2=x^3\} \subset \mathbb C^2$, its local parametrization is $\gamma (t)=(t^2,t^3)$. It is one-to-one off the origin, with inverse $t=y/x$, and is proper onto a sufficiently small cusp representative. The resolved function $x\circ \gamma =t^2$ has vanishing order two. Thus $dd^ c(\log |x|^2[A])=2[\{ 0\} ]$ locally. The whole intersection point is singular in $A$; its coefficient is obtained from the transverse order on the smooth parameter disc.
