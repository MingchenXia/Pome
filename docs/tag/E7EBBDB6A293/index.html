Proposition 6.21.1E7EBBDB6A293

Proposition 6.21.1.E7EBBDB6A293 On a pseudoconvex open subset of $\mathbb C^ n$, arbitrary complex values can be interpolated at a closed discrete set of distinct points. Consequently such an open set is holomorphically convex and is a domain of existence. Conversely a domain of holomorphy is pseudoconvex.

Proof. Use the existing Oka theorem to choose a nonnegative smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion $s$. Around the discrete points $z_ j$ choose pairwise disjoint balls $B(z_ j,2r_ j)\Subset \Omega $ forming a locally finite family. For example, take $2r_ j$ smaller than one quarter of both the distance to the other points and the distance to the complement, and also take $r_ j\leq 1/j$. Let $\theta _ j$ be smooth, supported in $B(z_ j,r_ j)$ and equal to one near $z_ j$, and put $g=\sum _ j a_ j\theta _ j$. This is smooth; $\alpha =\bar\partial g$ vanishes near every $z_ j$. Choose smooth $\eta _ j$ supported in $B(z_ j,2r_ j)$, equal to one on $B(z_ j,r_ j)$, and set

\[ L=n\sum _ j\eta _ j(z)\log \frac{|z-z_ j|^2}{r_ j^2}. \]

Off the discrete poles this is smooth. Its negative complex Hessian is bounded on each compact sublevel of $s$, since its only nonsmooth terms near a pole are plurisubharmonic logarithms with positive coefficient. There is a smooth convex increasing $\chi $ such that $p=\chi (s)+L$ is plurisubharmonic and

\[ \int _\Omega |\alpha |^2\exp (-p-|z|^2)\, \mathrm dV<\infty . \]

Here is the simultaneous choice in detail. On each compact band $m\leq s\leq m+1$, let $C_ m$ bound the negative Hessian of $L$, and let $\lambda _ m>0$ be a lower bound for $H_ s$. The integral $I_ m=\int _{\{ m\leq s\leq m+1\} }|\alpha |^2 \exp (-L-|z|^2)\, \mathrm dV$ is finite, since $\alpha =0$ near the finitely many poles in the band. Choose a smooth nondecreasing nonnegative function $b$ such that $b\geq C_ m/\lambda _ m$ on $[m,m+1]$ and $\int _{m-1}^ m b\geq \log ^+(2^ m I_ m)$ for $m\geq 1$. Such a function is obtained by a locally finite sum of nondecreasing smooth steps, the $m$th step starting at $m-1$ and reaching a sufficiently large constant by $m-1/2$; include a sufficiently large initial constant for $m=0$. Put $\chi (t)=C+\int _0^ t b$ and choose $C$ for the band $0\leq s\leq 1$. The chain rule gives $H_{\chi (s)}\geq \chi '(s)H_ s$, proving positivity off the poles, and the integral on the $m$th band is at most $2^{-m}$. Near a pole, $p$ is the sum of $n\log |z-z_ j|^2$ and a smooth plurisubharmonic function, so it is PSH there as well.

Apply the existing singular-weight $\bar\partial $ estimate with this $p$ and $q=|z|^2$, whose Hessian is the identity. It gives $\bar\partial v=\alpha $ and $\int |v|^2\exp (-p-|z|^2)<\infty $. Near $z_ j$, $v$ is holomorphic by the scalar distributional regularity theorem. The weight is a smooth positive factor times $|z-z_ j|^{-2n}$. A nonzero value $v(z_ j)$ would make its integral diverge, since the radial integral would contain $\int _0^ r t^{-1}\, dt$. Thus $v(z_ j)=0$ and $F=g-v$ is holomorphic with $F(z_ j)=a_ j$.

If a holomorphic hull of a compact $K$ were noncompact, its boundedness and closedness in $\Omega $ would supply distinct hull points tending to the boundary. Interpolating the values $j$ there contradicts the defining bound $|F|\leq \sup _ K|F|$ of the hull. The hull is therefore compact, and the Cartan–Thullen theorem applies. Conversely that theorem and the hull-exhaustion proposition give a smooth PSH exhaustion on every domain of holomorphy, proving pseudoconvexity. $\square$
