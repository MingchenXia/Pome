Theorem 20.5.10DDB973C0C1DD

Theorem 20.5.10.DDB973C0C1DD Let $Z_1,\ldots ,Z_ q$ be effective divisors on an $n$-dimensional complex manifold, where $1\leq q\leq n$. Suppose every intersection of $m$ distinct supports is empty or has codimension at least $m$. If $C_ k$ are the irreducible components of their common support intersection, then the classical product satisfies

\[ [Z_1]\wedge \cdots \wedge [Z_ q]=\sum _ k m_ k[C_ k], \qquad m_ k\in \mathbb Z_{>0}. \]

The sum is locally finite, and the product is zero when that intersection is empty. Its coefficients are independent of the order of the divisors.

Proof. Locally write $Z_ j=\operatorname {Div}(f_ j)$, with $f_ j$ holomorphic and nontrivial on every connected ambient component. Each nonempty $m$-fold intersection is in fact pure of codimension $m$. Induct on $m$: no component of the preceding pure intersection can be contained in the next divisor, by the assumed codimension bound; the identity principle on its connected regular locus rules out vanishing on any local branch. Then purity of a nontrivial holomorphic zero set gives exactly one dimension drop on every branch. This applies to every subfamily. The classical products and their symmetry exist by theorem 7.28.1, corollary 7.31.4. Here $L(\log |f_ j|^2)=\{ f_ j=0\} $ and the analytic codimension hypotheses give the required Hausdorff nullness. The case $q=1$ is the normalized Poincaré–Lelong formula. Inductively suppose the first $q-1$ factors form an effective integral cycle of pure codimension $q-1$. No component $A$ of this cycle is contained in $\{ f_ q=0\} $; otherwise their full intersection would have dimension at least $n-q+1$, contrary to the hypothesis. The recursive definition and local finiteness give

\[ [Z_ q]\wedge \sum _ A a_ A[A] =\sum _ A a_ A\, dd^ c\bigl (\log |f_ q|^2[A]\bigr ). \]

Each summand is the effective integral cycle proved in theorem 20.5.5. Every final irreducible component is a component of $A\cap \{ f_ q=0\} $ for at least one such $A$: locally the full intersection is their finite union, and all these nonempty intersections are pure of dimension $n-q$. Irreducibility and equal dimensions give the assertion. Thus its coefficient is a nonempty sum of positive integers. The formulas agree on overlaps by their equality with the intrinsically defined current. Symmetry of that classical product gives order independence. No conclusion about a varying-base family of cycles is needed. $\square$
