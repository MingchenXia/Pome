Theorem 10.98.2DE32EEA596CD

Theorem 10.98.2.DE32EEA596CD Let $X$ be a compact connected Kähler manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, and let $a,b\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ be nef. Then

74CB0E2B99CA 10.98.3
\begin{equation} \operatorname {Vol}(a-b) \geq a^ n-n\, a^{n-1}\cdot b. \end{equation}

Here the right side consists of ordinary cohomological intersections, and the volume is zero outside the pseudoeffective cone. If the right side is strictly positive, $a-b$ is big.

Proof. The assertion is immediate if $X$ is empty, so assume $X$ is nonempty. First suppose $n\geq 2$ and that $a,b$ are Kähler classes. Choose Kähler representatives $\gamma \in a$ and $\eta \in b$, and write

\[ A=\int _ X\gamma ^ n, \qquad C=\int _ X\gamma ^{n-1}\wedge \eta , \qquad \delta =\frac{nC}{A}. \]

Both $A$ and $C$ are strictly positive. If $\delta \geq 1$, the desired lower bound is nonpositive and follows from the nonnegativity of volume. We may therefore assume $0<\delta <1$.

The null-support construction and its regularization, theorem 5.64.2, corollary 5.64.4, give a closed positive current $T\in b$ with continuous potential and support in a closed Lebesgue-null set $\Sigma $, and smooth Kähler forms $T_ k\in b$ converging weakly to $T$. Choose decreasing open neighborhoods $U_ j$ of $\Sigma $ with $\overline U_{j+1}\subset U_ j$ and $\bigcap _ j\overline U_ j=\Sigma $. Choose $0\leq \rho _ j\leq 1$ smooth with $\operatorname {supp}\rho _ j\subset U_ j$ and $\rho _ j=1$ on $U_{j+1}$.

Fix a Gauduchon metric $\chi $, and put

\[ B=\int _ X\eta \wedge \chi ^{n-1}>0. \]

The trace identity and the support of $T$ give

\[ \int _ X\rho _ jT\wedge \chi ^{n-1} =\int _ XT\wedge \chi ^{n-1}=B. \]

For each $j$, choose a sufficiently large index $k(j)$ such that, after passing to an increasing choice of indices,

FE1FC41D41AE 10.98.4
\begin{equation} m_ j:=\int _{U_ j}T_{k(j)}\wedge \chi ^{n-1}\geq B-j^{-1}. \end{equation}

For brevity write $\tau _ j=T_{k(j)}$. All these choices may depend on $\chi $.

The smooth Monge–Ampère theorem, smooth Hermitian Monge–Ampère existence, applied with closed background $\gamma $, yields a Kähler form $\gamma _ j\in a$ satisfying

BE76273E23F2 10.98.5
\begin{equation} \gamma _ j^ n=(1-\delta )\gamma ^ n +\frac{\delta A}{B}\tau _ j\wedge \chi ^{n-1}. \end{equation}

Indeed, the right side is smooth and strictly positive and has integral $A$, since the Gauduchon trace of $\tau _ j\in b$ equals $B$. The scalar normalization in that theorem is consequently zero for the closed background, by Stokes' theorem.

We next estimate the trace of $\gamma _ j$ on the same small set $U_ j$. At each point, let $0<\lambda _1\leq \cdots \leq \lambda _ n$ be its eigenvalues relative to $\tau _ j$. Put $q_ j=(\tau _ j\wedge \chi ^{n-1})/\tau _ j^ n$ and $P_ j=\lambda _2\cdots \lambda _ n$. Then

\[ \gamma _ j\geq \lambda _1\tau _ j, \qquad \lambda _1P_ j\geq \frac{\delta A}{B}q_ j, \]

and therefore

08054B9DCC9E 10.98.6
\begin{equation} \int _{U_ j}\gamma _ j\wedge \chi ^{n-1} \geq \frac{\delta A}{B} \int _{U_ j}\frac{q_ j^2}{P_ j}\, \tau _ j^ n. \end{equation}

Cauchy–Schwarz, followed by the elementary mixed-wedge identity, gives

\[ m_ j^2 \leq \left(\int _{U_ j}\frac{q_ j^2}{P_ j}\, \tau _ j^ n\right) \left(\int _{U_ j}P_ j\, \tau _ j^ n\right), \qquad P_ j\tau _ j^ n\leq n\gamma _ j^{n-1}\wedge \tau _ j. \]

The last form is positive, so its integral on $U_ j$ is at most its integral on $X$, namely $nC$, because both factors are closed and remain in the fixed classes $a,b$. Since $\delta A=nC$, we obtain

58B9EC1F5AF1 10.98.7
\begin{equation} \int _{U_ j}\gamma _ j\wedge \chi ^{n-1} \geq \frac{m_ j^2}{B}\geq B-\frac{2}{j} \end{equation}

once $j>B^{-1}$. The constant and this threshold are allowed to depend on the fixed test metric; the determinant lower bound below will not.

The positive currents $\gamma _ j$ have bounded mass, because their Kähler cohomology class is fixed. After passing to a subsequence, let $\gamma _ j\rightharpoonup \xi _\chi $, a closed positive current in $a$. To locate its trace mass, fix $k$ and take $j>k$. Since $\rho _ k=1$ on $U_ j$, (10.98.7) gives

\[ \int _ X\rho _ k\gamma _ j\wedge \chi ^{n-1}\geq B-2/j. \]

First pass to the weak limit in $j$, then let $k\to \infty $. The cutoffs converge pointwise to the indicator of $\Sigma $; dominated convergence for the finite trace measure yields

\[ \int _\Sigma \xi _\chi \wedge \chi ^{n-1}\geq B. \]

The absolutely continuous part assigns no mass to $\Sigma $. Hence

1BAC4BA78448 10.98.8
\begin{equation} \int _ X\xi _{\chi ,\mathrm{sing}}\wedge \chi ^{n-1}\geq B, \qquad \int _ X\xi _{\chi ,\mathrm{ac}}\wedge \chi ^{n-1} \leq \int _ X(\gamma -\eta )\wedge \chi ^{n-1}. \end{equation}

Only this trace inequality is asserted; it is not an inequality between $\xi _{\chi ,\mathrm{sing}}$ and $T$ as currents.

On the other hand, (10.98.5) implies $\gamma _ j^ n\geq (1-\delta )\gamma ^ n$. The weak closedness established in lemma 10.97.1 therefore gives

CBD8309C9C77 10.98.9
\begin{equation} (\xi _{\chi ,\mathrm{ac}})^ n\geq (1-\delta )\gamma ^ n \quad \text{almost everywhere}. \end{equation}

The right side is strictly positive and independent of $\chi $. Apply proposition 10.97.4 with $\psi =\gamma -\eta $ and $\nu =(1-\delta )\gamma ^ n$. Equations (10.98.8) and (10.98.9) provide its hypotheses for every Gauduchon metric. The resulting positive closed current $R\in a-b$ satisfies

\[ (R_{\mathrm{ac}})^ n\geq (1-\delta )\gamma ^ n. \]

Integrating and using (10.98.1) proves (10.98.3) for Kähler classes.

For nef $a,b$, fix a Kähler class $w$ and apply the result just proved to $a+\varepsilon w$ and $b+\varepsilon w$. Their difference is exactly $a-b$, so the left side does not vary. Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ only uses continuity of the intersection polynomial on the right. In particular, this step does not assume continuity of volume.

For $n=1$, a real $(1,1)$-class on the compact connected curve is determined by its integral. The scalar Green operator represents every zero-integral smooth form as $dd^ c$ of a smooth function, by global complex Hessian potentials. It follows that a class of nonnegative integral has a smooth nonnegative representative and volume equal to that integral, while a class of negative integral is not pseudoeffective and has volume zero. Thus the asserted inequality reads $\max \{ a-b,0\} \geq a-b$ for the numerical degrees and is immediate.

Finally, a strictly positive lower bound for volume gives bigness by the positive-mass bigness criterion, bigness and positive non-pluripolar volume. $\square$
