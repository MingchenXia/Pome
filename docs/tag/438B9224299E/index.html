Lemma 7.29.1438B9224299E

Lemma 7.29.1.438B9224299E Let $B=B(a,R)\Subset \mathbb C^ n$, put $\psi =|z-a|^2-R^2$ and $\omega =dd^ c\psi $, and let $T$ be closed positive of bidimension $(p,p)$ on a neighborhood of $\overline B$. Suppose the locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions $f_0,\ldots ,f_ r$ are defined near $\overline B$, are nonpositive on $B$ and all equal $A\psi $ on a collar of $\partial B$, for some $A>0$. Write $M_ T=\int _ B T\wedge \omega ^ p$. Then

9135B1E39C61
\begin{align} \int _ B dd^ cf_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cf_ r\wedge T\wedge \omega ^{p-r} & =A^ rM_ T & & (0\leq r\leq p), \tag{7.29.2}\\ \int _ B(-f_0)\, dd^ cf_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cf_ r\wedge T \wedge \omega ^{p-r} & \leq \| \psi \| _{L^\infty (B)}A^{r+1}M_ T & & (0\leq r\leq p-1). \tag{7.29.3} \end{align}

The constants do not involve bounds for the $f_ j$ inside $B$. Moreover, if $R_1$ is closed positive of bidimension $(1,1)$ near $\overline B$, $a_1$ is smooth, $b_1$ is $L^1$ for the coefficient variations of $R_1$, and $dd^ c(b_1R_1)$ has measure coefficients, then $a_1=b_1$ on a collar implies

90ED91EDE196 7.29.4
\begin{equation} \int _ B a_1\, dd^ c(b_1R_1)=\int _ B b_1\, dd^ ca_1\wedge R_1, \end{equation}

provided the left-hand measure has finite mass near $\overline B$.

Proof. The bounded products, their locality, smooth-factor rules and symmetry are those of locality and positivity of bounded Bedford–Taylor products, decreasing continuity of bounded Bedford–Taylor products. They have finite mass near $\overline B$ by the bounded Chern–Levine–Nirenberg estimate in the interior and their fixed smooth factors on the collar. Replace each $dd^ cf_ j$ in turn by $A\omega $: the difference is $dd^ c$ of $(f_ j-A\psi )$ times a closed product, compactly supported in $B$. Its integral against the remaining closed power of $\omega $ is zero, by testing with a compact cutoff equal to one near that support. This proves (7.29.2). For $r\leq p-1$, apply integration by parts with a common boundary collar to $f_0$ and the smooth function $A\psi $, with base current $dd^ cf_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cf_ r\wedge T\wedge \omega ^{p-r-1}$. Dividing the resulting identity by $A$ gives

\[ \int _ B(-f_0)\, dd^ cf_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cf_ r\wedge T\wedge \omega ^{p-r} =\int _ B(-\psi )\, dd^ cf_0\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cf_ r \wedge T\wedge \omega ^{p-r-1}. \]

The mass identity with $r+1$ factors proves the bound.

For the final assertion put $h=(b_1-a_1)|_ B$, which is $L^1(R_1)$ and has compact support in $B$; extend it by zero when needed. Since $R_1$ is closed, on $B$ one has $dd^ c(b_1R_1)=dd^ ca_1\wedge R_1+dd^ c(hR_1)$. Choose a compact cutoff $\chi $ equal to one near the support of $hR_1$. Distributional integration by parts gives

\[ \int _ B a_1\, dd^ c(hR_1) =\int \chi a_1\, dd^ c(hR_1) =\int h\, dd^ c(\chi a_1)\wedge R_1 =\int _ B h\, dd^ ca_1\wedge R_1. \]

All integrals are finite: the smooth coefficients are bounded on $\overline B$, and $h\in L^1(R_1)$. Adding the smooth term proves (7.29.4). This extends the bounded common-collar argument using precisely the needed integrability. $\square$
