Lemma 10.79.116D7C5C5A2C24

Lemma 10.79.11.6D7C5C5A2C24 Let $X$ be a connected compact curve, let $V=H^0(X,K_ X)$, and use

\[ \mathcal S:H^1(X,\mathcal O_ X)\longrightarrow V^*, \qquad \mathcal S([\eta ])(u)=\int _ Xu\wedge \eta . \]

This Serre isomorphism maps $\Lambda _ X$ onto $\Gamma _ X$. Moreover $\pi _1(X)_{\rm ab}$ is free of rank $2g$, and integration currents give an isomorphism

\[ \pi _1(X)_{\rm ab}\longrightarrow H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb Z}) \]

whose real image is the de Rham class of the loop current, with the complex orientation of $X$.

Proof. We first justify the regular-value assertion used below. A smooth real function on a surface has critical values of measure zero. At a critical point where some second derivative is nonzero, the zero set of a suitable first derivative is a smooth curve by the inverse function theorem. Its critical subset maps into the critical values of the restricted one-variable function. Those values have measure zero: on the open set $|f'|<\delta $, disjoint intervals cover the compact critical set, and their image lengths total at most $\delta $ times the total domain length; then let $\delta \to 0$. Use countably many such curve neighborhoods. On the remaining critical set both first and second derivatives vanish. In a compact coordinate rectangle with bounded third derivatives, a square of side $r$ meeting that set has image of that set in an interval of length at most $Cr^3$, by Taylor's formula at a point of the set in the square. At most $C'r^{-2}$ squares are needed. Letting $r\to 0$ gives measure zero there too. Countable compact subrectangles and target coordinate intervals prove the assertion also for a smooth map to $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$.

Every class of $H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})$ is the connecting class of a smooth circle-valued function $f$. Indeed the exact sequence $0\to \underline{\mathbb Z}\to \mathcal A_{\mathbb R} \xrightarrow {\exp (2\pi \mathrm i\, \cdot )}\mathcal T\to 1$ has acyclic additive middle sheaf. Locally write $f=\exp (2\pi \mathrm i t_ i)$. Its real de Rham image is $\beta =dt_ i=f^*(dt)$, by the same connecting/sign calculation as in theorem 10.79.2. Choose a regular value of $f$. Its fiber $C$ is a compact oriented one-manifold, hence a finite union of circles. Orient it by requiring $dt$ followed by its orientation to give the orientation of $X$. On a small band of regular values, the vector field $\nabla t/|dt|^2$ gives a product $C\times (-\delta ,\delta )$: its flow changes $t$ at unit rate, and compactness of a slightly larger closed band ensures existence for these short times. Choose a one-form $\chi (t)dt$ of integral one supported in that band. On the circle it differs from $dt$ by an exact form, since the integral of the difference is zero and its primitive is periodic. Thus $\beta $ is cohomologous to $f^*(\chi (t)dt)$. For any closed one-form $u$, Stokes on the intervening cylinders makes $\int _{C_ t}u$ independent of $t$. Fubini in the product band therefore gives

853E7016EF11 10.79.12
\begin{equation} \int _ X\beta \wedge u=\int _ Cu. \end{equation}

An empty regular fiber gives zero and causes no exception. For a holomorphic $u$, the $(1,0)$ part of $\beta $ wedges with $u$ to zero. Hence $\mathcal S$ of its $(0,1)$ class is minus the period along $C$, which lies in $\Gamma _ X$. Joining the components to a base point changes no closed-form integral. This proves $\mathcal S\Lambda _ X\subset \Gamma _ X$.

Conversely any piecewise smooth loop on an oriented surface splits, after small homotopies in coordinate disks, into finitely many embedded oriented circles: choose finitely many coordinate arcs in transverse position, split at their finitely many double crossings and round the corners. The general coordinate-arc construction in the proof of lemma 10.77.6 justifies these operations; it precedes and does not use that lemma's cohomology hypothesis. Local primitives preserve all closed-form periods. An embedded circle has a two-sided collar, with normal coordinate $t$. The closed form $\beta =\chi (t)dt$, of normal integral one, extends smoothly by zero off the collar. Its periods on all loops are integers: put a loop in transverse position to the collar center and use on the complement of the center the primitives obtained by integrating from the two outer ends. A crossing contributes its sign, and all other increments cancel. The integral-period characterization in theorem 10.79.2 puts its class in $H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})$. Orient the normal coordinate so that $dt$ followed by the given circle orientation is positive. As in (10.79.12), $\int _ X\beta \wedge u=\int _ Cu$; using $-\beta $ under $\mathcal S$ realizes the positive period of $C$. Summing over the split loop proves $\Gamma _ X\subset \mathcal S\Lambda _ X$.

It remains to check the integral torsion assertion, rather than assume it from an integral duality theorem. The constant coefficient sequence $0\to \mathbb Z\to \mathbb R\to \mathbb R/\mathbb Z\to 0$ gives

\[ H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb R})\to H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z})\to H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})\to H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb R}). \]

The last arrow is injective by lemma 10.79.7, so the first arrow is onto. By the coefficient/monodromy theorem this means that every character of $G=\pi _1(X)_{\rm ab}$ into $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ lifts to a real character. If the finite torsion subgroup $T$ of $G=\mathbb Z^{2g}\oplus T$ were nonzero, it would have a nonzero character into $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$: quotient by a maximal proper subgroup gives a cyclic group of prime order, and embed that group as the corresponding roots of unity. Extend the character by zero on the free summand. No real character can lift it, since real characters kill torsion. Thus $T=0$.

Finally a loop's integration current is closed and has real de Rham class represented by the sum of its collar forms $\beta $ above. Indeed their integrals against every closed one-form coincide; corollary 10.43.11 and the perfect de Rham Hodge pairing of corollary 10.46.10 identify these classes. Its restriction to real forms is perfect: the real Laplacian commutes with conjugation and a real harmonic form pairs with its real Hodge star to give its positive squared norm. This gives a well-defined integral class: integer $H^1$ injects into real $H^1$, so it is independent of the chosen splitting or collars. Under $\mathcal S$ it maps to minus the loop's period. The period map is an isomorphism from $G$ onto $\Gamma _ X$, by the evaluation theorem and $T=0$. The equality of lattices just proved makes the loop-current map an isomorphism onto $H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})$. It is natural under orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms, by the integration-current characterization. Through isothermal coordinates the conclusion applies to every connected compact oriented smooth real surface by theorem 10.77.1, not just one with a previously chosen complex atlas. $\square$
