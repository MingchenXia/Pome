Proposition 7.24.1D0960BD969B2

Proposition 7.24.1.D0960BD969B2 For every $n\ge 3$ there is a nonnegative function $f\in C^2(\mathbb C^ n)$ such that the unique solution

\[ u\in \operatorname {PSH}(B)\cap C(\overline B),\qquad (dd^ cu)^ n=f\, dV_{2n},\qquad u|_{\partial B}=0, \qquad B=B(0,1), \]

does not belong to $C^{1,1}$ on any neighborhood of the origin. Here $dd^ c=i\partial \bar\partial /(2\pi )$.

Proof. Write $z=(z',w)\in \mathbb C^{n-1}\times \mathbb C$, and set

\[ \alpha =4,\qquad \beta =6n-7,\qquad \eta (t)=\begin{cases} \exp (-1/(1-t^2)),& 0\le t<1,\\ 0,& t\ge 1.\end{cases} \]

Define $f(z',w)=|z'|^\beta \eta (|w|/|z'|^\alpha )$ for $z'\ne 0$, and put $f(0,w)=0$. This is smooth off $\{ z'=0\} $: the function $\eta $ is even at zero and flat at $1$. On its support, $|w|\le |z'|^\alpha $. For a real derivative $D$ of order $j\le 2$, the chain rule gives

\[ |Df(z',w)|\le C_ j|z'|^{\beta -j\alpha }\quad (0<|z'|\le 1). \]

Indeed a $w$ derivative costs at most $|z'|^{-\alpha }$ and a $z'$ derivative at most $|z'|^{-1}$; the flat cutoff bounds every remaining factor. All these derivatives tend to zero at the origin since $\beta -2\alpha =6n-15>0$. The function vanishes near every point $(0,w)$ with $w\ne 0$. At the origin $f=O(|z|^\beta )$ and its first derivatives are $O(|z|^{\beta -\alpha })$, so their derivatives there also exist and are zero. Thus the zero extensions of the derivatives through order two are continuous and $f\in C^2(\mathbb C^ n)$.

The canonical continuous-density Dirichlet theorem supplies $u$ and uniqueness (apply it to the density $\pi ^ nf/n!$ relative to $(dd^ c|z|^2)^ n$). Unitary invariance of the data and uniqueness show that $u$ depends only on $r=|z'|$ and $t=|w|$; write its value as $U(r,t)$. It is increasing in each radius whenever the corresponding product of balls is contained in $B$. To see this, restrict to a complex line through the origin in that variable and apply the subharmonic maximum principle on centered discs. In particular $u(0)$ is the minimum. Also $u<0$ on $B$: it is subharmonic with zero boundary values, and is not constant since $f>0$ on a nonempty open subset.

Put $s(t)=U(0,t)$ for $0\le t\le 1$. The function $h(x)=s(e^ x)$ is convex on $(-\infty ,0)$: on an annulus the harmonic function interpolating its constant boundary values is affine in $\log |w|$, and dominates the subharmonic restriction of $u$ to the $w$ axis. Its limit at $-\infty $ is $s(0)$. Set

\[ R(t)=\min \{ t^{1/\alpha },\sqrt{1-t^2}\} ,\qquad b(t)=U(R(t),t),\qquad 0<t<1, \]

using the continuous boundary value $0$ when $R(t)^2+t^2=1$. These functions are continuous and $b\ge s$.

We first show that $h$ is locally affine wherever $b(t)>s(t)$. Fix such a $t$. On a sufficiently short closed interval $[t_1,t_2]\subset (0,1)$ containing $t$, the affine interpolant $L(x)$ of $h(\log t_1),h(\log t_2)$ satisfies $L(\log \tau )<b(\tau )$ for $t_1\le \tau \le t_2$, by continuity and the positive gap. On

\[ D=\{ (z',w)\in B:t_1<|w|<t_2,\ |z'|^\alpha <|w|\} \]

the density vanishes. On its two annular ends $u\ge L(\log |w|)$ by radial monotonicity; on its remaining boundary the value is $b$. The comparison principle for the bounded continuous solution with $(dd^ cu)^ n=0$ therefore gives $L(\log |w|)\le u$ throughout $D$; the comparison function is pluriharmonic since $w\ne 0$. On the axis convexity gives the reverse inequality. Consequently $h=L$ on $[\log t_1,\log t_2]$, as asserted.

Next $s(t)>s(0)$ for every $t>0$. Otherwise let $t_0>0$ be the last radius up to which $s$ equals $s(0)$. It is less than $1$, because $s(0)<0=s(1)$. We have $b(t_0)>s(0)$. This is immediate if $b(t_0)=0$. In the other case, equality would make $u$ constant on

\[ \{ |z'|<R(t_0),\ |w|<t_0\} \subset B \]

by radial monotonicity and the global minimum. This contradicts the positive density on an open subset of that product. Thus $b>s$ near $t_0$, and the preceding paragraph makes $h$ affine there. It is constant to the left, hence also to the right, contradicting the choice of $t_0$.

There must now be a sequence $\varepsilon _ k\downarrow 0$ with $b(\varepsilon _ k)=s(\varepsilon _ k)$. If $b>s$ on some $(0,\delta )$, the locally affine function $h$ would be affine on $(-\infty ,\log \delta )$. Its finite limit at $-\infty $ forces it to be constant there, contrary to the preceding paragraph. For small $\varepsilon =\varepsilon _ k$, write $\lambda =\varepsilon ^{1/\alpha }$; then $R(\varepsilon )=\lambda $ and

\[ u(0)\le u(z',w)\le s(\varepsilon ) \quad \text{if } |z'|\le \lambda ,\ |w|\le \varepsilon . \]

The whole closed product lies in $B$ for small $\varepsilon $.

Let $d=(\varepsilon \lambda ^{n-1})^{2/n}$ and define on the unit ball

\[ v(\zeta ',\zeta _ n) =\frac{u(\lambda \zeta ',\varepsilon \zeta _ n)-s(\varepsilon )}{d}. \]

It is bounded, continuous and plurisubharmonic, with $v(0)\le v\le 0$. Biholomorphic covariance and homogeneity give

\[ (dd^ cv)^ n =f(\lambda \zeta ',\varepsilon \zeta _ n)\, dV_{2n}(\zeta ). \]

The bounded Chern–Levine–Nirenberg estimate on the half ball gives

\[ \int _{B(0,1/2)}(dd^ cv)^ n\le C_ n(-v(0))^ n. \]

On the fixed set

\[ \{ 1/8<|\zeta '|<1/4,\quad |\zeta _ n|<\tfrac 12\, 8^{-\alpha }\} \subset B(0,1/2), \]

the argument of $\eta $ is at most $1/2$, because $\varepsilon =\lambda ^\alpha $. The density is therefore at least $c_ n\lambda ^\beta $. Combining the positive volume of that set with the preceding upper bound yields

\[ s(\varepsilon _ k)-s(0) \ge c_ n\varepsilon _ k^{(2\alpha +2(n-1)+\beta )/(n\alpha )} =c_ n\varepsilon _ k^{\, 2-1/(4n)}. \]

If $u$ were $C^{1,1}$ near the origin, invariance under $w\mapsto -w$ would give zero first derivative on that axis and hence $|s(t)-s(0)|\le Ct^2$. This contradicts the displayed lower bound. $\square$
