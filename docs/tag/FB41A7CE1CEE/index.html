Theorem 5.58.3FB41A7CE1CEE

Theorem 5.58.3.FB41A7CE1CEE Let $1\leq p<n$. The closed positive $\mathrm U(n)$-invariant currents of bidegree $(p,p)$ on $X$ are exactly

2A9B47FEBEC7 5.58.4
\begin{equation} T=u(t)\beta ^ p+(Du)(dt)\wedge \theta \wedge \beta ^{p-1}, \end{equation}

where $u$ is a nonnegative nondecreasing locally bounded function on $\mathbb R$ and $Du$ is its distributional derivative measure. There is a unique such $u$ when its right-continuous representative is chosen. Equivalently,

\[ T=(dd^ c\chi (t))^ p, \]

where $\chi $ is a finite convex nondecreasing function on $\mathbb R$. This potential is unique up to an additive constant. The product is the locally bounded Bedford–Taylor product on $X$.

Proof. First suppose $T$ is smooth. At a point on the last coordinate axis the stabilizer is $\mathrm U(n-1)$. Its diagonal phases annihilate the coefficients $dz_ I\wedge d\bar z_ J$ with unequal index sets; permutations of the first $n-1$ coordinates leave only two coefficients, according as the radial index occurs or does not. Consequently

\[ T=u(t)\beta ^ p+v(t)\gamma \wedge \beta ^{p-1}. \]

Both tensors are nonzero and independent for $1\leq p<n$. Testing a tangential complex $p$-plane and a plane containing the radial direction shows that positivity is equivalent to $u,v\geq 0$. These conditions are also sufficient, since both tensors are positive. As $d\gamma =-dt\wedge \beta $, closedness is exactly

\[ (u'-v)dt\wedge \beta ^ p=0, \]

and $dt\wedge \beta ^ p\ne 0$ in this range. Thus $v=u'$.

For a general $T$, apply lemma 5.58.2. On each compact polar interval the two smooth tensors above have smooth dual coefficient extractors. In particular distributional convergence of $T_\varepsilon $ extracts distributions $U,V$ on that interval from $u_\varepsilon (t),v_\varepsilon (t)$. One may construct the extractors by inverting their positive definite Gram matrix on the sphere and integrating there. Positivity makes $U,V$ positive Radon measures: alternatively their masses are bounded by a fixed multiple of the trace mass of $T_\varepsilon $ on the interval, as in coefficient measures and compactness of positive currents. Thus $DU=V$. These extractions agree on overlapping intervals.

An elementary one-dimensional distribution argument now identifies $U$. On an interval choose a cumulative function $g$ for $V$; Fubini applied to compactly supported tests gives $Dg=V$. Hence $D(U-g)=0$. A distribution with derivative zero is constant: every compactly supported smooth function of integral zero is the derivative of a compactly supported smooth function. It follows that $U=(g+c)dt$. The positivity of $U$ makes $u=g+c$ nonnegative almost everywhere, and $Du=V\geq 0$ makes it nondecreasing. Its right-continuous representative is finite and locally bounded; the interval descriptions agree. This proves the asserted formula and uniqueness, including its measure-valued second coefficient.

Put $w=u^{1/p}$ and choose an antiderivative $\chi $ of $w$. It is finite, convex, nondecreasing and locally Lipschitz. Smooth one-dimensional convolutions $\chi _ j$ are convex and nondecreasing and converge locally uniformly to $\chi $. For $w_ j=\chi _ j'$ the functions $w_ j$ are nonnegative and locally uniformly bounded, and $w_ j^ p\to u$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, by almost- everywhere convergence of convolutions at continuity points of $w$. The smooth radial Hessian has nonnegative tangential and radial eigenvalues, so $\chi _ j(t)$ is plurisubharmonic, and

DB4651A869BB 5.58.5
\begin{equation} (dd^ c\chi _ j(t))^ p =w_ j^ p\beta ^ p+D(w_ j^ p)(dt)\wedge \theta \wedge \beta ^{p-1}. \end{equation}

The right side converges distributionally to (5.58.4). Uniform convergence on compact annuli and uniform convergence of bounded Bedford–Taylor products identify the left limit with $(dd^ c\chi (t))^ p$; they also prove that $\chi (t)$ is plurisubharmonic. This proves existence and the converse. The coefficient $u$ fixes $\chi '=u^{1/p}$ almost everywhere, which proves uniqueness up to a constant. At a jump the measure $Du$ has mass $u(t+)-u(t-)$; no value of a product $p w^{p-1}Dw$ at that jump is being asserted. $\square$
