Example 20.6.50C462992AB3A

Example 20.6.5.0C462992AB3A On the three-dimensional quadric

\[ X=\{ x_1x_2+x_3x_4=0\} \subset \mathbb C^4, \qquad A=\{ x_1=x_3=0\} , \]

the effective Weil divisor $A$ is not a Cartier cycle at zero.

Proof. The rank-one matrix parametrization

\[ F:\mathbb C^4\longrightarrow X,\qquad (a,b,c,d)\longmapsto (ac,bd,ad,-bc) \]

is surjective. It is surjective as a germ at zero as well: every sufficiently small rank-one matrix factors as a column times a row with both factors small, by scaling the factors to equal norm. Thus the germ of $X$ is irreducible, since an analytic decomposition would pull back to one of the irreducible smooth germ $\mathbb C^4$. The quadric is pure of dimension three. Its nonzero points are regular, by its gradient, and its origin is singular: its lowest quadratic form has rank four, so cannot factor into two linear forms, and its reduced principal ideal has no linear term. Its tangent dimension there is four. In particular the singular set is the point zero.

Suppose $A$ were Cartier as a cycle. Its local description in the proposition would give a meromorphic function $f=g/h$ with $\operatorname {div}(f)=A$, where $g,h$ are holomorphic germs nonzero on $X$. Pullback by $F$ is defined: germwise surjectivity makes $h\circ F$ nonzero. Outside

\[ F^{-1}(0)=\{ a=b=0\} \cup \{ c=d=0\} , \]

the map is a submersion onto $X_{\rm reg}$. Indeed, for nonzero column $u=(a,b)$ and row $v=(c,d)$, the kernel of $(\dot u,\dot v)\mapsto \dot u v+u\dot v$ consists exactly of $(t u,-t v)$ and has dimension one. There the pullback of $A$ is the reduced hypersurface $a=0$. For example where $b\ne 0$ its normal coordinate is $a/b$, and where $a=0$ one has $b\ne 0$ off the displayed exceptional set. Pullback of divisors by this submersion therefore gives $\operatorname {div}(f\circ F)=[a=0]$ off that set. The exceptional set has codimension two, so it cannot support any additional hypersurface in a meromorphic divisor. The required smooth local factorization follows from the already proved Noetherianity of convergent power-series modules, local equations and currents of irreducible hypersurfaces: factorization into nonunits terminates by the ascending chain condition on principal ideals; each irreducible factor is associated to the prime equation of its hypersurface branch. Such factors are therefore prime. Applying this to the numerator and denominator of $f\circ F$ cancels all factors except one $a$, giving $f\circ F=a e$ for a holomorphic unit $e$ at zero.

For $t\in \mathbb C^*$ the transformation $(a,b,c,d)\mapsto (ta,tb,t^{-1}c,t^{-1}d)$ preserves $F$. For each fixed $t$ close to one, the equality of meromorphic germs $a e=t a\, e(ta,tb,t^{-1}c,t^{-1}d)$ permits cancellation of $a$. Evaluating at zero gives $e(0)=t e(0)$, impossible for $t\ne 1$ because $e$ is a unit. This proves non-Cartierness as a cycle, without confusing it with nonprincipality of the reduced ideal alone. $\square$
