Theorem 11.48.15E96E372480D4

Theorem 11.48.15.E96E372480D4 Define

\[ \Delta '_\tau =[D'+\tau ,\delta '+\tau ^*],\qquad \Delta _\tau =[D+\tau ,\delta +\tau ^*]. \]

These are nonnegative formally self-adjoint second-order operators with the same principal parts as $\Delta '$ and $[D,\delta ]$ respectively. On every smooth bundle-valued bidegree,

2F5B471E480A
\begin{align} \Delta ''& =\Delta ' +[\mathrm i\Theta ,\Lambda ]+[D',\tau ^*]-[D'',\bar\tau ^*], \tag{11.48.16}\\ \Delta ''& =\Delta '_\tau +[\mathrm i\Theta ,\Lambda ]+T_\omega , \tag{11.48.17}\\ \Delta _\tau & =\Delta '_\tau +\Delta ”. \tag{11.48.18} \end{align}

The operator $T_\omega $ is pointwise Hermitian and depends only on the first and second derivatives of the base metric, through $\partial \omega $ and $\partial \bar\partial \omega $ and their metric contractions. For every compactly supported smooth form $u$,

9CAE0D2A2536 11.48.19
\begin{equation} \| D''u\| ^2+\| \delta ''u\| ^2 =\| (D'+\tau )u\| ^2+\| (\delta '+\tau ^*)u\| ^2 +\int _ X\langle ([\mathrm i\Theta ,\Lambda ]+T_\omega )u,u\rangle \, \mathrm dV. \end{equation}

Proof. By the contraction identity, $\delta ''=-\mathrm i[\Lambda ,D']-\bar\tau ^*$. The Chern curvature relation $[D',D'']=\Theta \wedge $ and Jacobi give

\[ [D'',[\Lambda ,D']] =[\Lambda ,\Theta ]+\mathrm i[D',\delta '+\tau ^*]. \]

Multiply by $-\mathrm i$ and subtract $[D'',\bar\tau ^*]$ to obtain (11.48.16). Substitution of (11.48.12) combines its remaining three terms into

\[ \Delta '+[D',\tau ^*]+[\tau ,\delta ']+[\tau ,\tau ^*]+T_\omega =\Delta '_\tau +T_\omega , \]

proving (11.48.17). The mixed identity gives $[D'+\tau ,\delta '']=0$, and its formal adjoint is $[D'',\delta '+\tau ^*]=0$. Expanding $\Delta _\tau $ proves (11.48.18).

For either odd operator $Q=D'+\tau $ or $Q=D+\tau $, the graded bracket $[Q,Q^*]=QQ^*+Q^*Q$ has quadratic form $\| Qu\| ^2+\| Q^*u\| ^2$ and is formally self-adjoint. Adding the order-zero $\tau $ does not change the second-order principal part. Integrating (11.48.17) now gives the energy identity.

The pointwise curvature computation in proposition 10.33.6 uses only a unitary exterior coframe and the Chern curvature symmetry, so applies to the present Hermitian metric as well. In that coframe write $\mathrm i\Theta =\mathrm i\sum R_{j\bar k}\theta _ j\wedge \bar\theta _ k$. Write $\epsilon _ j,\bar\epsilon _ j$ for multiplication by $\theta _ j,\bar\theta _ j$. In a Chern-normal frame $R_{j\bar k}=-\partial _ j\partial _{\bar k}H$, so $R_{j\bar k}^*=R_{k\bar j}$, and that computation gives

\[ [\mathrm i\Theta ,\Lambda ] =\sum _{j,k}R_{j\bar k} (\epsilon _ j\iota _ k+\bar\epsilon _ k\bar\iota _ j-\delta _{jk}). \]

Taking its adjoint and exchanging $j,k$ proves it is Hermitian in every bidegree. The difference of the three formally self-adjoint operators $\Delta ''$, $\Delta '_\tau $ and this curvature operator is $T_\omega $. Since $T_\omega $ has order zero, formal self-adjointness implies pointwise Hermitian symmetry: testing its skew-adjoint part against arbitrary compactly supported coefficient vectors forces that smooth matrix to vanish at each point. The explicit formula (11.48.13) proves the claimed dependence on the base metric. $\square$
