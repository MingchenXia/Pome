Proposition 7.23.2EC436652E435

Proposition 7.23.2.EC436652E435 For $n\geq 2$ there is an open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ on which no positive Radon measure-valued $n$-linear map on all plurisubharmonic $n$-tuples extends the locally bounded Bedford–Taylor product. Here multilinearity means additivity and nonnegative homogeneity on the plurisubharmonic cone. Neither locality nor a convergence hypothesis is needed for this obstruction.

Proof. Use the canonical infinite-local-mass example in two variables, restricted to $|x|<1/4$, $|y|<e^{-1}$. Put

\[ L=-\log |y|,\qquad u(x,y)=(|x|^2-1)\sqrt L\quad (y\ne 0), \qquad u(x,0)=-\infty . \]

It is plurisubharmonic and smooth off $y=0$. The checked Hessian formula is

\[ H_ u=\begin{pmatrix} \sqrt L & -\bar x/(4\bar y\sqrt L) \\ -x/(4y\sqrt L) & (1-|x|^2)/(16|y|^2L^{3/2}) \end{pmatrix},\qquad \det H_ u=\frac{1-2|x|^2}{16|y|^2L}. \]

We need an additional gradient bound. Set $B=\operatorname {diag}(\sqrt L,1/(16|y|^2L^{3/2}))$. The diagonal entries of $B^{-1/2}H_ uB^{-1/2}$ are $1,1-|x|^2$, and its off-diagonal entry has modulus $|x|<1/4$. Consequently $H_ u\geq B/2$. The two first derivatives give

\[ |u_ x|^2/\sqrt L +16|y|^2L^{3/2}|u_ y|^2 =\bigl (|x|^2+(1-|x|^2)^2\bigr )\sqrt L \leq \frac{17}{16}\sqrt L. \]

Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives

\[ |\partial u(v)|^2\leq 4(-u)H_ u(v,\bar v) \quad (v\in \mathbb C^2), \]

since $-u\geq 15\sqrt L/16$ and $2(17/16)/(15/16)=34/15<4$.

On

\[ \Omega =\{ |x|<1/4,\ |y|<e^{-1},\ |z'|<1\} \subset \mathbb C^2\times \mathbb C^{n-2} \]

put $U=u+|z'|^2-2$; omit the last group when $n=2$. Its Hessian off $y=0$ is $\operatorname {diag}(H_ u,I)$. In the dual Hessian norm the square gradient has length at most $4(-u)+|z'|^2\leq 4(-U)$. Thus

\[ |\partial U(v)|^2\leq 4(-U)H_ U(v,\bar v),\qquad U<-1. \]

Its smooth Monge–Ampere measure has infinite mass on any compact product with nonempty $x,z'$ interior and $y$-disc centered at zero: the displayed determinant is bounded below by a positive constant times $1/(|y|^2(-\log |y|))$, whose planar integral diverges. Choose such a compact $K\Subset \Omega $ once and for all.

Let $\kappa =1/18$ and, for $s<0$, set

\[ f_ j(s)=\frac{\kappa j s}{j-s},\qquad f_ j'(s)=\frac{\kappa j^2}{(j-s)^2},\qquad f_ j''(s)=\frac{2\kappa j^2}{(j-s)^3}. \]

Define $\psi _ j=f_ j(U)$ off $y=0$ and $\psi _ j=-\kappa j$ on that hyperplane. Because $f_ j$ is increasing and convex, $\psi _ j$ is plurisubharmonic off the hyperplane. Its bounded continuous extension is plurisubharmonic by the canonical removable-set theorem, and $-\kappa j\leq \psi _ j<0$ on $\Omega $. The remainder $r_ j=U-\psi _ j$ is also plurisubharmonic. Indeed, off the hyperplane its Hessian satisfies

\[ H_{r_ j}\geq \left[1-f_ j'(U)-4(-U)f_ j''(U)\right]H_ U \geq \tfrac 12H_ U. \]

For the last inequality put $q=(-U)/j>0$ and use

\[ f_ j'(U)+4(-U)f_ j''(U) =\kappa \left((1+q)^{-2}+8q(1+q)^{-3}\right) \leq 9\kappa =\tfrac 12. \]

Moreover $r_ j<0$ and $r_ j\to -\infty $ on approaching $y=0$, so its upper-limit extension with value $-\infty $ is plurisubharmonic there. This gives the exact cone decomposition $U=\psi _ j+r_ j$ on all of $\Omega $.

Suppose the proposed map $T$ existed. Multilinearity and positivity give the measure inequality

\[ T(U,\ldots ,U)\geq T(\psi _ j,\ldots ,\psi _ j) =(dd^ c\psi _ j)^ n. \]

On compact subsets off the hyperplane, $f_ j(U)\to \kappa U$ with every derivative, since $f_ j$ and its derivatives converge locally to those of $s\mapsto \kappa s$. Fatou's lemma for the smooth positive densities on $K\setminus \{ y=0\} $ therefore gives

\[ \liminf _ j\int _ K(dd^ c\psi _ j)^ n \geq \int _{K\setminus \{ y=0\} }(dd^ c(\kappa U))^ n=+\infty . \]

This contradicts the finite mass of the fixed Radon measure $T(U,\ldots ,U)$ on $K$, proving the assertion. $\square$
