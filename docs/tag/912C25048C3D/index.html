Theorem 2.18.4912C25048C3D

Theorem 2.18.4.912C25048C3D Let $M$ be compact of positive dimension and let $P:E\to F$ be elliptic of positive order $d$, with equal bundle ranks. Then for every real $s$ the bounded operator

\[ P:H^{s+d}(M,E)\longrightarrow H^ s(M,F) \]

is Fredholm. Its kernel $K$ and the distributional kernel $K^*$ of its formal adjoint are finite dimensional and smooth, independent of $s$, and its range is

\[ \{ f\in H^ s(M,F):\langle f,v\rangle =0\text{ for every }v\in K^*\} . \]

Here the pairing with a smooth section is the distributional extension of the $L^2$ pairing. There are continuous direct sums

\[ H^ s(M,F)=P H^{s+d}(M,E)\oplus K^*,\qquad C^\infty (M,F)=P C^\infty (M,E)\oplus K^*. \]

They are orthogonal for $s=0$ in the first identity and in the $L^2$ pairing for the smooth identity. The index is $\dim K-\dim K^*$ and does not depend on the Sobolev order.

Proof. All distributional null solutions are smooth by the preceding theorem. Fix $s$, put $a=s+d$, and use its estimate with remainder $H^{a-1}$. On the kernel the $H^ a$ norm is bounded by the $H^{a-1}$ norm. Compactness of that inclusion makes the closed unit ball of the kernel compact in $H^ a$: a lower-order convergent subsequence is upgraded by the estimate on differences. An infinite orthonormal sequence would contradict compactness, so the kernel is finite dimensional. Its elements are exactly the fixed smooth space $K$.

Let $\Pi _ K$ be the finite-rank projection defined using an $L^2$ orthonormal basis of $K$. Pairing with its smooth members makes $\Pi _ K$ continuous in every Sobolev order. There is a bound

\[ \| u\| _{H^ a}\leq C_ s\| Pu\| _{H^ s} \qquad (\Pi _ Ku=0). \]

Otherwise choose a normalized sequence violating it, with $Pu_ j\to 0$. After a subsequence it converges in $H^{a-1}$; the general estimate on differences makes it converge in $H^ a$. Its limit has norm one, is in $K$ and has projection zero, a contradiction. If $Pu_ j$ converges in $H^ s$, subtract $\Pi _ Ku_ j$ and apply this bound to differences. The resulting sections converge in $H^ a$ and their limit maps to the target limit. The range is therefore closed.

Equal ranks make the principal symbol invertible. The adjoint symbol formula makes $P^*$ elliptic as well. The same argument gives its finite-dimensional smooth distributional kernel $K^*$. The annihilator of the range in $(H^ s)^*=H^{-s}$ consists precisely of distributional solutions of $P^*v=0$: test first against smooth domain sections and then use density. Thus it is exactly $K^*$. A closed linear subspace is the intersection of the kernels of its continuous annihilators, by Hilbert projection and Riesz representation. This proves the range description and finite codimension. Its $L^2$ projection onto $K^*$ is continuous on $H^ s$, giving the first direct sum and index.

For a smooth target, the Sobolev decomposition at $s=0$ writes $f=Pu+v$ with $u\in H^ d$ and $v\in K^*$. The right side $Pu=f-v$ is smooth, so regularity makes $u$ smooth. Conversely the range annihilates $K^*$ by integration by parts. Thus the smooth range is the common kernel of finitely many continuous smooth functionals, and is closed in the smooth topology. This proves the second direct sum and its orthogonality. The index formula uses the same smooth spaces for every $s$. $\square$
