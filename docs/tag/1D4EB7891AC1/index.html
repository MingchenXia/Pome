Theorem 19.11.11D4EB7891AC1

Ambient manifolds are connected unless stated otherwise. Fibers need not be connected; the section-envelope arguments treat their connected components separately.

Theorem 19.11.1.1D4EB7891AC1 Let $Z$ be smooth projective, $(F,h_ F)$ a singular semipositive line bundle with $\mathcal I(h_ F)=\mathcal O_ Z$, and suppose $K_ Z+F$ is pseudo-effective. Let $A$ be ample with a smooth metric, fix $m_0\geq 1$, and put $D=K_ Z+F+A/m_0$. For $\rho \in \operatorname {Pic}^0(Z)$ give $\rho $ a flat metric. In a local frame $e$ of the rational bundle $D$, let

\[ B_\rho (x)=\sup _{k,s}\{ |s/e^ k|_\rho ^{2/k}(x): s\in H^0(Z,kD+\rho ),\quad \int _ Z |s|_\rho ^{2/k}h_ Fh_ A^{1/m_0}\leq 1\} . \]

Integers are sufficiently divisible that the bundles exist. The upper semicontinuous regularization of $\log B_\rho $ is independent of $\rho $. It is the regularized envelope allowing all flat twists. The associated extremal singular metric on $D$ is thus independent of the chosen twist.

Proof. The envelope is computed separately on each connected component: restriction preserves admissibility, and a section on one component extends by zero to the others. We may therefore assume that $Z$ is connected.

Local submean estimates, using upper bounds for the metric weights, bound these logarithmic envelopes above on smaller charts. The bounds are uniform in flat twists: flat unitary frames have constant norm, and their transition functions have modulus one. Since $D$ is big, one divisible multiple has a nonzero section. Starting with this section, the Nadel construction below produces nonzero sections for every target flat twist and every positive multiple of its degree. This also establishes the required nonemptiness; no separate uniform generation theorem is needed.

Fix a target twist $\rho _0$ and a nonzero section $s\in H^0(Z,k_0D+\rho )$ normalized to root integral one. The integral is finite since $e^{-\phi _ F}$ is locally integrable. For the Nadel construction choose a positive smooth metric $h_ A'$ on $A$, retaining the prescribed $h_ A$ in the section envelopes. Their weight difference is bounded on compact $Z$. Take $k=\ell k_0$ with $\ell \to \infty $ and all required divisibilities. Write $kD+\rho _0=K_ Z+B_ k$. Choose local holomorphic frames of $K_ Z,F,A$ and unitary flat frames of $\rho ,\rho _0$. If $f_ s$ is the coefficient of $s$, put on $B_ k$ the local metric weight

\[ \phi _ F+\phi _{A'}/m_0+\frac{k-1}{k_0}\log |f_ s|^2. \]

These weights have the transition law of $B_ k$, since flat transition factors have modulus one and $B_ k=(k-1)D+F+A/m_0+\rho _0$. Thus they define a metric on the actual line bundle $B_ k$, without choosing a fractional flat line bundle. Its curvature is strictly positive, at least $c_1(A,h_ A')/m_0$. Its multiplier ideal $J_ k$ includes all these weights. It is independent of the flat twist. By theorem 10.102.1, lemma 10.103.1,

\[ h^0(Z,(kD+\rho _0)\otimes J_ k) =h^0(Z,(kD+\ell \rho )\otimes J_ k)>0. \]

Indeed $s^\ell $ belongs to the right side: after cancellation its weighted square norm is finite and comparable to the normalized integral of $|s|^{2/k_0}h_ Fh_ A^{1/m_0}$. Choose a section $u_ k$ on the left and normalize so that

354F33395764 19.11.2
\begin{equation} \int _ Z\frac{|u_ k|^2}{|s|^{2(k-1)/k_0}} h_ F(h_ A')^{1/m_0}=1. \end{equation}

Flat norms and frame factors are understood in this invariant ratio. Hölder's inequality, the bounded metric difference and the normalization of $s$ give $\int |u_ k|^{2/k}h_ Fh_ A^{1/m_0}\leq C^{1/k}$ for fixed $C$ independent of $k$. Rescale $u_ k$ by the fixed factor $C^{-1/2}$ to make it admissible. This changes its normalized logarithmic weight by $O(1/k)$ and leaves the limits below unchanged.

At a generic point of each prime divisor of $s$, the weight of $h_ F$ is bounded above. Integrability in (19.11.2) therefore forces $\operatorname {ord}u_ k\geq (\ell -1)\operatorname {ord}s$. Normality extends this divisibility across codimension two. Thus

\[ u_ k=s^{\ell -1}v_ k,\qquad v_ k\in H^0(Z,k_0D+\rho _0-(\ell -1)\rho ). \]

The weighted norm of $v_ k$ is one for the fixed local weight $w=|s|^{-2(k_0-1)/k_0}h_ F(h_ A')^{1/m_0}$, before this fixed rescaling. Its smooth bundle factor is fixed, and the remaining flat factors are unitary. We check both bounds on its smooth supremum norm, a step needed for the limit. On a finite buffered trivializing cover, $w$ is bounded below by a positive constant. Thus ordinary $L^2$ submean estimates bound $\sup |v_ k|$ above uniformly. For the converse, take coordinate balls $V\Subset U$ from the same fixed buffered cover. The space of sections $J(U)$ of the coherent ideal $J=\mathcal I(w)$ is a closed subspace of $\mathcal O(U)$ with its compact-open Fréchet topology: compact convergence preserves membership in each stalk by Krull closure for analytic modules. Every member is in $L^2(V,w)$: local integrability and a finite cover of $\overline V$ give a finite integral. The inclusion $J(U)\to L^2(V,w)$ has closed graph, because compact convergence and weighted $L^2$ convergence have the same almost-everywhere limit. The Fréchet closed graph theorem therefore bounds its norm by finitely many compact-open seminorms, hence by $C\sup _ K|f|$ for one compact $K\subset U$. All $v_ k$ lie in this same ideal. The fixed smooth bundle factors and unitary flat frames make these bounds independent of their flat twists. Summing over the finite cover gives $1=\| v_ k\| _ w^2\leq C(\sup |v_ k|)^2$. Therefore the smooth supremum is also uniformly bounded below.

Choose a fixed smooth metric $h_0$ on $k_0D$. The functions $f_ k=\log |v_ k|_{h_0,\mathrm{flat}}^2$ have a fixed lower curvature bound and uniformly bounded suprema on both sides. Normalized compactness of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions implies $L^1$ precompactness. Consequently $f_ k/k\to 0$ in $L^1$. In local frames,

\[ \frac1k\log |u_ k|_{\rho _0}^2 =\frac{\ell -1}{k}\log |s|_\rho ^2+\frac1k\log |v_ k|^2 \longrightarrow \frac1{k_0}\log |s|_\rho ^2 \quad \hbox{in }L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}. \]

Each term is bounded above by the regularized target envelope. The inequality passes to the limit almost everywhere and then everywhere by the canonical plurisubharmonic representatives. Take the supremum over $s,k_0,\rho $ and regularize. This gives the reverse inequality to the evident inclusion of the fixed twist among all twists. Equality follows. $\square$
