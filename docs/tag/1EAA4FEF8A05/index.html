Theorem 10.39.11EAA4FEF8A05

Theorem 10.39.1.1EAA4FEF8A05 Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, and let $L\to X$ carry a smooth positive metric. Fix distinct points $x_1,\ldots ,x_ N$, with $N\geq 1$. There is a constant $C>0$ such that

\[ H^0(X,L^{\otimes k})\longrightarrow \bigoplus _{j=1}^ N J_{x_ j}^ m L^{\otimes k} \]

is surjective for all integers $m\geq 0$ and $k\geq C(m+1)$.

Proof. Fix a positive metric $h$ on $L$ and put $\omega =c_1(L,h)$; this is a Kähler form. Give $K_ X^{-1}$ the metric dual to that induced by $\omega $ on $K_ X=\Lambda ^{n,0}T^*X$. Compactness gives $b\geq 0$ with

FB388D7D27DE 10.39.2
\begin{equation} c_1(K_ X^{-1},h_{K^{-1},\omega })\geq -b\omega . \end{equation}

Choose disjoint relatively compact coordinate balls centered at the $x_ j$. In each choose a nonpositive cutoff logarithm $\psi _ j$, equal to $\log |z-z(x_ j)|^2$ plus a constant near $x_ j$ and zero near and outside the boundary of its ball. The cutoff construction in weighted point extension on compact Kähler manifolds, Step 1, gives these functions; each is quasi-plurisubharmonic, smooth away from $x_ j$. Its Hessian is nonnegative in the inner ball and has a finite lower bound on the cutoff annulus. Hence there is $B\geq 0$, independent of $m$ and $k$, such that

\[ dd^ c\sum _ j\psi _ j\geq -B\omega . \]

Put $\Psi =(n+m)\sum _ j\psi _ j$. On $M=X\setminus \{ x_1,\ldots ,x_ N\} $ the line bundle and smooth metric

\[ F=L^{\otimes k}\otimes K_ X^{-1},\qquad h_ F=(h^ k\otimes h_{K^{-1},\omega })\mathrm e^{-\Psi } \]

therefore satisfy

E2EBA8B81545 10.39.3
\begin{equation} c_1(F,h_ F)\geq \bigl (k-b-(n+m)B\bigr )\omega \geq \omega \quad \hbox{if } k\geq b+(n+m)B+1. \end{equation}

The constant $C=b+1+nB$ has the property that $k\geq C(m+1)$ implies this last inequality.

Represent the prescribed jets in local holomorphic frames by polynomials of degree at most $m$. Smooth cutoffs supported in the chosen balls, equal to one near their centers, give a global smooth section $G$ of $L^{\otimes k}$ with exactly those holomorphic germs near the $x_ j$. Thus $g=\bar\partial G$ is closed and vanishes near every $x_ j$.

To apply the estimate to $F$-valued forms, use the canonical identification

\[ \mathcal I_ q(\alpha \otimes e) =(\alpha \wedge \tau )\otimes (e\otimes \tau ^{-1}), \]

where $e$ is a local frame of $L^{\otimes k}$, $\alpha $ has type $(0,q)$ and $\tau $ is a local nowhere zero holomorphic $n$-form. It is independent of $\tau $, preserves pointwise norms for $\omega $ and the indicated tensor metrics, and commutes with $\bar\partial $: the factor $\tau $ is appended on the right and is holomorphic. The weighted norm of $\mathcal I_1g$ is finite, since its support is a compact subset of $M$. By complete metrics and cutoffs away from analytic poles, applied to $\Psi $, $M$ has the auxiliary Kähler form and cutoffs required in the complete-metric line-bundle $L^2$ estimate. That estimate, (10.39.3) and $\mathcal I_ q$ give a section $u$ on $M$ satisfying

7EDB696C7087 10.39.4
\begin{equation} \bar\partial u=g,\qquad \int _ M |u|_{h^ k}^2\mathrm e^{-\Psi }\, \mathrm dV_\omega <\infty . \end{equation}

As $\Psi \leq 0$, this implies unweighted local square integrability near the missing points. The section $s=G-u$ is holomorphic on $M$ in the distributional sense, hence holomorphic there by holomorphic distributions and minimal weighted solutions, and is locally square integrable across the finite analytic set. By $L^2$ holomorphic removal across analytic subsets, it extends to a global holomorphic section of $L^{\otimes k}$.

Near $x_ j$, the section $u=G-s$ is now holomorphic. In its local frame write its coefficient as $a(z)$, with $z(x_ j)=0$. Smooth metric and volume comparisons, and the form of $\Psi $ near $x_ j$, show that (10.39.4) implies

\[ \int _{|z|<r}|a(z)|^2|z|^{-2(n+m)}\, \mathrm dV_{2n}(z)<\infty . \]

If the first nonzero homogeneous Taylor term of $a$ has degree $\ell $, orthogonality of different homogeneous degrees on the unit sphere gives a positive lower bound by a constant times

\[ \int _0^ r t^{2\ell -2m-1}\, \mathrm dt. \]

Here orthogonality follows by invariance of the sphere under $z\mapsto \mathrm e^{\mathrm i\theta }z$; the nonzero leading polynomial has strictly positive spherical square integral. The last integral diverges for $\ell \leq m$, including logarithmic divergence when $\ell =m$. Thus $u$ vanishes to order at least $m+1$ at every $x_ j$, and $s$ has the prescribed jets. If $a$ is identically zero the same conclusion holds. All the constants were fixed before $m,k$ and the prescribed jet values were chosen. $\square$
