Theorem 3.8.1E2B11E803D94

Theorem 3.8.1.E2B11E803D94 Every proper simply connected planar domain is biholomorphic to the unit disc. On a bounded such domain $D$, its positive Green function constructs a biholomorphism $F:D\to \mathbb D$ with $F(a)=0$ and

\[ G_ D(z,a)=-\log |F(z)|. \]

Proof. First let $D$ be bounded. The canonical planar-continuum regularity theorem makes every boundary point regular. The canonical Dirichlet/ Green theorem therefore supplies $G=G_ D(\cdot ,a)>0$, zero at every boundary point, with

\[ H(z)=G(z)+\log |z-a| \]

extending harmonically across $a$. The closed one-form $-H_ y\, dx+H_ x\, dy$ has a primitive $K$ on $D$. Indeed it is closed because $\Delta H=0$; its integrals on closed paths vanish by contracting the path and subdividing a contracting homotopy into coordinate rectangles on which Stokes' formula applies. Path integration then defines $K$, with $K_ x=-H_ y$ and $K_ y=H_ x$. Thus $H+\mathrm iK$ is holomorphic, and

\[ F(z)=(z-a)\exp (-H(z)-\mathrm iK(z)) \]

is holomorphic with just one zero, a simple zero at $a$. Its modulus is $e^{-G}<1$ off $a$, and tends to one at every boundary point. Consequently $F$ is proper as a map to $\mathbb D$: the preimage of a compact subset stays away from every boundary point, and is a closed subset of the bounded domain. Compactness of $\partial D$ gives the required uniform separation.

For completeness, a proper nonconstant holomorphic map between planar domains has a constant number of preimages counted with multiplicity. Its fibers are finite, since they are compact sets of isolated zeros. Around the finitely many preimages of $w_0$ choose disjoint small closed discs on whose boundaries $F-w_0$ is nonzero. For $w$ close to $w_0$, all its preimages lie in those discs: otherwise properness would give an extra preimage of $w_0$ by taking a convergent subsequence. On each boundary the integral

\[ \frac1{2\pi \mathrm i}\int \frac{F'(z)}{F(z)-w}\, dz \]

is a continuous integer, equal to the number of zeros by local factorization and Cauchy's formula. It is therefore constant for nearby $w$. The image of $F$ is open and closed in the disc, using properness for closedness, and hence is the whole disc. The locally constant total degree is one, since the fiber over zero consists only of the simple zero $a$. Thus every fiber is one simple point. The holomorphic inverse function theorem gives the holomorphic inverse.

Now let $D\ne \mathbb C$ be simply connected and possibly unbounded. Choose $b\notin D$. Path integration of $1/(z-b)$, with its additive constant normalized at one point, gives a holomorphic logarithm of $z-b$. Its half exponential is a branch $s$ of $\sqrt{z-b}$. This map is injective and has nonzero derivative. Its image $U$ is disjoint from $-U$: opposite values would have equal squares and hence come from the same point, whereas $s$ never vanishes. Choose $B(w_0,r)\subset U$. Then $B(-w_0,r)\cap U=\varnothing $, so

\[ z\longmapsto \frac1{s(z)+w_0} \]

is a bounded injective holomorphic map with nonzero derivative. It maps $D$ biholomorphically onto a bounded simply connected domain. Apply the bounded case and compose. No conformal representation or Riemann mapping existence theorem was used as a premise. $\square$
