Theorem 6.46.2751F9003E019

Theorem 6.46.2.751F9003E019 Let $F:X^ n\to Y^ m$ be a proper finite holomorphic map, with $n\geq 1$, and let $T$ be closed positive of bidimension $(p,p)$. For a finite holomorphic germ $H$ at $x$, put

\[ \sigma (H,x)=\inf \{ a>0:\ |H(z)|\geq c|z-x|^ a \text{ near }x\text{ for some }c>0\} . \]

Define the real upper multiplicity

\[ \overline\mu _ p(F,x)= \inf _ G\frac{\sigma (GF,x)^ p}{\mu _ p(G,F(x))}, \]

where $G:(Y,F(x))\to (\mathbb C^ n,0)$ and $GF$ is finite at $x$. For $p=0$ the ratio is one. Then

\[ \nu (F_*T,y)\leq \sum _{x\in \operatorname {Supp}T\cap F^{-1}(y)} \overline\mu _ p(F,x)\nu (T,x). \]

The set of $G$ in this definition is nonempty, and each denominator is a positive integer. No general integrality of $\overline\mu _ p$ is asserted.

Proof. A proper finite map splits over a small neighborhood of $y$ into disjoint neighborhoods of the finitely many points of its fiber; compactness of inverse images excludes additional points. It suffices to treat a single point $x=0$ of that fiber. The finite image germ $B=F(X,0)$ has dimension $n$. A cone-adapted $n$-row linear projection of $B$ supplies an admissible $G$, so the infimum is nonempty.

For any admissible $G$, its zero fiber in $Y$ has codimension at least $n$. To check this assertion even for a singular image $B$, recall the local intersection inequality $\dim (A\cap B)\geq \dim A+\dim B-m$ at their common point. It follows by taking $A\times B$ and imposing the $m$ diagonal coordinate equations, each of which lowers dimension by at most one. If a branch $A$ of $\{ G=0\} $ had codimension less than $n$, $A\cap B$ would have positive dimension. Its finite inverse image under $F$ would then have positive dimension, contradicting the point fiber of $GF$. Thus $\mu _ p(G,0)$ is defined and positive by the generic-slice theorem. The analytic Nullstellensatz applied to the isolated fiber of $H=GF$ gives powers of the source coordinates in the ideal of its components. Bounded coefficients give a finite exponent in the displayed definition of $\sigma $. The admissible exponents form an upper interval, so every $a>\sigma (H,0)$ is admissible.

The lower-bound theorem applied locally to $G$ on the support of $F_*T$ gives

\[ \mu _ p(G,0)\nu (F_*T,0)\leq \nu (G_*F_*T,0) =\nu (T,\log |GF|^2). \]

The required proper support is supplied by the finite composite and a smaller target neighborhood, as in the preceding proof. The bound $|GF(z)|\geq c|z|^ a$ and the common point pole give $\nu (T,\log |GF|^2)\leq a^ p\nu (T,0)$ by weight comparison. Let $a\downarrow \sigma (H,0)$, then take the infimum over $G$. All these constants are finite and the current numbers are finite; zero current numbers cause no undefined product. Sum the local pieces. For $p=0$, the finite fiber measure is exactly the sum of its atoms, proving the stated upper bound with equality. $\square$
