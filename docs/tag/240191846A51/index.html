Corollary 6.47.2240191846A51

Corollary 6.47.2.240191846A51 For $0<\lambda _1\leq \cdots \leq \lambda _ n$, put $\rho _\lambda (z)=\max _ j\lambda _ j\log |z_ j|^2$. For a closed positive current of bidimension $(p,p)$,

\[ (\lambda _1\cdots \lambda _ p)\nu (T,0) \leq \nu (T,\rho _\lambda ) \leq (\lambda _{n-p+1}\cdots \lambda _ n)\nu (T,0). \]

Both constants are sharp.

Proof. For integer coefficients, the logarithm of the monomial tuple differs by a bounded function from $\rho _\lambda $. Its proper weighted projection formula and the preceding lower and upper map bounds give the inequality. Integration over the first and last $p$ coordinate planes shows sharpness. For real coefficients choose positive integers $s_ j(k)=\lfloor k\lambda _ j\rfloor $ for sufficiently large $k$. Positive homogeneity and coefficientwise monotonicity are the established directional-weight comparison. If two positive coefficient vectors differ by a factor between $1-\epsilon $ and $1+\epsilon $, those same comparisons squeeze their numbers between the corresponding $(1\pm \epsilon )^ p$ multiples. Hence the directional number is continuous in positive coefficients. Divide the integer inequalities by $k^ p$ and pass to $\lambda $. For $p=0$ all numbers are the same point atom and all products are one. $\square$
