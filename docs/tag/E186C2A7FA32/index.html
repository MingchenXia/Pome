Theorem 6.45.2E186C2A7FA32

Theorem 6.45.2.E186C2A7FA32 Let $T$ be closed positive of bidimension $(p,p)$ near zero in $\mathbb C^ n$, $1\leq p\leq n$. Let $\varphi $ be an extended-continuous plurisubharmonic function with its sole pole at zero. Then

B144F857EFF6 6.45.3
\begin{equation} \nu (T,\varphi )\geq \nu (T,0)\, \nu \bigl ((dd^ c\varphi )^ p,0\bigr ). \end{equation}

No Hölder assumption on $e^\varphi $ is required.

Proof. Bounded changes of the weight preserve both numbers in this inequality, by the weight and product comparison theorems. Add $|z|^2$ and apply relative Richberg smoothing on the punctured ball with error at most one. Its extension by $-\infty $ at zero is still plurisubharmonic, since it tends to $-\infty $ there. We can therefore suppose that $\varphi $ is smooth off zero, and subtract a constant to make it negative on a larger ball.

Let $g$ range over a bounded connected neighborhood $\Omega $ of $\mathrm U(n)$ in $\mathrm{GL}_ n(\mathbb C)$, with inverses uniformly bounded. On one smaller source ball the joint weight $\varphi (gz)$ is plurisubharmonic and its low sublevels on $\operatorname {Supp}T$ are uniformly compact: a compact annulus has a finite lower bound for this weight, uniformly in $g$ on a slightly smaller parameter closure. The complete-pluripolar threshold theorem 6.39.8 thus applies without any Hölder condition. If $c>\nu (T,\varphi )$, the set $E_ c=\{ g:\nu (T,\varphi \circ g)\geq c\} $ misses the identity. It has no interior on the connected $\Omega $. Otherwise a coordinate ball crossing the boundary of its interior would have a proper complete-pluripolar threshold containing an open set, which is impossible for a nontrivial plurisubharmonic pole function. The same argument along a chain of balls propagates interior to all of $\Omega $. Hence $E_ c$ is locally pluripolar. The preceding lemma and countably many rational $c$ imply

F13CFDE93F11 6.45.4
\begin{equation} \nu (T,\varphi \circ g)\leq \nu (T,\varphi ) \quad \text{for Haar-almost every }g\in \mathrm U(n). \end{equation}

We compute its average by an integrable primitive, retaining its origin mass explicitly. Put

\[ B=\int _{\mathrm U(n)}(\varphi \circ g) (dd^ c(\varphi \circ g))^{p-1}\, dg. \]

This is an actual coefficient-integrable current, also after wedging with $T$. The uniform localized weighted estimate in proposition 7.30.3 proves this: all unbounded loci are $\{ 0\} $, their mutual Hausdorff conditions hold through $p$ factors, and all rotated weights have a common finite bound outside a fixed neighborhood of zero. The same estimate bounds the masses of the positive top products uniformly. Absolute Fubini and the defining recursion consequently give

1513DCD3B5E1 6.45.5
\begin{equation} dd^ c(B\wedge T)= \int _{\mathrm U(n)}T\wedge (dd^ c(\varphi \circ g))^ p\, dg. \end{equation}

Here $B\wedge T$ means the coefficient-integrable extension of the ordinary smooth-form product off zero, with zero coefficient atom at zero. Each weighted member has this property: its unweighted base is closed positive of bidimension $(1,1)$ and therefore has no point atom. The uniform weighted estimate proves integrability of the extension and identifies it with the averaged weighted members. Measurability can also be checked before averaging: use common decreasing smooth coordinate regularizations of $\varphi $. Their fixed-index weighted-current actions vary continuously with $g$, and their actual weak limits are measurable by the decreasing-product theorem. In particular its origin atom is the average of the individual origin atoms, by equality of positive measure integrals on Borel sets. There is no multiplication of $\varphi $ by a point atom.

Off zero $B$ is a smooth invariant $(p-1,p-1)$-form. Set $t=\rho (z)$ and $\theta =dd^ ct$. Isotropy at $(r,0,\ldots ,0)$ gives

\[ B=U(t)\theta ^{p-1} +V(t)\, dt\wedge d^ ct\wedge \theta ^{p-2},\qquad a(t)=U'(t)-V(t). \]

When $p=1$ the second term is absent and $V=0$. To check completeness of this form description, diagonal unitary phases on the $n-1$ transverse coordinates kill off-diagonal multiindices, and permutations make all transverse coefficients of each of the two possible kinds equal. These are the displayed tangential and radial basis elements. Direct differentiation, using $d^ cdt=-\theta $, gives

\[ d^ cB=a(t)d^ ct\wedge \theta ^{p-1},\qquad dd^ cB=a(t)\theta ^ p+a'(t)dt\wedge d^ ct\wedge \theta ^{p-1}. \]

The latter is the positive average of $(dd^ c(\varphi \circ g))^ p$. For $p<n$, positivity gives $a,a'\geq 0$ from its radial and tangential evaluations. For $p=n$, only the radial term survives and positivity gives $a'\geq 0$; it gives no extra tangential equation. Thus $a$ is nondecreasing in every case.

Here is the exact radial test calculation, including a nonsmooth $T$. Write $D(t)=D_ T(0,e^{t/2})$ for its normalized ball density. On annuli the Lelong–Jensen calculation gives the radial pushforward identities

\[ T\wedge \theta ^ p\longmapsto dD(t),\qquad T\wedge dt\wedge d^ ct\wedge \theta ^{p-1} \longmapsto D(t)\, dt. \]

The first is theorem 6.33.1. The second follows from its boundary flux $\int _{|z|=r}T\wedge d^ ct\wedge \theta ^{p-1}=D(\log r^2)$: first convolve $T$, use the smooth Stokes formula, then pass on each compact annulus. Good radii are co-countable, their densities converge, and are uniformly bounded on that annulus; dominated convergence gives precisely the stated measure identity. For a smooth nonincreasing cutoff $\chi (t)$ equal to one below some level and zero above a larger level, this yields

\begin{align*} \langle dd^ c(B\wedge T),\chi (\rho )\rangle & =\int U\chi '\, dD+ \int (U\chi ''+V\chi ')D\, dt\\ & =-\int a(t)\chi '(t)D(t)\, dt. \end{align*}

Indeed the subtracted term is the integral of $d(U\chi 'D)$, with compact support on this annulus, and is zero. All terms are finite there.

Apply this first with $T_0=\theta ^{n-p}$, whose density is identically one by theorem 5.57.11 and its trace normalization. Its products with $B$ satisfy the same integrability estimate. Send the cutoff's transition to $t\to -\infty $. The left side tends to the finite nonnegative origin mass of its positive measure. Since $a$ is nondecreasing, this proves that $\lim _{t\to -\infty }a(t)$ is finite and equals that mass, including when $p=n$. By (6.45.5), this mass is the average of $\nu ((dd^ c(\varphi \circ g))^ p,0)$, which by unitary invariance is $\nu ((dd^ c\varphi )^ p,0)=:\mu $. For general $T$, $D(t)\to \nu (T,0)$. The same cutoff calculation therefore gives

\[ \int _{\mathrm U(n)}\nu (T,\varphi \circ g)\, dg =\mu \nu (T,0). \]

Together with (6.45.4), this proves (6.45.3). In top degree we used the finite pole mass to determine the missing constant; the false punctured top-form homothety classification was never used. $\square$
