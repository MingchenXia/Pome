Lemma 9.53.87B10208C6D56

Lemma 9.53.8.7B10208C6D56 Let $J$ be formally integrable and let $\gamma $ be formally Kähler. Let $K=\Lambda ^{n,0}T^*M$. The operator $d''$ on sections of $K$ defines a partial connection with square zero. Its dual on $K^{-1}$ and the induced metric define a smooth Hermitian connection whose curvature is of type $(1,1)$. Denote its real unscaled curvature by $\operatorname {Ric}(\gamma )$. For a smooth real weight $\phi $, if

68B288E2D6BF 9.53.9
\begin{equation} \mathcal H(\phi )+\operatorname {Ric}(\gamma )\geq \gamma , \end{equation}

then every compactly supported smooth $(0,1)$ form $v$ satisfies

B23883666796 9.53.10
\begin{equation} \int _ M|v|_\gamma ^2e^{-\phi }\, dV_\gamma \leq \| d''v\| _\phi ^2+\| \delta _\phi ''v\| _\phi ^2. \end{equation}

Here $\delta _\phi ''$ is the formal adjoint with the displayed smooth weight. If $\gamma $ is complete, every distributionally closed $g\in L^2_{0,1}(e^{-\phi },\gamma )$ has a solution

1BE0D874AB82 9.53.11
\begin{equation} d''u=g,\qquad \int _ M|u|^2e^{-\phi }\, dV_\gamma \leq \int _ M|g|_\gamma ^2e^{-\phi }\, dV_\gamma . \end{equation}

Proof. The product rule on $(n,0)$ forms gives the partial connection on $K$, and $(d'')^2=0$ gives its square zero. For clarity, a smooth line frame with squared norm $H$ and partial-connection form $a''$ has unique metric connection with $a'=H^{-1}d'H-\overline{a''}$ and $(0,1)$ part $a''$. This formula transforms as a connection and verifies compatibility by differentiating $H$. Its $(0,2)$ curvature is zero by the partial connection equation, and its $(2,0)$ part is zero by compatibility and conjugation. For $E=K^{-1}$ equipped with its induced metric times $e^{-\phi }$, its real curvature is consequently $\operatorname {Ric}(\gamma )+\mathcal H(\phi )$.

Here is a pointwise justification of the Kähler identity for this connection, including the jets which are needed. At any point use lemma 9.53.5 with $s=5$. In these real coordinates $J-J_{\rm std}=O(r^5)$: the $(0,1)$ projection of each $dz_ j$ is $O(r^5)$ and its conjugate has the same property, which determines the matrix of $J$. The degree-two real Taylor polynomial $\gamma _2$ of $\gamma $ is a standard type $(1,1)$ form, and is closed. Indeed all its non-$(1,1)$ coefficients have zero Taylor polynomial through degree two, and $d\gamma _2$ is the degree-one Taylor polynomial of $d\gamma =0$. It is positive on a smaller ball. Thus $\gamma _2$ is an actual standard Kähler metric there and has exactly the same two-jet as $\gamma $ at the point.

The smooth frame $\eta =d'z_1\wedge \cdots \wedge d'z_ n$ of $K$ satisfies $d''\eta =O(r^4)$, since $d''d'z_ j=-d'd''z_ j$. Hence its partial-connection coefficients and their derivatives through order two vanish at the point. The metric of $\eta ^{-1}$ is the reciprocal of the squared norm of $\eta $, so it agrees through order two with the metric of the standard inverse canonical frame for $\gamma _2$. Use also the degree-two Taylor polynomial of $\phi $. The connection just defined, the exterior operators, their adjoints and Laplacians, and their curvature operators therefore have exactly the same pointwise coefficients as in that genuine Kähler model. Exterior operators use first jets, adjoints use first metric jets, and their second-order compositions and curvature use at most second jets; the type projections also have matching jets. Coefficients of a test form can be given the same two-jet in the two frames.

Apply the already proved local identity theorem 10.33.2 in the genuine model. Equality of these coefficients proves the same identity for $E$ at the chosen point, hence everywhere. This uses the proof of that local differential-operator identity, without presuming the existence of any holomorphic coordinates for $J$. There is a global isometry from scalar $(0,q)$ forms to $E$-valued $(n,q)$ forms, given in a local frame by $v\mapsto (-1)^{nq}\eta \wedge v\otimes \eta ^{-1}$. The connection terms on $\eta $ and its dual cancel, and the indicated sign makes this commute with $d''$ in consecutive degrees. Adjoints commute as well. On $(n,1)$ forms the curvature operator is precisely the Hermitian form of $\mathcal H(\phi )+\operatorname {Ric}(\gamma )$, by the coefficient formula in proposition 10.33.6. Its lower bound is the identity under (9.53.9). The integrated Kähler identity now gives (9.53.10).

The complete-domain argument requires no complex coordinate charts. For precision, take maximal distributional operators $T=d'':L^2_{0,0}\to L^2_{0,1}$ and $S=d'':L^2_{0,1}\to L^2_{0,2}$; if $n=1$ the last space is zero. They are closed and densely defined, and $ST=0$. The smooth complete gradient cutoffs of lemma 6.26.1 have the product rules

\[ d''(\chi v)=\chi d''v+d''\chi \wedge v, \quad \delta _\phi ''(\chi v)=\chi \delta _\phi ''v -(d''\chi \wedge )^*v. \]

Their errors are bounded by $C\| v\| _\phi /R$. On each compact support the operators are smooth first-order matrices in real coordinates. The joint cutoff and mollifier proof of lemma 6.26.4, using graph-norm density by cutoffs and mollification, applies verbatim to these matrices and the smooth positive weight. It identifies $T^*$ with the maximal $\delta _\phi ''$ and proves joint graph density. Thus (9.53.10) extends to $\operatorname {Dom}T^*\cap \operatorname {Dom}S$. By Cauchy–Schwarz, $|\langle g,v\rangle |^2\leq \| g\| _\phi ^2 (\| T^*v\| _\phi ^2+\| Sv\| _\phi ^2)$. The exact Hilbert-complex theorem Hilbert-complex solvability from the dual energy estimate, applied to this closed complex and $Sg=0$, proves (9.53.11). No boundary condition or global boundedness of weight derivatives is imposed beyond these maximal domains. $\square$
