5.63.1 Coefficient bounds, varying measures and support maxima2228D7B3000E

Use $dd^ c=i\partial \bar\partial /(2\pi )$. All topological spaces below are Hausdorff. Norms of currents on a closed set include its boundary mass. Supremum norms on a current support mean actual pointwise suprema, not ambient Lebesgue essential suprema.

Proposition 5.63.2.7ECC0248E832 In coordinates on an $n$-manifold write a positive current of bidimension $(p,p)$ as

\[ T=i^{(n-p)^2}\sum _{|I|=|J|=n-p}T_{I,J}\, dz_ I\wedge d\bar z_ J. \]

Its coefficient distributions are measures, its matrix is Hermitian, and $T_{M,M}\geq 0$. For arbitrary $\lambda _ j\geq 0$, put $\lambda _ I=\prod _{j\in I}\lambda _ j$. Then, as measures,

\[ \lambda _ I\lambda _ J|T_{I,J}| \leq 2^ p\! \sum _{\substack {|M|=n-p\\ I\cap J\subset M\subset I\cup J}} \lambda _ M^2 T_{M,M}. \]

Inclusions in this formula allow equality. The assertion includes $p=0$ and $p=n$.

Proof. Reality, order zero and diagonal positivity are the exact earlier result coefficient measures and compactness of positive currents; we prove only its additional quantitative refinement. Let $K=I^ c$, $L=J^ c$, and order these two $p$-element sets so their common elements occupy matching positions. Put $\alpha _ s=dz_{k_ s}$, $\beta _ s=dz_{l_ s}$. For $a\in (\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^ p$, the form

\[ \gamma _ a=\bigwedge _{s=1}^ p\frac{i}{4} (\alpha _ s+i^{a_ s}\beta _ s)\wedge (\bar\alpha _ s+i^{-a_ s}\bar\beta _ s) \]

is strongly positive. Direct four-term summation in each slot gives

\[ \sum _ a i^{\sum _ s a_ s}\gamma _ a =\bigwedge _{s=1}^ p i\alpha _ s\wedge \bar\beta _ s, \qquad \sum _ a\gamma _ a =\bigwedge _{s=1}^ p i(\alpha _ s\wedge \bar\alpha _ s+ \beta _ s\wedge \bar\beta _ s). \]

The first identity extracts $T_{I,J}$, up to a constant of modulus one in the complex-oriented volume form. Each $T\wedge \gamma _ a$ is a positive measure, so the triangle inequality for variation bounds $|T_{I,J}|$ by the second displayed expression wedged with $T$. Its at most $2^ p$ terms are positive diagonal terms. Every nonzero chosen $p$-index contains $K\cap L$ and lies in $K\cup L$; its complementary index therefore satisfies the asserted conditions on $M$. Each multiplicity is at most $2^ p$. This proves the unweighted estimate, including empty wedges.

For positive $\lambda _ j$, apply the constant diagonal linear change to the covector slots of $T$, at the same base point. The resulting current is positive: its dual transformation preserves every strongly positive test form. Its coefficients are $\lambda _ I\lambda _ JT_{I,J}$. The unweighted estimate for this current is the required weighted estimate. Finally replace each zero $\lambda _ j$ by $\lambda _ j+\varepsilon $ and let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, testing the measure inequality against nonnegative compactly supported continuous functions. $\square$

Lemma 5.63.3.192CB50CA0EA Let $X$ be locally compact, and let $\mu _ k,\mu $ be positive Radon measures with $\mu _ k\to \mu $ vaguely. Suppose $f_ k:X\to [-\infty ,\infty )$ are upper semicontinuous and decrease pointwise to $f$. Whenever the products $f_ k\mu _ k$ are locally finite signed Radon measures, every vague cluster limit $\nu $ of those products satisfies

\[ \nu \leq f\mu . \]

Existence of such a cluster limit also implies $f\in L^1_{\rm loc}(\mu )$. No local boundedness of the limit and no separability assumption on $X$ is needed.

Proof. We give the needed compact-set version of the upper weak inequality. In a locally compact Hausdorff space, for compact $C\subset U$ with $U$ open there is $0\leq \chi \leq 1$ in $C_ c(U)$ equal to one on $C$. Here are the topological ingredients of that assertion. Separating a point from each point of a compact set by disjoint neighborhoods and taking a finite subcover proves regularity. It gives $C\subset V\Subset U$ by a second finite subcover. The same finite separation argument proves normality of the compact Hausdorff closure of a slightly larger such neighborhood. The dyadic construction of nested open sets with $\overline{V_ r}\subset V_ s$ for dyadic $r<s$, starting with the two disjoint closed sets, gives the continuous function $x\mapsto \inf \{ r:x\in V_ r\} $; its sublevel and superlevel sets are open by these closure inclusions. Taking its complement and extending by zero gives the asserted compactly supported cutoff.

Thus vague convergence gives $\limsup _ k\mu _ k(C)\leq \mu (C)$: bound $1_ C$ by those cutoffs and shrink their support to $C$. The required outer approximation of $\mu (C)$ follows from inner regularity on $L\setminus C$, where $L$ is a compact neighborhood of $C$ with finite measure. It follows also that the $\mu _ k$ masses of any fixed compact set are uniformly bounded. If $g\geq 0$ is bounded, upper semicontinuous and compactly supported, its sets $\{ g\geq t\} $ are compact for $t>0$. Layer integration and the upper Fatou inequality, with that uniform mass bound, prove

\[ \limsup _ k\int g\, d\mu _ k\leq \int g\, d\mu . \]

Layer sets with $>$ or $\geq $ give the same integral; any exceptional levels have one-dimensional measure zero.

Take any convergent cofinal subnet defining $\nu $ and fix $0\leq \chi \in C_ c(X)$ and an index $k_0$. For $k\geq k_0$,

\[ \int \chi f_ k\, d\mu _ k \leq \int \chi \max \{ f_{k_0},-m\} \, d\mu _ k. \]

The right weight is bounded on the compact support of $\chi $; upper semicontinuity gives a finite upper bound there. Apply the preceding inequality to $\chi (\max \{ f_{k_0},-m\} +m)$ and subtract the convergent continuous term $m\int \chi \, d\mu _ k$. This bound holds for the full sequence and hence for the cofinal subnet as well. Hence

\[ \int \chi \, d\nu \leq \int \chi \max \{ f_{k_0},-m\} \, d\mu . \]

Let $m\to \infty $ and then $k_0\to \infty $. Both passages are decreasing convergence after subtraction from a common integrable upper bound on this compact set. The right side tends to $\int \chi f\, d\mu $. It cannot be minus infinity, since the left side is finite. Choose such a $\chi $ equal to one on any prescribed compact set to obtain $f\in L^1_{\rm loc}(\mu )$. The resulting inequality on all nonnegative $C_ c$ tests is the asserted order of Radon measures, by their regularity. $\square$

Theorem 5.63.4.949B825CA873 Let $\Omega =\{ \psi <0\} \Subset X$ be a smoothly bounded domain, where $\psi $ is smooth strictly plurisubharmonic near $\overline\Omega $ and $d\psi \ne 0$ on $\partial \Omega $. Let $T$ be closed positive of bidimension $(p,p)$ near $\overline\Omega $, with $p\geq 1$. For every plurisubharmonic $u$ defined near $\overline\Omega $,

\[ \sup _{\overline\Omega \cap \operatorname {Supp}T}u_+ =\sup _{\partial \Omega \cap \operatorname {Supp}T}u_+. \]

Here $u_+=\max \{ u,0\} $ and the supremum of this nonnegative function over the empty set is zero. Discontinuity of $u$ is allowed.

Proof. Put $\omega =dd^ c\psi >0$. First let $u$ be smooth and fix $b>\sup _{\partial \Omega \cap \operatorname {Supp}T}u$. For $\varepsilon >0$ the bounded plurisubharmonic function

\[ v=\max \{ u-b+\varepsilon \psi ,0\} \]

vanishes near $\partial \Omega \cap \operatorname {Supp}T$. Thus $vT$ has compact support in $\Omega $. The bounded recursive product locality and positivity of bounded Bedford–Taylor products is positive and gives

\[ \int _\Omega dd^ c(vT)\wedge \omega ^{p-1}=0 \]

by compact-support Stokes. On the open set $E=\{ u-b+\varepsilon \psi >0\} $ its integrand dominates $\varepsilon T\wedge \omega ^ p$. Consequently $E$ is disjoint from $\operatorname {Supp}T$: otherwise its positive trace measure would have positive mass on that open neighborhood. Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ gives $u\leq b$ on $\Omega \cap \operatorname {Supp}T$.

For general $u$, reuse the exact smooth-decreasing-majorant lemma 7.31.1 on $K=\overline\Omega $, whose strict function is $\psi $. It gives smooth plurisubharmonic $u_ j\downarrow u$ on a fixed neighborhood. For every real $b$ strictly above the boundary supremum, eventually $u_ j<b$ on the compact boundary support: the increasing open sets $\{ u_ j<b\} $ cover it. Apply the smooth conclusion to such $u_ j$ and then let $j\to \infty $ and $b$ decrease to the boundary supremum. If that supremum is minus infinity, use every real $b$. Taking positive parts gives the stated equality, because the closed domain contains its boundary. Empty support is harmless. If the boundary support is empty but interior support were nonempty, the same compact-support Stokes argument with the strictly positive form $\omega ^ p$ would give $T=0$ inside, a contradiction. $\square$
