Proposition 9.30.1774E0C4C33DB

Proposition 9.30.1.774E0C4C33DB There is a connected Hausdorff complex surface $X$ with a countable basis and a noninjective local biholomorphism $\pi :X\to \Delta ^2$, where $\Delta =\{ |z|<1\} $, such that pullback is an algebra isomorphism

\[ \pi ^*:\mathcal O(\Delta ^2)\longrightarrow \mathcal O(X). \]

Two global holomorphic functions give local coordinates at every point of $X$, but its global holomorphic functions do not separate every pair of points. Moreover $X$ is not holomorphically convex.

Proof. A Hartogs figure and its bridge. In coordinates $(z,w)$ put

\[ H=\bigl (\Delta \times \{ 1/2<|w|<1\} \bigr ) \cup \bigl (\{ |z|<1/2\} \times \Delta \bigr ), \]

and set

\[ Z=\{ |z-3/4|<1/8\} ,\qquad E=\{ |\operatorname {Re}w|<3/4,\ |\operatorname {Im}w|<1/8\} , \qquad B=Z\times E. \]

These are open subsets of $\Delta ^2$: on $Z$ we have $5/8<|z|<7/8$, and on $E$ we have $|w|<\sqrt{37}/8<1$. The sets $H$ and $B$ are connected. Their intersection has exactly two connected components

\[ U_+=\{ (z,w)\in B:|w|>1/2,\ \operatorname {Re}w>0\} ,\qquad U_-=\{ (z,w)\in B:|w|>1/2,\ \operatorname {Re}w<0\} . \]

Indeed $\operatorname {Re}w$ cannot be zero in this intersection. For either sign, each horizontal section in $E\cap \{ |w|>1/2\} $ is an interval containing respectively $5/8$ or $-5/8$; horizontal segments followed by a vertical segment at that real part connect any two points on the same side. Thus both displayed components are nonempty, open and closed relative to $H\cap B$.

Take separate copies of $H$ and $B$, and identify their points with the same coordinates precisely over $U_+$. Let $X$ be this quotient. Its quotient map is open: saturating an open set in either copy adds only its intersection with the open set $U_+$ in the other copy. Consequently the two copies embed as open subsets of $X$ and give complex charts, with identity transition on $U_+$. Their images meet, so $X$ is connected, and their countable bases give a countable basis for $X$. The coordinate map $\pi :X\to \Delta ^2$ is well defined, continuous and a local biholomorphism.

We check Hausdorffness, which would fail if an inappropriate part of an overlap were glued. Points with distinct coordinate images are separated by inverse images of disjoint neighborhoods in $\Delta ^2$. Distinct points with the same image must be the two copies of a point of $U_-$. A sufficiently small common coordinate neighborhood lies in $U_-$; its two images in $X$ are disjoint open neighborhoods. This proves Hausdorffness. The space is also paracompact, as required in the complex-manifold convention. Here are explicit standard details. A locally compact Hausdorff space with a countable basis has a countable basis of relatively compact open sets. From that basis construct compact $K_ j\subset \operatorname {Int}K_{j+1}$ exhausting the space, by adjoining finitely many relatively compact neighborhoods covering $K_ j$ and the first $j$ basis sets. Given any open cover, cover each compact layer $K_ j\setminus \operatorname {Int}K_{j-1}$ by finitely many open sets refining the cover and contained in $\operatorname {Int}K_{j+1}\setminus K_{j-2}$ (take negative-index sets empty). This is possible by local compactness and Hausdorff regularity: for a point and an open neighborhood, a compact neighborhood followed by finite separation from its closed complement gives an open neighborhood with closure inside it. The resulting layer refinement is locally finite. Thus the atlas just constructed is an atlas on a paracompact Hausdorff complex surface.

All functions come from the bidisc. We first prove the needed filling of $H$ directly from the existing Cauchy calculus holomorphic power series and Cauchy bounds. For $h\in \mathcal O(H)$ and $1/2<R<1$ define, on $\Delta \times \{ |w|<R\} $,

\[ F_ R(z,w)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _{|\zeta |=R} \frac{h(z,\zeta )}{\zeta -w}\, d\zeta . \]

The compact contour lies in $H$ for every $z\in \Delta $. Differentiation under the integral locally in $(z,w)$ proves that $F_ R$ is holomorphic. For $|z|<1/2$ the function $h(z,\cdot )$ is holomorphic on $\Delta $, so Cauchy's formula gives $F_ R=h$ there. If $1/2<|w|<R$, both $z\mapsto F_ R(z,w)$ and $z\mapsto h(z,w)$ are holomorphic on $\Delta $ and agree on $\{ |z|<1/2\} $. The one-variable identity theorem gives equality for every $z$. For two choices of $R$, the functions $F_ R$ agree on their common connected domain, since they agree on its nonempty open subset with $|z|<1/2$. They therefore define $F\in \mathcal O(\Delta ^2)$ extending $h$. This extension is unique by the identity theorem.

A holomorphic function $f$ on $X$ is a pair $h\in \mathcal O(H)$, $b\in \mathcal O(B)$ agreeing on $U_+$. Extend $h$ to $F$ as just proved. Then $b$ and $F|_ B$ agree on the nonempty open set $U_+$ and $B$ is connected, so the identity theorem gives $b=F|_ B$. Hence $f=F\circ \pi $. Conversely every such pullback is holomorphic, and its vanishing implies $F|_ H=0$, hence $F=0$. This proves the claimed algebra isomorphism. In particular the two components of $\pi $ are global holomorphic coordinate functions locally. However the two points over $(3/4,-5/8)\in U_-$ are distinct, and no global holomorphic function distinguishes them.

A noncompact hull. Let $K$ be the copy in $H\subset X$ of $\{ z=3/4,\ |w|=7/8\} $. This is compact. For integers $j\geq 2$ let $x_ j$ be the point of the $B$ copy with coordinates

\[ (z,w)=\left(3/4,\ i(1-1/j)/8\right). \]

For $f=F\circ \pi \in \mathcal O(X)$ the one-variable maximum principle in $w$ gives $|f(x_ j)|\leq \sup _ K|f|$. One may derive this bound directly from Cauchy: apply its estimate on the circle to $F(3/4,\cdot )^ m$, take the $m$-th root and let $m\to \infty $. Thus all $x_ j$ lie in the holomorphic hull of $K$. Their images tend to $(3/4,i/8)$, which belongs neither to $H$ nor to $B$ and hence is not in $\pi (X)$. If this hull were compact, its continuous image would be a compact, therefore closed, subset of $\Delta ^2$ containing these images and their limit. This is impossible. Thus $X$ is not holomorphically convex. $\square$
