Proposition 2.17.25AFFEC4D76E3

These variants describe intrinsic principal symbols and formal adjoints. The general statement also gives the polynomial obtained by conjugation with an exponential.

Proposition 2.17.2.5AFFEC4D76E3 For a differential operator $P:E\to F$ of order at most $d$, with local expression $P=\sum _{|\alpha |\leq d}a_\alpha \partial ^\alpha $, the degree-$d$ principal symbol is the intrinsic map

\[ \sigma _ P(x,\xi )=\sum _{|\alpha |=d}a_\alpha (x)\xi ^\alpha . \]

For a smooth real scalar $f$ and a section $u$,

\[ e^{-tf}P(e^{tf}u)=t^ d\sigma _ P(df)u+\sum _{0\leq j<d}t^ jQ_ j u, \qquad \operatorname {ord}Q_ j\leq d-j. \]

Composition has symbol $\sigma _{QP}=\sigma _ Q\sigma _ P$ in the sum of the specified orders. There is a unique formal adjoint, and

\[ \sigma _{P^*}(x,\xi )=(-1)^ d\sigma _ P(x,\xi )^*. \]

Injectivity of $\sigma _ P(x,\xi )$ for all real $\xi \ne 0$ is called ellipticity here. If the bundle ranks agree this is invertibility.

Proof. The product rule gives the scalar-conjugation identity. The highest power has every derivative landing on the exponential, each through its first derivative. A term of power $t^ j$ leaves at most $d-j$ derivatives on $u$. The left side is intrinsic, so its highest coefficient gives the asserted coordinate and frame invariance. Taking all highest-order derivatives on the section in a composition gives the symbol product, even if that product vanishes and the actual composite order drops.

Use smooth orthonormal local frames and write the density as $\rho (x)\, dx$. Integration by parts gives

\[ P^*v=\rho ^{-1}\sum _{|\alpha |\leq d}(-1)^{|\alpha |} \partial ^\alpha (\rho a_\alpha ^*v). \]

This is a differential operator with the claimed leading symbol. The integration identity on compactly supported test sections proves uniqueness, hence the formulas glue on overlaps. A cutoff equal to one near a compact intersection of supports gives the same identity for arbitrary sections with that compact intersection. Derivatives of the cutoff contribute zero, since one of the two sections is then zero near each such point. This also defines the action on distributional sections by transposition. The argument uses a density and does not require orientability. $\square$

Read the alternative statement and proof

Proposition 2.17.2.5AFFEC4D76E3 Let $P:C^\infty (M,E)\to C^\infty (M,F)$ be a differential operator of order at most $m$ between smooth real or complex bundles. In charts write $P=\sum _{|\alpha |\le m}A_\alpha \partial ^\alpha $ and use the symbol without Fourier factors,

\[ \sigma _ m(P)(x,\xi )=\sum _{|\alpha |=m}A_\alpha (x)\xi ^\alpha . \]

This is an intrinsic homogeneous map $\operatorname {Sym}^ mT^*M \to \operatorname {Hom}(E,F)$. Symbols multiply under composition, with the indicated orders even when the top symbol vanishes. For bundle metrics and a positive smooth density there is a unique formal adjoint $P^*$, and for real covectors

\[ \sigma _ m(P^*)(x,\xi )=(-1)^ m\sigma _ m(P)(x,\xi )^*. \]

Injectivity of the symbol for every nonzero real covector is the overdetermined ellipticity condition; for equal ranks it is equivalent to invertibility. This definition alone asserts no Fredholm conclusion.

Proof. For a real smooth function $f$ and a local section $u$, $e^{-tf}P(e^{tf}u)$ is a polynomial in $t$ of degree at most $m$. Repeated Leibniz differentiation gives its top coefficient $\sigma _ m(P)(x,df_ x)u(x)$. That expression is invariant under coordinates and frames, and every covector is $df_ x$ for a suitable local $f$. This proves intrinsicness. Applying the same Leibniz rule to $QP$ proves $\sigma _{m+\ell }(QP)=\sigma _\ell (Q)\sigma _ m(P)$. In arbitrary frames let $H_ E,H_ F$ be the metric matrices and let $\rho \, dx$ be the density, with the inner product linear first. Then

\[ P^*v=\rho ^{-1}H_ E^{-1} \sum _\alpha (-1)^{|\alpha |}\partial ^\alpha (\rho A_\alpha ^{\dagger }H_ Fv). \]

Integration by parts proves this formula for compactly intersecting supports. At each point a compactly supported test section detects every coefficient of a smooth section, proving uniqueness and hence agreement on overlapping frames. Only derivatives falling on $v$ contribute to the top symbol, proving its formula. For real bundles replace conjugate transpose by transpose. Finally an injective linear map between equal finite dimensions is bijective, proving the last equivalence without a regularity assertion. $\square$
