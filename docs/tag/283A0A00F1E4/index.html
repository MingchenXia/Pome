Theorem 5.51.1283A0A00F1E4

Theorem 5.51.1.283A0A00F1E4 For every $n\geq 2$ there are real functions $f_2,\ldots ,f_ n\in C_ c^\infty (\mathbb R)$ such that the compact arc

\[ K=\{ (x,f_2(x),\ldots ,f_ n(x)):0\leq x\leq 1\} \subset \mathbb C^ n \]

is not pluripolar. In particular its extension with $x\in \mathbb R$ is a closed, properly embedded smooth real curve which is not pluripolar.

Proof. We use the planar finite logarithmic energy criterion and Josefson's globalization theorem. We first construct all the sets and functions needed for the slicing argument.

Put $Q=2^{n+1}+1$. At each level $k$ choose $n$ bits $(b_ k,a_{2,k},\ldots ,a_{n,k})$ and encode them by the digit

\[ d_ k=2\left(2^{n-1}b_ k+\sum _{r=2}^ n2^{r-2}a_{r,k}\right). \]

Thus $d_ k$ ranges over $0,2,\ldots ,Q-3$. Let $D$ consist of all $\sum _{k\geq 1}d_ kQ^{-k}$. It is compact and contained in $[0,1]$. Every prefix of length $k$ gives an interval $I=[s,s+Q^{-k}]$, where $s$ is its finite sum. The starting points of distinct such intervals differ by at least $2Q^{-k}$: after multiplication by $Q^ k$ they are distinct even integers, since $Q$ is odd. The intervals are therefore separated by at least $Q^{-k}$. The tail after level $k$ is at most $(Q-3)Q^{-k}/(Q-1)<Q^{-k}$. If two sequences first differ at level $k$, their encoded points are at distance at least $Q^{-k}$. In particular the encoding is unique.

Fix all the bits $a=(a_{r,k})_{2\leq r\leq n,\ k\geq 1}$ and let $C_ a\subset D$ be the compact set obtained by varying just the $b_ k$. Different arrays $a$ give disjoint sets. There is a probability measure $\mu _ a$ on $C_ a$ assigning weight $2^{-k}$ to each of its $k$-prefix cylinders. To see this without assuming a product measure, take the uniform measure on the $2^ m$ points whose first $m$ bits $b_ k$ vary and whose later bits are zero. A weakly convergent subsequence exists on the compact interval. Its limit is supported on $C_ a$. For each fixed level the cylinders lie in finitely many disjoint compact intervals; continuous separating functions show that the limiting weights are $2^{-k}$. Every singleton consequently has measure zero. For a fixed $x\in C_ a$, the points whose code first differs from that of $x$ at level $k$ have measure $2^{-k}$. The separation estimate gives

\[ \int _{C_ a}\log \frac1{|x-y|}\, d\mu _ a(y) \leq \log Q\sum _{k\geq 1}k2^{-k}<\infty . \]

There is no contribution from $y=x$. The kernel is nonnegative on $[0,1]$, so Tonelli's theorem also gives finite double logarithmic energy. The planar energy criterion implies that every $C_ a$ is nonpolar.

Choose $\beta \in C_ c^\infty ((-1/3,4/3))$, with $0\leq \beta \leq 1$ and $\beta =1$ on $[0,1]$. For $2\leq r\leq n$, let $\psi _{r,k}(x)$ be the sum of $\beta (Q^ k(x-s))$ over the level-$k$ prefixes whose bit $a_{r,k}$ is one. The supports of the summands are disjoint: their lengths are less than $(5/3)Q^{-k}$ and their starting points differ by at least $2Q^{-k}$. Hence, for every integer $\ell \geq 0$,

\[ \| \psi _{r,k}^{(\ell )}\| _\infty \leq \| \beta ^{(\ell )}\| _\infty Q^{k\ell }. \]

On $D$, the function $\psi _{r,k}$ equals the encoded bit $a_{r,k}$. Indeed a point lies in its own prefix interval, where the appropriate bump is one, and lies outside every other prefix's bump support. Set

\[ c_ k=10^{-k^2},\qquad f_ r(x)=\sum _{k\geq 1}c_ k\psi _{r,k}(x). \]

For every $\ell $ the series of $\ell $th derivatives converges uniformly, because $\sum _ k10^{-k^2}Q^{k\ell }<\infty $. Repeated integration of the uniformly convergent derivative series identifies them as the derivatives of $f_ r$. Thus $f_ r$ is smooth. All the summands have support in a fixed compact subinterval of $(-1,2)$, so $f_ r$ is compactly supported. On $C_ a$ it has the constant value

\[ f_ r|_{C_ a}=c(a_ r):=\sum _{k\geq 1}a_{r,k}10^{-k^2}. \]

The common set of possible values $C'=\{ \sum _{k\geq 1}a_ k10^{-k^2}:a_ k\in \{ 0,1\} \} $ is also nonpolar. In fact the tail after the $k$th term is at most $c_ k/9$, since each subsequent ratio is at most $10^{-3}$. Two codes first differing at level $k$ are therefore separated by at least $c_ k/2$. Construct the probability measure with cylinder weights $2^{-k}$ by the same finite-measure limit argument. The prefix compact sets are disjoint, so it has no atoms, and for every $t\in C'$,

\[ \int _{C'}\log \frac1{|t-s|}\, d\nu (s) \leq \sum _{k\geq 1}2^{-k}(k^2\log 10+\log 2)<\infty . \]

Again the support has diameter less than one, so this proves finite logarithmic energy and nonpolarity.

Suppose now that $K$ were pluripolar. Josefson's theorem supplies $\varphi \in \operatorname {PSH}(\mathbb C^ n)$, not identically $-\infty $, with $\varphi |_ K=-\infty $. Fix any array $a$. The restriction

\[ z\longmapsto \varphi (z,c(a_2),\ldots ,c(a_ n)) \]

is subharmonic or identically $-\infty $. It is $-\infty $ on the nonpolar set $C_ a$, so the second alternative holds. Consequently $\varphi =-\infty $ on $\mathbb C\times (C')^{n-1}$. Restricting to one complex coordinate at a time and using the nonpolarity of $C'$ then proves successively that $\varphi =-\infty $ on $\mathbb C^ j\times (C')^{n-j}$ for $j=2,\ldots ,n$. This contradicts the choice of $\varphi $.

The parametrization of the extended graph is injective and an immersion, because its first coordinate is $x$. It is proper, because that coordinate is unbounded as $|x|\to \infty $, and its inverse on the image is the first-coordinate projection. Its image is therefore a closed embedded smooth curve. It contains the nonpluripolar compact arc $K$. $\square$
