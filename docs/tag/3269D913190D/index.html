Proposition 9.16.13269D913190D

Proposition 9.16.1.3269D913190D A nonempty closed connected locally convex subset $C$ of a finite dimensional real Euclidean space is convex. Consequently a connected open set $D$ is convex if and only if it admits a finite continuous locally convex exhaustion function.

Proof. For the first assertion, any two points can be joined by a polygonal path in $C$. In fact, each equivalence class for polygonal reachability is relatively open, since near every point $C$ agrees with a convex set; its complement, a union of other classes, is relatively open too. Connectedness gives a single class.

Fix $a,b\in C$ and a polygonal joining path of length $L$. Take paths whose lengths decrease to the infimum and are at most $L+1$. Parameterize them on $[0,1]$ with constant speed, inserting a constant path when needed. Their images lie in the compact set $C\cap \overline{B(a,L+1)}$ and their Lipschitz constants are at most $L+1$. A diagonal subsequence converges at every time in a countable dense subset of $[0,1]$, by compactness of the range. The common Lipschitz bound then makes it uniformly Cauchy, so it converges uniformly. Its limit joins $a$ to $b$ in $C$, and its length is at most the limiting lengths: for each finite partition the sum of successive distances passes to the uniform limit, and length is the supremum of these sums. It is therefore a shortest path. On every sufficiently short subarc around one of its points, local convexity permits replacement by the straight segment. Minimality forces equality between that subarc's length and the distance of its endpoints. In arclength parametrization it is consequently affine there: equality in the Euclidean triangle inequality places each intermediate point on that segment at its prescribed distance. The local affine directions agree on overlapping subarcs, so the whole path is the segment from $a$ to $b$. This proves convexity.

Let $h$ be a finite continuous locally convex exhaustion on $D$. Its epigraph

\[ E=\{ (x,t):x\in D,\ t\geq h(x)\} \]

is closed in the full Euclidean product: a convergent sequence in $E$ has its $x$-coordinates in one compact sublevel of the exhaustion, and continuity gives the limiting inequality. It is connected, being homeomorphic to $D\times [0,\infty )$, and is locally convex by local convexity of $h$. The first assertion makes $E$ convex, and its projection $D$ is convex. Conversely, if $D$ is a proper open convex set, its Euclidean boundary distance $d$ is concave. Indeed, the convex combinations of balls $B(x,r)\subset D$ and $B(y,s)\subset D$ give $B((1-t)x+ty,(1-t)r+ts)\subset D$; let $r\uparrow d(x)$, $s\uparrow d(y)$. Thus $-\log d$ is convex, and $|x|^2+\max \{ 0,-\log d(x)\} $ is a continuous convex exhaustion. For the full space use $|x|^2$. $\square$
