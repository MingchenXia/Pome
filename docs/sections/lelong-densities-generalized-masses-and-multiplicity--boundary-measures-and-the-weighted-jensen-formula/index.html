6.36 Boundary measures and the weighted Jensen formula0EBB82914BD2

0EBB82914BD2

In this section $X$ has dimension $n\geq 1$ and a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion, and $\varphi $ is semi-exhaustive on all of $X$. Work below a level $R$ with compact closed sublevel. Here, specifically, theorem 7.31.2 defines $\sigma =(dd^ c\varphi )^ n$ at the compact pole set and gives $(dd^ c\varphi _ a)^ n\to \sigma $ as $a\to -\infty $. This assertion is not made for an arbitrary non-Stein space or a base current supported entirely in the pole set.

Proposition 6.36.1.E5EFE4D4F285 The measure

8B18D766C123 6.36.2
\begin{equation} \mu _ r=(dd^ c\varphi _ r)^ n-\mathbf1_{\{ \varphi \geq r\} }\sigma \quad (r<R) \end{equation}

is positive, supported in $\{ \varphi =r\} $, and has total mass $M_1(\varphi ,r)$. It is weakly left continuous in $r$, and every PSH function $V$ near the compact low sublevel is $\mu _ r$-integrable. If $\varphi $ is smooth near its level and $d\varphi \ne 0$ there,

\[ \mu _ r=(dd^ c\varphi )^{n-1}\wedge d^ c\varphi \big |_{\{ \varphi =r\} }, \]

with the level oriented as the boundary of $B_\varphi (r)$.

Proof. Write $A_ r=(dd^ c\varphi _ r)^ n$. It vanishes in $B_\varphi (r)$ and equals $\sigma $ on $\{ \varphi >r\} $ by locality. If $r_ j\uparrow r$, the truncations converge locally uniformly, so $A_{r_ j}\to A_ r$ by bounded uniform continuity. For every nonnegative compact test function, $A_{r_ j}\geq \mathbf1_{\{ \varphi >r_ j\} }\sigma $ gives in the limit $A_ r\geq \mathbf1_{\{ \varphi \geq r\} }\sigma $. This proves positivity and the level support. Testing in a larger compact sublevel and using the truncation mass identity gives $\mu _ r(X)=\sigma (B_\varphi (r))=M_1(\varphi ,r)$. The same local uniform convergence and continuity from above of $\mathbf1_{\{ \varphi \geq r_ j\} }\sigma $ give left continuity. Since $0\leq \mu _ r\leq A_ r$ and $\varphi _ r$ is locally bounded, the weighted Chern–Levine–Nirenberg estimate, including its top-degree case, proves the asserted integrability of any PSH $V$.

For the smooth-level description smooth $\max \{ t,r\} $ by an even one-dimensional probability convolution $\chi _\epsilon $. On a neighborhood of the level, $\chi _\epsilon \circ \varphi $ is smooth PSH and tends locally uniformly to $\varphi _ r$. For a smooth test $h$ supported in that neighborhood, integration by parts yields

\[ \int h(dd^ c\varphi _ r)^ n =\lim _{\epsilon \downarrow 0} -\int \chi '_\epsilon (\varphi )^ n\, dh\wedge (dd^ c\varphi )^{n-1}\wedge d^ c\varphi . \]

Indeed every term containing two $d^ c\varphi $ factors vanishes in the chain-rule expansion. The derivative powers tend to the indicator of $\{ \varphi >r\} $ off the smooth level, which has zero smooth volume. Dominated convergence and smooth Stokes on this regular level give the stated boundary measure plus $\mathbf1_{\{ \varphi \geq r\} }\sigma $. The latter smooth measure gives the level zero mass, completing the identification. No generic regular-value assertion is needed. $\square$

Theorem 6.36.3.B30AA438CFC2 For every PSH $V$ near the compact low sublevels and every $r<R$,

EF7914F2FDA1 6.36.4
\begin{equation} \mu _ r(V)-\int _{B_\varphi (r)}V\, \sigma =\int _{-\infty }^ r M_{dd^ cV}(\varphi ,t)\, dt. \end{equation}

The boundary integral is finite. The other side is a nonnegative extended number; if the interior integral is $-\infty $, the left side means $+\infty $. In particular there is no subtraction of two infinite quantities. For $r_0<r<R$ the always finite difference formula is

5F5A84D35D74 6.36.5
\begin{equation} \mu _ r(V)-\mu _{r_0}(V) -\int _{\{ r_0\leq \varphi <r\} }V\, \sigma =\int _{r_0}^ r M_{dd^ cV}(\varphi ,t)\, dt. \end{equation}

The full formula is finite whenever $V$ is integrable for the interior $\sigma $ measure; for example if $V$ is bounded below there, or if $\varphi $ is locally bounded on the closed sublevel.

Proof. A finite inner truncation. Fix $a<r$ and put $\sigma _ a=(dd^ c\varphi _ a)^ n$. Initially let $V$ be smooth PSH. Bounded recursion and symmetry give

\[ A_ r-\sigma _ a =dd^ c\left((\varphi _ r-\varphi _ a) \sum _{j=0}^{n-1}(dd^ c\varphi _ r)^ j \wedge (dd^ c\varphi _ a)^{n-1-j}\right). \]

The primitive is compactly supported in the closed $r$-sublevel. Test against $V$ with a smooth cutoff equal to one there. On $\{ \varphi <r\} $ every term with $j\geq 1$ vanishes, since $\varphi _ r$ is constant; elsewhere the scalar difference is zero. Thus

178
\begin{align} \mu _ r(V)-\int _{B_\varphi (r)}V\, \sigma _ a & =\int (r-\varphi _ a)_+\, dd^ cV\wedge (dd^ c\varphi _ a)^{n-1} \notag \\ & =\int _ a^ r M_{dd^ cV}(\varphi ,t)\, dt. \tag{6.36.6} \end{align}

The last equality is Tonelli's theorem and the independent inner truncation mass, applied to the positive base $dd^ cV$ of bidimension $n-1$. This also covers $n=1$, where the empty Hessian power is one and that base is a measure. The left equality follows from $A_ r-\sigma _ a=\mu _ r-\mathbf1_{B_\varphi (r)}\sigma _ a$; near and above the level $r$, $\sigma _ a$ equals $\sigma $.

For general $V$, take smooth PSH $V_ k\downarrow V$ on a fixed neighborhood of a slightly larger compact sublevel, using lemma 7.31.1. For this fixed $a$, weighted CLN makes $V$ integrable for $\sigma _ a$, $A_ r$ and the trace coefficients of $(dd^ c\varphi _ a)^{n-1}$. Consequently $V_ k$ converges in those fixed coefficient $L^1$ spaces, and $dd^ cV_ k\wedge (dd^ c\varphi _ a)^{n-1}$ converges weakly to the corresponding product with $dd^ cV$. The test $(r-\varphi _ a)_+$ is bounded continuous with compact support, so the first integral on the right converges as well. This proves (6.36.6) for arbitrary $V$, with finite quantities.

The pole limit, including an unbounded $V$. It is insufficient to pass an unbounded weight through weak convergence alone. On the extended interval $[-\infty ,r)$ define

\[ F_ k(a)=\int _{B_\varphi (r)}V_ k\, \sigma _ a\quad (a>-\infty ), \qquad F_ k(-\infty )=\int _{B_\varphi (r)}V_ k\, \sigma . \]

Each $F_ k$ is finite and continuous. At a finite $a$, use local uniform continuity of $\varphi _ a$ and bounded-product continuity; at $a=-\infty $ use the specific classical convergence $\sigma _ a\to \sigma $ established at the start of this section. The open-sublevel indicator causes no problem: for all $a$ in a neighborhood of a fixed $a_0<r$, and also for all sufficiently negative $a$, the measures $\sigma _ a$ equal the fixed $\sigma $ on a neighborhood of the level $r$. A compact smooth cutoff reduces the remaining integral to an ordinary weak test against smooth $V_ k$. Equation (6.36.6) also shows that $F_ k$ is nondecreasing in $a$, and continuity extends this to the endpoint $-\infty $.

For every fixed $a$, monotone convergence after subtracting a common upper bound gives $F_ k(a)\downarrow F(a)$, where

\[ F(a)=\int _{B_\varphi (r)}V\, \sigma _ a, \qquad F(-\infty )=\int _{B_\varphi (r)}V\, \sigma . \]

The latter may be $-\infty $. The decreasing limit of the continuous nondecreasing functions is right continuous at $-\infty $. Explicitly, if $L>F(-\infty )$, choose $k$ with $F_ k(-\infty )<L$; continuity gives $F_ k(a)<L$ for all sufficiently negative $a$. Then $F(-\infty )\leq F(a)\leq F_ k(a)<L$. This works also when $F(-\infty )=-\infty $, for every finite $L$. It proves $F(a)\to F(-\infty )$ without a uniform tail assumption. Let $a\to -\infty $ in (6.36.6). The nonnegative integral on its right increases to the right side of (6.36.4), proving the extended identity.

Finally derive the difference formula from two finite-inner-truncation identities with the same $a<r_0$. Their difference involves only $\{ r_0\leq \varphi <r\} $, where $\varphi $ is locally bounded and $V$ is integrable by weighted CLN. This proves (6.36.5) directly with finite terms; it is not obtained by cancelling infinite pole integrals. $\square$

Corollary 6.36.7.5736E627F741 If $\sigma $ is supported on $P_\varphi $, then $r\mapsto \mu _ r(V)$ is finite, convex and nondecreasing, and

\[ \nu (dd^ cV,\varphi )=\lim _{r\to -\infty }\frac{\mu _ r(V)}r. \]

For $\varphi =\log |z-a|^2$, the measures $\mu _ r$ are the invariant probability measures on the spheres of radius $e^{r/2}$.

Proof. The finite-level formula has no annular interior term in this case. Its right derivative is the nonnegative nondecreasing mass function (up to the immaterial choice at countably many jumps), proving convexity and monotonicity. Its limiting slopes equal $\lim _{t\to -\infty }M_{dd^ cV}(\varphi ,t)$: bound the finite integral averages between the limiting infimum and the value at any fixed earlier level. Division by $r$ removes the finite value at a fixed reference level. For the radial weight, the classical full Monge–Ampere mass is one at $a$: off $a$ its logarithmic Hessian has rank $n-1$, and its truncated mass is one by lemma 6.33.8 with $T=1$. The boundary measure is invariant under unitary changes and has that mass, hence is the invariant sphere probability of invariant probability on unitary homogeneous spaces. This is consistent with the ordinary finite-radius Jensen theorem. $\square$

Corollary 6.36.8.8E082A87A9BA In coordinates centered at $a$, put $\varphi _\lambda =\max _ j\lambda _ j\log |z_ j-a_ j|^2$ for $\lambda _ j>0$. Then $\nu (T,\varphi _\lambda )$ is nondecreasing in every $\lambda _ j$, and

\[ (\min _ j\lambda _ j)^ p\nu (T,a) \leq \nu (T,\varphi _\lambda ) \leq (\max _ j\lambda _ j)^ p\nu (T,a). \]

For $\lambda _ j=1$ it is exactly $\nu (T,a)$.

Proof. All poles are the common point $a$ on the support; if $a$ is not in that support, work in a sufficiently small neighborhood where all numbers are zero. For unit coefficients the maximum logarithm differs from $\log \sum _ j|z_ j-a_ j|^2$ by a bounded function. Increasing a coefficient makes the weight more negative in a small coordinate polydisc. The localized comparison theorem and positive homogeneity therefore give monotonicity and the two bounds. For $p=0$ all weights have the same point pole and hence the same atom mass; no zero-power limit is needed. $\square$

Corollary 6.36.9.EF78B047777B For positive real $\lambda _1,\ldots ,\lambda _ n$ put

\[ a_\lambda (z)=\max _ j\lambda _ j\log |z_ j|^2, \qquad b_\lambda (z)=\log \sum _ j|z_ j|^{2\lambda _ j}. \]

Their classical full Monge–Ampere measures are

\[ (dd^ ca_\lambda )^ n=(dd^ cb_\lambda )^ n =\lambda _1\cdots \lambda _ n\, \delta _0. \]

Proof. Both functions are extended-continuous PSH with their only pole at zero and compact low sublevels. For $b_\lambda $, the elementary composition rule applies to the convex coordinatewise increasing function $(t_ j)\mapsto \log \sum e^{t_ j}$ and the PSH functions $\lambda _ j\log |z_ j|^2$: its Hessian chain rule is positive for smooth inputs, and decreasing local regularizations prove the claim at zero coordinates. Thus the classical products exist by theorem 7.31.2. The two weights differ by a function between zero and $\log n$, so have the same residual mass.

For positive integers $m_ j$, the proper map $F(z)=(z_1^{m_1},\ldots ,z_ n^{m_ n})$ has $\prod _ jm_ j$ covering sheets off the coordinate hyperplanes. Change variables there; the hyperplanes and their inverse images have zero smooth volume, so $F_*1=(\prod _ jm_ j)1$ as currents. Since $a_ m=a_{(1,\ldots ,1)}\circ F$, the projection formula and corollary 6.36.8 give $\nu (1,a_ m)=\prod _ jm_ j$. Positive homogeneity proves this for positive rational $\lambda _ j$. Coordinatewise rational bounds, the same elementary comparison and continuity of the numerical product prove $\nu (1,a_\lambda )=\prod _ j\lambda _ j$ for real coefficients.

It remains to exclude mass away from zero. Near a point with $z_ k\ne 0$ choose a holomorphic logarithm of $z_ k$ and use the coordinates $w_ j=z_ j\exp [-(\lambda _ k/\lambda _ j)\log z_ k]$ for $j\ne k$, together with $z_ k$. Each potential is then $\lambda _ k\log |z_ k|^2$ plus a locally bounded continuous PSH function of the $n-1$ variables $w_ j$ alone, respectively $\max \{ 0,\lambda _ j\log |w_ j|^2:j\ne k\} $ and $\log (1+\sum _{j\ne k}|w_ j|^{2\lambda _ j})$. Convolution in those variables tends locally uniformly to that function; every smooth approximation has zero $n$th Hessian power by degree, and bounded uniform continuity gives the same vanishing for the limit. For $n=1$ this is the constant function of no variables. Hence both measures are supported at zero; their previously computed residual masses give the formula. $\square$
