9.41 Completed tensors, gluing and cohomologyC6005F04267B

C6005F04267B

The functional-analytic spaces and maps are complex, Hausdorff and locally convex unless a quotient cohomology topology is explicitly allowed to be non-Hausdorff. We use the tensor seminorms and complete nuclear Fréchet theory of section 2.13. In particular, completed tensoring a strict short exact sequence of Fréchet spaces is strictly exact when its middle space or the fixed other factor is nuclear. Nuclear Fréchet spaces have equal projective and injective completed tensor topologies; we write $\widehat\otimes $ in that case. The open mapping and cohomological openness assertions are those proved in proposition 2.12.1, theorem 2.12.5.

Lemma 9.41.1.8B7B03395780 For complete Fréchet spaces the following hold.

  1. Product functionals separate points of $E\widehat\otimes _\varepsilon F$. For $\xi \in E'$, contraction $c_\xi :E\widehat\otimes _\varepsilon F\to F$ is continuous. If $j:F\to G$ is a continuous injection, then $\mathrm{Id}\widehat\otimes j$ is injective for the injective completed tensor topologies; $j(F)$ need not be closed.

  2. There is a natural topological isomorphism

    \[ E\widehat\otimes _\varepsilon \prod _{a\geq 0}F_ a \simeq \prod _{a\geq 0} (E\widehat\otimes _\varepsilon F_ a). \]

    If $E$ is nuclear this is also the completed projective tensor isomorphism. Finite direct sums are included.

  3. If $H$ is nuclear and $u:E\to G$ is any continuous map,

    9AB3D9A1E143 9.41.2
    \begin{equation} \ker (\mathrm{Id}_ H\widehat\otimes u) =H\widehat\otimes \ker u \end{equation}

    as closed topological subspaces of $H\widehat\otimes E$. No closed-image hypothesis on $u$ is needed.

Proof. On algebraic tensors, dominated functional extension gives

8C479FD0F455 9.41.3
\begin{equation} \varepsilon _{p,q}(t) =\sup _{|\xi |\leq p}q(c_\xi t). \end{equation}

The equality is just the seminorm norming formula $q(y)=\sup _{|\eta |\leq q}|\eta (y)|$ applied after contraction. It gives contraction continuity. Both this formula and the definition as a supremum of product tests remain valid on the completion: an algebraic Cauchy sequence converges uniformly over each indicated test family. Thus vanishing of all product tests implies that every completed injective seminorm vanishes, which implies the tensor is zero. If $(\mathrm{Id}\widehat\otimes j)t=0$, contraction gives $j(c_\xi t)=0$ for every $\xi $; injectivity of $j$ and the separation just proved give $t=0$.

A defining seminorm on a product has the form $q(y)=\max _{a\in I}q_ a(y_ a)$ for finite $I$. Formula (9.41.3) gives exactly

\[ \varepsilon _{p,q}(t) =\max _{a\in I}\varepsilon _{p,q_ a}(t_ a). \]

Hence the algebraic coordinate map is a topological embedding for the induced tensor topology. Its image is dense in the product of completions: approximate finitely many specified coordinates by algebraic tensors and insert each tensor vector into its single coordinate of $\prod F_ a$. A finite sum does all these approximations at once. Completion gives the claimed isomorphism. Nuclearity identifies the two tensor topologies also for the product, which is Fréchet.

For (3), put $K=\ker u$. It is closed, so $Q=E/K$ is Fréchet. Strict short-exact tensor preservation gives

\[ 0\longrightarrow H\widehat\otimes K \longrightarrow H\widehat\otimes E \longrightarrow H\widehat\otimes Q\longrightarrow 0. \]

The induced $Q\to G$ is a continuous injection. Part(1) and nuclear comparison make its tensor with $H$ injective. Factoring $u$ through $Q$ proves the exact kernel assertion. The topology is that of the first closed embedding above. $\square$

Corollary 9.41.4.50BD63BB3B28 Let $\mathcal A$ be a sheaf on a second-countable space such that its section spaces on open sets are Fréchet, restrictions are continuous, and each countable gluing sequence

\[ 0\longrightarrow \mathcal A(U)\longrightarrow \prod _ a\mathcal A(U_ a)\xrightarrow {\delta } \prod _{a,b}\mathcal A(U_ a\cap U_ b) \]

identifies $\mathcal A(U)$ topologically with $\ker \delta $. For a fixed nuclear Fréchet space $H$, the assignment $U\mapsto H\widehat\otimes \mathcal A(U)$, with the tensor restriction maps, is a sheaf and satisfies the same topological gluing assertion. One may replace each overlap by a countable open refinement on which equality of sections can be tested.

Proof. Apply the kernel and product assertions of the lemma to $\delta $. They give precisely the kernel of the completed tensor gluing map, without any assumption on its last image. An overlap refinement has the same kernel by the uniqueness axiom for the original sheaf. Every open cover in a second- countable space has a countable subcover: choose one cover member for each basis element contained in a member. Glue on this subcover, and use uniqueness on its intersections with each other member to obtain the arbitrary-cover sheaf axiom. $\square$

Proposition 9.41.5.F635AEC8E8F7 For countable-basis complex manifolds $X,Y$, pointwise external products induce a topological isomorphism

\[ \mathcal O(X)\widehat\otimes \mathcal O(Y) \simeq \mathcal O(X\times Y). \]

Proof. First let $X=D$, $Y=D'$ be polydiscs. For compact smaller closed polydiscs $K\Subset D$, $L\Subset D'$ put $p(f)=\sup _ K|f|$, $q(g)=\sup _ L|g|$. For an algebraic tensor $t=\sum f_ i\otimes g_ i$ let $T(z,w)=\sum f_ i(z)g_ i(w)$. By (9.41.3) and dominated functional extension,

\[ \varepsilon _{p,q}(t) =\sup _{w\in L}\sup _{|\xi |\leq p} \left|\xi \left(\sum _ i g_ i(w)f_ i\right)\right| =\sup _{(z,w)\in K\times L}|T(z,w)|. \]

These seminorms define compact convergence on the product. Holomorphic Taylor expansion and its Cauchy coefficient bounds in holomorphic power series and Cauchy bounds show that polynomials in $(z,w)$ are dense there. Each is a finite sum of separated monomials. The target is complete, by the local holomorphic limit criterion already used in theorem 2.13.4. Thus completion gives the result; nuclearity identifies the tensor topologies.

Cover $Y$ by countably many coordinate polydiscs $V_ a$ and cover every overlap $V_ a\cap V_ b$ by countably many coordinate polydiscs $W_{abj}$. For fixed polydisc $D$, the local product isomorphisms hold on $D\times V_ a$ and $D\times W_{abj}$. Holomorphic sections identify topologically with the kernel of this gluing sequence: restrictions are continuous, and a compact set is covered by finitely many smaller coordinate pieces, so the inverse gluing map is continuous for compact convergence. The preceding corollary and product compatibility identify the two kernels, giving the result for $D\times Y$. Now cover $X$ and refine its overlaps in the same way, with fixed nuclear factor $\mathcal O(Y)$. The already proved polydisc-times-$Y$ case gives all local vertical isomorphisms, and a second kernel comparison proves the general assertion. Empty spaces and dimension-zero charts obey the same argument (their local function spaces are $0$ or $\mathbb C$). $\square$

Proposition 9.41.6.574665EBC99C Let $C^\bullet $ be a complex of Fréchet spaces with closed boundary images and let $H$ be nuclear Fréchet. Then

\[ H^ q(H\widehat\otimes C^\bullet ) \simeq H\widehat\otimes H^ q(C^\bullet ) \]

topologically, by the canonical product of a vector and a cohomology class. All cohomology spaces here are Fréchet, and the tensored complex has closed boundary images. For the assertion in one degree $q$, closedness of $B^ q(C)$ alone suffices.

Proof. Write $Z^ q=\ker d^ q$, $B^ q=\operatorname {im}d^{q-1}$. They are closed, hence Fréchet. The differential $C^{q-1}\to B^ q$ is open and surjective by open mapping. The kernel lemma identifies cycles with $H\widehat\otimes Z^ q$. Strict exactness applied to $0\to Z^{q-1}\to C^{q-1}\to B^ q\to 0$ makes the tensored differential onto $H\widehat\otimes B^ q$; the latter embeds as a closed subspace of $H\widehat\otimes C^ q$. Apply it again to $0\to B^ q\to Z^ q\to H^ q(C)\to 0$. The quotient topology gives exactly the displayed isomorphism. $\square$

We will use a topological part of the elementary long exact sequence construction. For a degreewise strict short exact sequence $0\to A^\bullet \xrightarrow {i}B^\bullet \xrightarrow {p}C^\bullet \to 0$ of Fréchet complexes, all maps in its algebraic cohomology long exact sequence are continuous for quotient cohomology topologies. For the connecting map take the closed Fréchet subspace $L=(p^ k)^{-1}(Z^ k(C))\subset B^ k$. The surjection $L\to Z^ k(C)$ is open. The map $b\mapsto [i^{-1}db]\in H^{k+1}(A)$ is continuous and is constant on its fibers: two lifts differ by an element of $A^ k$, whose differential is a boundary. It descends continuously to $Z^ k(C)$, vanishes on $B^ k(C)$ by lifting a primitive in $C^{k-1}$, and so gives a continuous connecting map on $H^ k(C)$. Exactness uses actual images: a connecting class is zero precisely when a lift can be adjusted by an $A$-element to a cycle; a $B$-cycle mapping to a $C$-boundary can be adjusted by a lifted primitive to an $A$-cycle; and an $A$-cycle becoming a $B$-boundary is obtained by the connecting construction from the projection of that primitive. These three statements prove exactness at every position. No boundary-closure assumption enters this construction.

Theorem 9.41.7 (Completed Künneth theorem).2A05F95A1DE7 Let $E^\bullet ,F^\bullet $ be complexes concentrated in nonnegative degrees, with Fréchet terms, continuous differentials and closed boundary images. Suppose every $E^ p$ is nuclear. Put

\[ T^ k=\bigoplus _{p+q=k}E^ p\widehat\otimes F^ q,\qquad D(x\otimes y)=d_ E x\otimes y+(-1)^ p x\otimes d_ F y. \]

The sums are finite. The total differential has closed images, and the canonical cross-product map is a topological isomorphism

8EF894FC2975 9.41.8
\begin{equation} \bigoplus _{p+q=k}H^ p(E)\widehat\otimes H^ q(F) \xrightarrow {\ \simeq \ }H^ k(T). \end{equation}

The same statement holds for complexes bounded below, after shifting their starting degrees and keeping the Koszul sign.

Proof. Let $Z_ E^ p,B_ E^ p$ be cycles and boundaries in $E^ p$. Regard the graded spaces $Z_ E^\bullet $ and $Q^ p=E^ p/Z_ E^ p\simeq B_ E^{p+1}$ as complexes with zero horizontal differential. The indicated isomorphism is topological by open mapping. There is a degreewise strict short exact sequence of complexes

\[ 0\longrightarrow Z_ E^\bullet \longrightarrow E^\bullet \longrightarrow Q^\bullet \longrightarrow 0. \]

The spaces $Z_ E^ p,B_ E^ p,Q^ p,H^ p(E)$ are nuclear Fréchet by closed-subspace/quotient permanence. Tensor with each $F^ q$ and take the finite diagonals. Strict tensor exactness yields a degreewise strict short exact sequence of total complexes

\[ 0\longrightarrow W=\operatorname {Tot}(Z_ E^\bullet \widehat\otimes F^\bullet ) \longrightarrow T \longrightarrow V=\operatorname {Tot}(Q^\bullet \widehat\otimes F^\bullet ) \longrightarrow 0. \]

Their outer differentials are only $(-1)^ p d_ F$ on horizontal degree $p$. The one-factor proposition therefore gives

\[ H^ k(W)=\bigoplus _{p+q=k}Z_ E^ p\widehat\otimes H^ q(F), \qquad H^{k-1}(V)=\bigoplus _{p+q=k}B_ E^ p\widehat\otimes H^ q(F), \]

topologically, with $B_ E^0=0$. The connecting map $H^{k-1}(V)\to H^ k(W)$ is the sum of the natural inclusions

C3183758C848 9.41.9
\begin{equation} B_ E^ p\widehat\otimes H^ q(F) \longrightarrow Z_ E^ p\widehat\otimes H^ q(F). \end{equation}

To verify the formula, take $b=d_ E x\in B_ E^ p$ and a cycle $y\in F^ q$. The lift $x\otimes y$ in horizontal degree $p-1$ has total differential $b\otimes y$, proving the formula on product classes. Their span is dense in each completed tensor. Both the connecting map, by the construction above, and the asserted inclusion are continuous, and the target $H^ k(W)$ is Hausdorff, so the formula holds everywhere.

Each inclusion in (9.41.9) is a closed embedding by strict tensor exactness (its quotient $H^ p(E)$ is nuclear). The connecting maps in every degree are thus injective with closed images. The long exact sequence now says $H^ k(W)\to H^ k(T)$ is surjective with this closed kernel. It is open by cohomological openness for Fréchet complexes. Its quotient is therefore topologically $H^ k(T)$; strict exactness of $0\to B_ E^ p\to Z_ E^ p\to H^ p(E)\to 0$ identifies that quotient with the left side of (9.41.8). On product classes the map is the stated cross product, so this is its canonical continuous extension and is natural.

In particular $H^ k(T)$ is Hausdorff Fréchet. Its boundary space is closed in the closed cycle space and hence in $T^ k$. All totalizations in this argument have finite diagonals; there is no infinite total-series convergence or unproved completion of later spectral-sequence pages. $\square$

Theorem 9.41.10.52191CAACAB5 In the preceding notation suppose instead that all $E^ p$ are nuclear Fréchet and all $H^ p(E)$ are finite dimensional. No closed-image assumption is made on either complex. Then the canonical map

\[ \bigoplus _{p+q=k}H^ p(E)\otimes H^ q(F) \xrightarrow {\ \simeq \ }H^ k(T) \]

is a topological isomorphism. A finite-dimensional tensor factor means its finite-copy topology on the other factor; the latter cohomology may be non-Hausdorff. The tensor in this formula is algebraic, with no Hausdorff completion of that quotient cohomology.

Proof. The map $d_ E:E^{p-1}\to Z_ E^ p$ has finite-codimensional image. By the Fréchet finite-codimension criterion, corollary 2.12.2 makes $B_ E^ p$ closed. Choose a finite-dimensional subspace $L^ p\subset Z_ E^ p$ mapping isomorphically onto $H^ p(E)$. These form a zero- differential subcomplex $L^\bullet \subset E^\bullet $. The quotient complex $A^\bullet =E^\bullet /L^\bullet $ has nuclear Fréchet terms and is acyclic. Indeed the induced cohomology map $H^ p(L)\to H^ p(E)$ is an isomorphism; the long exact sequence just established gives $H^ p(A)=0$. Consequently all boundary images in $A$ equal closed kernels.

Every row $A^\bullet \widehat\otimes F^ q$ is exact, by strict tensor exactness on its closed cycle/boundary short exact sequences; their middle spaces are nuclear. Its first-quadrant total complex is acyclic, as the following finite staircase shows. For a total $k$-cycle, its component with smallest vertical degree $q$ is a horizontal cycle. Use horizontal exactness to subtract the total differential of a component of bidegree $(k-q-1,q)$, removing it and adding only a component with vertical degree $q+1$. Continue. At vertical degree $k$ horizontal degree is zero, where exactness and absence of negative degree mean the horizontal kernel is zero. The procedure ends after finitely many steps and writes the original cycle as a total boundary. The signs in $D$ affect only the next, unprocessed component.

Strict tensor exactness also gives a degreewise strict sequence of total complexes

\[ 0\to \operatorname {Tot}(L^\bullet \widehat\otimes F^\bullet ) \to T\to \operatorname {Tot}(A^\bullet \widehat\otimes F^\bullet )\to 0. \]

The acyclicity just proved makes the first inclusion a cohomology isomorphism. It is a topological isomorphism on quotient cohomology by the cohomological openness theorem, which allows non-Hausdorff cohomology. The first total complex is a finite direct sum in each degree of finite copies of $F$ with signed differential; its cohomology is exactly $\bigoplus _{p+q=k}L^ p\otimes H^ q(F)$ with that topology. Identifying $L^ p$ with $H^ p(E)$ proves the formula. Changing the chosen representatives changes product cocycles by boundaries, so the resulting map is the canonical cross product, independent of the choices. $\square$
