9.15 Complete Hartogs domains and Taylor coefficientsF6F3F90BB780

F6F3F90BB780

Proposition 9.15.1.202C805DC8A2 Let $n\geq 2$, let $D\subset \mathbb C^{n-1}$ be open and let $R:D\to (0,\infty ]$ be lower semicontinuous. The complete Hartogs open set

\[ H=\{ (z,w):z\in D,\ |w|<R(z)\} \]

is pseudoconvex if and only if $D$ is pseudoconvex and $u=-\log R$ is PSH or identically $-\infty $ on each component. Every $f\in \mathcal O(H)$ has an expansion $f(z,w)=\sum _{j\geq 0}c_ j(z)w^ j$, with $c_ j\in \mathcal O(D)$, converging normally on $H$. If $H$ is a domain of existence of $f$, then

\[ u=\left(\limsup _{j\to \infty }j^{-1}\log |c_ j|\right)^*. \]

Consequently every PSH function on an open set is locally of this form, with the identically zero coefficients assigned logarithm $-\infty $.

Proof. If $H$ is pseudoconvex, restricting its continuous PSH exhaustion to the closed zero section gives an exhaustion of $D$. The existing Oka proof gives the joint directional submean property of $-\log d_ H((z,w),\xi )$. Its restriction to $w=0$ and the fixed vertical direction is $-\log R(z)$. It is usc; the submean and local alternative theorems make it PSH or identically $-\infty $ on each component. Conversely, on $D\times \mathbb C$ the function $v(z,w)=u(z)+\log |w|$ is PSH, with its natural pole values. Its sublevel $\{ v<0\} =H$ is pseudoconvex by the preceding proposition and the existing product property. Components where $u=-\infty $ give the entire product directly.

Near every point of $D$, lower semicontinuity supplies a common positive fiber radius. Cauchy's formula there makes the Taylor coefficients holomorphic on the base, and these definitions agree on overlaps. On a compact subset of $H$ its circled fiberwise hull is compact in $H$, and a dilation by some fixed factor $t>1$ is still compact in $H$. The fiberwise Cauchy estimate gives $|c_ j(z)w^ j|\leq M t^{-j}$ on the original compact set (for $w=0$ the positive-degree terms vanish). This proves normal convergence.

Put $a_ j=j^{-1}\log |c_ j|$ for $j\geq 1$. On relatively compact base neighborhoods Cauchy's estimate using a common inner fiber radius bounds the $a_ j$ uniformly above. The regularized limsup $a=(\limsup _ j a_ j)^*$ is PSH or identically $-\infty $ on each component; identically zero terms may be omitted from each envelope, with an empty tail giving $-\infty $. The fiberwise convergence radius gives $\limsup _ j a_ j\leq u$; since $u$ is usc, also $a\leq u$. The same series defines a holomorphic function on $H_ a=\{ (z,w):\log |w|+a(z)<0\} $. Indeed, near a point of this set, usc of $a$ gives a strict margin for a small product neighborhood, and the subharmonic Hartogs lemma applied to $a_ j$ gives a geometric normal majorant there. Its sum equals $f$ on $H$. If $a<u$ anywhere, $H_ a$ has a component strictly larger than the corresponding component of $H$, and is connected over that connected base component through its zero section. This extension contradicts the domain-of-existence hypothesis. Thus $a=u$. For the final assertion, restrict a PSH function $u$ to a ball $D$, put $R=\mathrm e^{-u}$, and use the already proved existence of a holomorphic function with domain of existence $H$. The formula just proved gives the claimed local representation, including poles of $u$. $\square$
