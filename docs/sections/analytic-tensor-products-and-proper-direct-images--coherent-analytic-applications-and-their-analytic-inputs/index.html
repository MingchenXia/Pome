9.42 Coherent analytic applications and their analytic inputs34BD178F719E

34BD178F719E

Here is the precise reduction from coherent analytic geometry to the preceding functional analysis. It retains arbitrary coherent sheaves and nonreduced analytic spaces in the analytic input category. For such a category, the required analytic section theorems are the following.

  1. Section spaces of coherent sheaves have Hausdorff Fréchet topologies, with continuous restrictions and the topological gluing property used in corollary 9.41.4.

  2. Every coherent sheaf admits a countable cover by patches $U$ embedded in polydiscs $D$, with finite presentations whose sections give continuous exact sequences

    DDFEC4904A07 9.42.1
    \begin{equation} \mathcal O(D)^ r\longrightarrow \mathcal O(D)^ s \longrightarrow \mathcal F(U)\longrightarrow 0. \end{equation}

    Overlaps can be covered by countably many such patches. Analytic external products are right exact, and these presentations remain exact on sections after external product with a polydisc, and then after a presentation in the other variable. These are the local presentation, analytic external-product and Cartan section-exactness inputs.

  3. Countable covers used for cohomology have acyclic finite intersections and admit common countable acyclic refinements. The product rectangles needed below are acyclic for the external product. The canonical comparison with sheaf cohomology is the acyclic-cover theorem 10.43.16. For the relative statement, $R^ q\pi _*$ is the sheafification of $U\mapsto H^ q(U\times Y,\mathcal S)$.

  4. For the relative statement, the global coherent Cartan vanishing theorem has been established on Stein spaces in the analytic category. The chosen finite intersections $V_ J$ are Stein, as are the products $X\times V_ J$ for Stein $X$. In particular their higher coherent cohomology vanishes, so $(X\times V_ b)$ is an acyclic cover for $\mathcal F\boxtimes \mathcal G$. A Stein basis of $X$ has these same properties for the sheafification argument.

These inputs require their analytic proofs in the stated category. The following conclusions are conditional on these explicit inputs; the functional arguments supply the tensor, nonclosed gluing-image and completion steps in full.

Proposition 9.42.2.7BF5CAEB536D Under the first two analytic inputs, all coherent section spaces are nuclear, and external products give natural topological isomorphisms

\[ \mathcal F(X)\widehat\otimes \mathcal G(Y) \simeq (\mathcal F\boxtimes \mathcal G)(X\times Y). \]

Proof. In (9.42.1), the last map is a continuous surjection onto a Hausdorff Fréchet space. Its kernel is closed, and open mapping identifies its quotient topology with the stated topology of $\mathcal F(U)$. Nuclearity of $\mathcal O(D)^ s$ and closed quotient permanence give local nuclearity. Global sections are a closed gluing kernel in a countable product of these local nuclear spaces, hence nuclear.

For two polydiscs the tensor product isomorphism is proposition 9.41.5. Present $\mathcal F$ on $U\subset D$ as above and let the other factor be a polydisc $D'$. The tensor of the section presentation with $\mathcal O(D')$ is right exact: factor its first map through the closed kernel of its last surjection and apply strict tensor exactness to the resulting two short exact sequences. The analytic presentation of $\mathcal F\boxtimes \mathcal O_{D'}$ on $U\times D'$ gives another right exact row. Their first two terms are identified by the polydisc case, so their quotient terms are canonically topologically isomorphic. Now present $\mathcal G$ on $V\subset D'$. Apply the same argument, using the just established $\mathcal F$-times-polydisc case for the first two terms, to get the formula on $U\times V$. All quotient topologies are justified by the given Fréchet section topology and open mapping.

Fix such a local $U$ and cover arbitrary $Y$, refining each overlap by local presentation patches. The local product formulas identify all terms of the two gluing sequences. The kernel and product lemma and corollary 9.41.4 identify their first terms even when the final gluing image is not closed. Repeat with a cover of $X$ to obtain the assertion globally. $\square$

For clarity, the topological cohomology comparison on a product can be established without completing a spectral sequence. Let $\mathcal U=(U_ a)$, $\mathcal V=(V_ b)$ be countable covers, and write $U_ I,V_ J$ for finite ordered intersections, with repeated indices permitted. For a sheaf $\mathcal S$ on $X\times Y$, suppose every $U_ I\times V_ J$ is acyclic. Define

\[ C^{p,q}=\prod _{|I|=p+1,\ |J|=q+1} \mathcal S(U_ I\times V_ J), \qquad D=\delta _ X+(-1)^ p\delta _ Y. \]

The algebraic identification

\[ H^ k(\operatorname {Tot}C)=H^ k(X\times Y,\mathcal S) \]

follows from the checked finite-comparison lemma 10.43.15 as follows. Use the Godement resolution $\mathcal I^\bullet $ of $\mathcal S$ and the triple array

\[ \prod _{I,J}\mathcal I^ r(U_ I\times V_ J),\qquad D=\delta _ X+(-1)^ p\delta _ Y+(-1)^{p+q}d_{\mathcal I}. \]

For fixed $p,q$ its augmented $r$-column is exact by the assumed rectangle acyclicity. For fixed $r$, the two augmented Čech directions successively have exact rows because Godement terms and their open restrictions are flabby, by lemma 10.43.14. Applying finite comparison successively in these directions identifies the triple total with both $\operatorname {Tot}C$ and $\mathcal I^\bullet (X\times Y)$. Each total degree has finitely many triples, so these are the actual direct-sum totalizations covered by that lemma.

This comparison is topological whenever the section spaces are Fréchet with continuous restrictions and sheaf cohomology carries its acyclic-Čech quotient topology. Indeed, for the rectangle cover $\mathcal W=(U_ a\times V_ b)$ the map

8D2B0BFD4C04 9.42.3
\begin{equation} (Ac)_{(a_0,b_0)\ldots (a_ k,b_ k)} =\sum _{p+q=k} c_{a_0\ldots a_ p\, ;\, b_ p\ldots b_ k} \big |_{U_{a_0\ldots a_ k}\times V_{b_0\ldots b_ k}} \end{equation}

is a continuous chain map from the double total to the ordinary Čech complex of $\mathcal W$. In the omission formula, the terms with an omission strictly to either side of the shared index $p$ give $A\delta _ X$ and $(-1)^ pA\delta _ Y$; the two terms at the shared index cancel between consecutive $p$. This proves $\delta A=AD$. The same map on Godement terms commutes with the resolution differential and is the identity on the augmented global section complex. The preceding comparisons therefore show that it induces the canonical cohomology isomorphism. Both its source and target are Fréchet complexes, so cohomological openness makes this isomorphism topological. The same argument with a common acyclic refinement shows independence of the chosen acyclic-cover quotient topology.

Corollary 9.42.4.FAB99B827E4B Under the analytic inputs above, suppose the cohomologies of $\mathcal F$ on $X$ and of $\mathcal G$ on $Y$ are Hausdorff in every degree for their acyclic-Čech topologies. Then the completed cross product is a topological isomorphism

\[ \bigoplus _{p+q=k}H^ p(X,\mathcal F)\widehat\otimes H^ q(Y,\mathcal G) \simeq H^ k(X\times Y,\mathcal F\boxtimes \mathcal G). \]

If instead all cohomologies of one factor are finite dimensional, the same formula holds with the algebraic finite-dimensional tensor factors and without Hausdorff assumptions on the other factor's cohomology.

Proof. Take the two countable acyclic Čech complexes $E,F$. Their terms are countable products of nuclear Fréchet section spaces. Product compatibility in both variables and the section tensor formula identify

\[ E^ p\widehat\otimes F^ q \simeq \prod _{I,J} (\mathcal F\boxtimes \mathcal G)(U_ I\times V_ J), \]

with the Čech differentials. The double-total comparison just proved identifies its cohomology topologically with product sheaf cohomology. A Hausdorff quotient of the closed cycle space has a closed boundary subspace. Thus the Hausdorff assumption is exactly the closed-range hypothesis needed in theorem 9.41.7. Its canonical cross-product formula gives the first assertion. The finite-dimensional assertion follows instead from theorem 9.41.10. In particular, when a compact-factor finiteness theorem has been established for coherent sheaves in the analytic category, it supplies exactly this finite-dimensional hypothesis. $\square$

Corollary 9.42.5.781692B04304 Under the analytic inputs, let $\pi :X\times Y\to X$ be the projection and assume $H^\bullet (Y,\mathcal G)$ is Hausdorff. For Stein $X$ one has naturally

\[ H^ q(X\times Y,\mathcal F\boxtimes \mathcal G) \simeq \mathcal F(X)\widehat\otimes H^ q(Y,\mathcal G). \]

For general $X$ and every open $U\subset X$ one has

\[ \Gamma \bigl (U,R^ q\pi _*(\mathcal F\boxtimes \mathcal G)\bigr ) \simeq \mathcal F(U)\widehat\otimes H^ q(Y,\mathcal G). \]

If just $H^ q(Y,\mathcal G)$ is finite dimensional, regardless of other degrees, then $R^ q\pi _*(\mathcal F\boxtimes \mathcal G) \simeq \mathcal F\otimes H^ q(Y,\mathcal G)$.

Proof. For Stein $X$, the assumed global Stein coherent vanishing applies to $X$ and to every Stein rectangle $X\times V_ J$. Thus the product cover is acyclic for the external product. The acyclic-cover theorem and section/product compatibility identify its cohomology complex with $\mathcal F(X)\widehat\otimes \check C^\bullet (\mathcal V,\mathcal G)$. Apply proposition 9.41.6 and the acyclic-cover comparison. The space $H^ q(Y,\mathcal G)$ is nuclear Fréchet by closed cycle/quotient permanence. Hence corollary 9.41.4 makes the assignment

\[ U\longmapsto \mathcal F(U)\widehat\otimes H^ q(Y,\mathcal G) \]

a sheaf, using symmetry of the tensor factors. Its comparison with $R^ q\pi _*$ is an isomorphism on the Stein basis by the first assertion and the stipulated sheafification description. It is therefore an isomorphism of sheaves and gives the stated formula on every open set.

If $H^ q(Y,\mathcal G)$ is finite dimensional, its boundary space in the Čech cycle space is closed by corollary 2.12.2. The one-degree version of the one-factor proposition applies even if other boundary images are not closed. Its finite-dimensional tensor factor is just a finite direct sum of $\mathcal F(U)$, already a sheaf. The same basis argument gives the final assertion. $\square$
