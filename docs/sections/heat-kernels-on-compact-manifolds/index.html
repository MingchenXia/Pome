3.10 Heat kernels on compact manifoldsB8A97FFD89FE

B8A97FFD89FE

We use $dd^ c=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial /(2\pi )$. The Laplacians below are nonnegative. Scalar heat is $h_ t=\exp (-tL)$, where $L=\mathrm d^*\mathrm d$. For a compact Kähler manifold $(X,\omega )$ of dimension $n$, put $\mathrm dV=\omega ^ n/n!$. A positive $(1,1)$-current has a positive trace measure $\rho =T\wedge \omega ^{n-1}/(n-1)!$. All constants in the heat estimates depend on fixed metrics, and not on a heat time in $(0,1]$.

Lemma 3.10.1.126365F8EBD6 On a connected compact smooth Riemannian manifold of real dimension $m\geq 1$, scalar heat has a nonnegative symmetric smooth kernel $h_ t(x,y)$ of total integral one, and

\[ h_ t(x,y)\leq C t^{-m/2} \exp \left(-\frac{d(x,y)^2}{Ct}\right),\qquad 0<t\leq 1. \]

Proof. We first specify the elliptic input used to construct heat. Use the compact Laplace-type resolvent and Green operator, spectral decomposition for a compact quadratic-form domain. The compact Sobolev inclusion, the coercive weak resolvent, and interior elliptic regularity give a compact self-adjoint inverse of $L+1$ on $L^2$. The spectral theorem therefore gives an orthonormal eigenbasis of smooth functions for $L$, with nonnegative eigenvalues tending to infinity. Define $h_ t$ by the spectral multipliers $e^{-t\lambda }$. Elliptic estimates and $\sup _{\lambda \geq 0}(1+\lambda )^ N e^{-t\lambda }<\infty $ show that it maps every negative Sobolev space into every positive one for $t>0$. Applying this at half the time, and then using Sobolev evaluation and self-adjointness, gives a smooth kernel. The same construction applies to a metric connection Laplacian and to the Hodge Laplacian: their principal part is scalar, their coercive shifted forms have the same compactness and regularity proof, and their spectral multipliers have the same estimates. The spectral construction also gives the heat equation, semigroup property, and convergence to the initial distribution. The scalar maximum principle gives positivity; $L1=0$ gives conservation of constants, and self-adjointness gives symmetry and conservation of integrals.

Here is a proof of the kernel bound. We use the compact Nash inequality

73DAD6727BB9 3.10.2
\begin{equation} \| v\| _2^{\, 2+4/m} \leq C\bigl (\| \mathrm dv\| _2^2+\| v\| _2^2\bigr ) \| v\| _1^{\, 4/m}. \end{equation}

For completeness, on $\mathbb R^ m$, splitting the Fourier integral at radius $R$ gives $\| v\| _2^2\leq C R^ m\| v\| _1^2+ R^{-2}\| \mathrm dv\| _2^2$; optimization proves its Euclidean version. Choose a finite smooth partition with $\sum _ j\chi _ j^2=1$ in coordinate charts. Metric and volume norms are comparable to the Euclidean ones on their supports. Apply the Euclidean inequality to each $\chi _ jv$, use $\| \chi _ jv\| _1\leq C\| v\| _1$ and $\sum _ j\| \mathrm d(\chi _ jv)\| _2^2\leq C(\| \mathrm dv\| _2^2+\| v\| _2^2)$, and use $\sum _ j a_ j^{1+2/m}\geq N^{-2/m}(\sum _ j a_ j)^{1+2/m}$. This proves (3.10.2). The argument extends to $H^1$ functions by smooth approximation.

Fix a real Lipschitz function $\psi $ with $|\mathrm d\psi |\leq 1$ almost everywhere and a real number $\lambda $. Put $E_ t=e^{\lambda \psi }h_ t e^{-\lambda \psi }$. For nonnegative smooth initial data, write $u=E_ tf$ and, for $p\geq 2$, $Y=\int u^ p$. Integration by parts gives

\[ \frac{Y'}p =-(p-1)\int u^{p-2}|\mathrm du|^2 +\lambda (p-2)\int u^{p-1} \langle \mathrm du,\mathrm d\psi \rangle +\lambda ^2\int u^ p|\mathrm d\psi |^2. \]

The identity is also valid for Lipschitz $\psi $: the multipliers are in $W^{1,\infty }$ and the integration by parts uses only their first weak derivatives. One may first regularize positive powers at zero. Young's inequality implies

A5D366E25358 3.10.3
\begin{equation} Y'\leq -\| \mathrm d(u^{p/2})\| _2^2+C p^2\lambda ^2Y,\qquad p\geq 2. \end{equation}

In particular $\| E_ tf\| _ q\leq e^{Cq\lambda ^2t}\| f\| _ q$ for $q\geq 2$. We do not need such an assertion for $q=1$. Positivity extends all resulting norm bounds to arbitrary data.

Take $p\geq 4$ and $q=p/2$. Applying (3.10.2) to $u^{p/2}$ and using the preceding $L^ q$ bound gives

\[ Y'\leq -c\, \frac{Y^{1+2/m}}{\| u\| _{p/2}^{\, 2p/m}} +aY,\qquad \| u\| _{p/2}^{\, p}\leq e^{at}\| f\| _{p/2}^{\, p}, \quad a=Cp^2(1+\lambda ^2). \]

Choose the fixed constant in $a$ large enough for both inequalities. For $Z=e^{-at}Y$ and $b=\| f\| _{p/2}^{\, p}$, these give $Z'\leq -cZ^{1+2/m}/b^{2/m}$. Integration, discarding the nonnegative initial reciprocal power, yields

3E18288F9091 3.10.4
\begin{equation} \| E_ tf\| _ p\leq (C/t)^{m/(2p)} e^{Cp(1+\lambda ^2)t}\| f\| _{p/2}. \end{equation}

For data merely in $L^{p/2}$ use positive smooth approximation; the estimate is independent of the approximants.

Iterate (3.10.4) with $p_ k=2^{k+2}$ and successive time intervals $\delta _ k=3t\, 4^{-k-1}$, $k\geq 0$. Their sum is $t$, $\sum _ k p_ k\delta _ k< Ct$, and $\sum _ k1/p_ k=1/2$. Also $\sum _ k k/p_ k<\infty $, so the product of the time factors is at most $Ct^{-m/4}$. The semigroup property, followed by the $p_ k\to \infty $ limit on the compact manifold, proves

\[ \| E_ t\| _{2\to \infty } \leq Ct^{-m/4}e^{C(1+\lambda ^2)t}. \]

One may justify the increasing-time limit by continuity in positive time with values in $C^0$, since $E_ sf$ is a fixed continuous multiplier times a smooth heat solution. The adjoint semigroup is obtained by replacing $\lambda $ by $-\lambda $. Duality gives the same $1\to 2$ estimate. Splitting the time into two halves now gives

\[ \| E_ t\| _{1\to \infty } \leq Ct^{-m/2}e^{C(1+\lambda ^2)t}. \]

By continuity of its kernel,

\[ h_ t(x,y)\leq Ct^{-m/2} \exp \bigl (Ct+C\lambda ^2t-\lambda (\psi (x)-\psi (y))\bigr ). \]

Take $\psi (z)=d(z,y)$, which is Lipschitz with constant one, and minimize over $\lambda \geq 0$. This proves the assertion. $\square$

Lemma 3.10.5.56A174D59425 Let $\rho $ be a finite positive measure on a compact Riemannian manifold of real dimension $m$. Suppose $\rho (B_ r(x))\leq A r^{m-2}$ for all $x$ and $0<r\leq r_0$, where $m\geq 2$. Then

\[ 0\leq h_ t\rho (x)\leq C(A+\rho (X))/t,\qquad 0<t\leq 1. \]

If $F\in L^1(X,\mathrm dV)$ is nonnegative, then

\[ t\int _ X (h_ t\rho )F\, \mathrm dV\longrightarrow 0. \]

Proof. For $t<r_0^2$, split the Gaussian kernel bound into the ball $B_{\sqrt t}(x)$ and the annuli with radii $2^ j\sqrt t$, as long as they are below $r_0$. Their contributions are bounded respectively by $CA/t$ and $CA\, 2^{(j+1)(m-2)}e^{-4^ j/C}/t$. The rest is bounded by $Ct^{-m/2}e^{-r_0^2/(Ct)}\rho (X)$, which is at most $C\rho (X)/t$. The remaining times are bounded by the same assertion after enlarging $C$. For the last claim, truncate $F$ at a number $M$. Conservation of the scalar heat integral gives

\[ t\int (h_ t\rho )F \leq C(A+\rho (X))\int _{\{ F>M\} }F\, \mathrm dV +Mt\, \rho (X). \]

First let $t$ tend to zero, then let $M$ tend to infinity. $\square$
