9.22 A smooth boundary for a proper biholomorphic image4047ADF10136

4047ADF10136

Lemma 9.22.1.9D4AAB621920 Let $(P,Q):\mathbb C^2\to \mathbb C^2$ be a polynomial automorphism and fix $S>1$. Put

\[ D_0=\{ |P/S|<1\} ,\qquad D_ N=\{ |Q+T(P/S)^ N|<T\} ,\quad T=\lceil e^{\sqrt N}\rceil . \]

On any fixed ball, $\partial D_ N$ tends to $\partial D_0$ in $C^ m$ for every fixed $m$, with the interior sides agreeing. More precisely, given a ball $B_ R$, an integer $m$ and $\epsilon >0$, for sufficiently large $N$ there is a smooth diffeomorphism $\Phi _ N$ of $\mathbb R^4$, equal to the identity outside $B_{R+2}$, such that

\[ \| \Phi _ N-\operatorname {id}\| _{C^ m} +\| \Phi _ N^{-1}-\operatorname {id}\| _{C^ m}<\epsilon , \qquad \Phi _ N(D_0)\cap B_ R=D_ N\cap B_ R. \]

The norms of differences include the zeroth derivative.

Proof. Write $p=P/S$, $a=Q/T$ and $\delta _ N=(16\sqrt N)^{-1}$. All derivatives of $P,Q$ on $\overline B_{R+3}$ are fixed before $N$ is chosen. On the band $||p|-1|<2\delta _ N$ we have $|p|^ N\geq e^{-\sqrt N/4}$ for large $N$, so $|a/p^ N|<1/2$. The logarithm given by its power series at one therefore defines the smooth real function

\[ r_ N=|p|^2\exp \left(\frac2N\operatorname {Re} \log (1+a/p^ N)\right)-1. \]

Its negative set on this band is exactly $D_ N$. For each fixed derivative order $l$, repeated product and chain differentiation gives

\[ \| r_ N-(|p|^2-1)\| _{C^ l} \leq C_ lN^{A_ l}e^{-c\sqrt N} \]

on the band, for constants depending on the fixed polynomials and ball. Indeed derivatives of $p^{-N}$ contribute only powers of $N$ and bounded derivatives of $p$, while its size is at most $e^{\sqrt N/4}$; the factor $T^{-1}$ contributes $e^{-\sqrt N}$. Each term involving the logarithm has at least one such small factor. Its power series and all fixed-order differentiated series converge uniformly when $|a/p^ N|<1/2$. This proves the estimate, with some $c>0$.

Outside the narrower band $||p|-1|<\delta _ N$, the two domains agree on the fixed ball: if $|p|\leq 1-\delta _ N$, then $|p^ N+a|\leq e^{-\sqrt N/16}+O(e^{-\sqrt N})<1$; if $|p|\geq 1+\delta _ N$, the reverse triangle inequality gives $|p^ N+a|\geq e^{\sqrt N/32}-O(e^{-\sqrt N})>1$. Choose a cutoff in $|p|$ which is one on the narrower band and zero outside the wider one. Its derivatives have only polynomial growth in $\sqrt N$. Multiplying the difference above by this cutoff and also by a spatial cutoff equal to one on $B_{R+2}$ and supported in $B_{R+3}$, then extending by zero, gives $e_ N$ with arbitrarily small $C^ l$ norm on the whole space. The function $|p|^2-1+e_ N$ defines $D_ N$ on the ball: on the transition band both unmodified defining functions have the same sign, so their convex interpolation preserves that sign.

The gradient of $|p|^2-1$ is nonzero near $|p|=1$, since $P$ is a coordinate of an automorphism. It has a positive lower bound on the relevant compact band. The same holds for $r_ t=|p|^2-1+t e_ N$, $0\leq t\leq 1$, when $N$ is large. The vector field

\[ X_ t=-e_ N\frac{\nabla r_ t}{|\nabla r_ t|^2} \]

is smooth on the band and zero near its edges. Extend it by zero there, and multiply by a spatial cutoff equal to one on $B_{R+1}$ and supported in $B_{R+2}$. Its time-one flow transports the two defining functions near $B_ R$, since $\frac d{dt}r_ t(\Phi _ t(x))=0$ there. The displacement can be made less than the spatial margin. The differential equations for the flow and its derivatives show that the flow and its inverse tend to the identity in $C^ m$ when $e_ N\to 0$ in $C^{m+1}$: differentiate the flow equation successively and integrate the resulting linear bounds. This proves all assertions. $\square$

Theorem 9.22.2.CE10A564601F There is a proper domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^2$ with smooth boundary which is biholomorphic to $\mathbb C^2$.

Proof. Fix a large integer $T_1>10$, let $F_1$ be the identity, and put $P_1=w$, $P_0=z$. Inductively choose integers $N_ j$ and $T_ j=\lceil e^{\sqrt{N_ j}}\rceil $, and let $H_ j$ add $T_ j(P_{j-1}/T_{j-1})^{N_ j}$ to the other coordinate of $F_{j-1}$. Thus $F_ j=H_ j\circ F_{j-1}$ is an alternating polynomial shear composition, with determinant one, and its newly changed coordinate is

\[ P_ j=P_{j-2}+T_ j(P_{j-1}/T_{j-1})^{N_ j}. \]

Set $D_ j=\{ |P_ j|<T_ j\} $. The two coordinates $P_{j-1},P_{j-2}$ of $F_{j-1}$ form a polynomial automorphism, up to their order. Consequently the preceding lemma applies at every step.

Take $R_ j=jR_0$, with $R_0>4T_1+10$, and a positive summable sequence $\epsilon _ j$ with $\sum \epsilon _ j<1/10$. Choose $N_ j\geq j^2$ sufficiently large that:

  1. $T_ j>2(j+2)$;

  2. there is a diffeomorphism $\Phi _ j$ transporting $D_{j-1}$ to $D_ j$ on $B_{R_ j}$, with both $\Phi _ j$ and its inverse within $\epsilon _ j$ of the identity in $C^ j$ on $\mathbb R^4$;

  3. $\| F_ j^{-1}-F_{j-1}^{-1}\| _{C^ j(\overline B_ j)}<2^{-j}$.

All these conditions can be imposed at the same step. The second is the lemma, with all earlier polynomials fixed. For the third, on $B_{j+1}$ the inverse shear differs from the identity by a monomial with coefficient $T_ j/T_{j-1}^{N_ j}$. Its derivatives of each fixed order tend to zero because $j+1<T_{j-1}/2$ and $\log T_ j=O(\sqrt{N_ j})$. The derivatives of $F_{j-1}^{-1}$ are already fixed, so the chain rule in $F_ j^{-1}=F_{j-1}^{-1}\circ H_ j^{-1}$ proves the claim. Increase $T_1$ or use a smaller first target ball if necessary for the first step; the stated $T_1>10$ already suffices.

We describe the local limit of the domains carefully. For fixed $k$, the compositions $\Phi _ j\circ \cdots \circ \Phi _{k+1}$ converge in $C^\infty $, together with their inverses, to a smooth diffeomorphism. To check this, fix a derivative order $m$. After finitely many indices all maps and their inverses are $C^ m$-close to the identity by a summable amount. The chain rule bounds their first derivatives by a convergent product; at each higher order it gives the same product times already bounded lower derivatives and a summable error. Induction gives bounded derivatives and the Cauchy property at every order. The inverse limits are inverses by uniform convergence and the common displacement bound.

On $B_{R_ k-2}$ these compositions transport $D_ k$ to $D_ j$: all intermediate points move by less than $1/10$ and remain inside the balls where the transport identities hold. Thus $D_ j$ converges locally, with its interior side, to a smooth open set $D_\infty $. Equivalently, a point is in $D_\infty $ if a neighborhood of it is contained in all sufficiently late $D_ j$. The diffeomorphism description on each smaller ball proves this characterization and its consistency on overlaps. It also proves that the boundary is smooth, that compact interior sets eventually lie in $D_ j$, and that limits of bounded sequences $x_ j\in D_ j$ belong to $\overline{D_\infty }$. Since $D_1=\{ |w|<T_1\} $ and the total displacement is less than $1/10$, the limit contains a neighborhood of zero and excludes a neighborhood of $(0,2T_1)$. Let $\Omega $ be its component containing zero. It is proper and has smooth boundary: locally the interior side of a smooth hypersurface is connected, so a boundary patch of this component is a boundary patch of $D_\infty $.

We next prove convergence of the forward maps on $\Omega $. Given a compact $K\Subset \Omega $, choose a connected relatively compact open neighborhood in $\Omega $ containing $K$ and zero. For all sufficiently large $j$ it lies in $D_ j$. The holomorphic functions $P_ j/T_ j$ map this neighborhood to the disc and vanish at zero. Normal-family compactness and the maximum principle give a constant $0<\eta _ K<1$ with $|P_ j/T_ j|\leq 1-\eta _ K$ on $K$ for all late $j$. Otherwise a locally uniform subsequential limit would have modulus one at an interior point and vanish at zero, a contradiction. Hence

\[ \| F_{j+1}-F_ j\| _ K \leq T_{j+1}(1-\eta _ K)^{N_{j+1}}. \]

The series converges because $\log T_ j=O(\sqrt{N_ j})$ and $N_ j\geq j^2$. Thus $F_ j\to F$ locally uniformly on $\Omega $, and $\det F'=1$ by convergence of derivatives.

By the third inductive condition, $G_ j=F_ j^{-1}$ converges locally uniformly on all of $\mathbb C^2$ to a holomorphic map $G$ with $\det G'=1$ and $G(0)=0$. If $V$ is a bounded target ball, then $G_ j(V)\subset D_ j$ for large $j$, since the active coordinate of $F_ j(G_ j(z))=z$ is bounded by the radius of $V<T_ j$. Local convergence of the domains and maps gives $G(V)\subset \overline{D_\infty }$. The map $G$ is open, so $G(\mathbb C^2)\subset \operatorname {int}\overline{D_\infty } =D_\infty $. Connectedness and $G(0)=0$ place its image in $\Omega $.

For $x\in \Omega $, take limits in $G_ j(F_ j(x))=x$, using a fixed compact target neighborhood of $F(x)$, to get $G(F(x))=x$. For $z\in \mathbb C^2$, the points $G_ j(z)$ eventually lie in a fixed compact neighborhood of $G(z)\in \Omega $, so limits in $F_ j(G_ j(z))=z$ give $F(G(z))=z$. These identities prove that $F$ and $G$ are inverse biholomorphisms, completing the construction. $\square$
