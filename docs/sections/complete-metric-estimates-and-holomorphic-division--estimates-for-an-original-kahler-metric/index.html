6.27 Estimates for an original Kähler metric89E648091250

89E648091250

Use the conventions and maximal Dolbeault operators of section 6.26. All manifolds are Hausdorff, second countable and without boundary. In particular the curvature is unscaled, $\mathcal H(f)=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial f$, and all inverse energies use the semidefinite quotient convention. Here $m$-semipositivity means nonnegativity of the Nakano form on tensors of rank at most $m$, as in the strict version of proposition 10.33.6.

Lemma 6.27.1.13C546CE0C2F Let $\gamma \geq \omega $ be two Hermitian metrics and let $(E,h)$ be a smooth Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle. In degree $(n,k)$, $0\leq k\leq n$,

0F966B814C47 6.27.2
\begin{equation} |u|_\gamma ^2\, \mathrm dV_\gamma \leq |u|_\omega ^2\, \mathrm dV_\omega . \end{equation}

Fix $1\leq q\leq n$ and suppose the curvature of $E$ is $m$-semipositive, where $m\geq \min (n-q+1,\operatorname {rank}E)$. If $B_\omega =[\mathrm i\Theta (E),\Lambda _\omega ]$ and similarly for $\gamma $, then these operators are semipositive on $(n,q)$ and

32414668D927 6.27.3
\begin{equation} |g|_{B_\gamma ^{-1},\gamma }^2\, \mathrm dV_\gamma \leq |g|_{B_\omega ^{-1},\omega }^2\, \mathrm dV_\omega . \end{equation}

No invertibility of either curvature operator is required.

Proof. At the point under consideration choose coordinates and a fiber frame such that $\omega =\mathrm i\sum _ j\mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j$, $\gamma =\mathrm i\sum _ j\gamma _ j\mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j$, $\gamma _ j\geq 1$, and $h$ is the identity. Write $\Gamma =\prod _ j\gamma _ j$ and $\gamma _ J=\prod _{j\in J}\gamma _ j$, with empty product one. An $(n,k)$-form has coefficients $u_ J$ in the fixed holomorphic top form and increasing barred indices. Then

\[ |u|_\gamma ^2=\Gamma ^{-1}\sum _ J\gamma _ J^{-1}|u_ J|^2, \qquad \mathrm dV_\gamma =\Gamma \, \mathrm dV_\omega . \]

This proves (6.27.2); at $k=0$ there is equality. The same calculation gives the pairing density.

Write $\mathrm i\Theta (E)=\mathrm i\sum _{j,l}R_{j\bar l} \mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ l$ in this frame. Proposition 10.33.6, applied to the coframe $\sqrt{\gamma _ j}\mathrm dz_ j$, gives

A64C7F4820EA 6.27.4
\begin{equation} \Gamma \langle B_\gamma v,v\rangle _\gamma =\sum _{|S|=q-1}\gamma _ S^{-1} \sum _{j,l}\gamma _ j^{-1}\gamma _ l^{-1} \langle R_{j\bar l}v_{jS},v_{lS}\rangle . \end{equation}

For each $S$, the inner sum is nonnegative: the tensor $(\gamma _ j^{-1}v_{jS})_ j$ has zero rows for $j\in S$ and rank at most $\min (n-q+1,\operatorname {rank}E)$. The same rank test proves semipositivity of both operators. As $\gamma _ S\geq 1$, replacing $\gamma _ S^{-1}$ in this nonnegative sum by $\gamma _ S^{-2}$ gives a lower bound. Define $Dv$ by $(Dv)_ J=\gamma _ J^{-1}v_ J$. The resulting bound is exactly

129500DAFFAD 6.27.5
\begin{equation} \Gamma \langle B_\gamma v,v\rangle _\gamma \geq \langle B_\omega Dv,Dv\rangle _\omega , \qquad \Gamma \langle g,v\rangle _\gamma =\langle g,Dv\rangle _\omega . \end{equation}

If the inverse energy for $\omega $ is finite, its defining Cauchy–Schwarz inequality applied to $Dv$ implies

\[ |\langle g,v\rangle _\gamma |^2 \leq \Gamma ^{-1}|g|_{B_\omega ^{-1},\omega }^2 \langle B_\gamma v,v\rangle _\gamma . \]

Taking the supremum over $v$ proves (6.27.3). This argument also handles zero denominators; when the original energy is infinite the claimed inequality is automatic. Only pointwise diagonalization is used; no smooth eigenframe is needed. $\square$

Theorem 6.27.6.D64DD0A9DB51 Let $X$ admit a complete Kähler metric $\widehat\omega $, and let $\omega $ be any other Kähler metric. Let $(E,h)$ have $m$-semipositive curvature, with $m\geq \min (n-q+1,\operatorname {rank}E)$ and $1\leq q\leq n$. If $g\in L^2_{n,q}(X,E;\omega ,h)$ is closed and

\[ M=\int _ X|g|_{B_\omega ^{-1},\omega }^2\, \mathrm dV_\omega <\infty , \]

then a primitive $u$ exists with the estimate for the original metric

50E076EEA847 6.27.7
\begin{equation} D''u=g,\qquad \int _ X|u|_\omega ^2\, \mathrm dV_\omega \leq M. \end{equation}

Proof. For $\varepsilon >0$ put $\omega _\varepsilon =\omega +\varepsilon \widehat\omega $. It is complete: an $\omega _\varepsilon $-Cauchy sequence is $\widehat\omega $-Cauchy by the length lower bound $\sqrt\varepsilon $, and its limit also gives convergence for the smooth local $\omega _\varepsilon $ metric. The preceding lemma shows that $g$ is globally $L^2$ for $\omega _\varepsilon $ and its inverse energy is at most $M$. The curvature rank hypothesis is intrinsic and unchanged. Theorem 6.26.5 therefore gives

\[ D''u_\varepsilon =g,\qquad \int _ X|u_\varepsilon |_{\omega _\varepsilon }^2 \, \mathrm dV_{\omega _\varepsilon }\leq M. \]

Choose $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$. On each compact set, $\omega _{\varepsilon _ j}\leq C\omega $ for $\varepsilon _ j\leq 1$. The coefficient formula of the lemma, in degree $(n,q-1)$, then bounds the original norm density by $C^{q-1}$ times the varying one. Thus the $u_{\varepsilon _ j}$ are bounded locally in $L^2$. Diagonal weak compactness gives a local weak limit $u$ with $D''u=g$. For fixed $k$ and $j\geq k$, the norm-density comparison gives

\[ \int _ K|u_{\varepsilon _ j}|_{\omega _{\varepsilon _ k}}^2 \, \mathrm dV_{\omega _{\varepsilon _ k}}\leq M \]

on any compact $K$. Its density is now fixed and equivalent to a coefficient $L^2$ norm, so weak lower semicontinuity gives the same bound for $u$. As $k\to \infty $, these fixed norm densities increase to the original $\omega $ density: monotonicity follows from (6.27.2) and the limit from its coefficient formula. Monotone convergence, followed by compact exhaustion, proves (6.27.7). In primitive degree $(n,0)$ the density is already metric-independent. $\square$

Lemma 6.27.8.94482D8B91F7 For a smooth Hermitian bundle and smooth Hermitian base metric, let $g$ be a smooth datum of type $(p,q)$, $1\leq q\leq n$, with a globally $L^2$ distributional primitive. Its minimum-norm primitive is smooth and coclosed, even if $g$ is not globally $L^2$.

Proof. If the primitive has type $(p,q-1)$, $q\geq 1$, the solution set is $u_0+\ker T$, where $T$ is the maximal distributional $D''$ operator between the corresponding $L^2$ spaces. The kernel is closed and contains the range of the preceding maximal $D''$ operator. Hilbert projection gives a unique minimum orthogonal to that kernel, hence its formal codifferential is zero distributionally. This argument does not require $u_0$ to belong to $\operatorname {Dom}T$ when $g$ is not globally $L^2$; only the differences lie in the kernel. This is the affine argument of lemma 6.25.12. The local matrix graph approximation, H1 estimate and elliptic bootstrap proved in theorem 6.26.5 use only the local equations $D''u=g$, $\delta ''u=0$. They therefore apply here in each relatively compact chart. When $q=1$ the codifferential is zero for degree reasons. No completeness or boundary regularity is needed for this local conclusion. $\square$

Theorem 6.27.9.B68EDCF522DA Suppose $(X,\omega )$ is Kähler with a smooth PSH exhaustion and $(E,h)$ is $m$-semipositive. Fix $1\leq q\leq n$ with $m\geq \min (n-q+1,\operatorname {rank}E)$. Every locally $L^2$ closed $(n,q)$-form with finite original inverse energy $M$ has a primitive satisfying (6.27.7). If $g$ is smooth the primitive can be chosen smooth.

Proof. Shift the exhaustion $\rho $ to be nonnegative, and take levels $c_ j\uparrow \infty $. The relatively compact open sets $X_ j=\{ \rho <c_ j\} $ have smooth PSH exhaustions $\rho _ j=\rho +(c_ j-\rho )^{-1}$: for a positive sublevel bound $A$, its closed sublevel lies in $\{ \rho \leq c_ j-1/A\} $, a compact subset of $X_ j$. Empty sublevels are omitted. By lemma 6.26.8, each $X_ j$ admits a complete Kähler metric. Since $\overline X_ j$ is compact in $X$, the datum is globally $L^2$ there for the original smooth metrics. Apply theorem 6.27.6 on $X_ j$. It gives $u_ j$ with the original squared norm at most $M$. Weak diagonal limits on the increasing sublevels preserve the equation and the original norm bound, by restriction continuity, weak lower semicontinuity and monotone convergence. Finally use lemma 6.27.8 for smooth data. $\square$

Corollary 6.27.10.7B8770608F52 Let $(X,\omega )$ be weakly PSH-exhausted Kähler and let $(L,h)$ be a holomorphic line bundle. Let $\phi $ be smooth and real, and suppose $\mathrm i\Theta (L,h)+\mathcal H(\phi )$ has nonnegative $\omega $-eigenvalues $\lambda _1\leq \cdots \leq \lambda _ n$. For every locally $L^2$ closed $L$-valued $(n,q)$-form, $1\leq q\leq n$, such that the right side below is finite, there is a primitive with

8CA99039303D 6.27.11
\begin{equation} D''u=g,\qquad \int _ X|u|_{\omega ,h}^2\mathrm e^{-\phi }\, \mathrm dV_\omega \leq \int _ X\frac{|g|_{\omega ,h}^2}{\lambda _1+\cdots +\lambda _ q} \mathrm e^{-\phi }\, \mathrm dV_\omega . \end{equation}

Use the zero-denominator convention. Smooth data admit a smooth choice.

Proof. Apply the preceding theorem to $h\mathrm e^{-\phi }$. Its curvature is the assumed semipositive form. The exact all-q eigenvalue action in proposition 10.33.6 bounds its inverse energy by the displayed integral. The weight need not itself be PSH. $\square$

Lemma 6.27.12.D1744F9C1C65 For a positive continuous function $\lambda $ on $[0,\infty )$ and an integer $N\geq 1$, there is a smooth convex function $\chi $ such that

4E8BA81880DF 6.27.13
\begin{equation} \chi ,\chi ',\chi ''\geq \lambda ,\qquad (1+\chi '+\chi '')^ N\mathrm e^{-\chi }\leq \lambda ^{-1} \quad \hbox{on }[0,\infty ). \end{equation}

Proof. Replace $\lambda $ by a nondecreasing continuous majorant at least one; for example use the smooth step construction on its compact-interval suprema from (6.26.9). For each $p\geq 1$ choose strictly increasing integers $k_ p\geq p+1$ so large that

\[ b_ p=\lambda (p+1)^{1/k_ p}/p\in [1/p,1/(p-1))\quad (p\geq 2). \]

For $p=1$ only $b_1\geq 1$ is needed. Set $a_0=\cdots =a_{k_1-1}=\mathrm e\lambda (2)$ and

\[ a_ k=b_ p\mathrm e^{1/\sqrt{k}}\quad (k_ p\leq k<k_{p+1}), \qquad d_ k=a_0a_1\cdots a_ k, \qquad f(t)=\sum _{k\geq 0}d_ kt^ k. \]

The sequence $a_ k$ is nonincreasing, tends to zero, and satisfies $a_ k\geq 1/k$ for $k\geq 1$. At the first join use $b_1\mathrm e^{1/\sqrt{k_1}}\leq \mathrm e\lambda (2)$; at the later joins use $b_ p<1/(p-1)\leq b_{p-1}$. The geometric means $d_ k^{1/k}$ tend to zero, so the series is entire. Its positive coefficients and $(k+1)a_{k+1}\geq 1$ give $f''\geq f'\geq f$ on $t\geq 0$. For $0\leq t\leq 1$, $f(t)\geq a_0\geq \lambda (t)$. For $t\in [p,p+1]$, the term $k=k_ p$ gives

\[ f(t)\geq d_ kp^ k\geq (a_ kp)^ k \geq (b_ pp)^{k_ p}=\lambda (p+1)\geq \lambda (t). \]

We check the derivative upper bound, including both parities of the series. For all $m$ there are constants $C,c>0$ independent of $m$ such that

0EC514D3F94F 6.27.14
\begin{equation} \frac{d_{2m}}{d_ m^2}\leq C\mathrm e^{-c\sqrt m}. \end{equation}

Indeed beyond finitely many indices the factors $a_ k$ are a nonincreasing block sequence $b_ p$ times $\mathrm e^{1/\sqrt k}$. Pair the block factors with indices $m+j$ and $j$; each ratio is at most one once $j\geq k_1$, and the finitely many other ratios have a fixed bound. The remaining exponent is

\[ \sum _{j=m+1}^{2m}j^{-1/2}-\sum _{j=1}^ m j^{-1/2} \leq -(4-2\sqrt2)\sqrt m+2, \]

by comparison with the integrals of $t^{-1/2}$. This proves (6.27.14), absorbing small $m$ into $C$. Since $a_ k\leq a_0$ and is nonincreasing,

\[ \frac{d_{2m+2}}{d_ m^2}\leq a_0^2 \frac{d_{2m}}{d_ m^2}, \qquad \frac{d_{2m+3}}{d_ md_{m+1}}\leq \frac{d_{2m+2}}{d_ m^2}. \]

Multiplication by any quadratic polynomial in $m$ leaves these ratios bounded. The coefficient of $t^{2m}$ in $f^2$ is at least $d_ m^2$, and its coefficient of $t^{2m+1}$ is at least $2d_ md_{m+1}$. Comparing with the coefficients $(k+1)(k+2)d_{k+2}$ of $f''$ therefore gives $f''\leq C_1f^2$. As $f\geq \lambda \geq 1$ and $f'\leq f''$,

\[ \lambda (1+f'+f'')^ N\mathrm e^{-f} \leq (1+2C_1)^ N f^{2N+1}\mathrm e^{-f}\leq C_2<\infty . \]

Take $\chi =f+A$ with $A\geq \max \{ 0,\log C_2\} $. Its first two derivatives still dominate $\lambda $, as does $\chi $, and the last inequality proves (6.27.13). $\square$

Corollary 6.27.15.E30B7AED0EB3 If $X$ has a smooth PSH exhaustion and a positive holomorphic line bundle $L$, then the smooth analytic groups $H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X,L)$ vanish when $p+q>n$.

Proof. Choose its positive metric $h$ and put $\omega _0=\mathrm i\Theta (L,h)$, a Kähler form. Let $\rho \geq 0$ be a smooth PSH exhaustion. For a smooth closed datum $g$, choose a continuous $C\geq 1$ such that $\mathcal H(\rho )\leq C\omega _0$ and $\mathrm i\partial \rho \wedge \bar\partial \rho \leq C\omega _0$. For any increasing convex $\chi $, the forms

\[ h_\chi =h\mathrm e^{-\chi (\rho )},\qquad \omega _\chi =\mathrm i\Theta (L,h_\chi ) =\omega _0+\mathcal H(\chi (\rho )) \]

satisfy $\omega _0\leq \omega _\chi \leq C(1+\chi '+\chi '')\omega _0$, where the derivatives are evaluated at $\rho $. Thus pointwise covector norms decrease, while volume increases by at most $C^ n(1+\chi '+\chi '')^ n$. Put $F=C^ n|g|_{\omega _0,h}^2$. Its integral over each compact exhaustion band is finite. Choose continuous $\lambda \geq 1$ so large on every band $[k,k+1]$ that $\int _ X F/\lambda (\rho )\, \mathrm dV_{\omega _0}<\infty $; the same compact-band step construction gives such a majorant. Apply lemma 6.27.12 with $N=n$. It gives

\[ \int _ X|g|_{\omega _\chi ,h_\chi }^2\, \mathrm dV_{\omega _\chi } \leq \int _ X F(1+\chi '+\chi '')^ n\mathrm e^{-\chi (\rho )} \, \mathrm dV_{\omega _0}<\infty . \]

Moreover $\chi ''\geq 1$ implies $\omega _\chi \geq \mathrm i\partial \rho \wedge \bar\partial \rho $, so $|\mathrm d\rho |_{\omega _\chi }^2\leq 2$. The exhaustion and corollary 6.26.3 make this metric complete. Since its unscaled curvature is exactly $\omega _\chi $, the all-bidegree formula in proposition 10.33.6 gives $B=(p+q-n)\operatorname {Id}$. Here $q\geq 1$ and this integer is positive. The complete Kähler theorem gives a primitive with finite norm for these modified metrics; its minimum-norm choice is smooth. This proves the analytic Dolbeault quotient assertion. $\square$
