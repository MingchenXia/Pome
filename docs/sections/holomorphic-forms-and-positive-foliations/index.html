9.75 Holomorphic forms and positive foliationsB11968DD41AC

B11968DD41AC

Ambient manifolds are connected unless stated otherwise. Fibers need not be connected; the section-envelope arguments treat their connected components separately.

Lemma 9.75.1.597700D273A6 Every compact Kähler manifold $Z$ admits a Kähler form $a$ and a holomorphic two-form $\tau $ with $[a+\tau +\bar\tau ]\in H^2(Z,\mathbb Q)$.

Proof. The rational lattice comparison makes rational classes dense in real degree-two cohomology. Choose one sufficiently close to a fixed Kähler class. Its Hodge $(1,1)$ component is still Kähler, since the Kähler cone is open. Represent that component by $a$ and the $(2,0)$ component by its holomorphic representative $\tau $. The $(0,2)$ component is the complex conjugate. The Kähler Hodge decomposition proves the claim. $\square$

Lemma 9.75.2.B52F345AB247 Let $\mathcal F\subset T_ X$ be saturated and have $\mu _{\alpha ,\min }(\mathcal F)>0$ on compact Kähler $X$. Every holomorphic two-form on $X$ restricts to zero on $\Lambda ^2\mathcal F$. The assertion remains true on a smooth modification, for the induced foliation and pulled-back movable class.

Proof. The reflexive exterior square is a quotient of the reflexive tensor square. Its minimum slope is therefore at least $2\mu _{\alpha ,\min }(\mathcal F)>0$ by proposition 10.107.1. A two-form gives a morphism from this exterior square to $\mathcal O_ X$; Hom vanishing makes it zero. In rank one there is no exterior square. For a modification $\pi $, define the pulled-back movable functional by pairing with the pushforward of divisor classes. It is nonnegative on positive classes and annihilates exceptional divisors. Determinants of induced subsheaves and quotients change only by exceptional divisors. Pushing their generic maps down proves preservation of the extremal slopes. Also holomorphic two-forms on the modification descend off the exceptional image and extend coefficientwise across its codimension-two complement by Hartogs. This proves the last assertion without any new choice of a particular two-form. $\square$

Theorem 9.75.3.CDF6A16B105C Let $X$ be compact Kähler and let $\mathcal F\subset T_ X$ be a nonzero saturated involutive sheaf. If $\mu _{\alpha ,\min }(\mathcal F)>0$ for a movable class, it is induced by a meromorphic fibration whose smooth general fibers are rationally connected.

Proof. By the tautological slope criterion, $\mathcal F^*$ is not pseudo-effective. The conormal criterion and the cycle-space leaf quotient give a meromorphic fibration inducing $\mathcal F$. Its quotient has a compact Kähler model by the class-C image theorem, as in [Lemma 8.2, Ou2025].

We first specify the models needed below. For a holomorphic fibration $f:U\to V$ of compact Kähler manifolds, the geometric flattening theorem [Theorem 1.7, Campana2004] gives a meromorphic fiber-cycle map $V\dashrightarrow \mathcal C(U)$. Resolve this map by a modification $V'\to V$. The pulled-back incidence family $G\subset V'\times U$ is irreducible and has fibers of pure dimension $\dim U-\dim V$, by [Definition 1.5, Theorem 1.6 and Proposition 1.9, Campana2004], and its evaluation $G\to U$ is bimeromorphic. Resolve $G$ inside the compact Kähler product to obtain $U'$. A divisor of $U'$ whose image in $V'$ has codimension at least two must have image of codimension at least two in $G$: otherwise that image, of dimension $\dim U-1$, would force a fiber dimension greater than $\dim U-\dim V$. It is therefore exceptional over $U$ as well. This constructs the required neat model. Further source modifications preserve this property while the base is fixed.

Apply this construction after resolving the leaf quotient, then take its relative maximal rationally connected quotient and resolve its maps. The relative quotient is the cycle-space input [Theorem 2.7 and Proposition 2.8, Campana2004]; its specialization identifies the rational quotient of a very general fiber. Its quotient fibers are rationally connected. Their residual bases are not uniruled by the projective section theorem [Theorem 1.1, GHS2001] and its Kähler reduction [Lemma 8.10, Ou2025]. In that reduction, a rationally connected fibration over a curve has no holomorphic two-forms: both degree-one and degree-two forms on its general fiber vanish, and the cotangent filtration gives the assertion on the total space. Its smooth Kähler model is then projective by the vanishing-to-projectivity criterion, so the section theorem applies. These quotient and class-C existence results remain explicit external inputs.

Apply the neat-model construction once more to the resolved relative quotient, modifying its base $Z$ and keeping $Y$ fixed. We obtain compact Kähler manifolds and holomorphic fibrations

\[ \pi :\widehat X\longrightarrow X,\qquad p=s\circ r:\widehat X\xrightarrow {r}Z\xrightarrow {s}Y, \]

where $\pi $ is a modification and every $p$-exceptional or $r$-exceptional divisor is $\pi $-exceptional. Here exceptional for a fibration means that its image has codimension at least two. Let $\widehat{\mathcal F}$ be the induced foliation and $\beta =\pi ^*\alpha $. Its minimum slope is still positive by lemma 9.75.2.

Put $\mathcal H=\ker (ds)$, saturated, and suppose its rank $h$ is positive. The differential defines a holomorphic map $\widehat{\mathcal F}\to r^*T_ Z$. Let $\mathcal J$ be its image and $\overline{\mathcal J}$ its saturation in $r^*T_ Z$. The image has rank $h$ and is a torsion-free quotient of $\widehat{\mathcal F}$. Off a codimension-two subset of $Z$, $\mathcal H\subset T_ Z$ is a subbundle, and on its inverse image $\overline{\mathcal J}=r^*\mathcal H$. Consequently

\[ \det \overline{\mathcal J} =r^*\det \mathcal H\otimes \mathcal O_{\widehat X}(E), \]

where $E$ is supported on $r$-exceptional divisors. Neatness and the definition of $\beta $ give $\beta \cdot E=0$. Saturation adds an effective divisor, so

\[ \deg _\beta (r^*\det \mathcal H) =\deg _\beta \overline{\mathcal J} \geq \deg _\beta \mathcal J \geq h\mu _{\beta ,\min }(\widehat{\mathcal F})>0. \]

Choose a rational Hodge class on $Z$ by lemma 9.75.1, say $[a+\tau +\bar\tau ]$. The holomorphic two-form $r^*\tau $ vanishes on $\Lambda ^2\widehat{\mathcal F}$ by lemma 9.75.2. At generic points the differential maps onto $r^*\mathcal H$, so $\tau |_{\Lambda ^2\mathcal H}=0$. On every smooth $s$-fiber the rational class therefore restricts to the Kähler class of $a$. Every such fiber is projective by the integral projectivity criterion.

Let $Y^\circ $ be the nonempty open set where $s$ is smooth; its complement is a proper analytic subset, and $Y^\circ $ is connected. We use independently Fujiki's deformation theorem [Proposition 2.3, Fujiki1981]: in a proper smooth family with connected fibers over a connected base, locally dominated by a proper Kähler family, one uniruled fiber makes every fiber uniruled. Here $s^{-1}(Y^\circ )$ is Kähler and the identity supplies that domination. The very general residual fiber is not uniruled by the relative MRC input. Hence every fiber over $Y^\circ $ is not uniruled. Projective BDPP, [Theorem 2.6, BDPP2012], makes all their canonical bundles pseudo-effective. This is the canonical-bundle uniruledness implication of BDPP, with precisely the open-set fiber hypothesis needed to apply theorem 10.105.2 on $Z$ with $L=0$. It follows that $K_{Z/Y}-D(s)$ is pseudo-effective.

The determinant $\det \mathcal H^*$ differs from this class only by $s$-exceptional divisors. At a general divisor mapping onto a divisor of $Y$, local coordinates $t_1=z_1^ m$ give the correction $m-1$ in $D(s)$ after saturating the differential; elsewhere in codimension one the smooth tangent sequence applies, except for the $s$-exceptional divisors. Reflexive determinants are determined by these codimension-one data. The pullback of each $s$-exceptional divisor is supported on $p$-exceptional divisors, hence on $\pi $-exceptional divisors. Pulling back the pseudo-effective class and pairing with $\beta $ therefore gives $\deg _\beta (r^*\det \mathcal H^*)\geq 0$, contradicting the strict positive degree of $r^*\det \mathcal H$. Thus $\mathcal H=0$, and the general fibers of $p$ are rationally connected.

The relative-adjoint application uses the smooth Kähler form $a$ on $Z$ itself; no smoothness of a fiber-integrated form is required. $\square$

Corollary 9.75.4.745947D9E1A8 If an involutive saturated sheaf $\mathcal E\subset T_ X$ has $\mu _\alpha (\mathcal E)>0$, its first Harder–Narasimhan piece is an algebraic foliation with rationally connected general leaves. In particular $X$ is uniruled and $K_ X$ is not pseudo-effective.

Proof. The first piece is involutive by corollary 10.107.2 and its minimum slope equals its positive slope. Apply the theorem. A covering family of rational curves has positive anticanonical degree and nonnegative degree against every pseudo-effective class, as proved below. Thus $K_ X$ cannot be pseudo-effective. $\square$
