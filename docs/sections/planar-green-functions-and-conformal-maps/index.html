3.8 Planar Green functions and conformal maps7528EC445E3A

7528EC445E3A

Use the positive planar Green kernel with singularity $-\log |z-a|$. It is $2\pi $ times the kernel normalized by $-\Delta G=\delta _ a$.

Theorem 3.8.1.E2B11E803D94 Every proper simply connected planar domain is biholomorphic to the unit disc. On a bounded such domain $D$, its positive Green function constructs a biholomorphism $F:D\to \mathbb D$ with $F(a)=0$ and

\[ G_ D(z,a)=-\log |F(z)|. \]

Proof. First let $D$ be bounded. The canonical planar-continuum regularity theorem makes every boundary point regular. The canonical Dirichlet/ Green theorem therefore supplies $G=G_ D(\cdot ,a)>0$, zero at every boundary point, with

\[ H(z)=G(z)+\log |z-a| \]

extending harmonically across $a$. The closed one-form $-H_ y\, dx+H_ x\, dy$ has a primitive $K$ on $D$. Indeed it is closed because $\Delta H=0$; its integrals on closed paths vanish by contracting the path and subdividing a contracting homotopy into coordinate rectangles on which Stokes' formula applies. Path integration then defines $K$, with $K_ x=-H_ y$ and $K_ y=H_ x$. Thus $H+\mathrm iK$ is holomorphic, and

\[ F(z)=(z-a)\exp (-H(z)-\mathrm iK(z)) \]

is holomorphic with just one zero, a simple zero at $a$. Its modulus is $e^{-G}<1$ off $a$, and tends to one at every boundary point. Consequently $F$ is proper as a map to $\mathbb D$: the preimage of a compact subset stays away from every boundary point, and is a closed subset of the bounded domain. Compactness of $\partial D$ gives the required uniform separation.

For completeness, a proper nonconstant holomorphic map between planar domains has a constant number of preimages counted with multiplicity. Its fibers are finite, since they are compact sets of isolated zeros. Around the finitely many preimages of $w_0$ choose disjoint small closed discs on whose boundaries $F-w_0$ is nonzero. For $w$ close to $w_0$, all its preimages lie in those discs: otherwise properness would give an extra preimage of $w_0$ by taking a convergent subsequence. On each boundary the integral

\[ \frac1{2\pi \mathrm i}\int \frac{F'(z)}{F(z)-w}\, dz \]

is a continuous integer, equal to the number of zeros by local factorization and Cauchy's formula. It is therefore constant for nearby $w$. The image of $F$ is open and closed in the disc, using properness for closedness, and hence is the whole disc. The locally constant total degree is one, since the fiber over zero consists only of the simple zero $a$. Thus every fiber is one simple point. The holomorphic inverse function theorem gives the holomorphic inverse.

Now let $D\ne \mathbb C$ be simply connected and possibly unbounded. Choose $b\notin D$. Path integration of $1/(z-b)$, with its additive constant normalized at one point, gives a holomorphic logarithm of $z-b$. Its half exponential is a branch $s$ of $\sqrt{z-b}$. This map is injective and has nonzero derivative. Its image $U$ is disjoint from $-U$: opposite values would have equal squares and hence come from the same point, whereas $s$ never vanishes. Choose $B(w_0,r)\subset U$. Then $B(-w_0,r)\cap U=\varnothing $, so

\[ z\longmapsto \frac1{s(z)+w_0} \]

is a bounded injective holomorphic map with nonzero derivative. It maps $D$ biholomorphically onto a bounded simply connected domain. Apply the bounded case and compose. No conformal representation or Riemann mapping existence theorem was used as a premise. $\square$

Proposition 3.8.2.3F05222ECC9E Let $K\subset \mathbb C$ be nonpolar and compact, and let $\Omega $ be the unbounded component of $\mathbb C\setminus K$. Then $u=V_ K^*$ is positive and harmonic on $\Omega $, is bounded near its finite boundary, and satisfies

\[ \lim _{|z|\to \infty }(u(z)-\log |z|)\in \mathbb R,\qquad \lim _{\Omega \ni z\to \xi }u(z)=0 \quad (\xi \in \partial \Omega \setminus P) \]

for a polar set $P$. It is the least positive harmonic function on $\Omega $ with these growth and quasi-everywhere zero boundary conditions. It is the unique such function bounded near the finite boundary. On every bounded complementary component $V_ K^*=0$.

Proof. The logarithmic family and equilibrium-support theorems give $u\in \mathcal L^+$, $u\geq 0$, and maximality off $K$. In one complex variable maximality is harmonicity. Its logarithmic growth makes it nonconstant on $\Omega $, so the minimum principle gives strict positivity there. It is bounded on a neighborhood of the compact $K$ by local boundedness. Outside a large disc, $u-\log |z|$ is bounded and harmonic; inversion gives a bounded harmonic function on a punctured disc. Canonical puncture removability gives the asserted finite limit.

The raw envelope is zero on $K$. Its discrepancy from $V_ K^*$ is polar, so $u(\xi )=0$ off a polar subset $P$ of $K$. Upper semicontinuity and nonnegativity give the stated full boundary limit there. If $B$ is a bounded complementary component, every polynomial is bounded on $B$ by its supremum on $\partial B\subset K$. The polynomial extremal formula therefore gives $V_ K=0$ on $B$, and consequently $V_ K^*=0$ on that open component.

Let $v$ be any other positive harmonic function on $\Omega $ with the stated logarithmic asymptotic and quasi-everywhere zero boundary limits. The harmonic function $v-u$ is bounded below near $K$, since $v>0$ and $u$ is bounded there, and is bounded near infinity, since the logarithmic terms cancel. Its boundary limits are zero off the union of the two polar exceptional sets. The complement of $\Omega $ contains the nonpolar set $K$, so the canonical Myrberg theorem makes $\Omega $ Greenian. The canonical planar boundary minimum principle with polar exceptions now gives $v-u\geq 0$. Its planar unbounded-domain version needs no extra boundary condition at infinity. If $v$ is bounded near $K$, the same lower-bound argument applies to $u-v$; interchange the functions to get equality. This proves both minimality and bounded-boundary uniqueness. $\square$

Example 3.8.3.FADCC209A003 Quasi-everywhere zero boundary limits alone do not specify a Green function uniquely. In the unit disc, for every $t>0$ the functions

\[ -\log |z|+t\operatorname {Re}\frac{1+z}{1-z} \]

are positive harmonic off zero, have the required logarithmic pole, and tend to zero at every boundary point except $1$. In the exterior of the closed unit disc, the corresponding functions

\[ \log |z|+t\operatorname {Re}\frac{z+1}{z-1} \]

are positive harmonic, have the required finite logarithmic limit at infinity, and again have zero boundary limits except at $1$. The exceptional singleton is polar. The added Poisson term is positive in each indicated domain, diverges at $1$, and is harmonic across the finite pole in the first formula. Thus the weaker boundary condition must be supplemented by minimality, or by the bounded boundary condition above, when it is used as a characterization.

For a finite pole on a bounded domain, the canonical Green function is likewise the least function meeting the weaker conditions. If $v$ is another such function, $v-G$ has a removable pole and is bounded below near the boundary, since $v>0$ and $G$ is bounded there. The polar boundary minimum principle gives $v\geq G$. If $v$ is also bounded near the boundary, apply that principle to $G-v$ to get equality. Here boundedness of the canonical $G$ near the boundary follows from $G(z,a)\leq \log (C/|z-a|)$ with $C>\operatorname {diam}D$.
