6.29 Singular-weight division on complete Kähler domainsADBD6CEEE8D1

ADBD6CEEE8D1

Use the conventions and smooth bundle division interfaces of section 6.28.

Lemma 6.29.1.E7FE74257DC3 Let $\phi $ be PSH on an open subset $U\subset \mathbb C^ n$, not identically $-\infty $ on any component, and let $K_ j\subset \operatorname {Int}K_{j+1}$ be a compact exhaustion. There are global smooth functions $\psi _ j\geq \phi +2^{-j}$ such that $\psi _ j$ is PSH near $K_ j$, $\psi _ j\leq \psi _{j-1}$ on $K_{j-1}$, and $\psi _ j\to \phi $ on every fixed compact set, decreasing there for all large $j$. No curvature sign is asserted outside a neighborhood of $K_ j$.

Proof. First choose a smooth global majorant $b\geq \phi +1$: a locally finite relatively compact chart cover has finite upper bounds $M_ i$ for $\phi $ on the respective supports, and a subordinate smooth partition gives $b=\sum _ i\theta _ i(M_ i+1)$. Near each $K_ j$, the strictly PSH function $|z|^2$ allows lemma 7.31.1 to give smooth PSH $a_{j,l}\downarrow \phi $ on one fixed neighborhood. Choose a term and put $v_ j=a_{j,l}+2^{-j}$. If $\psi _{j-1}$ has been constructed, the pointwise limit of these candidates is at most $\psi _{j-1}-2^{-j}$ on $K_{j-1}$. The increasing open sets where a candidate is below $\psi _{j-1}$ cover that compact set, so one term works everywhere on it. Make the term fine enough also that $\int _{K_ j}(a_{j,l}-\phi )\, \mathrm dV\leq 2^{-j}$.

Take a smooth cutoff equal to one near $K_ j$ and compactly supported in this fixed neighborhood; glue $v_ j$ to $b+2^{-j}$ by the ordinary cutoff combination. Both branches dominate $\phi +2^{-j}$, so the resulting global smooth $\psi _ j$ does too. It equals $v_ j$ near $K_ j$ and has the asserted order on $K_{j-1}$. On any fixed relatively compact open set it is eventually decreasing and PSH, and tends to $\phi $ in $L^1$ by the chosen error and $2^{-j}$ shift. The decreasing PSH limit is therefore the canonical representative $\phi $ at every point, as in the local regularization lemma. This proves the claim without globally preserving curvature. $\square$

Corollary 6.29.2.E63E9389A415 Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$, possess a complete Kähler metric, let $g=(g_1,\ldots ,g_ r)$, $r\geq 1$, be holomorphic and not identically zero on any component, and set $m=\min (n,r-1)$, $Z=\{ g=0\} $. Let $\epsilon >0$ and let $\phi $ be PSH, not identically $-\infty $ on any component, with its usual upper-semicontinuous representative. For every holomorphic $f$ with

\[ I=\int _{\Omega \setminus Z}|f|^2|g|^{-2(m+1+\epsilon )} \mathrm e^{-\phi }\, \mathrm dV<\infty , \]

there are holomorphic $h_1,\ldots ,h_ r$ on $\Omega $ such that $f=\sum _ jg_ jh_ j$ and the exact estimate (6.28.15) holds with this $\phi $ and $I$.

Proof. Keep $U=\Omega \setminus Z$ and its fixed complete Kähler metric $\gamma $ constructed in the smooth-weight proof. If $r=1$, take $h=f/g$ on $U$. Its requested norm equals the input norm, and the weight's local lower bounds give the same $L^2$ holomorphic extension across $Z$. Thus assume $m=\min (n,r-1)\geq 1$. Identify functions with their multiples of $\mathrm dz_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm dz_ n$, as in corollary 6.28.14.

Use the quotient sequence $0\to S\to \mathcal O^ r\to Q'\to 0$ on $U$, with $|v|_{Q'}^2=|v|^2/|g|^2$. Let $K_ j$ exhaust $U$ and choose the preceding majorants $\psi _ j$; write $\iota $ for the inclusion of $S$. Give $L_ j=\mathcal O$ the smooth metric $h_{L_ j}=|g|^{-2(m+\epsilon )}\mathrm e^{-\psi _ j}$. Let $B_ j$ denote the curvature operator of $S\otimes L_ j$ in degree $(n,1)$ for $\gamma $. Flatness of $\mathcal O^ r$ gives $R_ S=-C_\beta $ and $R_{\det Q'}=\operatorname {tr}C_\beta =\mathcal H(\log |g|^2)$. Therefore

\[ R_{S\otimes L_ j}=(m+\epsilon )\operatorname {tr}C_\beta \otimes I_ S -C_\beta +\mathcal H(\psi _ j)\otimes I_ S. \]

The rank-m trace inequality used in (6.28.6) applies to every tensor here, since $m=\min (n,\operatorname {rank}S)$. Its alternating coefficient formula gives

\[ B_ j\geq (\epsilon /m)T_\beta + [\mathcal H(\psi _ j),\Lambda _\gamma ], \qquad T_\beta =(\beta ^*\wedge )(\beta ^*\wedge )^*. \]

Here the identity on $S$ in the second term is understood. Since $\psi _ j$ is PSH near $K_ j$, choose a nonnegative smooth function $a_ j$, zero on a neighborhood of $K_ j$, so that

95B04F2194E0 6.29.3
\begin{equation} B_ j+a_ j^2\operatorname {Id}\geq (\epsilon /m)T_\beta \quad \hbox{on }U. \end{equation}

To make this choice explicit, the operator norm of the negative part of $[\mathcal H(\psi _ j),\Lambda _\gamma ]$ is continuous and zero on that neighborhood. Choose a smooth majorant of its square root by a locally finite partition, and multiply by a smooth cutoff zero near $K_ j$ and one outside a slightly larger neighborhood where the negative part is still zero. This proves the stated inequality.

Set $F=\beta ^*\wedge f$, a smooth closed $(n,1)$-form on $U$. All pairings in the next paragraph use the smooth $j$-th metrics and $\gamma $. For compactly supported smooth $v$, contraction duality, (6.29.3) and theorem 10.33.2 give

\begin{align*} |\langle F,v\rangle _ j|^2 & \leq M\int _ U\langle (B_ j+a_ j^2\operatorname {Id})v,v\rangle _ j\, \mathrm dV_\gamma \\ & \leq M\bigl (\| \delta ”_ jv\| _ j^2+\| D”v\| _ j^2+ \| a_ jv\| _ j^2\bigr ), \qquad M=(m/\epsilon )I. \end{align*}

The input norm is at most $I$, because $\psi _ j\geq \phi $ and the top-degree metric density cancels as before. Apply Hilbert representation in the closure of the three-component range $v\mapsto (\delta ''_ jv,D''v,a_ jv)$. It produces $(u_ j,w_ j,z_ j)$ with total squared norm at most $M$, and the distributional equation

FC57A5C2AE65 6.29.4
\begin{equation} F=D''u_ j+\delta ''_ jw_ j+a_ jz_ j. \end{equation}

The second component $w_ j$ is an $L^2$ limit of $D''v$ and is therefore closed. On the neighborhood of $K_ j$ where $a_ j=0$, closedness of $F$ gives $D''\delta ''_ jw_ j=0$. Thus $w_ j$ is smooth harmonic there by lemma 6.28.1. In dimension one its degree $(n,2)$ space is simply zero.

Take the fixed complete gradient cutoffs $\chi _ R$ for $\gamma $ from lemma 6.26.1. For each fixed $R$ and all sufficiently large $j$, their support is inside the neighborhood of $K_ j$ where the error is harmonic. The compact integration-by-parts estimate used above gives

5469D891D79F 6.29.5
\begin{equation} \| \chi _ R\delta ''_ jw_ j\| _ j^2 \leq C R^{-2}\| w_ j\| _ j^2\leq CM/R^2. \end{equation}

The constant depends only on the fixed gradient cutoff bound; no derivative of $\psi _ j$ enters the product rule. On a fixed compact $K$, $\psi _ j$ is eventually bounded above by one fixed smooth $\psi _ k$. Thus its weighted norms bound a fixed ordinary coefficient norm there. Choose $R$ with $\chi _ R=1$ on $K$, take the upper limit in $j$ in the last estimate, and then let $R\to \infty $. This proves $\delta ''_ jw_ j\to 0$ in ordinary $L^2(K)$.

The same fixed lower weighted bounds give local ordinary $L^2$ boundedness of $u_ j$. A diagonal weak limit $u$ satisfies $D''u=F$ by (6.29.4): on each fixed compact the last term is eventually zero, and the middle term tends to zero as just proved. For fixed $k$ and $j\geq k$, the squared $\psi _ k$ norm on $K_ k$ is at most the squared $\psi _ j$ norm, hence at most $M$. Weak lower semicontinuity gives that bound for $u$ in this fixed norm. Then the densities $1_{K_ k}|g|^{-2(m+\epsilon )}\mathrm e^{-\psi _ k}$ increase to $|g|^{-2(m+\epsilon )}\mathrm e^{-\phi }$ on $U$. Monotone convergence proves the exact singular-weight bound for $u$.

Finally $h=G^*f+\iota u$ is closed, $Gh=f$, and the orthogonal norm identity gives (6.28.15), since the first summand has squared weighted norm $I$. Distributional holomorphy makes its coefficients holomorphic on $U$. The PSH weight is locally bounded above on $\Omega $, so the final weighted bound again gives $h\in L^2_{\rm loc}$ across $Z$. Apply lemma 6.28.12, holomorphic distributions and minimal weighted solutions to extend $h$ holomorphically to all of $\Omega $. Each Hilbert representation above uses smooth global metrics. $\square$
