9.32 Convexity and Stein spaces23E942B93E3B

23E942B93E3B

Complex spaces in this section are Hausdorff and have a countable basis; their structure sheaves may be nonreduced. Scalar smooth functions are understood through ambient extensions, as in lemma 9.49.1. Fix $q\geq 1$. Besides the strong $q$-convexity convention there, call a $C^ k$ function, $2\leq k\leq \infty $, weakly $q$-convex if locally it has a $C^ k$ ambient extension whose Levi form is nonnegative on a subspace of codimension at most $q-1$. For $q=1$ this is $C^ k$ plurisubharmonicity, with the same ambient convention as ambient plurisubharmonicity on reduced spaces. Levi forms use $\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial $; their signs agree with the normalization of $dd^ c$ used elsewhere.

9.32.1 Weak convexity and intersections49B453F3DFDA

Lemma 9.32.2.1139FC60D126 Weak $q$-convexity is independent of the local embedding and of the nilpotent structure. It is preserved by restriction to analytic subspaces. If $v_ j$ is weakly $q_ j$-convex and $c_ j>0$, then $\sum _{j=1}^ s c_ jv_ j$ is weakly $q$-convex, where

\[ q=1+\sum _{j=1}^ s(q_ j-1). \]

It is strongly $q$-convex wherever one $v_ j$ is strongly $q_ j$-convex. An increasing convex scalar composition preserves weak $q$-convexity; it preserves strong $q$-convexity where its first derivative is positive. These assertions hold in class $C^ k$.

Proof. For two embeddings, use holomorphic ambient lifts $H,H'$ of their transition and inverse, as in lemma 9.49.1. If $f$ is an extension in the first embedding, then

\[ f(H'(z'))+|z'-H(H'(z'))|^2 \]

extends the same scalar function in the second. Indeed it is the restriction to the graph $z'\mapsto (H'(z'),z')$ of $f(z)+|z'-H(z)|^2$. The holomorphic change of variables $(z,z')\mapsto (z,z'-H(z))$ identifies the latter with $f(z)+|z'|^2$. Its Levi form has a nonnegative subspace of codimension at most $q-1$. Intersecting that subspace with the graph tangent preserves this codimension bound. The same argument with positive subspaces is the strong assertion already used in lemma 9.49.1, and preserves class $C^ k$. The underlying local analytic subset is the same for a space and its reduction. Restricting an ambient extension proves the subspace assertion, including subspaces with a nonreduced structure.

At a point, put the finitely many extensions in one embedding. Intersect their nonnegative subspaces. The resulting codimension is at most $\sum _ j(q_ j-1)$, and the sum of the Levi forms is nonnegative there. It is positive there when a summand has a positive form on its corresponding subspace. For a scalar function $\chi $ the chain rule gives

\[ H(\chi (v))=\chi '(v)Hv+\chi ''(v)\partial v\otimes \overline{\partial v}. \]

This proves the composition assertions. If the required positive or nonnegative dimension is nonpositive, the condition is vacuous. $\square$

An exhaustion below is finite and continuous with compact closed sublevels. Such a function is bounded below: otherwise values tending to $-\infty $ would occur in one compact closed sublevel. It may therefore be shifted to be nonnegative. A space is strongly, respectively weakly, $q$-convex if it has a smooth exhaustion with that convexity outside a compact exceptional set. Strong $q$-completeness means that the exceptional set is empty, as in section 9.49. With the auxiliary convention $q=0$, strong $0$-convexity means compactness, with exceptional set $K=X$: a Levi form cannot have a positive subspace of codimension at most $-1$ at a point outside $K$. Only the empty space can be strongly $0$-complete. This convention is not a degree-zero coherent vanishing assertion.

Proposition 9.32.3.8B6B09BDCA63 Every weakly $q$-convex space has an everywhere weakly $q$-convex smooth exhaustion. Strong and weak $q$-convexity, and strong $q$-completeness, depend only on the reduction and are inherited by closed analytic subspaces. If $\psi $ is an exhaustion with the specified convexity outside $K$, each sublevel $X_ c=\{ \psi <c\} $ containing $K$ has the same convexity property. For a strongly $q$-complete exhaustion every sublevel is strongly $q$-complete.

Proof. Choose $c>\max _ K\psi $ when $K$ is nonempty. Take a smooth convex function $\chi $ which is zero on $(-\infty ,c]$, has positive first derivative on $(c,\infty )$, and tends to infinity. For example, integrate a smooth nondecreasing function which is zero on $(-\infty ,c]$, positive above $c$, and one above $c+1$. Then $\chi (\psi )$ is an exhaustion. It is constant near $K$ and weakly $q$-convex elsewhere by the preceding lemma. For $K$ empty no change is necessary.

Reduction invariance and restriction follow from that lemma. Restriction of an exhaustion to a closed subspace is an exhaustion, and its exceptional set is the compact intersection with $K$. On $X_ c$ use $1/(c-\psi )$. This has the required Levi sign off $K$. Its closed $M$-sublevel is empty for $M\leq 0$; for $M>0$ it is a closed subset of the compact set $\{ \psi \leq c-1/M\} $ contained in $X_ c$. Thus it is an exhaustion with the asserted exceptional set. $\square$

Theorem 9.32.4.B8D34CACDCCC Let $U_ j$ be weakly $q_ j$-convex open subsets of a complex space $X$, $1\leq j\leq s$, with $s\geq 1$. Put $U=\bigcap _ jU_ j$ and $q=1+\sum _ j(q_ j-1)$. Then $U$ is weakly $q$-convex. If one $U_ j$ is strongly $q_ j$-complete, then $U$ is strongly $q$-complete. If instead that $U_ j$ has a strongly $q_ j$-convex exhaustion outside $K_ j$, the same conclusion with “convex” holds provided $K_ j\cap U$ is relatively compact in $U$.

Proof. The empty intersection causes no difficulty. Choose nonnegative smooth weakly $q_ j$-convex exhaustions $\psi _ j$ everywhere on the factors, by proposition 9.32.3. Their sum on $U$ is weakly $q$-convex by lemma 9.32.2. It is an exhaustion: its closed $M$-sublevel is contained in

\[ \bigcap _ j\{ x\in U_ j:\psi _ j(x)\leq M\} . \]

Each set in this finite intersection is compact in the Hausdorff space $X$, and their intersection is contained in $U$. The sublevel is closed there and therefore compact.

For a complete factor use its nonnegative strongly $q_ j$-convex exhaustion instead; the sum is strongly $q$-convex everywhere. For the last assertion use the given strongly $q_ j$-convex exhaustion, shifted to be nonnegative, without flattening it. Use everywhere weak exhaustions on the other factors. The sum is strongly $q$-convex outside $K_ j\cap U$ and is still an exhaustion. This exceptional set is closed in $U$, so the relative compactness hypothesis makes it compact in $U$. $\square$

The exceptional-set qualification cannot be omitted. In $X=\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$, the open set $U_1=X$ is strongly $1$-convex with exceptional set $X$, while $U_2=\mathbb C\times \mathbb P^1$ is weakly $1$-convex with exhaustion $|z|^2$. The intersection $U_2$ is not strongly $1$-convex. For any compact exceptional set, choose $z$ outside its projection to $\mathbb C$. Restriction to the entire compact fiber $\{ z\} \times \mathbb P^1$ would then be strictly plurisubharmonic, contrary to the maximum principle.

Recall the $q$-Runge definition in theorem 9.51.13: each compact $L\subset U$ must lie in a sublevel $X_ b\Subset U$ of a smooth exhaustion which is strongly $q$-convex outside $\overline{X_ b}$. Call $U$ $q$-Runge complete when the exhaustion can be strongly $q$-convex on all of $X$. Thus every sublevel of a strongly $q$-complete exhaustion is $q$-Runge complete. More generally, every sublevel of a strongly $q$-convex exhaustion containing its exceptional set is $q$-Runge. Indeed, choose $b<c$ above the maximum of the exhaustion on $L$ and, in the latter case, above its maximum on the exceptional set. Then $L\subset X_ b\Subset X_ c$ and the required convexity holds outside $\overline{X_ b}$.

9.32.5 Holomorphic powers and ambient sums8130155C3104

We use the intrinsic Fréchet topology of theorem 9.51.5 on $\mathcal O_ X(U)$. Choose a countable cover by relatively compact embedded patches $U_ i=A_ i\cap D_ i$, where $D_ i$ is a polydisc and $A_ i$ carries the full analytic subspace structure. The maps

\[ Q_ i:\mathcal O(D_ i)\longrightarrow \mathcal O_ X(U_ i) \]

are continuous open surjections by corollary 9.51.14, applied to the coherent presentation $\mathcal O_{D_ i}\to \mathcal O_{A_ i}$. Fix compact polydiscs $L_{i,n}\Subset D_ i$ increasing to $D_ i$, with every compact subset contained in their interiors eventually.

Lemma 9.32.6.FE34E9B148D1 Let $U=A\cap D\Subset X$ be such a patch and $f\in \mathcal O(X)$. If $|f|\leq \theta <1$ on $\overline U$, then for each continuous seminorm $p$ on $\mathcal O_ X(U)$ and each $r\in (\theta ,1)$ there is a constant $C$ independent of $m$ such that

\[ p(f^ m|_ U)\leq Cr^ m\qquad (m\geq 0). \]

In particular $f^ m|_ U\to 0$ in the full intrinsic section topology.

Proof. Lift the actual section $f|_ U$ to $F\in \mathcal O(D)$ by the open surjection above, and put $W=\{ z\in D:|F(z)|<r\} $. Its intersection with $A$ is all of $U$, with its original structure. The restriction $Q_ W:\mathcal O(W)\to \mathcal O_ X(U)$ is continuous. One way to see this directly is to apply coherent-morphism continuity in theorem 9.51.5 to $\mathcal O_ W\to i_*\mathcal O_ U$; the topology of the latter's sections is the same intrinsic topology, by the local presentations. Consequently there are a compact $L\Subset W$ and $C>0$ with $p(Q_ W(G))\leq C\sup _ L|G|$ for $G\in \mathcal O(W)$. Use $G=F^ m$ and $|F|<r$ on $W$. The power represents the full section $f^ m$, not merely its reduced point values. This proves the estimate and convergence for every defining seminorm. $\square$

Here is the convergence criterion we will use. Suppose a finite block $(h_{j,l})_{1\leq l\leq N_ j}$ of global holomorphic sections is given for each $j\geq 1$. Suppose that for $i\leq j$ there are lifts $H_{i,j,l}\in \mathcal O(D_ i)$ with

434628413F46 9.32.7
\begin{equation} Q_ i(H_{i,j,l})=h_{j,l}|_{U_ i},\qquad \sup _{L_{i,j}}|H_{i,j,l}|<2^{-j}/N_ j. \end{equation}

Empty blocks are omitted. For $j<i$ choose any lifts by surjectivity. Then

\[ \rho =\sum _{j,l}|h_{j,l}|^2 \]

has a smooth plurisubharmonic ambient representative on every $U_ i$, namely $\sum _{j,l}|H_{i,j,l}|^2$ on $D_ i$. Indeed, on any smaller compact polydisc the Cauchy estimates, using a fixed larger compact polydisc, bound every differentiated tail by a constant times $\sum _ jN_ j(2^{-j}/N_ j)^2\leq \sum _ j4^{-j}$. This proves convergence of every derivative and the nonnegative Levi form. Equivalently the series $\sum _{j,l}H_{i,j,l}(z)\overline{H_{i,j,l}(\overline w)}$ converges holomorphically on $D_ i\times \overline{D_ i}$, so the representative is even real analytic. No common neighborhood for an arbitrary sequence of unrelated smooth extensions is being assumed.

Proposition 9.32.8.A13AE3DAF423 Every holomorphically convex complex space has a nonnegative smooth plurisubharmonic exhaustion. Here the holomorphic hull is tested by all sections of the full structure sheaf, using their scalar values.

Proof. The hull is closed, contains its compact set, and is idempotent, by the same inequalities as in holomorphic hulls and domains of holomorphy. Inductively take hulls of compact neighborhoods to obtain compact holomorphically convex sets $K_ j$, with $K_ j\subset \operatorname {int}K_{j+1}$ and union $X$. At stage $j$ also include $\bigcup _{i\leq j}\overline U_ i$ and the next compact of an ordinary compact exhaustion before taking the hull. Local compactness supplies the compact neighborhoods, and holomorphic convexity makes their hulls compact.

For $B_ j=K_{j+2}\setminus \operatorname {int}K_{j+1}$ choose finitely many $g_{j,l}\in \mathcal O(X)$ such that

\[ \max _ l|g_{j,l}(x)|>1\quad (x\in B_ j),\qquad \max _ l\sup _{K_ j}|g_{j,l}|<1. \]

This follows from separation from the hull $K_ j$, rescaling, and compactness of $B_ j$. If $B_ j$ is empty take an empty block. For a nonempty block, its first displayed maximum has a minimum strictly greater than one on $B_ j$.

For $i\leq j$ the preceding power lemma makes $g_{j,l}^ m|_{U_ i}$ tend to zero intrinsically. The image under $Q_ i$ of $\{ H:\sup _{L_{i,j}}|H|<2^{-j}/N_ j\} $ is an open zero neighborhood. Finitely many such conditions and finitely many $l$ can be met by one sufficiently large exponent $m_ j$. Increase it also so that $\sum _ l|g_{j,l}|^{2m_ j}\geq j$ on $B_ j$. Set $h_{j,l}=g_{j,l}^{m_ j}$. The controlled lifts (9.32.7) give a smooth nonnegative plurisubharmonic sum $\rho $.

It is an exhaustion. A point outside $K_{N+1}$ first belongs to some $K_ m$ with $m\geq N+2$, hence lies in $B_{m-2}$ and has $\rho \geq m-2\geq N$. Thus each closed sublevel is contained in one compact $K_ j$ and is closed there. If the space is compact and the annuli are eventually empty, the same argument applies. $\square$

9.32.9 Local separation and the Stein criterion93CB9A98F642

Global holomorphic sections locally separate points if each $x$ has a neighborhood $V$ such that every $y\in V\setminus \{ x\} $ satisfies $f(y)\ne f(x)$ for some $f\in \mathcal O(X)$. As in theorem 9.26.1, independent differentials are not part of this hypothesis.

Proposition 9.32.10.4B62D397B0C6 If global holomorphic sections locally separate points of a complex space $X$, then $X$ has a nonnegative smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function. If $X$ is also holomorphically convex, the function can be chosen to be an exhaustion.

Proof. Fix $x$. Choose a local embedding with $x$ at the origin, and a closed unit ambient ball whose intersection with $X$ is compactly contained in a local separation neighborhood and in the patch. At each point of $X\cap \{ |z|=1\} $ choose a global function vanishing at $x$ and, after scaling, having squared modulus greater than two there. A finite selection gives $v=\sum _{l=1}^ N|f_ l|^2$, with $v(x)=0$ and $v>2$ on that compact boundary. If the boundary is empty the selection may be empty. On the relative ball put $b=(1+|z|^2)/3$ and use the normalized regularized maximum from Euclidean Richberg smoothing, with both parameters $\eta \in (0,1/24)$. Define

\[ u_ x=M_\eta (v,b)\text{ on the relative ball},\qquad u_ x=v\text{ elsewhere}. \]

The boundary inequality makes these formulas agree on a relative collar by branch deletion. Thus $u_ x$ is globally smooth and nonnegative. Local holomorphic lifts of the $f_ l$ make $v$ ambient plurisubharmonic; the regularized maximum is also so. Near $x$ the maximum is exactly $b$, proving strictness there. These statements remain valid at an isolated point by taking a ball with empty relative boundary.

Choose such functions $u_ j$, with neighborhoods covering $X$ on which $u_ j=b_ j>v_ j$ and hence is strict, so that their compact modification supports form a locally finite family. To make this choice, use compact sets $C_ n\subset \operatorname {int}C_{n+1}$ exhausting $X$ as in lemma 9.49.1. On each compact annulus $C_ n\setminus \operatorname {int}C_{n-1}$ choose finitely many of the strict neighborhoods, with the corresponding closed relative balls inside $\operatorname {int}C_{n+1}\setminus C_{n-2}$; the first missing $C_ n$ are empty. The resulting family is locally finite. Write $v_ j=\sum _ l|f_{j,l}|^2$, so $u_ j=v_ j$ outside its ball.

Choose arbitrary lifts $F_{i,j,l}$ of $f_{j,l}|_{U_ i}$ to $D_ i$. For each $j$, finitely many suprema with $i\leq j$ on $L_{i,j}$ are finite. Choose $a_ j>0$ so small that $\sqrt{a_ j}F_{i,j,l}$ satisfy (9.32.7) for all these indices. For an empty block take any positive $a_ j$. The preceding convergence criterion makes $\sum _ j a_ jv_ j$ smoothly ambient plurisubharmonic. Put

\[ u=\sum _ j a_ jv_ j+\sum _ j a_ j(u_ j-v_ j). \]

The second sum is locally finite, so $u$ is smooth and equals the nonnegative sum $\sum _ j a_ ju_ j$ pointwise.

To check positivity, fix a point and choose a neighborhood meeting only finitely many modification supports. Remove their finitely many blocks from the ambient square series on a patch $D_ i$ at that point. The remaining series is still smooth plurisubharmonic. Add ambient plurisubharmonic extensions of these finitely many modified $u_ j$, using lemma 9.32.2 to put them in the same embedding. At least one $u_ j$ is strict near the point; its extension can be chosen strict there by lemma 9.49.1. Its coefficient is positive. Thus the resulting ambient representative of $u$ is strict. Finally, under holomorphic convexity add the nonnegative exhaustion of proposition 9.32.8. The sum stays strict and its closed sublevels stay compact. $\square$

Lemma 9.32.11.C690C430BBD1 Suppose $H^1(X,\mathcal I)=0$ for every coherent ideal sheaf $\mathcal I\subset \mathcal O_ X$. Then $X$ is holomorphically convex, global holomorphic sections separate every pair of points, and finitely many global sections give a closed local embedding near every point. More precisely, arbitrary finite jets, of independently specified orders, can be prescribed at any closed discrete set.

Proof. Let $D=\{ x_ j\} $ be closed discrete and let $r_ j\geq 0$. Its points are at most countable, since each can be isolated by a distinct member of a countable basis. Define an ideal sheaf with stalk $\mathfrak m_{x_ j}^{r_ j+1}$ at $x_ j$ and the full local ring off $D$. On a neighborhood containing just one $x_ j$ it is the corresponding power of the point ideal; off $D$ it is the full structure sheaf. These ideals agree on overlaps and are coherent by Oka's coherence theorem, kernels and exact sequences of coherent modules. Local finiteness of $D$ is essential here. The quotient is the sheaf supported on $D$ with these finite jet stalks, so its global sections are the product of all the jet spaces. The cohomology exact sequence gives the surjection

\[ \mathcal O(X)\longrightarrow \prod _ j\mathcal O_{X,x_ j}/\mathfrak m_{x_ j}^{r_ j+1}. \]

This establishes the full jets, including nilpotents. Two reduced values give global point separation.

If the hull of a compact set $K$ were not relatively compact, choose points $x_ j$ in it escaping a compact exhaustion, with no repetitions or accumulation. The interpolation just proved gives $f(x_ j)=j$. But the hull inequality bounds all these numbers by $\sup _ K|f|<\infty $, a contradiction. The hull is closed, hence compact once relatively compact. This proves holomorphic convexity.

Fix $x$ and put $e=\dim _{\mathbb C}\mathfrak m_ x/\mathfrak m_ x^2$. Use first jets to choose global $f_1,\ldots ,f_ e$ with value zero at $x$ whose germs represent a basis of this space. In an embedding with ambient coordinates $z_1,\ldots ,z_ N$ centered at $x$, lift them to holomorphic functions $F_1,\ldots ,F_ e$. The kernel of

\[ (z_1,\ldots ,z_ N)/(z_1,\ldots ,z_ N)^2 \longrightarrow \mathfrak m_ x/\mathfrak m_ x^2 \]

is exactly the space of linear parts of the full defining ideal. Choose $h_{e+1},\ldots ,h_ N$ in that ideal whose linear parts form a basis of the kernel. Then $(F_1,\ldots ,F_ e,h_{e+1},\ldots ,h_ N)$ has invertible Jacobian at $x$. By the holomorphic inverse and submersion theorems it is an ambient coordinate system after shrinking. Since all the $h_ j$ vanish in the full structure sheaf, the analytic subspace lies scheme-theoretically in its first $e$ coordinate directions. Its inclusion there, with the remaining defining ideal, is a closed local embedding given by the global $f_ j$. This argument also covers $e=0$. $\square$

Theorem 9.32.12.C337CA91C905 For a complex space $X$ the following conditions are equivalent:

  1. $X$ is holomorphically convex and its global holomorphic sections locally separate points.

  2. $X$ is holomorphically convex, its global holomorphic sections separate every pair of points, and finitely many of them give a closed local embedding near each point.

  3. $X$ has a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion.

  4. $H^1(X,\mathcal I)=0$ for every coherent ideal sheaf $\mathcal I\subset \mathcal O_ X$.

A space satisfying these equivalent conditions is called Stein. It has arbitrary discrete finite-jet interpolation as in lemma 9.32.11.

Proof. The second condition implies the first. The first implies the third by proposition 9.32.10. The third implies the fourth by the already proved coherent vanishing theorem 9.51.13, with $q=1$ and empty exceptional set. That theorem was proved from strict exhaustions and local coherent resolutions, without using any of these Stein characterizations. Finally the fourth implies the second and the jet assertion by the preceding lemma. $\square$

Corollary 9.32.13.001E137924A4 A complex space is Stein if and only if its reduction is Stein. Closed analytic subspaces and finite products of Stein spaces are Stein. Every finite intersection of weakly $1$-convex open subsets of a complex space is weakly $1$-convex; it is Stein as soon as one factor is Stein. In particular finite intersections of Stein open subsets are Stein. A closed Stein analytic subspace has a fundamental family of Stein open neighborhoods.

Proof. Reduction and closed subspaces follow from proposition 9.32.3 and the strict-exhaustion characterization. For products, take nonnegative strict exhaustions and add their pullbacks; in product embeddings the sum is strictly plurisubharmonic, and its closed sublevels are closed subsets of products of compact sublevels. The intersection statements are theorem 9.32.4 with all $q_ j=1$, together with the Stein characterization. The neighborhood assertion is exactly the $q=1$ case of theorem 9.49.8 under that characterization. $\square$
