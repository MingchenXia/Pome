6.22 Relative smoothing with a variable strict lower bound081E25F8CA73

081E25F8CA73

We use the normalized regularized maximum constructed in the existing Euclidean Richberg proof. For fixed positive parameters $\eta _ i$ it is smooth, convex, increasing in each variable and invariant under a common translation. It lies between $\max t_ i$ and $\max (t_ i+\eta _ i)$. A branch satisfying $t_ i+\eta _ i\leq \max _{j\ne i}(t_ j-\eta _ j)$ may be deleted without changing the function. Its nonnegative first derivatives sum to one. Consequently its composition with smooth functions having complex Hessians at least the same Hermitian form $\Gamma _ x$ also has complex Hessian at least $\Gamma _ x$: the chain-rule second-derivative term is positive semidefinite. These are the existing operator and proof, with their probability normalization retained.

Theorem 6.22.1 (Relative Richberg smoothing).857A88CAB3FA Let $X$ be a second countable complex manifold and let $u$ be a continuous real-valued plurisubharmonic function on $X$. Suppose that on an open set $\Omega \subset X$ its distributional complex Hessian satisfies $H_ u\geq \gamma $, where $\gamma $ is a continuous positive definite Hermitian form on $TX|_\Omega $. For every continuous $\lambda :\Omega \to (0,\infty )$ there is a continuous PSH function $v$ on $X$, smooth and strictly PSH on $\Omega $, such that

\[ v=u\text{ on }X\setminus \Omega ,\qquad u\leq v\leq u+\lambda \text{ on }\Omega ,\qquad H_ v\geq (1-\lambda )\gamma \text{ on }\Omega . \]

Proof. The cases $\Omega =\varnothing $ and $\dim X=0$ have the solution $v=u$. For positive dimension put $\kappa (x)=\min \{ \lambda (x),1/2\} $ and $\Gamma _ x=(1-\kappa (x))\gamma _ x$. This form is positive and dominates $(1-\lambda )\gamma $. Choose a countable locally finite family of coordinate balls $B_ i=B(c_ i,r_ i)\Subset \Omega $ whose concentric balls of radius $r_ i/3$ cover $\Omega $. One construction uses a compact exhaustion: cover each compact annulus by finitely many inner coordinate balls whose outer balls remain between the two neighboring larger annuli. The union is locally finite in $\Omega $; it need not be locally finite at points outside $\Omega $. For each ball choose a smooth function $0\leq b_ i\leq 1$ supported in it and positive on its inner ball. Extend these functions by zero to $X$. The series $b=\sum _{i\geq 1}2^{-i}b_ i$ converges uniformly on $X$, so it is continuous there, is positive on $\Omega $, and vanishes on its complement. Thus

\[ e(x)=\min \{ \lambda (x)/2,1,b(x)\} \quad (x\in \Omega ) \]

is continuous and positive on $\Omega $, and tends to zero at every boundary point in $X$. We will obtain $0\leq v-u\leq e$ there. In the $i$th coordinates let $A_ i>0$ be a lower bound on $\kappa (x)$ times the smallest eigenvalue of $\gamma _ x$ on $\overline B_ i$. Choose $\delta _ i>0$ with

\[ \delta _ i<A_ i/4,\qquad \delta _ i r_ i^2<\min _{\overline B_ i}e, \qquad \eta _ i=\delta _ i r_ i^2/6. \]

For sufficiently small $h_ i>0$, the usual radial convolution is defined on a neighborhood of $\overline B_ i$ and satisfies

\[ 0\leq u*\rho _{h_ i}-u<\eta _ i,\qquad H_{u*\rho _{h_ i}}\geq \gamma -(A_ i/4)I \quad \text{on }B_ i. \]

The first assertion follows from continuity and the existing monotone PSH regularization. For the second, positivity of the distributional matrix $H_ u-\gamma $ survives convolution, whereas convolution of the continuous coefficient matrix $\gamma $ tends uniformly to $\gamma $ on this compact ball. Define

\[ u_ i=u*\rho _{h_ i} +\delta _ i\bigl ((2r_ i/3)^2-|z-c_ i|^2\bigr ) \quad \text{on }B_ i. \]

Its complex Hessian is at least $\gamma -(A_ i/2)I$, hence at least $\Gamma $, since $\kappa \gamma \geq A_ i I$ in these coordinates. The same elementary radius estimates as in the Euclidean construction give

\[ \begin{aligned} u_ i& >u+\eta _ i& & \text{on }B(c_ i,r_ i/3),\\ u_ i& <u-\eta _ i& & \text{if }\sqrt7\, r_ i/3<|z-c_ i|<r_ i,\\ u_ i+\eta _ i& <u+e& & \text{on }B_ i. \end{aligned} \]

For the last inequality use $2\eta _ i+4\delta _ i r_ i^2/9=7\delta _ i r_ i^2/9<e$.

At $x\in \Omega $, apply the fixed-parameter regularized maximum to the finitely many $u_ i(x)$ with $x\in B_ i$, and call the value $v(x)$. It is smooth near every point. Indeed an inner ball supplies a branch $u_ j-\eta _ j>u$ on a neighborhood of the point, while every branch whose domain boundary passes through the point satisfies $u_ i+\eta _ i<u$ in its nearby outer shell. Such branches may all be deleted by the operator's deletion property. Local finiteness then leaves one fixed finite set of smooth branches on a smaller neighborhood. The Hessian chain rule gives $H_ v\geq \Gamma $ intrinsically, since all branch lower bounds are the same tensor $\Gamma _ x$ in any coordinate system. The upper and lower operator bounds give $u\leq v\leq u+e$.

Define $v=u$ on $X\setminus \Omega $. Because $e$ tends to zero at boundary points, this extension is continuous there. On $\Omega $ it is PSH, and its boundary limsup is $u$. The existing PSH boundary gluing proposition, applied to $\max \{ u,v\} =v$ on $\Omega $, therefore makes it PSH on all of $X$. Finally $H_ v\geq \Gamma \geq \gamma /2>0$ on $\Omega $, so it is strictly PSH there and satisfies the requested lower bound. The inequalities with $\lambda $ follow from $e\leq \lambda /2$. $\square$
