2.19 Concrete completed tensor productsB34754B4805B

B34754B4805B

All locally convex spaces are Hausdorff and complex. Completed projective and injective tensors use the exact seminorm conventions of section 2.13. The compact spaces in the continuous-function assertion need not be Hausdorff.

Proposition 2.19.1.84C71E271314 For arbitrary discrete index sets $I,J$, coefficient multiplication induces an isometric isomorphism

\[ \ell ^1(I)\widehat\otimes _\pi \ell ^1(J) \simeq \ell ^1(I\times J),\qquad x\otimes y\longmapsto (x_ i y_ j)_{i,j}. \]

Empty index sets give the zero space.

Proof. For $t=\sum _ r x_ r\otimes y_ r$, its coefficient matrix $a$ satisfies

\[ \| a\| _{\ell ^1(I\times J)}\leq \sum _ r\| x_ r\| _1\| y_ r\| _1. \]

Conversely the coordinate-basis decomposition of a finite matrix has projective cost $\sum _{i,j}|a_{ij}|$. Every algebraic tensor is approximated in projective norm by tensors with finite coefficient matrices: truncate each of its finitely many $\ell ^1$ vectors. Thus the two norms agree on the algebraic tensor product. Its completion maps isometrically onto a closed subspace containing every finite matrix; these are dense in $\ell ^1(I\times J)$, proving surjectivity. An $\ell ^1$ family has countable support, since each set where its modulus is at least $1/m$ is finite. Hence these truncations and sums are valid also for uncountable index sets. $\square$

Proposition 2.19.2.96A052277244 For Banach spaces $E,F$, the unit ball of $E\widehat\otimes _\varepsilon F$ is the polar, under the canonical pairing, of the unit ball of $E'\widehat\otimes _\pi F'$. This is a statement about polar sets, not an identification of the whole continuous dual with a projective tensor product. For arbitrary discrete $I,J$ there is an isometric isomorphism

\[ c_0(I)\widehat\otimes _\varepsilon c_0(J) \simeq c_0(I\times J). \]

Here a family belongs to $c_0(I)$ if only finitely many entries have modulus at least $\eta $, for every $\eta >0$.

Proof. For algebraic tensors the injective norm is exactly the supremum of $|(\xi \otimes \eta )(t)|$ over unit $\xi \in E'$, $\eta \in F'$. Its pairing with $E'\otimes _\pi F'$ is therefore bounded by the product of these two norms and extends to the completions. The closed projective unit ball is the closed convex hull of simple unit tensors: the projective infimum gives finite decompositions of cost at most $1+\epsilon $, and completion and rescaling give the closed-ball assertion. Conversely simple unit tensors already test the injective norm. The same supremum formula persists in the completion, by uniform convergence of their bounded test functions. These observations prove the polar assertion.

For clarity, $c_0(I)'=\ell ^1(I)$ with its exact norm. If $L$ is a bounded functional, put $a_ i=L(e_ i)$. Testing on finitely many coordinates with suitable phases gives $\sum _{i\in A}|a_ i|\leq \| L\| $ for every finite $A$. Thus $a\in \ell ^1(I)$ and finite-coordinate approximation gives $L(x)=\sum _ i a_ i x_ i$; its norm is $\sum _ i|a_ i|$ by the same finite phase tests. For the coefficient matrix of an algebraic tensor in $c_0(I)\otimes c_0(J)$ this dual description gives

\[ \| t\| _\varepsilon =\sup _{i,j}|a_{ij}|. \]

The upper bound follows by summing two unit $\ell ^1$ families, and the reverse bound by coordinate evaluations. A product matrix $x_ i y_ j$ vanishes at infinity: outside finite coordinate sets one factor is arbitrarily small and the other is bounded. The same holds for finite sums. Finite matrices are dense in $c_0(I\times J)$, so the isometric map of completions is onto, including empty sets. $\square$

Proposition 2.19.3.C9CDFE118A86 For arbitrary compact spaces $K,L$, multiplication of separated functions gives an isometric isomorphism

\[ C(K)\widehat\otimes _\varepsilon C(L)\simeq C(K\times L). \]

Proof. Let $a(x,y)=\sum _ r f_ r(x)g_ r(y)$. For unit $\xi \in C(K)'$ the function $h_\xi (y)=\xi (\sum _ r f_ r g_ r(y))$ has sup norm at most $\| a\| _\infty $. A second unit functional on $C(L)$ therefore gives $\| t\| _\varepsilon \leq \| a\| _\infty $; two Dirac evaluations give the reverse inequality. No measure-representation theorem for these duals is required.

We prove density directly, retaining the full compact-space scope. Given $a\in C(K\times L)$, the map $x\mapsto a_ x=a(x,\cdot )$ is continuous from $K$ into the Banach space $C(L)$: each $x\in K$ has a neighborhood $U_ x$ with

\[ \sup _{y\in L}|a(x',y)-a(x,y)|<\epsilon \qquad (x'\in U_ x). \]

Indeed continuity gives product neighborhoods at each $(x,y)$, and a finite subcover of the $y$-factor gives one common $U_ x$. The image $Q=\{ a_ x:x\in K\} $ is therefore compact in $C(L)$. Choose finitely many $a_{x_ i}$ whose open $\epsilon $-balls cover $Q$ and put

\[ b_ i(x)=\max \{ \epsilon -\| a_ x-a_{x_ i}\| _\infty ,0\} ,\qquad \theta _ i(x)=\frac{b_ i(x)}{\sum _ j b_ j(x)}. \]

These are continuous, their sum is one, and $\theta _ i(x)>0$ implies $\| a_ x-a_{x_ i}\| _\infty <\epsilon $. The tensor $\sum _ i\theta _ i\otimes a_{x_ i}$ therefore approximates $a$ uniformly within $\epsilon $. The algebraic tensor image is dense. Completing its already proved norm equality gives the asserted isomorphism. Empty compact spaces again give zero. $\square$
