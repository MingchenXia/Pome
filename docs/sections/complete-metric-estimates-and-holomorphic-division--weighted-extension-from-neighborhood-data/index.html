Theorem 6.31.1.5C460CFF2115 Let $(X,\omega )$ be weakly pseudoconvex Kähler, $(L,h)$ a smooth Hermitian holomorphic line bundle and $(E,H)$ a smooth Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle. Let $\sigma $ be a holomorphic section of $E$, put $t=|\sigma |_ H^2$, $Y=\{ \sigma =0\} $ and $U=\{ t<1\} $. Assume first that $Y$ is proper on every component and nonempty, and let $p\geq 1$ be the largest codimension of its irreducible components. Suppose, on $X\setminus Y$, that

79352F870E30 6.31.2
\begin{equation} \mathrm i\Theta (L,h)\geq \left(\frac pt+\frac\epsilon {1+t}\right) \langle \mathrm i\Theta (E,H)\sigma ,\sigma \rangle _ H, \qquad \epsilon >0. \end{equation}

For every holomorphic $K_ X\otimes L$ section $f$ on $U$ with $I=\int _ U|f|_{\omega ,h}^2\, \mathrm dV_\omega <\infty $, there is a global holomorphic section $F$ with $F|_ Y=f|_ Y$ and

C9D0B52C523C 6.31.3
\begin{equation} \int _ X\frac{|F|_{\omega ,h}^2}{(1+t)^{p+\epsilon }}\, \mathrm dV_\omega \leq \left(1+\frac{(p+1)^2}{\epsilon }\right)I. \end{equation}

The equality on $Y$ means equality of values on the reduced set. On a component with $\sigma \equiv 0$ take $F=f$, and on a component with $Y$ empty take $F=0$. These conventions also settle the cases of an empty $Y$ or of codimension zero only, without a logarithmic weight argument.

Proof. It suffices to treat components with proper nonempty $Y$; the same global integer $p$ can be used on all of them. Define on $X$

\[ h_0=(1-t^{(p+1)/2})f\quad (t<1),\qquad h_0=0\quad (t\geq 1). \]

This is locally $L^2$. It has distributional derivative

4DC1065B0573 6.31.4
\begin{equation} g=D''h_0=-\tfrac {p+1}2 t^{(p-1)/2}\bar\eta \wedge f \quad \hbox{on }U,\qquad g=0\quad \hbox{off }U, \qquad \eta =\langle D'\sigma ,\sigma \rangle _ H. \end{equation}

Here wedge order determines only an irrelevant common sign if the holomorphic top form is put first. To check the derivative without extending $f$ beyond $U$, multiply $h_0$ by a smooth cutoff equal to one for $t\leq 1-2d$ and zero for $t\geq 1-d$. On a compact set its extra derivative is bounded in norm by $C|f|\mathbf1_{\{ 1-2d<t<1\} }$: its derivative is $O(d^{-1})$ and $|1-t^{(p+1)/2}|\leq C d$ on that layer. Since $f$ is $L^2$ on $U$, this error tends to zero in local $L^2$ as $d\downarrow 0$. The other terms converge in local $L^2$ as well. The section is $C^1$ near $Y$, since $p\geq 1$ and $|\sigma |^{p+1}$ is $C^1$. Thus (6.31.4) holds globally, $g$ is locally $L^2$ and $D''g=0$. Global continuity across $t=1$ is not needed for either $h_0$ or $g$.

On $X\setminus Y$ put $\Phi =p\log t+\epsilon \log (1+t)$ and equip $L$ with $h e^{-\Phi }$. Chern-normal differentiation gives

\[ \mathcal H(\log t)\geq -\frac Rt,\qquad \mathcal H(\log (1+t)) =\frac{\mathrm i\langle D'\sigma ,D'\sigma \rangle _ H}{1+t} -\frac{\mathrm i\eta \wedge \bar\eta }{(1+t)^2} -\frac R{1+t}, \quad R=\langle \mathrm i\Theta (E)\sigma ,\sigma \rangle _ H. \]

The precise Cauchy–Schwarz inequality is $\mathrm i\eta \wedge \bar\eta \leq t\, \mathrm i\langle D'\sigma ,D'\sigma \rangle _ H$. It implies, using (6.31.2),

B1C1A543A240 6.31.5
\begin{equation} \mathrm i\Theta (L,h e^{-\Phi }) \geq \frac\epsilon {(1+t)^2} \mathrm i\langle D'\sigma ,D'\sigma \rangle _ H \geq \frac\epsilon {t(1+t)^2}\, \mathrm i\eta \wedge \bar\eta \geq 0. \end{equation}

The first inequality follows by replacing the negative rank-one term with at most $t$ times the derivative norm term; this leaves exactly its coefficient $(1+t)^{-2}$.

In a unitary frame, the curvature operator of $\mathrm i\eta \wedge \bar\eta $ on $(n,1)$ is the positive rank-one matrix whose quadratic form is squared contraction by $\bar\eta $. Hence (6.31.4) and (6.31.5), with the semidefinite dual definition of inverse energy, give

68717EBB5263 6.31.6
\begin{equation} |g|_{[\mathrm i\Theta (L,h e^{-\Phi }),\Lambda _\omega ]^{-1}}^2 \leq \frac{(p+1)^2}{4\epsilon }t^ p(1+t)^2|f|_{\omega ,h}^2 \leq \frac{(p+1)^2}\epsilon e^\Phi |f|_{\omega ,h}^2 \quad \hbox{on }U. \end{equation}

In this display the dual coefficient energy uses $h$ for both datum and tests; multiplying it by $e^{-\Phi }$ gives the actual bundle energy density. Off $U$ the datum is zero. No ordinary global $L^2$ bound for $g$ in the singular weight is assumed.

Let $X_ c=\{ \psi <c\} $ exhaust $X$ by a smooth PSH exhaustion. By lemma 6.28.11, $X_ c\setminus Y$ admits a complete Kähler metric. Apply the original-metric clause of theorem 6.28.2 to the smooth weighted line bundle there, with its original $\omega $. The datum is locally $L^2$ and closed; (6.31.6) gives a solution $u_ c$ with

\[ D''u_ c=g,\qquad \int _{X_ c\setminus Y}|u_ c|_{\omega ,h}^2t^{-p}(1+t)^{-\epsilon } \, \mathrm dV_\omega \leq (p+1)^2I/\epsilon . \]

These fixed smooth positive densities on compact subsets of $X\setminus Y$ give local weak $L^2$ bounds. A diagonal weak limit solves $D''u=g$ there; lower semicontinuity followed by exhaustion gives the same global estimate. Since $t$ is bounded above on compact sets, this estimate also implies ordinary local $L^2$ across $Y$. Extend $u$ over this measure-zero set. The analytic-removal lemma 6.28.12 applies to $u$ and the locally $L^2$, hence locally $L^1$, datum $g$, so $D''u=g$ on $X$. The locally $L^2$ section $F=h_0-u$ is distributionally holomorphic. Apply holomorphic distributions and minimal weighted solutions to its coefficient in local holomorphic coordinates and a holomorphic line frame. The resulting holomorphic representatives agree on overlaps, since they agree almost everywhere, so $F$ is a holomorphic section.

Near $Y$, $h_0$ is continuous, so $u=h_0-F$ is continuous there. At a smooth point of $Y$ of codimension $d\leq p$, choose transverse coordinates $z'\in \mathbb C^ d$. Holomorphicity of $\sigma $ and its vanishing along $Y$ give $t\leq C|z'|^2$ on a smaller chart. If $u$ were nonzero at that point, continuity and a smooth local frame would force its weighted norm to dominate the divergent transverse integral $\int _{|z'|<r}|z'|^{-2p}\, \mathrm dV(z')$. Thus $u=0$ on every component's dense regular part and, by continuity, on all of $Y$. Therefore $F|_ Y=f|_ Y$.

Finally weighted Cauchy–Schwarz on $U$, using $|h_0|\leq |f|$, and $1+t^ p\leq (1+t)^ p$ for the integer $p\geq 1$, gives

\[ |F|^2\leq (1+t^{-p})|u|^2+(1+t^ p)|h_0|^2, \qquad \frac{|F|^2}{(1+t)^ p}\leq t^{-p}|u|^2+|f|^2. \]

Off $U$ the latter bound holds with $f=0$ since $F=-u$. Multiply by $(1+t)^{-\epsilon }$, integrate, and use the preceding bound for $u$. This proves (6.31.3). On components with $\sigma \equiv 0$ the datum is already global and the estimate for $F=f$ has constant one. Components with no zero set require no interpolation and $F=0$ suffices. $\square$

Corollary 6.31.7.20E76F1409EA Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ be pseudoconvex, let $\varphi ,\psi $ be PSH on $\Omega $, with $\psi $ finite and continuous, and let $\sigma =(\sigma _1,\ldots ,\sigma _ r)$ be holomorphic. Put $t=\sum _ j|\sigma _ j|^2$, $Y=\{ t=0\} $, and let $p$ be the largest codimension of its proper nonempty irreducible components when such components exist. Fix $\epsilon >0$ and set $C_{p,\epsilon }=1+(p+1)^2/\epsilon $. For data holomorphic on the indicated neighborhood, there is a holomorphic $F$ on $\Omega $ with $F|_ Y=f|_ Y$ satisfying, respectively,

8135F4E6E5E2
\begin{align} U_-& =\{ t<e^{-\psi }\} ,& \int _\Omega \frac{|F|^2e^{-\varphi +p\psi }}{(1+t e^\psi )^{p+\epsilon }}\, \mathrm dV & \leq C_{p,\epsilon }\int _{U_-}|f|^2e^{-\varphi +p\psi }\, \mathrm dV, \tag{6.31.8}\\ U_+& =\{ t<e^\psi \} ,& \int _\Omega \frac{|F|^2e^{-\varphi }}{(e^\psi +t)^{p+\epsilon }}\, \mathrm dV & \leq C_{p,\epsilon }\int _{U_+}|f|^2 e^{-\varphi -(p+\epsilon )\psi }\, \mathrm dV. \tag{6.31.9} \end{align}

In the first case $f\in \mathcal O(U_-)$; in the second case $f\in \mathcal O(U_+)$. If $Y$ is empty take $F=0$. If its only components have codimension zero take $p=0$ and use $F=f$ on those ambient components and zero on the others.

Proof. First let $\varphi ,\psi $ be smooth and assume the respective right-hand integral $I$ is finite. In the first case give the trivial rank-$r$ bundle the squared metric $e^\psi I$ and the trivial line bundle the squared metric $e^{-\varphi +p\psi }$. Its section norm is $t_-=t e^\psi $, and

\[ R_ E=-\mathcal H(\psi )I,\qquad \theta _ L=\mathcal H(\varphi )-p\mathcal H(\psi ) \geq -\left(p+\frac{\epsilon t_-}{1+t_-}\right)\mathcal H(\psi ). \]

This is precisely (6.31.2). In the second case use squared metrics $e^{-\psi }I$ and $e^{-\varphi -(p+\epsilon )\psi }$ instead. Now $t_+=t e^{-\psi }$, $R_ E=\mathcal H(\psi )I$, and

\[ \theta _ L=\mathcal H(\varphi )+(p+\epsilon )\mathcal H(\psi ) \geq \left(p+\frac{\epsilon t_+}{1+t_+}\right)\mathcal H(\psi ). \]

Apply theorem 6.31.1, identifying scalar functions with coefficients of the fixed holomorphic volume form; its common norm-volume factor cancels. In the second case the identity $e^{-(p+\epsilon )\psi }(1+t e^{-\psi })^{-p-\epsilon } =(e^\psi +t)^{-p-\epsilon }$ gives the stated denominator.

For the finite-integral singular case, first treat an ambient component on which $\varphi \equiv -\infty $. Finiteness forces $f=0$ on its data domain, so $F=0$ works there. Here a zero function has zero weighted density even where the weight is infinite. On all other components $\varphi $ is locally integrable. Choose pseudoconvex $\Omega _ j\Subset \Omega $ exhausting $\Omega $, by pseudoconvex products and relatively compact exhaustions, and let $d_ j\downarrow 0$. Radial convolution at sufficiently small radii on a neighborhood of $\overline\Omega _ j$ gives smooth PSH $\varphi _ j\geq \varphi $ converging pointwise to $\varphi $, as in the Levi criterion and decreasing convolution. Because $\psi $ is continuous, the same construction can give smooth PSH $v_ j$ with $\psi \leq v_ j\leq \psi +d_ j$ near $\overline\Omega _ j$. For the first case use $\psi _ j=v_ j$; for the second use $\psi _ j=v_ j-d_ j$, so $\psi -d_ j\leq \psi _ j\leq \psi $. The respective data domains on $\Omega _ j$ are thus subsets of $U_-$ and $U_+$, respectively. Their data integrals are bounded by $e^{p d_ j}I$ in the first case and $e^{(p+\epsilon )d_ j}I$ in the second. There is no need to extend $f$ across either neighborhood boundary or to have a global smooth PSH approximation on $\Omega $.

Apply the smooth cases on $\Omega _ j$. If the zero set there has smaller maximal codimension, its proof still applies with the same larger integer $p$: this integer enters only as an upper bound for codimensions and in the prescribed bundle weights. Components on which it is empty or identically zero are settled directly as above. The resulting $F_ j$ have locally uniform ordinary $L^2$ bounds: $\varphi _ j$ are uniformly bounded above on each fixed compact set, $\psi _ j$ are uniformly bounded there and $t$ is bounded. Thus the positive output densities have a fixed positive lower bound on that set. Holomorphic mean-value and Cauchy estimates give locally uniform bounds on $F_ j$ and their derivatives. Diagonal compact convergence therefore gives a holomorphic $F$, with $F|_ Y=f|_ Y$. Pointwise convergence of the output densities and Fatou's lemma on each compact set, followed by exhaustion and $d_ j\to 0$, give the exact stated constant. Points with $\varphi =-\infty $ use the usual nonnegative extended-density convention and Fatou's lemma unchanged.

If $I=+\infty $, only existence of the extension remains to be proved. The section $f$ on a neighborhood of $Y$ defines a section of $\mathcal O_\Omega /\mathcal I_ Y$, where the reduced analytic ideal $\mathcal I_ Y$ is coherent. The smooth strict-PSH exhaustion of Oka's boundary-distance criterion makes $\Omega $ strongly $1$-complete. The fully proved coherent vanishing theorem 9.51.13 gives $H^1(\Omega ,\mathcal I_ Y)=0$, so the quotient exact sequence lifts this section to a global holomorphic $F$. The asserted integral inequality then has right-hand side $+\infty $. This argument is used only for that vacuous-estimate case. All finite-integral estimates were proved by the explicit $L^2$ construction above. $\square$
