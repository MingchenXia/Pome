5.64 Null-supported Kähler currentsCF37B5381263

CF37B5381263

We use $dd^ c=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial $. A set in a complex manifold is called null if it has Lebesgue measure zero in local real coordinates; this is equivalent to having measure zero for any smooth positive volume form.

Lemma 5.64.1.0B34D9269CEA Let $V\subset \mathbb R^ m$, $m\geq 1$, be connected and open. The zero set of a nonzero real-analytic function on $V$ is null.

Proof. Let $h$ be such a function. No point of $V$ has all derivatives of $h$ equal to zero. Indeed, the set of points with this property is closed, by continuity of every derivative, and open, by the convergent Taylor expansion. If nonempty, it would therefore be all of $V$.

For $x\in \{ h=0\} $ choose a multi-index $\alpha $ of least total degree such that $D^\alpha h(x)\ne 0$. Its degree is positive. Choose $j$ with $\alpha _ j>0$ and put $\beta =\alpha -e_ j$. Then $D^\beta h(x)=0$ and $\partial _ jD^\beta h(x)\ne 0$. Consequently

\[ \{ h=0\} \subset \bigcup _{\beta ,j}\{ D^\beta h=0,\ \partial _ jD^\beta h\ne 0\} . \]

Each set on the right is locally the graph of a smooth function of $m-1$ variables, by the implicit function theorem. Such a graph is null by Fubini's theorem. A countable subcover of these graph charts exists because $V$ is second countable. There are only countably many pairs $(\beta ,j)$, so their union is null. $\square$

Theorem 5.64.2.E682ACE02BAF Let $(X,\omega )$ be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension $n\geq 0$. There are a continuous function $u\in \operatorname {PSH}(X,\omega )$, with $u>0$, and a closed null set $\Sigma \subset X$ such that

\[ T=\omega +dd^ cu\geq 0,\qquad \operatorname {Supp}T\subset \Sigma . \]

Thus $T$ belongs to the Kähler class $[\omega ]$. For $n\geq 1$ and $X\ne \varnothing $ it is nonzero. Its coefficient measures are singular with respect to Lebesgue measure. The set $\Sigma $ can be chosen locally contained in countably many proper real-analytic level sets of pluriharmonic functions.

Proof. If $X=\varnothing $, all assertions are vacuous with $\Sigma =\varnothing $. In dimension zero $X$ is a finite discrete set and every $(1,1)$-form or current is zero. Take $u=1$, $T=0$ and $\Sigma =\varnothing $. We may henceforth assume $n\geq 1$.

Local functions with a strict minimum. Fix $x\in X$. By local smooth differential primitives, there is a smooth real potential $\rho $ for $\omega $ in coordinates centered at $x$. The Taylor expansion has the form

\[ \rho (z)=\rho (0)+\operatorname {Re}P(z) +\sum _{j,k}a_{j\bar k}z_ j\bar z_ k+o(|z|^2), \]

where $P$ is a holomorphic polynomial of degrees one and two and $(a_{j\bar k})$ is positive definite. Subtracting $\operatorname {Re}P$ changes neither $dd^ c\rho $ nor strict plurisubharmonicity. Thus, after decreasing the coordinate ball, $\rho $ has a strict minimum at its center, with $\rho (z)>\rho (0)$ for $z\ne 0$ in that ball.

Choose a smaller ball $U_ x$ whose closure lies in this potential chart, and choose numbers

\[ \rho (x)<\ell _ x<h_ x<\min _{\partial U_ x}\rho . \]

The open set $V_ x=\{ z\in U_ x:\rho (z)<\ell _ x\} $ contains $x$. Moreover $\{ z\in U_ x:\rho (z)\leq h_ x\} $ is relatively compact in $U_ x$: the strict boundary inequality gives an open collar of $\partial U_ x$ on which $\rho >h_ x$. Choose finitely many of these sets $V_ x$ covering $X$ and denote the corresponding data by $V_ i,U_ i,\rho _ i,\ell _ i,h_ i$, $1\leq i\leq N$. For any choice $c_ i\in (\ell _ i,h_ i)$ define

\[ b_ i=\begin{cases} \max \{ c_ i-\rho _ i,0\} & \text{on }U_ i,\\ 0& \text{on }X\setminus U_ i. \end{cases} \]

This is continuous, is zero in a collar of $\partial U_ i$, and is $\omega $-psh. Indeed, on $U_ i$ its local potential is $\rho _ i+b_ i=\max \{ \rho _ i,c_ i\} $, which is plurisubharmonic by maxima and gluing of plurisubharmonic functions; near the boundary and outside $U_ i$ the function $b_ i$ is zero. Also $b_ i>0$ on $V_ i$, for every permitted choice of $c_ i$. Thus these positivity sets cover $X$ independently of all subsequent choices of constants.

Choosing constants. For each $i<j$, the open set $U_ i\cap U_ j$ has at most countably many connected components. Indeed, each component is open and contains an element of a fixed countable basis not contained in any other component. On any such component $W$, the smooth function $\rho _ i-\rho _ j$ is pluriharmonic, since its $dd^ c$ is zero. It is real analytic: on small coordinate balls a real pluriharmonic function is the real part of a holomorphic function, by smooth complex Hessian potentials on balls.

If $(\rho _ i-\rho _ j)|_ W$ is constant, say $A_{ijW}$, impose

\[ c_ i-c_ j\ne A_{ijW}. \]

There are only countably many forbidden affine hyperplanes in the parameter box $B=\prod _ i(\ell _ i,h_ i)\subset \mathbb R^ N$. Each is null by Fubini's theorem, while $B$ has positive finite Lebesgue measure. Their union cannot exhaust $B$. Fix a choice of constants outside this union and define

\[ u=\max _{1\leq i\leq N}b_ i. \]

It is continuous and $\omega $-psh by quasi-plurisubharmonic representatives and maxima(3), and $u>0$ because the $V_ i$ cover $X$.

The tie set and the support. Put

\[ \Sigma _{ij}=\{ b_ i=b_ j=u\} ,\qquad \Sigma =\bigcup _{i<j}\Sigma _{ij}. \]

These are closed sets because all functions involved are continuous. A point of $\Sigma _{ij}$ has $b_ i=b_ j=u>0$, so lies in $U_ i\cap U_ j$, and there

\[ \rho _ i-\rho _ j=c_ i-c_ j. \]

On a component where $\rho _ i-\rho _ j$ is constant this level set is empty, by our choice. On every other component it is a proper real-analytic zero set, hence null by lemma 5.64.1. This lemma applies in countably many real coordinate balls; on each ball the function cannot vanish identically, by real-analytic continuation on the connected component. Countably many components and finitely many pairs $(i,j)$ show that $\Sigma $ is null and give the asserted local containment in proper real-analytic level sets.

At a point outside $\Sigma $ exactly one $b_ i$ realizes the maximum. The strict inequalities and $u>0$ persist on a neighborhood. On a possibly smaller neighborhood $u=b_ i=c_ i-\rho _ i$, and therefore $\omega +dd^ cu=0$. This proves the support inclusion. Positivity and closedness follow from $u\in \operatorname {PSH}(X,\omega )$ and $\mathrm d\omega =0$. The formula $T-\omega =dd^ cu$ identifies their classes directly.

Finally, for $n\geq 1$, distributional integration by parts gives

\[ \int _ XT\wedge \omega ^{n-1}=\int _ X\omega ^ n>0, \]

since $dd^ c(\omega ^{n-1})=0$. Hence $T\ne 0$. Positive currents have measure coefficients, and all those measures are supported on the null set $\Sigma $, so they are singular. $\square$

The tie set is not asserted to be a complex analytic or pluripolar set. Its defining equalities are real analytic on overlaps, together with the inequalities selecting the maximizing branches. In particular, the construction allows real hypersurfaces in the support.

Lemma 5.64.3.7D6EE0FC8776 Let $(X,\omega )$ be a compact Kähler manifold and let $u$ be a continuous $\omega $-psh function on $X$. There are smooth functions $v_ j,w_ j$ on $X$ such that

\[ v_ j\downarrow u,\qquad w_ j\downarrow u \quad \text{uniformly on }X, \]

and

\[ \omega +dd^ cv_ j\geq 0,\qquad \omega +dd^ cw_ j\geq \frac1{j+1}\omega . \]

In particular the Kähler forms $\omega +dd^ cw_ j$ all belong to $[\omega ]$ and converge weakly to $\omega +dd^ cu$.

Proof. If $X=\varnothing $ there is nothing to prove. For a zero-dimensional component take $v_ j=w_ j=u$; all forms vanish and the assertions about positivity are vacuous there. On the positive-dimensional components apply gentle quasi-equisingular approximation with $\theta =\omega $ and with $\omega $ also the Kähler reference form. It gives $\Phi _ j\downarrow u$ pointwise, with

\[ \omega +dd^ c\Phi _ j\geq -\varepsilon _ j\omega , \qquad \varepsilon _ j=c'2^{-j}\downarrow 0. \]

The functions $\Phi _ j$ have gentle analytic singularities of type $(2^{-j},\mathcal I(2^ ju))$. Since $u$ is bounded, $\mathcal I(2^ ju)=\mathcal O_ X$: every holomorphic germ is square integrable against the locally bounded positive weight $\exp (-2^ ju)$ after shrinking its domain. Equivalently, the local ideal generators in these analytic presentations have no common zero. Thus $\Phi _ j$ is everywhere finite. By gentle analytic singularities, $\exp (2^ j\Phi _ j)$ is smooth, and, being positive everywhere, has a smooth logarithm. Hence every $\Phi _ j$ is smooth. This is the same smoothness argument as in smooth approximation of bounded Kähler potentials.

Choose a constant $C\geq \sup _ X\Phi _1$ and put

\[ v_ j=C+\frac{\Phi _ j-C}{1+\varepsilon _ j}. \]

Since $\Phi _ j-C\leq 0$, $\Phi _{j+1}\leq \Phi _ j$ and $\varepsilon _{j+1}\leq \varepsilon _ j$, we have

\[ \frac{\Phi _{j+1}-C}{1+\varepsilon _{j+1}} \leq \frac{\Phi _ j-C}{1+\varepsilon _{j+1}} \leq \frac{\Phi _ j-C}{1+\varepsilon _ j}. \]

Thus $v_ j$ decreases. Its pointwise limit is $u$, and

\[ \omega +dd^ cv_ j =\frac{\omega +dd^ c\Phi _ j+\varepsilon _ j\omega }{1+\varepsilon _ j}\geq 0. \]

Also $v_ j-C\leq 0$. Consequently the functions

\[ w_ j=C+\frac{j}{j+1}(v_ j-C) \]

decrease to $u$, by the same sign argument, and

\[ \omega +dd^ cw_ j =\frac{j}{j+1}(\omega +dd^ cv_ j)+\frac1{j+1}\omega \geq \frac1{j+1}\omega . \]

Both convergences are uniform by Dini's theorem. Explicitly, for a decreasing continuous sequence $f_ j$ with continuous limit $u$ on compact $X$, the closed sets $\{ f_ j-u\geq \delta \} $ decrease and have empty intersection for every $\delta >0$; compactness makes one of them empty. This applies to $v_ j$ and $w_ j$. Finally uniform convergence implies distributional convergence of $dd^ cw_ j$ to $dd^ cu$, by pairing with $dd^ c$ of a smooth test form. The displayed formulas also show equality of the classes. $\square$

Corollary 5.64.4.3149285306C2 Every Kähler class on a compact Kähler manifold contains a closed positive current with a continuous global potential and null support, which is a weak limit of Kähler forms in that class whose global potentials converge uniformly and monotonically.

Proof. Apply lemma 5.64.3 to the continuous potential supplied by theorem 5.64.2. $\square$
