10.43 Sheaves, resolutions and cohomology1F8A57819EE7

1F8A57819EE7

All spaces in this section are topological spaces; no separation hypothesis is imposed unless stated. Sheaves and complexes are sheaves of abelian groups. The same constructions apply to modules over a fixed commutative ring, with linear morphisms. An additional module structure over a sheaf of rings will be used only where explicitly specified. We work with the usual axiom of choice. Empty products of groups and sections over the empty set are zero.

A presheaf $P$ assigns a group $P(U)$ to each open set, with identity and composable restriction maps. It is a sheaf if compatible sections on an open cover glue uniquely. Its stalk at $x$ is

\[ P_ x=\underset {U\ni x}{\mathop{\rm colim}}P(U): \quad s_ x=t_ x\ \Longleftrightarrow s|_ W=t|_ W\text{ for some open }W\ni x. \]

These are the sheaf and stalk conventions used in the existing analytic sheaf algebra; no second coherence theorem is needed.

Proposition 10.43.1.C3C8B03F46AB Every presheaf $P$ has a sheafification $P^\# $ with the same stalks, characterized by

\[ \operatorname {Hom}(P^\# ,F)=\operatorname {Hom}(P,F) \quad \text{for every sheaf }F. \]

A morphism of sheaves is injective, surjective or an isomorphism exactly when the respective condition holds on every stalk. Kernels are computed on sections. Images and cokernels are the sheafifications of their presheaf versions, and are computed on stalks. Consequently exactness is stalkwise, sheafification preserves short exact sequences of presheaves, and the section functor $\Gamma (U,-)$ is left exact. The sheafification of the constant presheaf $M$ is the sheaf $\underline M$ of locally constant functions with values in the discrete group $M$.

Proof. Form the disjoint union $E=\coprod _ xP_ x$. A section $s\in P(U)$ gives $[s,U]=\{ s_ x:x\in U\} \subset E$. These sets form a basis: their intersection is $[s,W]$, where $W\subset U\cap V$ is the open set on which $s_ x=t_ x$. The projection $E\to X$ maps each basic set homeomorphically to its domain. Let $P^\# (U)$ be its continuous sections. They glue by the elementary pasting property for continuous maps. Every such section is locally of the form $x\mapsto s_ x$, so evaluation at $x$ identifies $(P^\# )_ x$ with $P_ x$. Group operations are defined locally on representatives and are independent of choices. The map $P\to P^\# $ sends $s$ to its germs. Given $P\to F$, send every local representative $s$ to its image in $F$ and glue; equality of germs makes the local images agree. This proves existence and uniqueness in the displayed property. If $P$ is already a sheaf, local representatives glue uniquely and this map is an isomorphism. Conversely, continuous sections of any local homeomorphism to $X$ recover its fibers as stalks, so the description also identifies sheaves with their spaces of germs.

A sheaf section is zero if and only if all its germs are zero: local vanishing and uniqueness of gluing prove this. Thus stalkwise injection implies injection on sections. If a sheaf map is stalkwise surjective, every section of its target has local lifts. For a stalkwise isomorphism these lifts are unique on overlaps and glue, proving the isomorphism assertion. The kernel presheaf is a sheaf by gluing. A germ in its kernel is represented by a section whose image vanishes after a restriction, which proves the kernel formula on stalks. The same representative argument gives the stalk image and cokernel after sheafification. For example, a section in the sheaf image has local lifts, which may fail to glue globally. These facts prove the exactness assertions: whenever a germ maps to zero, shrink a representative to make its image zero and then lift it at the stalk. For an objectwise exact sequence of presheaves, this is the same elementary argument for a filtered colimit of groups. Finally, a target section in the kernel of the second map of a sheaf sequence has unique local lifts through its first map, and they glue. This proves left exactness of sections. Local representatives of the constant presheaf are exactly locally constant maps, proving the last assertion. $\square$

For example, local functions of a prescribed local regularity, continuous sections of a bundle, and arbitrary maps $U\to M$ with pointwise group operations are sheaves, by pasting. The germ-space construction and its universal property apply also to presheaves of sets: the same proof omits only the group operations. If the stalks are nonempty, the germ projection is onto. The constant presheaf need not be a sheaf: different constants on two disjoint nonempty open sets need not glue to a constant. Nor need the quotient presheaf of two sheaves be a sheaf. On the circle the map $C(U,\mathbb R)\to C(U,S^1)$, $a\mapsto e^{2\pi i a}$, has kernel $C(U,\mathbb Z)$ and local lifts. Its quotient sheaf is $C(-,S^1)$. The identity of $S^1$ has no global lift: a lift along $e^{2\pi it}$ would differ from $t$ by a continuous integer, hence a constant, in contradiction with equal values at $t=0,1$. Local lifts on arcs therefore give a compatible family in the quotient presheaf with no global lift.

Proposition 10.43.2.64E16AF43980 For a continuous map $f:X\to Y$, the direct image $(f_*F)(V)=F(f^{-1}V)$ is a sheaf and is left exact. There is an exact inverse-image functor $f^{-1}$, with

\[ (f^{-1}B)_ x=B_{f(x)},\qquad \operatorname {Hom}_ X(f^{-1}B,F)=\operatorname {Hom}_ Y(B,f_*F). \]

Its unit $B\to f_*f^{-1}B$ pulls sections back along $f$ and its counit $f^{-1}f_*F\to F$ sends a germ of a section near a whole inverse image to its germ at $x$. These maps need not be isomorphisms. For a finite $q$-sheet covering, $f_*\underline M$ is locally $\underline M^ q$, with transition maps permuting the sheets; it need not be a constant sheaf.

Proof. The direct-image assertion follows by gluing on inverse images and left exactness of sections. Define $f^{-1}B$ by sheafifying

\[ U\longmapsto \underset {V\supset f(U)}{\mathop{\rm colim}}B(V), \qquad V\subset Y\text{ open}. \]

A representative has a germ at $f(x)$. Every such germ is obtained by restricting to $U\subset f^{-1}V$. If two representatives have the same germ at $f(x)$, they agree on a smaller neighborhood $W$ of $f(x)$; restrict $U$ further to $U\cap f^{-1}W$. This proves the stalk formula and, by proposition 10.43.1, exactness. For a map $B\to f_*F$, a section $b\in B(V)$ gives a section of $F$ on $f^{-1}V$; restrict these sections and use the sheafification property to obtain $f^{-1}B\to F$. Conversely pull $b$ back to $f^{-1}B$ and apply a map to $F$. The two constructions undo each other on local representatives, proving the adjunction and its stated maps. Equivalently, the space of germs of $f^{-1}B$ is the pullback of the space of germs of $B$; the same basic neighborhoods verify this directly.

Over an evenly covered open $V$, sections on $f^{-1}V=\coprod _{j=1}^ qV_ j$ are $q$ independently locally constant functions on $V$. A change of sheet labels permutes these functions, locally constantly on each overlap. In the connected double cover of the circle, global sections of $f_*\underline{\mathbb Z}$ form $\mathbb Z$, whereas global sections of the constant sheaf $\underline{\mathbb Z}^2$ form $\mathbb Z^2$. These abelian groups are not isomorphic: their quotients by twice the group have respectively two and four elements. Thus the local description cannot in general be promoted to a global constant-sheaf isomorphism. $\square$

Lemma 10.43.3.FE20ED71F10C A degreewise short exact sequence of complexes $0\to A^\bullet \xrightarrow {i}B^\bullet \xrightarrow {p}C^\bullet \to 0$ of abelian groups gives a natural long exact sequence

\[ \cdots \to H^ r(A)\to H^ r(B)\to H^ r(C) \xrightarrow {\partial }H^{r+1}(A)\to \cdots . \]

In a commutative diagram of exact five-term sequences, the middle vertical map is injective if the second and fourth maps are injective and the first is surjective; it is surjective if the second and fourth are surjective and the fifth is injective.

Proof. For a cocycle $c\in C^ r$, choose $b\in B^ r$ with $pb=c$. There is a unique $a\in A^{r+1}$ with $ia=db$, and $da=0$ because $i(da)=d^2b=0$. Put $\partial [c]=[a]$. Changing $b$ by $ik$ changes $a$ by $dk$; changing $c$ by $dc_0$ and a lift by $db_0$ changes nothing else. This proves well-definedness and additivity. At $H^ r(B)$, a closed $b$ whose image is $dc_0$ can be replaced by $b-db_0$ for a lift $b_0$ of $c_0$, after which it is $ia$ with $a$ closed. At $H^ r(C)$, if $db=ia$ and $a=dk$, then $b-ik$ is a closed lift; conversely a closed lift gives zero connecting class. At $H^{r+1}(A)$, if $ia=db$, then $pb$ is closed and its connecting class is $[a]$; conversely every connecting class maps to zero in $H^{r+1}(B)$. These checks prove exactness at every position. Applying a morphism to these lifts proves naturality. The same lift calculation applies to an ungraded differential group by repeating it in every integer degree; its long exact sequence is the corresponding cyclic exact sequence.

For the injective five-lemma assertion, let $a_3$ map to zero below. Its image in the fourth group is zero by fourth-map injectivity, so $a_3$ comes from $a_2$. The image of $a_2$ below comes from $b_1$; lift $b_1$ to $a_1$, subtract its image from $a_2$, and use second-map injectivity. The resulting $a_3$ is zero. For surjectivity, start with $b_3$ and lift its image in the fourth group to $a_4$. Its image in the fifth group is zero by fifth-map injectivity; lift $a_4$ to $a_3$. The difference $b_3-\varphi _3a_3$ comes from $b_2$; lift $b_2$ using second-map surjectivity and add its image to $a_3$. This gives the desired preimage. $\square$

10.43.4 An explicit flabby resolution6799E9E3519C

A resolution of $F$ is an augmented complex $0\to F\to L^0\to L^1\to \cdots $ that is exact on stalks; its terms in negative complex degrees are zero. Acyclicity of its terms is a separate additional condition.

A sheaf $F$ is flabby if $F(V)\to F(U)$ is onto for every inclusion of open sets $U\subset V$. It is equivalent to require extensions to $X$, since an extension to $X$ can be restricted to $V$. Flabbiness is local on the base. More precisely, it is enough that every section on an open $U$ extend across each point $x$: there is an open $W\ni x$ with an extension on $W$ agreeing on $U\cap W$. To see this, order its open-domain extensions by extension and apply gluing to chains. A maximal domain must be $X$, since otherwise the stated local extension enlarges it. Conversely a flabby sheaf has this property. If the sheaf is flabby on each member of an open cover, extend the restriction of a section to such a member near $x$ to obtain this same property.

Define the functor

\[ (GF)(U)=\prod _{x\in U}F_ x, \qquad \eta _ F:F\longrightarrow GF,\quad s\longmapsto (s_ x)_ x. \]

Restriction in $GF$ forgets factors. Thus $GF$ is a flabby sheaf, and $\eta _ F$ is injective. Both $G$ and $\Gamma (X,G-)$ are exact: a short exact sequence gives exact stalk sequences, and their products are exact, since each coordinate can be lifted separately. The same statements hold on every open subset.

Theorem 10.43.5.E24E008710DC Put $C^{-1}F=F$, $C^ qF=G^{q+1}F$ for $q\ge 0$, and

\[ d^{-1}=\eta _ F,\qquad d^ q=\sum _{i=0}^{q+1}(-1)^ i\delta _ i^ q, \quad \delta _ i^ q=G^ i\eta _{G^{q+1-i}F}\quad (q\ge 0). \]

Then $0\to F\to C^0F\to C^1F\to \cdots $ is a functorial resolution by flabby sheaves. Each functor $\Gamma (U,C^ q-)$ is exact. Restriction of this resolution to an open set $U$ is the same construction on $U$.

Proof. For every sheaf map $a:B\to B'$, its action on germs gives $G(a)\eta _ B=\eta _{B'}a$. Applied successively inside iterates of $G$, this identity gives, for $i<j$,

\[ \delta _ j^{q+1}\delta _ i^ q=\delta _ i^{q+1}\delta _{j-1}^ q. \]

Indeed, after removing the common outer $G^ i$, this is precisely naturality of $\eta $ for the map obtained by inserting $\eta $ in the $(j-i)$th position. In the expansion of $d^{q+1}d^ q$ these two composites have opposite signs; every term occurs in exactly one such pair. Thus $d^2=0$, including $d^0\eta _ F=0$.

Fix $x$. Evaluation at $x$ gives a well-defined map $e_ x^ B:(GB)_ x\to B_ x$: represent a germ by an arbitrary assignment $(b_ y)$ near $x$ and take $b_ x$. It satisfies $e_ x^ B\eta _{B,x}=\mathrm{id}$ and $e_ x^{B'}G(a)_ x=a_ xe_ x^ B$. Set $h^ q=e_ x^{G^ qF}:(C^ qF)_ x\to (C^{q-1}F)_ x$ for $q\ge 0$. Then $h^{q+1}\delta _0^ q=\mathrm{id}$ and, for $i\ge 1$, $h^{q+1}\delta _ i^ q=\delta _{i-1}^{q-1}h^ q$. Hence

\[ h^{q+1}d^ q+d^{q-1}h^ q=\mathrm{id},\qquad h^0\eta _{F,x}=\mathrm{id}. \]

This proves exactness on every stalk, hence sheaf exactness. Each $C^ qF$ is flabby by its outer $G$. Exactness of $G$ inductively and of $\Gamma (U,G-)$ proves the assertion about sections. The constructions commute with sheaf maps by their definitions. On $V\subset U$, $(G_ XF)(V)=\prod _{x\in V}F_ x=(G_ U(F|_ U))(V)$; iteration proves the restriction assertion. $\square$

Definition 10.43.6.8B7E0E3149DB Here $C^\bullet F$ means the unaugmented complex with terms $C^ qF$ for $q\ge 0$ and zero terms for $q<0$. The symbol $C^{-1}F=F$ above was used only to describe the augmentation and its stalk homotopy. Define

\[ H^ q(U,F)=H^ q\bigl (\Gamma (U,C^\bullet F),d\bigr ) \quad (q\ge 0),\qquad H^ q(U,F)=0\quad (q<0). \]

A sheaf is acyclic on $U$ if these groups vanish for $q>0$.

Theorem 10.43.7.847001406E77 One has $H^0(U,F)=F(U)$. Every short exact sheaf sequence $0\to F\to B\to Q\to 0$ gives a natural long exact sequence

\[ 0\to F(U)\to B(U)\to Q(U)\to H^1(U,F)\to \cdots . \]

In particular $B(U)\to Q(U)$ is onto if $H^1(U,F)=0$. Every flabby sheaf is acyclic on every open subset. If $X=U\cup V$, there is a natural Mayer–Vietoris sequence

\[ \cdots \to H^ r(X,F)\to H^ r(U,F)\oplus H^ r(V,F) \to H^ r(U\cap V,F)\to H^{r+1}(X,F)\to \cdots , \]

where the middle restriction difference is $b|_{U\cap V}-a|_{U\cap V}$.

Proof. Left exactness of sections identifies the kernel in degree zero with $F(U)$. By theorem 10.43.5, applying $\Gamma (U,C^\bullet -)$ to a short exact sequence gives a degreewise short exact sequence of complexes; apply lemma 10.43.3.

We first prove an extension fact without using cohomology. If $0\to A\to B\xrightarrow {p}Q\to 0$ is exact and $A$ flabby, then $B(U)\to Q(U)$ is onto. Given $t\in Q(U)$, order all its partial lifts $b\in B(W)$, $W\subset U$ open, by extension. Chains have upper bounds by gluing, so take a maximal lift. If $x\in U\setminus W$, choose a local lift $b_0$ on $W_0\ni x$. On $W\cap W_0$, the difference $b-b_0$ is the image of a section of $A$, by left exactness of sections. Extend this section to $W_0$ using flabbiness, add it to $b_0$, and glue with $b$. This contradicts maximality. Thus $W=U$. If also $B$ is flabby, lifting a section of $Q(U)$ to $B(U)$ and extending to $X$ shows that $Q$ is flabby.

Now suppose $F$ is flabby. Set $Z^0=F$ and $Z^{q+1}=\ker (d^{q+1}:C^{q+1}F\to C^{q+2}F)$. Exactness of the resolution gives $0\to Z^ q\to C^ qF\to Z^{q+1}\to 0$. The extension fact shows inductively that every $Z^ q$ is flabby and that these sequences are exact on sections over every open set. Hence the complex defining $H^ q(U,F)$ is exact for $q>0$.

Finally each $C^ qF$ is a sheaf and flabby, so

\[ 0\to C^ qF(X)\to C^ qF(U)\oplus C^ qF(V) \to C^ qF(U\cap V)\to 0 \]

is exact: the kernel is the sheaf gluing condition and surjectivity follows by extending the desired section from $U\cap V$ to $V$. Apply the cochain long exact sequence and the restriction assertion of theorem 10.43.5. $\square$

Theorem 10.43.8.9C38CEDEED0B Let $0\to F\to L^0\xrightarrow {d}L^1\to \cdots $ be a resolution of sheaves of abelian groups. If every $L^ j$ is acyclic on $X$, then there are natural isomorphisms

\[ H^ q\bigl (L^\bullet (X)\bigr )\simeq H^ q(X,F),\qquad q\ge 0. \]

They commute with morphisms of augmented resolutions. Thus a morphism between two acyclic resolutions inducing the identity on $F$ induces an isomorphism on section cohomology. Exactness of a resolution alone, without its acyclicity hypothesis, is not asserted to have this property.

Proof. Put $Z^0=F$ and $Z^ j=\ker (L^ j\to L^{j+1})$ for $j\ge 1$. For $j\ge 1$ there is a short exact sequence $0\to Z^{j-1}\to L^{j-1}\to Z^ j\to 0$. Its long exact sequence identifies

\[ Z^ j(X)/dL^{j-1}(X)\simeq H^1(X,Z^{j-1}) \]

and gives connecting isomorphisms $H^ s(X,Z^ j)\simeq H^{s+1}(X,Z^{j-1})$ for $s\ge 1$. For fixed $q\ge 1$, the first quotient with $j=q$ is exactly $H^ q(L^\bullet (X))$. Follow it by the $q-1$ connecting isomorphisms ending at $H^ q(X,Z^0)=H^ q(X,F)$. No shift past $Z^0$ is made. For $q=0$, left exactness gives $\ker d=F(X)$ directly. Naturality of every connecting map, proved in lemma 10.43.3, proves the naturality assertions. $\square$

Proposition 10.43.10.0C328241C3B1 Let $\mathcal A$ be a sheaf of unital rings on $X$ such that every open cover $(U_ i)$ admits sections $\phi _ i\in \mathcal A(X)$ with locally finite supports, $\operatorname {Supp}\phi _ i\subset U_ i$ and $\sum _ i\phi _ i=1$. Every sheaf $F$ of $\mathcal A$-modules is acyclic on $X$. In particular, on a Hausdorff smooth manifold with a countable basis, all sheaves of $C^\infty $-modules are acyclic.

Proof. The support of a section means its closed germ support: a zero germ is zero in a neighborhood. Give $GF$ the $\mathcal A$-action $(a s)_ x=a_ xs_ x$. The maps $\eta $, their iterates and all coface maps are $\mathcal A$-linear, so the Godement differential is linear over $\mathcal A$. Let $z\in C^ qF(X)$ be closed, $q>0$. Exactness on stalks gives local sections $b_ i\in C^{q-1}F(U_ i)$ with $db_ i=z$. Multiply by a subordinate $\phi _ i$. The section $\phi _ i b_ i$ extends by zero: it is zero on $U_ i\setminus \operatorname {Supp}\phi _ i$, and $U_ i$ together with $X\setminus \operatorname {Supp}\phi _ i$ covers $X$. The locally finite sum $b=\sum _ i\phi _ i b_ i$ is a sheaf section and $db=\sum _ i\phi _ i z=z$. This proves acyclicity.

For the final assertion use the existing smooth partition construction: exhaust each connected component by compact unions of chart balls, choose finitely many compactly supported bumps on each compact annular layer subordinate to the given cover, and divide by their positive locally finite sum. Its proof uses only real coordinates, as in manifold cutoffs, partitions and compact-support Stokes, Stokes' formula on compact domains with corners, and applies in every real dimension. The partition supports are contained in their open chart domains. $\square$

Corollary 10.43.11.EE08E1840318 For a Hausdorff smooth manifold $M$ with a countable basis, the smooth de Rham complex computes $H^ q(M,\underline{\mathbb R})$. If $M$ is oriented, inclusion of smooth forms in real currents induces an isomorphism of their de Rham cohomology groups. On a complex manifold, for every $p$, inclusion of smooth $(p,\bullet )$-forms in $(p,\bullet )$-currents induces isomorphisms

\[ H^ q(\mathcal E^{p,\bullet }(X),\bar\partial ) \simeq H^ q(X,\Omega _ X^ p) \simeq H^ q(\mathcal D'^{p,\bullet }(X),\bar\partial ). \]

Here $\Omega _ X^ p$ is the sheaf of holomorphic p-forms. These groups are ordinary section cohomology; no compact-support or singular-cohomology comparison is asserted. More generally, if $E$ is a holomorphic vector bundle and $\mathcal O(E)$ its sheaf of holomorphic sections, then

\[ H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X,E) :=H^ q(\mathcal E^{p,\bullet }(X,E),\bar\partial _ E) \simeq H^ q(X,\Omega _ X^ p\otimes _{\mathcal O_ X}\mathcal O(E)) \simeq H^ q(\mathcal D'^{p,\bullet }(X,E),\bar\partial _ E), \]

compatibly with the inclusion of smooth $E$-valued forms in currents.

Proof. In dimension zero the assertions are immediate on the discrete coordinate balls. In positive dimension the real homotopy formula the de Rham homotopy formula on a coordinate ball gives exactness of the smooth de Rham sheaf complex in positive degrees, and its degree zero kernel is the locally constant sheaf. The same local exactness for currents follows from theorem 5.54.2: a closed current is a smooth closed form plus an exact current, and the smooth form has a local primitive. Closed scalar distributions are locally constant by distributions with zero gradient. On an oriented $M$, integration defines the inclusion of smooth forms in currents and it induces the identity on this degree-zero kernel. Each sheaf of forms or currents is a $C^\infty $-module, hence acyclic by proposition 10.43.10. Apply theorem 10.43.8 to the two resolutions.

For $\bar\partial $, local Dolbeault exactness for forms and currents for forms and currents supplies positive-degree exactness. In degree zero, holomorphic distributions and minimal weighted solutions applied to the coefficients says precisely that the kernel consists of holomorphic p-forms. Complex manifolds have their canonical orientation. These sheaves are again $C^\infty $-modules. The same comparison theorem proves all three isomorphisms. Their differentials need only be additive; they are not claimed to be $C^\infty $-linear. It is the Godement differential used to prove acyclicity of each individual sheaf that is module-linear.

For the bundle assertion, define $\bar\partial _ E$ coefficientwise in a local holomorphic frame. Changes of frame are holomorphic matrices, whose $\bar\partial $ is zero, so the local operators agree, for smooth forms and for distribution coefficients. The same local frames reduce positive-degree exactness to the scalar local exactness already used, and identify the degree-zero kernel with $\Omega _ X^ p\otimes _{\mathcal O_ X}\mathcal O(E)$ by the scalar holomorphy lemma. Each smooth or current term is still a sheaf of $C^\infty $-modules and hence is acyclic. The inclusion commutes with $\bar\partial _ E$ and is the identity on this kernel. Applying theorem 10.43.8 proves both isomorphisms and their compatibility, with no global trivialization of $E$ required. $\square$

On an unoriented real manifold, ordinary currents defined as duals of compactly supported ordinary forms instead resolve the real orientation local system. Indeed, choose an orientation in each coordinate ball. The closed degree-zero generator is integration against $1$ in that orientation, and changing the chosen orientation changes this generator by its sign. These signs are exactly the transition maps of the orientation local system. Tensoring this current complex with that local system cancels the signs and gives a resolution of $\underline{\mathbb R}$; locally the system has constant signs, so the differential and all local exactness statements persist. This is the orientation-twisted version of the real-current comparison.

Corollary 10.43.12.B5B64990368D Every real closed $(1,1)$-current $T$ has locally a real distribution $u$ with $T=i\partial \bar\partial u$. If $T$ is smooth, $u$ can be chosen smooth.

Proof. Local real current exactness, used in corollary 10.43.11, gives $T=dS$; replace $S$ by its real part. Write $S=S^{1,0}+S^{0,1}$, so $S^{1,0}=\overline{S^{0,1}}$. Since $T^{0,2}=0$, one has $\bar\partial S^{0,1}=0$. By local Dolbeault exactness for forms and currents, on a smaller neighborhood $S^{0,1}=\bar\partial v$. Therefore

\[ T=\partial \bar\partial v+\bar\partial \partial \bar v =\partial \bar\partial (v-\bar v) =i\partial \bar\partial (2\operatorname {Im}v). \]

Take $u=2\operatorname {Im}v$. For smooth $T$, first use the smooth real homotopy to choose smooth $S$, and then the smooth clause of the same local complex exactness theorem to choose smooth $v$. $\square$

For an open cover $\mathcal U=(U_ i)$ use all ordered index tuples, including repetitions, without an alternating restriction, and put

\[ \check C^ r(\mathcal U,F)=\prod _{i_0,\ldots ,i_ r} F(U_{i_0}\cap \cdots \cap U_{i_ r}),\quad (\delta c)_{i_0\ldots i_{r+1}} =\sum _{j=0}^{r+1}(-1)^ jc_{i_0\ldots \widehat i_ j\ldots i_{r+1}}. \]

Restrictions to the common intersection are understood. Equal omissions cancel in pairs, so $\delta ^2=0$. Write $\check H^ r(\mathcal U,F)$ for its cohomology; the gluing axiom gives $\check H^0(\mathcal U,F)=F(X)$.

Lemma 10.43.14.E9E0A2A2589C For every cover, $\check H^ r(\mathcal U,F)=0$ for $r>0$ if $F$ is flabby. The same holds for a module over a sheaf of rings with a partition of unity subordinate to this cover.

Proof. For a partition $(\phi _ i)$, extend $\phi _ jc_{j i_0\ldots i_{r-1}}$ by zero and put $(hc)_{i_0\ldots i_{r-1}}=\sum _ j\phi _ jc_{j i_0\ldots i_{r-1}}$. The sum is locally finite. Expanding the finite omission sums gives

\[ (\delta hc)_{i_0\ldots i_ r} =\sum _ j\phi _ j\bigl (c_{i_0\ldots i_ r}-(\delta c)_{j i_0\ldots i_ r}\bigr ), \qquad \delta h+h\delta =\mathrm{id}\quad (r>0). \]

For $F=GB$, choose for each point one index of a set containing it. The characteristic functions of this partition of the underlying set act on $GB(U)=\prod _{x\in U}B_ x$. The same formula is defined pointwise, with exactly one summand at each point; no local finiteness or continuity of these characteristic functions is needed. Thus $GB$ is Čech-acyclic.

For flabby $F$, in $0\to F\to GF\to Q\to 0$ the quotient $Q$ is flabby, and sections are onto on every intersection, by the extension fact in theorem 10.43.7. Taking products gives a short exact sequence of Čech complexes. Its long exact sequence, $\check H^0=\Gamma $, and surjectivity $GF(X)\to Q(X)$ give $\check H^1(\mathcal U,F)=0$. For $r>1$, they give $\check H^ r(\mathcal U,F)\simeq \check H^{r-1}(\mathcal U,Q)$. Induction, simultaneously over all flabby sheaves, proves the result. $\square$

Lemma 10.43.15.9D23E3CA1553 Let $D^{a,b}$, $a,b\ge 0$, be a double complex whose horizontal and vertical differentials $\delta ,d$ commute. Give its total complex the differential $\delta +(-1)^ a d$; its square is zero because the two cross terms have opposite signs and $\delta d=d\delta $. If each augmented horizontal row $0\to A^ b\to D^{0,b}\to D^{1,b}\to \cdots $ is exact and its augmentation commutes with the vertical differential, then $A^\bullet \to \operatorname {Tot}D$ induces an isomorphism on cohomology. The analogous assertion holds for exact augmented vertical columns.

Proof. A total degree n has only $n+1$ components, so all the following eliminations are finite. For a total cocycle, its component at $(n,0)$ is horizontally closed, by the cocycle equation at $(n+1,0)$. If $n>0$, subtract the total differential of a horizontal preimage in $(n-1,0)$. This kills that component and changes only the component at $(n-1,1)$. The next cocycle equation makes this component horizontally closed. Repeat until only the component at $(0,n)$ remains. It is in the kernel of $\delta $, hence comes from a unique $a\in A^ n$; the vertical cocycle equation gives $da=0$. This proves surjectivity, also for $n=0$ without an elimination.

If the image of $a\in A^ n$ is the total differential of $b$ of degree $n-1$, its components with positive horizontal degree vanish. Start with the highest horizontal component of $b$. Its horizontal differential is zero; if that degree is positive, subtract the total differential of a horizontal preimage. This preserves the differential of $b$ and kills that component. Continue until $b$ lies in $(0,n-1)$; it is horizontally closed, hence comes from $a'\in A^{n-1}$, and $da'=a$ by injectivity of the augmentation. For $n=0$ there is no incoming boundary. This proves injectivity. Transposing the indices and multiplying the $(a,b)$-component by $(-1)^{ab}$ identifies the total differential with $d+(-1)^ b\delta $, proving the vertical assertion with its signs. All augmentations and conclusions commute with double-complex maps. $\square$

Theorem 10.43.16.678E4157C53F For every open cover there is a natural map $\check H^ r(\mathcal U,F)\to H^ r(X,F)$, injective in degree one. Consequently all refinement maps in degree one are injective. If every nonempty finite intersection of members of $\mathcal U$ is acyclic for $F$, this map is an isomorphism in every degree. Refinement maps on Čech cohomology are independent of the chosen refinement function. Consequently the direct limit $\check H^ r(X,F)$ over covers is defined and has a natural map to $H^ r(X,F)$. No equality of these two global theories for an arbitrary space is asserted here.

Proof. Use $D^{a,b}=\check C^ a(\mathcal U,C^ bF)$. Restriction commutes with the Godement differential. Its horizontal rows are exact after augmentation by $C^ bF(X)$, by the preceding Čech lemma. Thus lemma 10.43.15 gives $H^*(\operatorname {Tot}D)\simeq H^*(X,F)$. The inclusion of $\check C^ a(\mathcal U,F)$ in $D^{a,0}$ gives the natural map. If a degree-one Čech cocycle becomes a total boundary, it is the total differential of $b\in D^{0,0}$. Its $(0,1)$-component is zero, so $db=0$ and each component of $b$ is already a section of $F$. The original cocycle is therefore the Čech boundary $\delta b$. This proves degree-one injectivity; compatibility with refinement then proves injectivity of refinement maps in that degree. If the cover is acyclic, the vertical columns are exact after this augmentation: they are products of the complexes defining the cohomology on the intersections. Cohomology commutes with these products, because kernels are coordinatewise and a family of boundaries has a family of preimages by choice. The vertical comparison lemma proves the desired isomorphism.

If $V_ j\subset U_{\rho (j)}$, restriction defines a cochain map $\rho ^*$. For two choices $\rho ,\sigma $, set, in positive degree,

\[ (hc)_{j_0\ldots j_{r-1}} =\sum _{k=0}^{r-1}(-1)^ k c_{\rho (j_0)\ldots \rho (j_ k)\sigma (j_ k)\ldots \sigma (j_{r-1})}, \]

restricted to $V_{j_0}\cap \cdots \cap V_{j_{r-1}}$, and set $h=0$ in degree zero. In $\delta h+h\delta $, omission of an index strictly before or after the change from $\rho $ to $\sigma $ occurs twice with opposite signs. The remaining internal adjacent-change terms cancel between consecutive k; the two endpoints are $\sigma ^*c-\rho ^*c$. Thus the maps induce the same map on cohomology. The double-complex construction commutes with refinement and with coefficient maps, so its map to $H^ r(X,F)$ also does. Covers, taken as sets of open subsets, form a set directed by common intersection refinement. The usual filtered colimit of these groups and maps is therefore defined; the compatible comparison maps descend to it. $\square$
