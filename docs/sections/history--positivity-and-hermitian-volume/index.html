25.4 Positivity, Hermitian geometry, and volumeF9B38BEB0BCF

F9B38BEB0BCF

Local positive currents become global geometric objects through their cohomology classes. On a compact Kähler manifold, the $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm c}$-lemma identifies closed currents in a fixed real $(1,1)$-class with quasi-plurisubharmonic potentials, up to a constant on each connected component. This is the bridge from the local theory to cohomology, positivity, and geometric loci. Nefness, bigness, and pseudo-effectivity describe different kinds of positivity; their geometric loci record where positive representatives can or cannot be made regular.

Volumes and restricted positivity turn these analytic descriptions into numerical invariants. Boucksom's work relates positive currents, divisorial decompositions, and volumes; later results compare non-Kähler loci, null loci, and restricted volumes. Such comparisons depend on the class and on the subvarieties being tested. Pome keeps the hypotheses of these statements visible rather than treating all positivity loci as interchangeable. The theory of non-pluripolar mass explains how singular potentials enter their analytic definitions.

Hermitian geometry brings a different cohomological structure. Bott–Chern and Aeppli classes retain information lost when one uses only de Rham cohomology, and their pairing is natural for positivity questions. Gauduchon metrics satisfy $\partial\bar\partial\omega^{n-1}=0$, a weaker condition than the Kähler condition $\mathrm d\omega=0$. Gauduchon's existence theorem makes this condition available within a conformal class. Lamari's positivity criterion uses dual testing against Gauduchon forms; see Lamari. These ideas organize Hermitian geometry, torsion, and Gauduchon duality.

The absence of closedness changes the meaning of a volume. In the Kähler setting, Stokes' theorem fixes the mass of bounded potentials in a class. In the Hermitian setting, upper mass bounds and positive lower mass bounds are separate properties of a background. Work of Guedj–Lu and subsequent developments studies these bounds and their role in equations and singularity volumes. The distinction between a bound for each individual potential and a uniform bound over a family is essential. It is also why a theorem for a closed big class cannot be transferred verbatim to an arbitrary positive Hermitian form.

The chapter on Gauduchon variational volume develops an additional variational viewpoint using Gauduchon data and duality. It should be read alongside, rather than identified with, the cohomological volume of a closed class or the mass attached to an ordinary singularity type. The historical strands meet in their use of positivity and nonlinear measures, but their admissible objects and normalizations differ. Several results in the Hermitian contribution belong to a working research exposition; original references and precise source revisions remain under Sources.
