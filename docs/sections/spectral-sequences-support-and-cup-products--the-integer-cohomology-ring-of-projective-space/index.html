10.75 The integer cohomology ring of projective spaceD599A476FAC0

D599A476FAC0

Cohomology with integer coefficients means constant-sheaf cohomology, with the previously constructed cup product. Let $\Theta $ denote the multiplicative augmentation comparison from real sheaf cohomology to de Rham cohomology, and $\Psi _ q$ the successive positive connecting comparison in the opposite direction. We retain their exact relation

\[ \Theta _ q^{-1}=\epsilon _ q\Psi _ q,\qquad \epsilon _ q=(-1)^{q(q+1)/2}. \]

Thus the two conventions are distinguished even in even degree. The complex orientation is used for every projective subspace.

Lemma 10.75.1.419BAFBD0FA6 For $n\geq 0$ the integer cohomology groups of $\mathbb P^ n$ are

\[ H^ q(\mathbb P^ n,\underline{\mathbb Z})= \begin{cases} \mathbb Z,& q=0,2,\ldots ,2n,\\ 0,& \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

In particular they have no torsion. For $0\leq k\leq m\leq n$, restriction to a linear $\mathbb P^ m$ is an isomorphism in degree $2k$.

Proof. The assertion for $n=0$ is the cohomology of a point. Suppose $n\geq 1$ and let $H=\mathbb P^{n-1}$ be a hyperplane. Both spaces are compact Hausdorff manifolds, and the complement is the affine chart $\mathbb C^ n$. The compact closed subset $H$ is strongly paracompact in $\mathbb P^ n$: a cover near $H$ admits a finite shrinking there, which supplies the required locally finite ambient refinement. The precise relative/support comparison theorem 10.69.3 therefore gives

\[ H^ q(\mathbb P^ n,H;\underline{\mathbb Z}) =H_ c^ q(\mathbb C^ n,\underline{\mathbb Z}). \]

The complement is open and has compact closure in $\mathbb P^ n$, as required for this last identification. Apply integral cap duality theorem 10.71.5 to the oriented real $2n$-manifold $\mathbb C^ n$. Radial contraction and the proved singular prism identity give its homology $\mathbb Z$ in degree zero and zero elsewhere. Consequently the displayed relative group is $\mathbb Z$ for $q=2n$ and zero in every other degree. This is an integer calculation, not a conclusion from the real groups.

Use the pair long exact sequence lemma 10.69.2. In degrees $0\leq q\leq 2n-2$ restriction to $H$ is an isomorphism, since its adjacent relative terms vanish. In degree $2n-1$ the relative term and the corresponding group of $H$ vanish, so the group of $\mathbb P^ n$ vanishes. At the top the exact segment is

\[ 0=H^{2n-1}(H,\underline{\mathbb Z})\longrightarrow \mathbb Z \longrightarrow H^{2n}(\mathbb P^ n,\underline{\mathbb Z}) \longrightarrow H^{2n}(H,\underline{\mathbb Z})=0. \]

Thus this top group is exactly $\mathbb Z$, with no extension or torsion ambiguity. All higher groups vanish by the same sequence. Induction proves the group calculation. Iterating the restriction isomorphisms along a linear flag proves the final assertion; any specified linear subspace can be included in such a flag by extending a vector-space basis. $\square$

Theorem 10.75.2.0CD4D885B4B4 Let $h=c_1^{\mathbb Z}(\mathcal O(1))$ in the exponential and frame convention of theorem 10.40.2. Then

\[ H^*(\mathbb P^ n,\underline{\mathbb Z}) =\mathbb Z[h]/(h^{n+1}),\qquad |h|=2. \]

For every $0\leq k\leq n$, $h^ k$ is a primitive generator of degree $2k$. Restriction to a linear subspace of dimension at least $k$ preserves this generator. Writing $h_{\mathbb R}$ for its coefficient image and $\omega $ for the normalized Fubini–Study form, the comparison conventions give

\[ \Psi _{2k}[\omega ^ k]=h_{\mathbb R}^ k,\qquad \Theta _{2k}(h_{\mathbb R}^ k)=(-1)^ k[\omega ^ k]. \]

Accordingly, on a linear $\mathbb P^ k$ the cap-normalized integer trace sends $h^ k$ to $(-1)^ k$. Equivalently the generator $-h$ has positive Fubini–Study image under $\Theta $, and its top powers have complex-orientation trace $1$.

Proof. The line/frame classification defines $h$ integrally before any real comparison. By theorem 10.40.5, with its specified positive connecting convention,

\[ h_{\mathbb R}=\Psi _2[\omega ]. \]

The line here and its normalized curvature are the existing projective line and Fubini–Study objects. Naturality of the exponential sequence makes $h$ restrict to the same Chern class on every linear projective subspace.

The exact sign identity and multiplicativity in theorem 10.58.9, corollary 10.58.11 give $\Theta _2(h_{\mathbb R})=-[\omega ]$, and therefore $\Theta _{2k}(h_{\mathbb R}^ k)=(-1)^ k[\omega ^ k]$. Since $\epsilon _{2k}=(-1)^ k$, the first comparison formula follows as well, including $k=0$. No unsigned interchange of the two comparison maps is made.

On $\mathbb P^ k$, integral cap duality and the orientation trace corollary 10.72.4 identify top integer cohomology with $\mathbb Z$. This trace commutes with the coefficient map to $\mathbb R$. Its real version is integration in the multiplicative augmentation convention, by theorem 10.73.1. The previously proved normalization in theorem 10.50.3 is $\int _{\mathbb P^ k}\omega ^ k=1$. Hence

\[ \operatorname {tr}_{\mathbb P^ k}(h^ k)=(-1)^ k. \]

This is a unit in $\mathbb Z$, so $h^ k$ is primitive in that top integer group. The preceding lemma makes restriction from $H^{2k}(\mathbb P^ n,\underline{\mathbb Z})$ an isomorphism onto this group. It follows that $h^ k$ is primitive there also. This step uses the full integer computation, so no torsion class has been lost through a real comparison.

The graded ring map $\mathbb Z[t]\to H^*(\mathbb P^ n,\underline{\mathbb Z})$ sending $t$ to $h$ is surjective, since its powers give a basis in every nonzero degree. The power $h^{n+1}$ vanishes by degree. Every lower homogeneous power generates an infinite cyclic group, so no polynomial of degree at most $n$ lies in the kernel unless all its coefficients vanish. The kernel is therefore exactly $(t^{n+1})$. For $n=0$, $h=0$ and this is the ring $\mathbb Z$ of a point. Finally $\Theta _2(-h_{\mathbb R})=[\omega ]$, and multiplying the trace formula by $(-1)^ k$ proves the positive-generator assertion. $\square$
