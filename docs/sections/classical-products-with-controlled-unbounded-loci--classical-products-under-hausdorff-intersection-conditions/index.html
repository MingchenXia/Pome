7.30 Classical products under Hausdorff intersection conditions835411DBC290

835411DBC290

Write $\omega _0=dd^ c|z|^2$ in coordinates, with $dd^ c=\frac{i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial $. Trace mass and coefficient variation are equivalent on compact coordinate sets by coefficient measures and compactness of positive currents. The unbounded locus $L(u)$ is the complement of the open set where $u$ is locally bounded; it need not equal its pole set.

Lemma 7.30.1.162A0666C4BB For a smooth real function $\chi $ and a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $v$, put $B_\chi (v)=dd^ c(\chi v)-\chi \, dd^ cv$. On relatively compact coordinate balls, this is a finite sum of Hessians of bounded plurisubharmonic functions with smooth bounded coefficients. The bounds depend only on a bound for $v$ and on $\chi $. If $v_ j\downarrow v$ with uniformly bounded limits there, the plurisubharmonic functions in this representation can be chosen decreasing with $j$. The representation holds also after wedging with any already defined closed positive base current.

Proof. For nonnegative bounded plurisubharmonic $v$ and smooth nonnegative plurisubharmonic $\chi $, the identity is

DBD412CA248A 7.30.2
\begin{equation} B_\chi (v)=\tfrac 12dd^ c(v+\chi )^2-\tfrac 12dd^ cv^2 -\tfrac 12dd^ c\chi ^2-\chi \, dd^ cv. \end{equation}

It follows by expanding the square, first for smooth functions and then distributionally for bounded functions. The squares are plurisubharmonic: in the smooth case their Hessians are the sum of a nonnegative multiple of the original Hessian and a nonnegative gradient form; decreasing regularization proves the assertion in general. They have uniform bounds and preserve decreasing limits. Add a constant to $v$ to make it nonnegative; the correction to $B_\chi (v)$ is a constant multiple of $dd^ c\chi $. Write a general smooth $\chi $ as $\chi ^+-\chi ^-$, with both functions smooth, nonnegative and plurisubharmonic on the ball, by adding a sufficiently large multiple of $|z|^2$ and a constant. Linearity in $\chi $ proves the assertion. The bounded recursive product and its smooth-factor rules in locality and positivity of bounded Bedford–Taylor products justify the same identities against a base current. In particular, products of several such errors have bounded-current mass estimates by the bounded Chern–Levine–Nirenberg estimate, with no unbounded potential inserted into a bounded Chern–Levine–Nirenberg inequality. $\square$

Proposition 7.30.3.1AC7DC75B159 Let $S,A_1,\ldots ,A_ q$ be closed in a coordinate open set $U$, and let $1\leq q\leq p\leq n$. Suppose

E561BB270A1B 7.30.4
\begin{equation} \mathcal H^{2p-2m+1}(S\cap A_{j_1}\cap \cdots \cap A_{j_ m})=0 \quad (1\leq j_1<\cdots <j_ m\leq q). \end{equation}

For compact $L\subset \operatorname {int}K\Subset U$, there are neighborhoods $V_ j$ of $K\cap A_ j$ and a constant $C$ such that every closed positive current $T$ of bidimension $(p,p)$ supported in $S$ and all smooth plurisubharmonic $u_ j\leq 0$ near $K$ satisfy

1D4FB6D7DCBB
\begin{align} \| u_1dd^ cu_2\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cu_ q\wedge T\| _ L & \leq C\| T\| _ K\prod _{j=1}^ q\sup _{K\setminus V_ j}|u_ j|, \tag{7.30.5}\\ \| dd^ cu_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cu_ q\wedge T\| _ L & \leq C\| T\| _ K\prod _{j=1}^ q\sup _{K\setminus V_ j}|u_ j|. \tag{7.30.6} \end{align}

The constant may depend on $S$, the $A_ j$, the compact sets and the dimensions; it is independent of the potentials and the mass of $T$. The same estimates hold for bounded plurisubharmonic potentials, using the bounded products.

Proof. We prove the two estimates, denoted $a_ q,b_ q$, by induction on $q$. The assertion $b_0$ is trace domination and $a_0$ is unused. It suffices to work near each point of $L$ and use finitely many neighborhoods. All intermediate compact sets lie in $\operatorname {int}K$; the neighborhoods $V_ j$ will be shrunk finitely many times. Normalize the bounds outside $V_ j$ to at most one. For arbitrary bounds divide $u_ j$ by $\sup _{K\setminus V_ j}|u_ j|+\varepsilon $, and let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ at the end. This also handles zero bounds.

A bounded Hessian factor. Assume $a_{q-1},b_{q-1}$ are known. On a compact region where one Hessian potential, say $u_2$, has a uniform bound, the weighted estimate $a_ q$ follows from these two earlier estimates. To see this, cover that region by finitely many balls $B_0\Subset B_1$ where $u_2$ is bounded. The bounded collar construction gives a bounded plurisubharmonic $\widetilde u_2$ equal to $u_2$ near $B_0$ and to a fixed quadratic function on a collar of $\partial B_1$, with bounds depending only on the given bound and the balls. Choose $\zeta \geq 0$, equal to one on $B_0$, with compact support in $B_1$ and derivative support in that fixed quadratic collar. Then

\[ dd^ c(\zeta \widetilde u_2) =\zeta \, dd^ c\widetilde u_2+F, \]

where $F$ is a fixed smooth form with controlled coefficients. Put $R=dd^ cu_3\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cu_ q\wedge T$ and use a constant positive trace form $\tau $ of degree $(p-q+1,p-q+1)$. Integration by parts gives

\[ \int u_1dd^ c(\zeta \widetilde u_2)\wedge R\wedge \tau =\int \zeta \widetilde u_2\, dd^ cu_1\wedge R\wedge \tau . \]

The right side is bounded by $b_{q-1}$ for $u_1,u_3,\ldots ,u_ q$. The term with $u_1F$ on the left is bounded by $a_{q-1}$ for the same family. Since $-u_1$ and $dd^ c\widetilde u_2\wedge R\wedge \tau $ are nonnegative, this bounds its inner-ball integral and proves the assertion. It also applies when other Hessian potentials are bounded replacements: assign the empty closed set to each such potential. All required intersection conditions for the remaining potentials are inherited. The outer support of $\zeta $ is $B_1$, and its inner value-one region contains the target; no reversed support radii occur.

The projection cylinder. Translate the target point to zero; outside $S$ there is nothing to prove. Set $s=p-q$. If zero belongs to all $A_ j$, apply lemma 7.29.6 to $F=S\cap A_1\cap \cdots \cap A_ q$. Choose a sufficiently small vertical ball $B''\subset \mathbb C^{n-s}$ whose boundary at $z'=0$ misses $F$. Compactness and closedness give a base ball $B'\subset \mathbb C^ s$, a thin outer vertical corona and neighborhoods $W_ j$ of the $A_ j$ in the cylinder such that

AC44B2B308D3 7.30.7
\begin{equation} S\cap \overline W_1\cap \cdots \cap \overline W_ q \cap (\overline B'\times \text{outer vertical corona}) =\varnothing . \end{equation}

For example, neighborhoods of sufficiently small common distance from the closed $A_ j$ have this property; otherwise a convergent sequence in the compact corona would lie in $F$ in the limit. Choose $0\leq \chi _ j\leq 1$ supported in $W_ j$, equal to one near $A_ j$ in the closed cylinder and near zero. Their derivative supports in the cylinder are a positive distance from $A_ j$. If zero does not belong to some $A_ j$, first shrink the cylinder to be disjoint from that $A_ j$, and choose its $\chi _ j$ with small compact support in the interior, equal to one near zero; this gives the same compact support property without a projection argument.

Take $\chi (z')\geq 0$ compactly supported in $B'$ and equal to one near zero. The form $\alpha _ s=\chi (z')(dd^ c|z'|^2)^ s$ is closed, since its $z'$ degree is already maximal. The whole product of localized factors with $T\wedge \alpha _ s$ has compact support in $B'\times B''$, by (7.30.7). This applies to its $dd^ c$ primitive as well. Consequently

41488626314D 7.30.8
\begin{equation} \int dd^ c(\chi _1u_1)\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ c(\chi _ qu_ q) \wedge T\wedge \alpha _ s=0. \end{equation}

Proof of $b_ q$. Expand every localized Hessian as $\chi _ jdd^ cu_ j+B_{\chi _ j}(u_ j)$. The term with no error is nonnegative and equals the desired product near zero. For a term with $h\geq 1$ errors, restrict to their joint derivative support. Cover this compact set by finitely many balls on which the $h$ error potentials are uniformly bounded; shrink the $V_ j$ to avoid these neighborhoods. By lemma 7.30.1, the errors become finite sums of $h$ bounded plurisubharmonic Hessians with smooth coefficients. The remaining $q-h$ Hessians form a positive closed base current. Its mass is bounded by $b_{q-h}$, and the bounded Chern–Levine–Nirenberg estimate controls the extra $h$ factors. This bounds every error term, including terms with several derivatives of different cutoffs. Equation (7.30.8) bounds the main term.

Proof of $a_ q$. Let $h(z)=|z_{s+1}|^2$. Compactly supported distributional integration by parts gives

\begin{align*} & \int \chi _1u_1\, dd^ c(\chi _2u_2)\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ c(\chi _ qu_ q)\wedge T\wedge \alpha _ s\wedge dd^ ch\\ & =\int h\, dd^ c(\chi _1u_1)\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ c(\chi _ qu_ q)\wedge T\wedge \alpha _ s. \end{align*}

On the right, the main term is bounded by the just-proved $b_ q$; every error is bounded as in its proof. On the left, the term with no error is nonpositive and is the desired weighted product near zero. Every other term has a Hessian error with index at least two. The same local algebra expresses it with bounded Hessian potentials on its derivative support, leaving $u_1$ as the only weight. The bounded-factor argument above, using $a_{q-1},b_{q-1}$, bounds each of these terms. For $q=1$ there are no left errors. This proves the weighted bound against the positive form $\alpha _ s\wedge dd^ ch$ of degree $(p-q+1,p-q+1)$.

To recover trace bounds in both arguments, use finitely many good coordinate systems whose constant positive test forms span all real forms of the required degree. The decomposable positive generators span by strong and weak positivity of differential forms, and unitary generators suffice by Gram–Schmidt. Good unitary systems have full invariant measure by the sphere-avoidance lemma. They are dense: a nonempty open set of the compact unitary group has positive invariant measure, since finitely many of its translates cover the group. Thus a finite spanning family can be perturbed to good systems while remaining spanning. The cylinders and cutoffs may differ; intersect their finitely many inner neighborhoods and the resulting $V_ j$. Trace domination then gives both norm estimates near zero. Finite covering proves the proposition. Decreasing local convolution and the bounded Bedford–Taylor continuity theorem extend it to bounded potentials. All convolutions and cutoff supports are taken in buffered compact sets inside $K$; convolution dominates the original negative function and therefore preserves its outside-$V_ j$ bound on the sets used in the estimates. $\square$

Proof. Everything is local in coordinates; put $A_ j=L(u_ j)$ and $S=\operatorname {supp}T$. Work with negative potentials on a compact coordinate neighborhood. The preceding estimates apply uniformly to their bounded truncations, decreasing smooth regularizations and all decreasing approximants, because these dominate the limits and have the same outside-$V_ j$ lower bound.

Construction and integrability. Induct on the total number $q$ of potential slots, counting the weight as one slot. For $q=1$, the uniform weighted estimate and monotone convergence for the fixed trace of $T$ show $u_1\in L^1(T)$. Then $u_{1,j}T\to u_1T$ by dominated convergence, and $dd^ c(u_1T)$ is positive closed by bounded truncation. Suppose the theorem is proved with fewer than $q$ slots. Put $R=dd^ cu_2\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cu_ q\wedge T$, already positive closed by induction. The analogous $R_ N$ for simultaneous bounded truncations converge to $R$. For fixed $m$ and $N\geq m$,

\[ -\max \{ u_1,-m\} \leq -\max \{ u_1,-N\} . \]

The uniform weighted estimate bounds the latter weight against $R_ N$. For that fixed bounded lower semicontinuous weight, weak convergence and decreasing continuous majorants of upper semicontinuous functions give the limiting integral inequality. Then let $m\to \infty $ for the fixed limiting measure. This proves coefficient integrability and its uniform local bound. Define the new Hessian product by $dd^ c(u_1R)$. Bounded truncations of the first potential against this fixed $R$ converge in coefficient $L^1$, so positivity and closedness follow from the bounded recursion. A compact test cutoff and the weighted bound also control its trace mass. Thus every product used below is defined before its continuity is asserted. The same argument gives uniform weighted bounds for arbitrary decreasing families.

The local induction for continuity. Induct additionally on the number $m$ of limit functions unbounded near the point under consideration. When $m=0$ the bounded Bedford–Taylor theorem applies. Before treating $m>0$, replace each function bounded near the point, and all its approximants, by a global maximum with a fixed plurisubharmonic quadratic function on the working coordinate neighborhood. Choose that quadratic below the common lower bound near the point and above the common upper bound outside a slightly larger ball. The new functions agree with the old ones near the point, are bounded on the entire working neighborhood and are equal to the fixed smooth quadratic on an outer corona. These modifications preserve decreasing limits. Only the other $m$ functions can now be unbounded anywhere used in the argument. Their unbounded loci still satisfy the hypotheses.

Choose the modifications and cutoffs together. If a bounded slot is present, first fix a cylinder and make its quadratic modification in concentric balls compactly contained there; choose its cutoff with compact support and derivatives in that quadratic corona. This cutoff supplies the required compact support by itself. If every slot is unbounded, use the sphere-avoidance cylinder of the preceding proof. For any other bounded slot use the same construction. For the other functions derivatives are supported away from $A_ j$. Use the closed, positive test form

\[ \alpha =\chi (z')(dd^ c|z'|^2)^{p-q}\wedge dd^ c|z_{p-q+1}|^2. \]

All localized primitives have compact support relative to $S$. Smooth approximants of a bounded modified function can be chosen equal to its fixed quadratic on that derivative support, using the same smooth-maximum construction as in theorem 7.29.5.

We record precisely how derivative remainders pass to the limit. If a remainder contains $B_{\chi _ j}$ for one of the $m$ unbounded slots, its support has a neighborhood where that slot is uniformly bounded. The local representation (7.30.2) expresses it as bounded plurisubharmonic Hessians with smooth coefficients. On each such neighborhood at most $m-1$ slots remain unbounded, so the induction at this same $q$ applies. If the derivative belongs to a slot bounded from the outset, the error is a fixed smooth form, and removing it leaves fewer than $q$ potential slots. The induction on $q$ applies. For several errors use a finite partition on their joint supports and the same representation for every bounded error slot. The transformed potentials decrease and have empty local unbounded locus, so all intersection hypotheses are inherited from the remaining original slots. This proves convergence of every remainder, also with any mixture of fixed limits and decreasing approximants, and with sequential smooth regularization parameters.

Localized potentials $\chi _ j u_ j$ cause no additional product premise: near $A_ j$, $\chi _ j$ is constant, and elsewhere $u_ j$ is bounded. There they are finite differences of bounded plurisubharmonic functions by the same square decomposition. Their lower-slot products are therefore defined by expansion and are independent of the expansion by coefficient recursion and linearity. All lower-slot integration by parts below is legitimate for these closed signed currents as well.

Every weighted weak limit has the right upper bound. Write $R_ j=dd^ cu_{2,j}\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cu_{q,j}\wedge T$, so $R_ j\to R$ by the already-proved theorem with $q-1$ slots. The positive currents $-u_{1,j}R_ j$ have uniformly bounded local mass. Choose any subsequential weighted limit $W$. Fixed upper semicontinuous truncations of the weight and their decreasing continuous majorants, followed by fixed-measure monotone limits, give $W\leq u_1R$, exactly by decreasing continuous majorants of upper semicontinuous functions. Thus $D=u_1R-W$ is positive.

The reverse localized inequality. For a vector $v=(v_1,\ldots ,v_ q)$ define

\[ J(v)=\int \chi _1v_1\, dd^ c(\chi _2v_2)\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ c(\chi _ qv_ q)\wedge T\wedge \alpha . \]

Its main term is the expected weighted product times $\chi _1\cdots \chi _ q$; all other terms are the remainders just treated. Fix an approximant index $j$ and choose smooth plurisubharmonic majorants $w_ l\downarrow u_{l,j}$, with the fixed quadratic branch on bounded-slot derivative supports. Starting with $J(u)$, replace the weight $\chi _ lu_ l$ by $\chi _ lw_ l$ in turn, then interchange it with the next potential by integration by parts. The term in that replacement containing only $\chi _ i dd^ cv_ i$ is nonnegative, since $w_ l\geq u_ l$. Every other term contains a cutoff derivative. Hence after all $q$ replacements,

7103B94D137C 7.30.9
\begin{equation} J(u)\leq J(w)+\sum _ l|E_ l(w,u)|, \end{equation}

where each $E_ l$ is an integral with a derivative remainder and weight $w_ l-u_ l$; the other slots are fixed limits or the $w_ i$. The final all-smooth integral can be rotated back to weight slot one by ordinary integration by parts.

For completeness, each interchange has smooth first factor $\chi _ lw_ l$ and second factor $\chi _{l+1}u_{l+1}$, against the closed signed current containing $q-2$ other localized Hessians and $T\wedge \alpha $. The second factor is coefficient-integrable by the already-proved theorem with $q-1$ slots and the local difference description above. Its $dd^ c$ is also a defined lower-slot current. The primitive has compact support by the cylinder construction. Distributional integration by parts, testing with a compact cutoff equal to one there, proves the interchange. Thus no unproved unbounded product identity is used in (7.30.9).

Hold $w_1$ fixed and let the other smoothing parameters tend to zero. The $q-1$ localized Hessian factors in $J(w)$ converge by the unweighted theorem with $q-1$ slots, and the weight is smooth. Then let $w_1\downarrow u_{1,j}$: the signed lower-slot measure is fixed and its coefficient integrability follows from the main weighted bound and the remainder bounds, so dominated convergence applies. Consequently $J(w)\to J(u_{\bullet ,j})$. The remainder convergence proved above gives limits $e_{l,j}$ for $E_ l$ in the same order. As $j\to \infty $ they tend to zero: the two terms coming from $w_ l-u_ l$ have identical limit in every lower-$m$ or lower-$q$ remainder. Therefore

\[ J(u)\leq \liminf _ j J(u_{\bullet ,j}). \]

Expand both sides into their main term and remainders. Along the subsequence defining $W$, all remainders converge to the same values. It follows that

\[ \int \chi _1\cdots \chi _ q\, D\wedge \alpha \leq 0. \]

This is a nonnegative measure integral, and all cutoffs equal one near the target point. Use finitely many good unitary systems whose degree-$(p-q+1,p-q+1)$ forms span, as in the estimate proof. The trace of $D$ vanishes near that point, and coefficient domination gives $D=0$. Every subsequence has the same further limit, proving weighted convergence. Applying $dd^ c$ proves unweighted convergence with $q$ Hessian factors. Smooth approximation now gives symmetry; the coefficient recursion gives multilinearity, constants, locality and compatibility on overlaps. This completes both inductions. $\square$
