Lemma 13.31.1 (Absolutely continuous mass and non-pluripolar mass).C7894CAD8593 Let $T$ be a closed positive $(1,1)$-current on a complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$. Suppose that $\langle T^ n\rangle $ is locally finite. Write $T_{\mathrm{ac}}$ for the absolutely continuous part of its coefficient measures, with respect to local Lebesgue measure. Its coefficients form a measurable positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix almost everywhere. Then

\[ (T_{\mathrm{ac}})^ n\leq \langle T^ n\rangle \]

as measures. The left side means the pointwise exterior power of this measurable tensor; no closedness of $T_{\mathrm{ac}}$ is asserted. In particular, if $X$ is compact Kähler and $T\in \alpha $, then

\[ \int _ X(T_{\mathrm{ac}})^ n\leq \operatorname {Vol}(\alpha ), \]

where volume is the positive top intersection number defined by non-pluripolar mass.

Proof. We first record the small measure-theoretic extension of Lebesgue differentiation needed below. If $\mu $ is a locally finite complex Radon measure on a Euclidean domain and $\mu =f\, \mathrm dx+\mu _ s$ is its Lebesgue decomposition, then, for a smooth nonnegative compactly supported mollifier $\rho $ of integral one,

58A08DA1A586 13.31.2
\begin{equation} \mu *\rho _ r\longrightarrow f \quad \hbox{almost everywhere as }r\downarrow 0. \end{equation}

This is local, so multiply $\mu $ by a compactly supported cutoff equal to one on a neighborhood of the compact set under consideration. It is enough to treat a finite measure on $\mathbb R^ d$. The assertion for $f\, \mathrm dx$ is Lebesgue differentiation.

Here are the details for its singular part. For a finite positive measure $\nu $, let

\[ M\nu (x)=\sup _{r>0}\frac{\nu (Q(x,r))}{|Q(x,r)|}, \qquad Q(x,r)=x+(-r,r)^ d. \]

The cube-selection argument in the proof of Lebesgue differentiation applies with $\nu (Q)$ in place of $\int _ Q|f|$: for a compact subset of $\{ M\nu >t\} $, choose finitely many witnessing cubes, retain disjoint cubes by decreasing side length, and cover the original cubes by their threefold enlargements. It gives

\[ |\{ M\nu >t\} |\leq \frac{3^ d}{t}\nu (\mathbb R^ d). \]

For completeness, $x\mapsto \nu (Q(x,r))$ is lower semicontinuous by Fatou's lemma, since the cubes are open; thus the superlevel set above is open, and inner regularity justifies the compact-subset reduction. If $\rho $ is supported in the unit ball, then $|\mu _ s*\rho _ r|\leq C_\rho M|\mu _ s|$. Choose a Lebesgue-null Borel carrier $N$ of $|\mu _ s|$ and a compact set $K\subset N$ with $|\mu _ s|(\mathbb R^ d\setminus K)<\eta $. At every $x\notin K$, the convolution of $\mathbf1_ K\mu _ s$ is zero for all sufficiently small $r$. Consequently

\[ \big |\{ x\notin K:\limsup _{r\downarrow 0}|\mu _ s*\rho _ r(x)|>t\} \big | \leq \frac{3^ dC_\rho \eta }{t}. \]

Since $K$ is null, this bounds the measure of the whole limsup set. Letting $\eta \downarrow 0$ and then taking rational $t>0$ proves (13.31.2). Real and imaginary parts, or total variation domination, treat complex measures.

Work now in a coordinate ball and write $T=dd^ cu$ with $u$ plurisubharmonic, using local potentials of closed positive currents. First suppose that $u$ is locally bounded. On each smaller ball its radial regularizations $u_ r=u*\rho _ r$ are smooth plurisubharmonic and decrease to $u$ as $r\downarrow 0$ by smooth plurisubharmonicity and radial regularization. Distributional differentiation commutes with convolution, so

\[ dd^ cu_ r=(dd^ cu)*\rho _ r. \]

Apply (13.31.2) to the finitely many coefficient measures. The Hermitian matrices of $dd^ cu_ r$ converge almost everywhere to that of $T_{\mathrm{ac}}$. Positivity is preserved by this limit. For a nonnegative smooth compactly supported function $h$, Fatou's lemma and the continuity of the determinant give

\[ \int h(T_{\mathrm{ac}})^ n \leq \liminf _{r\downarrow 0}\int h(dd^ cu_ r)^ n =\int h(dd^ cu)^ n. \]

The equality follows from decreasing continuity of Bedford–Taylor products, for example along any sequence $r\downarrow 0$. Both sides use the same exterior-power convention, so the coordinate factor $n!$ and the fixed normalization of $dd^ c$ occur on both sides. This proves the desired measure inequality for locally bounded potentials.

For general $u$, shrink the ball so that $u\leq 0$ and put

\[ u_ j=\max \{ u,-j\} ,\qquad E_ j=\{ u>-j\} ,\qquad S_ j=\mathbf1_{E_ j}dd^ cu_ j,\qquad \nu _ j=\mathbf1_{E_ j}(dd^ cu_ j)^ n. \]

By increasing non-pluripolar truncations the positive currents $S_ j$ increase to $\langle T\rangle $; by the first-degree non-pluripolar restriction,

\[ \langle T\rangle =\mathbf1_{\{ u>-\infty \} }T. \]

The pole set of a plurisubharmonic function has Lebesgue measure zero, so the absolutely continuous part of this limit is $T_{\mathrm{ac}}$. Write $A_ j$ for the Hermitian density matrix of $(S_ j)_{\mathrm{ac}}$. Then

\[ 0\leq A_ j\uparrow A \quad \hbox{almost everywhere}, \]

where $A$ is the density matrix of $T_{\mathrm{ac}}$. To justify the last identification explicitly, decompose the positive increments $S_1,S_2-S_1,S_3-S_2,\ldots $ into absolutely continuous and singular coefficient measures on a relatively compact ball. Their positive trace measures have summable total mass, bounded by the trace mass of $T$. The singular parts of their sum are carried by a countable union of Lebesgue-null carriers. Thus the absolutely continuous part of their sum is the sum of their absolutely continuous parts. Positivity of all increments, tested on the countable set of rational vectors, gives the asserted simultaneous matrix order and convergence almost everywhere. Multiplication by a Borel indicator commutes with taking the absolutely continuous part, so $A_ j=\mathbf1_{E_ j}(dd^ cu_ j)_{\mathrm{ac}}$ as density matrices.

The bounded-potential case, restricted to $E_ j$, therefore gives

\[ \nu _ j\geq \det (A_ j)\, \mathrm dV_*, \]

where $\mathrm dV_*$ denotes the fixed positive coordinate volume factor for the top exterior power. Determinants are continuous and increasing on the cone of positive semidefinite Hermitian matrices. The measures $\nu _ j$ increase to $\langle T^ n\rangle $ by increasing non-pluripolar truncations. Monotone convergence now yields

\[ \langle T^ n\rangle \geq \det (A)\, \mathrm dV_*=(T_{\mathrm{ac}})^ n. \]

The inequality is invariant under holomorphic coordinate changes and hence holds globally. On a compact Kähler manifold all these non-pluripolar products are locally finite; integrating the inequality and applying the positive mixed-mass bound with all classes equal to $\alpha $ proves the last assertion, with volume interpreted through positive mixed intersection numbers. $\square$

Corollary 13.31.3 (The lower determinant-volume comparison).1E1CF458220D For every big class $\alpha $,

\[ \sup _{T\in \alpha ,\ T\geq 0}\int _ X(T_{\rm ac})^ n \geq \operatorname {Vol}(\alpha ). \]

Proof. For a same-class approximation put $T_ j=(\pi _ j)_*(\kappa _ j+[E_ j])$. This is closed positive in $\alpha $. Outside the exceptional image and the image of $E_ j$, analytic sets of Lebesgue measure zero, it is the smooth transform of $\kappa _ j$. Consequently its absolutely continuous tensor there is that smooth tensor. Change of variables on the isomorphism locus gives

\[ \int _ X((T_ j)_{\rm ac})^ n=\int _{Y_ j}\kappa _ j^ n. \]

Deleting the excluded analytic sets changes neither integral of these nonnegative densities. The right-hand side tends to $\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha )$, proving the claim. $\square$

Corollary 13.31.4.04768594F11F For every pseudo-effective class $\alpha \in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$,

\[ \operatorname {Vol}(\alpha ) =\sup _{\substack {T\in \alpha \\ T\geq 0}} \int _ X(T_{\mathrm{ac}})^ n, \]

where the supremum is over closed positive $(1,1)$-currents. Both sides are zero if $\alpha $ is not big. If class volume and the empty supremum are assigned the value zero outside the pseudo-effective cone, the equality holds for every real $(1,1)$-class.

Proof. For a big class, corollary 13.31.3 gives the lower bound for the supremum, and lemma 13.31.1 gives the upper bound for each closed positive current in the class. If $\alpha $ is pseudo-effective but not big, then $\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha )=0$ by bigness and positive non-pluripolar volume. The same domination lemma bounds every integral in the supremum by zero. Such currents exist by pseudo-effectivity, and their absolutely continuous determinants are nonnegative, so the supremum is zero. Outside the pseudo-effective cone there is no closed positive current in the class, and the asserted value is the specified convention. $\square$
