10.35 Grassmannians and finite generating spacesB7996859E455

B7996859E455

Write $\operatorname {Gr}_ r(V)$ for the subspaces of codimension $r$ in a complex vector space $V$ of dimension $d$, where $1\leq r\leq d$. Its compact topology is the existing topology of orthogonal projectors; it is independent of the auxiliary Hermitian inner product.

Proposition 10.35.1.C0EB2A25CDC8 The space $\operatorname {Gr}_ r(V)$ is a compact connected complex manifold of dimension $r(d-r)$. Its tautological sequence is

\[ 0\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow \underline V\longrightarrow Q \longrightarrow 0,\qquad S_ a=a,\quad Q_ a=V/a. \]

There are natural holomorphic identifications

\[ T\operatorname {Gr}_ r(V)=\operatorname {Hom}(S,Q),\qquad H^0(\operatorname {Gr}_ r(V),Q)=V. \]

The Plücker map $j:\operatorname {Gr}_ r(V)\to \mathbb P(\Lambda ^ rV^*)$ is a holomorphic embedding and $\det Q=j^*\mathcal O(1)$.

Proof. For a decomposition $V=A\oplus W$, with $\dim W=r$, subspaces transverse to $W$ are graphs of matrices $Z:A\to W$. This set is open in the projector topology: invertibility of the projection onto $A$ is an open matrix condition. Its graph matrix and inverse depend continuously on the projector. On overlapping charts a graph basis is changed by an invertible block matrix, so the new graph matrix is obtained by matrix multiplication and inversion. These operations are holomorphic where the block is invertible. The charts therefore give the asserted complex structure and dimension. The graph vectors give a holomorphic frame of $S$; the images of a fixed basis of $W$ give a holomorphic frame of $Q$. Compactness is the closed bounded projector model. Connectedness follows from transitivity of the unitary group and its path connectedness: diagonalizing a unitary matrix gives the path obtained by replacing its eigenvalues $e^{i\theta _ l}$ by $e^{it\theta _ l}$. The finite-dimensional diagonalization follows by taking an eigenvector and its invariant orthogonal complement successively.

A curve of graphs through $a$ has derivative a map $a\to V/a$. Changing the complement changes a lift by a map into $a$, so this identification is intrinsic. In graph charts it is holomorphic and fiberwise an isomorphism, proving the tangent-bundle identity.

For Plücker, choose independent forms $\xi _1,\ldots ,\xi _ r$ annihilating $a$ and set $j(a)=[\xi _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \xi _ r]$. Changing the basis multiplies the wedge by a nonzero scalar. Its contraction kernel is exactly $a$, proving injectivity. In the graph chart above take the rows of the quotient map $(v_ A,v_ W)\mapsto v_ W-Zv_ A$. Their wedge has its $W$ coordinate equal to one, and its coordinates with one $W$ index replaced by an $A$ index are the entries of $Z$, with fixed signs. Thus the map is holomorphic and these coordinate ratios recover every graph coordinate; its differential is injective. Compactness makes the continuous injection a homeomorphism onto its image. These same coordinate ratios give its local holomorphic inverse on that image, proving embedding. The pairing

\[ (\bar v_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \bar v_ r, \xi _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \xi _ r) \longmapsto \det (\xi _ l(v_ k)) \]

identifies $\det (V/a)$ with the dual of its annihilator wedge line. It is independent of the lifts $v_ k$ and varies holomorphically, proving the determinant assertion with the indicated dual-projective convention.

It remains to prove the global-section assertion, rather than infer it from particular projective subspaces. For $\sigma \in H^0(Q)$ and $\xi \in V^*\setminus \{ 0\} $ consider $\xi (\sigma (a))$ for $a\subset \ker \xi $ of dimension $d-r$. The allowed $a$ form the compact connected Grassmannian $\operatorname {Gr}(d-r,\ker \xi )$, including its point cases. This scalar is holomorphic in $a$, hence constant by the local maximum principle on a compact connected complex manifold. Denote it $F(\xi )$. It is holomorphic in $\xi $: locally choose $v_0$ with $\xi (v_0)\ne 0$, and map fixed independent vectors in $\ker \xi _0$ to $v-\xi (v)v_0/\xi (v_0)$. Any fixed $d-r$ of them remain independent near $\xi _0$ and give a holomorphic choice of $a\subset \ker \xi $. The empty choice handles $d=r$. Moreover $F(t\xi )=tF(\xi )$ for $t\ne 0$. Continuity on the compact unit sphere gives $|F(\xi )|\leq C|\xi |$, so its singularity at zero is removable by the existing bounded holomorphic removal theorem bounded holomorphic removal and connected complements (or one-variable removal when $d=1$). Its Taylor expansion and homogeneity show $F(\xi )=\xi (v)$ for one $v\in V$. For every $a$ and every $\xi $ annihilating $a$, $\xi (\sigma (a))=\xi (v)$; hence $\sigma (a)=v\bmod a$. Conversely these constant-vector quotient sections are holomorphic. Their common kernel is $\bigcap _ a a=0$, proving the claimed isomorphism. All assertions also cover $r=d$, when the Grassmannian is one point. $\square$

Lemma 10.35.2.F9789093E482 Let $X$ be a countable-basis complex manifold and $E$ a holomorphic bundle. Let $B$ be a countable-basis complex manifold of dimension $b$, and let $A_ x:H^0(X,E)\to F_ x$ be surjective linear maps into a holomorphic bundle of rank $R\geq 1$ on $B$. Suppose evaluation of each section is holomorphic in $x\in B$, and the induced map of section spaces is continuous for compact convergence. If $p\geq b+R$, some $p$ sections span every $F_ x$ under these maps. The same $p$ sections can be required to do this for finitely or countably many such families satisfying their respective bounds.

Proof. The space of holomorphic sections with compact convergence is Fréchet. Countably many coefficient suprema in compact local frames define its topology. A Cauchy sequence has locally uniform coefficient limits, holomorphic by the Cauchy formula. The limits agree on overlaps and form a global section. Thus its $p$-fold product is a complete metrizable vector space. Use the proved Baire nested-ball argument in proposition 2.10.3.

For a compact $K\subset B$, tuples failing to span at some point of $K$ form a closed set. In local frames failure is vanishing of all $R$-minors; the sum of their squared norms has positive minimum on $K$ precisely for a spanning tuple. Uniform convergence on $K$ proves the closed assertion. We prove this closed set has empty interior. By pointwise surjectivity and compactness choose finitely many sections $t_1,\ldots ,t_ N$ whose images span on a neighborhood $O$ of $K$. Perturb a given tuple by

\[ s_ j+\sum _{l=1}^ N\lambda _{jl}t_ l,\qquad 1\leq j\leq p. \]

The map from $O\times \mathbb C^{pN}$ to the $p$-fold fiber bundle of $F$ is a holomorphic submersion: its parameter derivative is onto. Concretely, invert a nonzero $R$-minor of the $t_ l$ evaluations; the corresponding $R$ parameters per column may be replaced by its fiber value, leaving the other parameters as coordinates.

Rank-$k$ matrices in $\operatorname {Hom}(\mathbb C^ p,\mathbb C^ R)$ are smooth of complex codimension $(R-k)(p-k)$: after an invertible $k$-minor, the lower-right block is the product of the lower-left block, the inverse minor, and the upper-right block. This gives exactly $(R-k)(p-k)$ independent equations, also for $k=0$. Their inverse images under the displayed submersion therefore have dimension at most $b+pN-(p-R+1)\leq pN-1$ for $k<R$. Their parameter projections have Lebesgue measure zero. Here no Sard theorem is needed: on a compact coordinate box a smooth map from real dimension $a$ to $c>a$ is Lipschitz. Cover its domain by $O(\delta ^{-a})$ cubes of side $\delta $; the images fit in that many balls of radius $C\delta $, with total outer volume $O(\delta ^{c-a})\to 0$. Countably many boxes cover each stratum. Consequently arbitrarily small parameters avoid every bad rank on $O$. These finite-section perturbations tend to the given tuple in the section topology, proving density of good tuples on $K$.

Take a countable compact exhaustion of each $B$. The resulting bad sets, for all specified families and all exhaustion members, are a countable union of closed sets with empty interior. Baire supplies a tuple outside their union, proving the lemma. $\square$

Theorem 10.35.3.CD7786C05633 Let $E$ have rank $r\geq 1$ on a countable-basis complex $n$-manifold. If $E$ is globally generated, some subspace $V\subset H^0(X,E)$ with $\dim V\leq n+r$ generates it everywhere. If every first-jet map and every distinct-pair evaluation of global sections is onto, one may choose a single $V$ surjecting onto all of them with

\[ \dim V\leq \max \{ nr+n+r,\, 2(n+r)\} . \]

These statements do not require compactness of $X$.

Proof. Apply lemma 10.35.2 first to evaluation over $X$, of rank $r$ and base dimension $n$. For the second assertion the first-jet bundle has rank $r(n+1)$: in local coordinates/frame a first jet is a value and its $n$ first derivatives, whose transition matrix has holomorphic entries by the chain and frame-change rules. Its evaluation depends holomorphically on the base and continuously on sections by Cauchy's derivative formula. Apply the lemma simultaneously to that bundle over $X$, and to $\operatorname {pr}_1^*E\oplus \operatorname {pr}_2^*E$ over $X\times X\setminus \Delta $, of rank $2r$ and dimension $2n$. Their required bounds are exactly the two numbers displayed. Taking the span of the selected tuple gives $V$; linear dependencies only reduce its dimension. Empty bases impose no condition. $\square$

Proposition 10.35.4.0CE84812A13F For any finite generating space $V\subset H^0(X,E)$ the map $\psi _ V(x)=\ker (V\to E_ x)$ is holomorphic to $\operatorname {Gr}_ r(V)$, and $E=\psi _ V^*Q$ canonically. Every globally generated bundle has a smooth Griffiths semipositive metric whose dual is Nakano seminegative. A line bundle with a globally generated positive tensor power has a smooth semipositive metric. If every first jet of $E$ is generated, the metric can instead be chosen with Nakano negative dual, and is Griffiths positive. In particular an ample line bundle has a smooth positive metric.

If $V$ also generates every first jet and every distinct pair, $\psi _ V$ is an injective immersion. It is an embedding when $X$ is compact (or when this map is proper). For a line bundle the converse holds: an injective immersive $\psi _ V$ makes all those evaluation maps onto.

Proof. Write evaluation as a full-rank holomorphic matrix $B(x)$ in a local frame. Inverting an $r$-minor expresses its kernel as a graph holomorphically, proving the map assertion. Evaluation identifies $V/\ker B(x)$ with $E_ x$, giving the bundle isomorphism. Give $V$ an inner product and use its quotient metric on $E$. The checked subbundle/quotient curvature formula proposition 10.34.9 for a flat ambient bundle gives Griffiths nonnegativity of the quotient. Its dual is a holomorphic subbundle of the flat dual, whose Nakano curvature is the negative Gram form $-\left|\sum _ jB_ jv_ j\right|^2$; hence is Nakano seminegative. These assertions use that exact formula, without assuming duality preserves Nakano positivity. For a line bundle a positive tensor power's quotient metric has the unique positive metric root; curvature divides by that tensor exponent, proving its metric claim.

For the strict assertion choose a finite first-jet generating space by lemma 10.35.2. At $x$, differentiation of evaluation on its kernel is a surjection

\[ S_ x\longrightarrow T_ x^*X\otimes E_ x,\qquad s\longmapsto d_ xs. \]

Its dual $T_ xX\otimes E_ x^*\to S_ x^*$ is injective. It is, up to a sign, the second fundamental form of $E^*\subset \underline{V^*}$: differentiating the identically zero pairing of a local dual section with a local kernel section gives that identification. The flat subbundle curvature on a tensor $u$ is therefore exactly minus the squared norm of this dual map applied to $u$. It is strictly negative for $u\ne 0$. Rank-one tests and the dual curvature transpose then give Griffiths positivity of $E$. For an ample line choose any very ample positive tensor power and take its metric root as above.

Onto pair evaluations prevent equal kernels at distinct points: a common kernel would force the joint evaluation image to have dimension $r$, instead of $2r$. For $v\in T_ xX$, the derivative of the kernel map is, under $Q_ x=E_ x$, the map $s\mapsto -\partial _ v(Bs)$ for $s\in \ker B(x)$. This follows by differentiating $B(x(t))s(t)=0$ in graph coordinates. If $v\ne 0$, first-jet surjectivity gives a section with value zero and nonzero derivative along $v$. The derivative of $\psi _ V$ is therefore injective. Compactness, or properness, makes an injective immersion a topological embedding; local holomorphic inverse charts then give the holomorphic embedding assertion. For rank one, $Q=\mathcal O(1)$ on the dual projective space. Linear homogeneous forms separate pairs there and generate every first jet: in an affine chart their ratios include the constant1 and all affine coordinates. Restriction along an injective immersion preserves pair separation and makes their derivative covectors span $T_ x^*X$. Pulling back these linear sections gives exactly the claimed surjections from $V$, proving the converse. $\square$

Example 10.35.5.75477746633A The line bundle $\det Q$ on $\operatorname {Gr}_ r(V)$ is very ample in the first-jet/pair sense. The quotient $Q$ is not very ample for $2\leq r<d$; for $r=d$ it is very ample on the one-point base.

Proof. Pullback of the preceding linear projective sections along the Plücker embedding generates all required jets and pairs of $\det Q$. For $Q$, the preceding global-section computation gives dimension $d$, whereas its first-jet fiber has dimension $r(1+r(d-r))$. For $d-r=k\geq 1$, their difference is $k(r^2-1)>0$ if $r\geq 2$, so evaluation cannot be onto. If $r=d$ the base is a point, the jet fiber equals $Q=V$, and there are no distinct pairs. $\square$

Example 10.35.6.384A806754ED On $X=\mathbb C$ the trivial line bundle and $V=\operatorname {span}\{ 1,e^ z,e^{\sqrt2z}\} $ generate every first jet and every distinct pair. The classifying map $z\mapsto [1:e^ z:e^{\sqrt2z}]$ is an injective immersion which is not a topological embedding.

Proof. The first two sections already span each value/derivative jet. Equality of projective images gives a period $2\pi i k$ with both $k$ and $\sqrt2k$ integers, forcing $k=0$. Thus distinct evaluation rows are independent, proving the pair assertion; the derivative never vanishes. Pigeonhole subdivision of $[0,1)$ into $N$ equal intervals applied to the $N+1$ fractional parts of $0,\sqrt2,\ldots , N\sqrt2$ gives $1\leq k_ N\leq N$ with distance of $\sqrt2k_ N$ to an integer less than $1/N$. Such integers have an unbounded subsequence, since $\sqrt2$ is irrational. Along it the images of $2\pi i k_ N$ tend to $[1:1:1]$, the image of zero, while the parameters do not tend to zero. The inverse on the image is not continuous. The missing compactness/properness qualification is therefore necessary in the general embedding assertion. $\square$
