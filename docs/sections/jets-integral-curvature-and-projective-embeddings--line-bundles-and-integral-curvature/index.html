10.40 Line bundles and integral curvature8B611D824BF4

8B611D824BF4

Manifolds are Hausdorff, second countable and without boundary. A constant sheaf is denoted by an underline. Cohomology of sheaves means the Godement cohomology already constructed. For the de Rham comparison write $\Psi _ q:H^ q_{\rm dR}(M,\mathbb R)\to H^ q(M,\underline{\mathbb R})$ for the successive positive resolution connecting maps in theorem 10.43.8, corollary 10.43.11. The same convention applies to complex coefficients. No singular-cohomology comparison is needed here. We use

\[ dd^ c=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial , \qquad c_1(L,h)=-dd^ c\log |e|_ h^2 =\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\Theta (L,h) \]

in a holomorphic frame $e$.

Lemma 10.40.1.E84E5EF6C697 For every sheaf $F$ of abelian groups, the natural map $\check H^1(X,F)\to H^1(X,F)$ is an isomorphism. Here the left side is the direct limit over open covers. For a short exact sheaf sequence $0\to F\to B\to Q\to 0$, a section $q\in Q(X)$ and local lifts $a_ i\in B(U_ i)$, the cocycle $a_ j-a_ i$ represents the connecting class $\partial q\in H^1(X,F)$ under this isomorphism.

Proof. Use the Godement complex $C^\bullet F$ and the total-complex convention $\delta +(-1)^ a d_ G$ of theorem 10.43.16, where $a$ is the Čech degree. Given a global degree-one cocycle $z$, sheaf exactness gives a cover and $b_ i\in C^0F(U_ i)$ with $d_ Gb_ i=z$. On overlaps $b_ i-b_ j$ lies in $\ker d_ G=F$. The total differential of $b=(b_ i)$ is $(-(b_ i-b_ j),z)$, so this Čech cocycle maps to $[z]$. This proves surjectivity; injectivity, including compatibility with refinements, is the cited theorem.

For the last assertion choose a global Godement lift $A\in C^0B(X)$ of the augmentation of $q$; its existence follows from exactness of $\Gamma (X,C^0-)$. The global section $d_ GA$ comes from a unique $z\in C^1F(X)$ and represents $\partial q$. The differences $v_ i=a_ i-A$ lie in $C^0F(U_ i)$ and satisfy $d_ Gv_ i=-z$, while $\delta v=(a_ j-a_ i)$. Thus the total differential of $v$ is $((a_ j-a_ i),-z)$, proving the asserted sign. The same proof uses multiplicative notation for abelian multiplicative sheaves. $\square$

Theorem 10.40.2.EA66BB854401 The tensor-product group $\operatorname {Pic}(X)$ of holomorphic line bundles on a complex manifold $X$ is naturally $H^1(X,\mathcal O_ X^*)$. With the frame convention

000537B2987D 10.40.3
\begin{equation} e_ i=g_{ij}e_ j,\qquad g_{ij}g_{jk}=g_{ik}, \end{equation}

the identification sends a line bundle to the class of $(g_{ij})$. The exponential sheaf sequence

\[ 0\longrightarrow \underline{\mathbb Z}\longrightarrow \mathcal O_ X \xrightarrow {\ f\mapsto \exp (2\pi \mathrm i f)\ } \mathcal O_ X^*\longrightarrow 1 \]

is exact. Its long exact sequence contains

4C4BC52B37E3 10.40.4
\begin{equation} H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})\longrightarrow H^1(X,\mathcal O_ X) \longrightarrow \operatorname {Pic}(X) \xrightarrow {\ c_1^{\mathbb Z}\ }H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb Z}) \longrightarrow H^2(X,\mathcal O_ X), \end{equation}

where $c_1^{\mathbb Z}$ is the connecting map. The group of smooth complex line bundles is likewise $H^1(X,\mathcal A_{\mathbb C}^*)$, and its exponential connecting map is an isomorphism onto $H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})$; here $\mathcal A_{\mathbb C}$ is the sheaf of smooth complex functions. Equivalently smooth Hermitian line bundles are classified by the same group, using smooth circle-valued transition functions. These smooth classification assertions hold on any real smooth manifold as well.

Proof. Holomorphic frames give the cocycle in (10.40.3). Replacing $e_ i$ by $a_ i e_ i$ changes it to $a_ i g_{ij}a_ j^{-1}$, hence by a coboundary. Conversely a cocycle glues the trivial lines by identifying $z e_ i$ with $z g_{ij}e_ j$. The cocycle identity makes this an equivalence relation, and the trivializations give the local bundle charts. A change by a coboundary gives an isomorphism by the same frame change. Any bundle isomorphism has this local form. Passing to a common refinement treats different covers, and tensor products and duals multiply and invert cocycles. Apply lemma 10.40.1. The proof works identically for smooth nonzero or circle-valued functions. A smooth line bundle has a smooth metric by the partition construction of smooth line-bundle metrics and curvature changes, whose partition formula uses only smooth transition functions. Dividing its local frames by their positive lengths makes its transitions circle-valued. Conversely the Euclidean metrics glue for circle-valued transitions. Rescaling a metric by the square of a positive smooth function gives a unitary isomorphism, so its choice does not change the classification.

A nowhere zero holomorphic germ has a holomorphic logarithm after shrinking its domain: divide by its value at the center and use the convergent power series for $\log (1+w)$ on $|w|<1$. The exponential kernel consists exactly of locally constant integers. This proves sheaf exactness; it does not assert a logarithm on each whole overlap. The sheaf long exact sequence gives (10.40.4). The identical local argument gives the smooth complex exponential sequence. Its additive middle sheaf is a smooth-function module and is acyclic by proposition 10.43.10; its $H^1$ and $H^2$ vanish, so its connecting map is an isomorphism. Alternatively use $0\to \underline{\mathbb Z}\to \mathcal A_{\mathbb R} \to \mathcal T\to 1$, where $\mathcal T$ is the sheaf of smooth circle-valued functions. The inclusion of the holomorphic sequence in the smooth complex sequence, and the morphism from either to the circle sequence given by $f\mapsto \operatorname {Re}f$ and $g\mapsto g/|g|$, are the identity on $\underline{\mathbb Z}$. Naturality therefore identifies all the connecting classes of the same underlying line bundle. $\square$

Theorem 10.40.5.F699EBC79AEB Let $L$ be a smooth Hermitian line bundle on a smooth manifold $M$, and let $D$ be a compatible connection, with curvature $\Theta (D)$. Its real coefficient class satisfies

\[ c_1^{\mathbb Z}(L)_{\mathbb R} =\Psi _2\! \left(\left[\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\Theta (D)\right]\right) \quad \hbox{in }H^2(M,\underline{\mathbb R}). \]

This is independent of the compatible connection. For a holomorphic line bundle with a smooth metric, it is the de Rham class of the canonical form $c_1(L,h)$ in the opening convention.

Proof. In unit frames $u_ i=t_{ij}u_ j$ the connection is $d+A_ i$, where $A_ i$ is imaginary-valued and

\[ A_ i-A_ j=t_{ij}^{-1}dt_{ij}. \]

Compatible connections exist: for a subordinate smooth partition $(\rho _ l)$ set $A_ i=\sum _ l\rho _ l t_{il}^{-1}dt_{il}$ on $U_ i$, extending each summand by zero from $U_ i\cap U_ l$. The supports of $\rho _ l$ make these extensions smooth and the sum locally finite. The cocycle identity gives exactly the displayed transformation law. Then $\Theta =dA_ i$ glues and is closed and imaginary-valued. The difference of two compatible connections is a global imaginary one-form, so their normalized curvatures differ by an exact real form.

Write $\mathcal Z^ r_{\mathbb R}$ for the sheaf of closed smooth real $r$-forms. The logarithmic derivative gives a commutative diagram of exact sheaf sequences

\[ \begin{array}{ccccccccc} 0& \to & \underline{\mathbb Z}& \to & \mathcal A_{\mathbb R} & \xrightarrow {\exp (2\pi \mathrm i\, \cdot )}& \mathcal T& \to & 1\\ & & \downarrow & & \Vert & & \downarrow \ell \\ 0& \to & \underline{\mathbb R}& \to & \mathcal A_{\mathbb R} & \xrightarrow {d}& \mathcal Z^1_{\mathbb R}& \to & 0, \end{array} \qquad \ell (t)=\frac{t^{-1}dt}{2\pi \mathrm i}. \]

Local logarithms and the real Poincaré lemma prove the exactness and that $\ell (t)$ is a closed real form. In the sequence $0\to \mathcal Z^1_{\mathbb R}\to \mathcal A^1_{\mathbb R} \xrightarrow {d}\mathcal Z^2_{\mathbb R}\to 0$, put

\[ b_ i=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }A_ i,\qquad \omega =db_ i=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\Theta (D). \]

The crucial signs are

\[ b_ j-b_ i=-\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }t_{ij}^{-1}dt_{ij} =\ell (t_{ij}). \]

By lemma 10.40.1, the connecting image of $\omega $ is $[\ell (t)]\in H^1(M,\mathcal Z^1_{\mathbb R})$. Following by the connecting map of the lower row is exactly the de Rham resolution comparison: it is the succession of positive connecting maps in theorem 10.43.8. Naturality of the displayed diagram identifies its value with the real image of the upper-row class $c_1^{\mathbb Z}(L)$. Thus no cover with simply connected intersections, or logarithms on whole overlaps, was assumed.

In a holomorphic frame $e_ i$ write $h_ i=|e_ i|_ h^2$. The compatible connection with canonical $(0,1)$-part has form $\partial \log h_ i$; indeed metric compatibility determines its $(1,0)$-part by $dh_ i/h_ i$. In the unit frame $e_ i/\sqrt{h_ i}$ its form is $A_ i=\tfrac 12(\partial -\bar\partial )\log h_ i$. Hence

\[ \frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }dA_ i =-\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial \log h_ i =c_1(L,h). \]

This also proves the required normalization directly. An arbitrary complex connection differs from a compatible one by a global complex one-form; its normalized curvature has the same complex de Rham class, although its representative need not be real. $\square$

On $\mathbb P^1$ the canonical frames $X_0,X_1$ of $\mathcal O(1)$ satisfy $X_0=z^{-1}X_1$. Thus this proof uses $g_{01}=z^{-1}$, not its inverse. The Fubini–Study squared norm is $|X_0|^2=(1+|z|^2)^{-1}$, and

\[ c_1(\mathcal O(1),h_{\rm FS}) =dd^ c\log (1+|z|^2),\qquad \int _{\mathbb P^1}c_1(\mathcal O(1),h_{\rm FS}) =\frac1\pi \int _{\mathbb C}\frac{dx\, dy}{(1+|z|^2)^2}=1. \]

The integral is $2\int _0^\infty r(1+r^2)^{-2}dr=1$. Consequently the connecting class and the canonical positive curvature have the same, explicitly positive, sign. This is the existing Fubini–Study computation of the Fubini–Study curvature computation, used as a sign check.

Theorem 10.40.6.3A11E04C8D5D Let $X$ be any complex manifold and $\omega $ a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form. Suppose its de Rham class is the image of $a\in H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})$. There are a holomorphic line bundle $L$ and a smooth Hermitian metric $h$ with

\[ c_1^{\mathbb Z}(L)=a,\qquad c_1(L,h)=\omega . \]

Conversely every such curvature form has integral de Rham class.

Proof. Choose a smooth Hermitian line bundle of class $a$ using theorem 10.40.2, and a compatible connection $D_0$. Put $\omega _0=\mathrm i\Theta (D_0)/(2\pi )$. By theorem 10.40.5, $\omega -\omega _0=d\beta $ for a global smooth real one-form $\beta $. The compatible connection $D=D_0-2\pi \mathrm i\beta $ has normalized curvature $\omega $. Its $(0,1)$-part squares to zero because $\omega ^{0,2}=0$.

Here is the needed local rank-one integration, without a general bundle integrability theorem. In a smooth local frame write $D''=\bar\partial +A^{0,1}$. The curvature equation gives $\bar\partial A^{0,1}=0$, since the wedge square of a scalar one-form is zero. The smooth local Dolbeault lemma local Dolbeault exactness for forms and currents gives, on a smaller coordinate neighborhood, $A^{0,1}=\bar\partial f$. The nonzero frame $e^{-f}e$ is annihilated by $D''$. Ratios between these frames are nonzero smooth functions annihilated by $\bar\partial $, hence are holomorphic. They define a holomorphic line bundle structure with canonical operator $D''$. Metric compatibility now makes $D$ its Chern connection, by the local calculation in the preceding theorem. Its curvature is therefore exactly $\omega $, and the underlying smooth class is still $a$. Naturality of the exponential sequences proves $c_1^{\mathbb Z}(L)=a$. The converse is the preceding theorem. $\square$

Proposition 10.40.7.95F15B630D64 Under the de Rham and Dolbeault comparisons of corollary 10.43.11, the coefficient inclusion $\underline{\mathbb C}\to \mathcal O_ X$ induces $[\eta ]\mapsto [\eta ^{0,q}]$ from degree-$q$ complex de Rham cohomology to $H^ q(X,\mathcal O_ X)$.

Proof. The projection of a degree-$q$ form to its $(0,q)$-component is a map from the de Rham resolution to the Dolbeault resolution, since $(d\eta )^{0,q+1} =\bar\partial \eta ^{0,q}$. On the augmented kernels it is precisely the coefficient inclusion. Naturality of that comparison proves the claimed map, including its sign. $\square$

Theorem 10.40.8 (Lefschetz $(1,1)$ theorem).7CEC8FD0F9CB Let $X$ be a compact Kähler manifold. Write $a_{\mathbb C}$ for the complex de Rham image of $a\in H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})$, and use the canonical type subspaces of theorem 10.47.13. Then

\[ c_1^{\mathbb Z}(\operatorname {Pic}(X)) =\{ a\in H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb Z}): a_{\mathbb C}\in H^{1,1}(X)\} . \]

In particular the real Chern classes are exactly the integral real de Rham classes of type $(1,1)$, where integral means in the image of $H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})\to H^2_{\rm dR}(X,\mathbb R)$. This includes every integral lift, rather than discarding classes whose real image is zero.

Proof. By proposition 10.40.7, the coefficient map $H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb C})\to H^2(X,\mathcal O_ X)$ sends a closed form to the Dolbeault class of its $(0,2)$-component. The Kähler Hodge theorem identifies this with projection to $H^{0,2}(X)$: choose the unique harmonic de Rham representative and split it by type. Each component is Dolbeault harmonic and represents the indicated Dolbeault class. Its $(0,2)$ class vanishes exactly when that component is zero. An integral class has real image, so complex conjugation exchanges its harmonic $(0,2)$ and $(2,0)$ components. Thus its image in $H^2(X,\mathcal O_ X)$ is zero exactly when it is of type $(1,1)$. Exactness of (10.40.4) now proves the asserted equality with the full integral group. The real-image formulation follows from theorem 10.40.5. $\square$

Corollary 10.40.9.B92385CA0FAE Let $X$ be compact Kähler, $L$ a holomorphic line bundle, and $\theta $ a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form in its real Chern class. There is a smooth metric $h$ on this same $L$ with $c_1(L,h)=\theta $. In particular $L$ is positive if its real Chern class contains a Kähler form.

Proof. Take a smooth metric $h_0$. The exact real $(1,1)$-form $\theta -c_1(L,h_0)$ equals $dd^ c\varphi $ for a smooth real $\varphi $ by the compact Kähler $(1,1)$-current $dd^ c$ decomposition. The canonical metric transformation formula gives $c_1(L,h_0e^{-\varphi })=\theta $. $\square$

Theorem 10.40.10.4B30A0968D23 For a compact complex manifold $X$, the following are equivalent:

  1. $X$ admits a holomorphic embedding as an algebraic submanifold of a finite-dimensional complex projective space;

  2. $X$ has a positive holomorphic line bundle;

  3. $X$ has a Kähler form whose de Rham class is integral;

  4. $X$ has a Kähler form with class in $I^2(X)_{\mathbb Q}=\bigcup _{m\geq 1}m^{-1}I^2(X)$, where $I^2(X)$ is the integral image in real de Rham cohomology.

Proof. An embedding pulls back the positive Fubini–Study metric of $\mathcal O(1)$, proving (1)$\Rightarrow $(2). Its normalized curvature, or that of any positive line bundle, is an integral Kähler form by theorem 10.40.5; thus (2)$\Rightarrow $(3). Conversely (3)$\Rightarrow $(2) is theorem 10.40.6. By theorem 10.39.5, a positive line bundle has all sufficiently high powers very ample, giving a holomorphic embedding with closed complex submanifold image $Y$. The following finite homogeneous consequence of the already proved the Remmert–Stein extension theorem, Noetherianity of convergent power-series modules shows that this image is algebraic. Its inverse image in $\mathbb C^{N+1}\setminus \{ 0\} $ is an analytic cone with every branch of dimension at least one. Remmert–Stein extends it across $0$ to an analytic cone $C$. Choose finite local holomorphic equations $f_ j=\sum _{k\geq 0}P_{jk}$ at $0$, with $P_{jk}$ homogeneous of degree $k$. Scaling on $C$ shows that every $P_{jk}$ vanishes on $C$. Conversely their simultaneous vanishing implies that of the $f_ j$ near $0$, and scaling then identifies their common zero set with $C$.

The ideal of holomorphic germs generated by all $P_{jk}$ is finitely generated. Each generator is a finite combination of the $P_{jk}$, so a finite subcollection of these homogeneous polynomials generates the same germ ideal. Their global common zero set is still $C$: if they vanish at $z$, fix any other $P_{jk}$ and evaluate its germ relation at $tz$ for sufficiently small nonzero $t$. Homogeneity gives $P_{jk}(z)=0$. This holds for every $j,k$; the preceding paragraph then puts $z$ in $C$. Projectivizing the finite equations proves that $Y$ is algebraic. The empty case uses the equation $1$, and full projective space uses no equations. Thus (2)$\Rightarrow $(1). Finally (3) implies (4), and multiplying a form in (4) by its positive integer denominator gives (3). $\square$

Remark 10.40.11.CECCCE9F65C5 Compact manifolds have finitely many components; all assertions apply componentwise. On a zero-dimensional compact component the degree-two groups, curvature forms and differentials vanish, and a finite set embeds in projective space. Positivity and the Kähler condition on the zero tangent space are vacuous. Embeddings of finitely many positive-dimensional components may be put into disjoint projective linear subspaces, and a common sufficiently high power works for all. The notation $I^2(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ explicitly means the rational span of the integral image. No identification with the image of all of $H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb Q})$, no singular integral lattice or integral Poincaré duality, and no ambient-dimension bound for the projective embedding is asserted here. Those conclusions require their respective additional comparison or projection theorem. In particular a positive real cohomology class is not declared integral.
