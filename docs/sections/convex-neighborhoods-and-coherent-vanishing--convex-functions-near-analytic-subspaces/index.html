9.49 Convex functions near analytic subspaces00D84C9FEF45

00D84C9FEF45

Complex spaces here are Hausdorff with a countable basis. Smooth functions mean restrictions of smooth functions in local holomorphic embeddings; statements about functions depend only on the reduction. Fix an integer $q\geq 1$. A smooth function is strongly $q$-convex if locally it has an ambient extension whose Levi form has a positive subspace of codimension at most $q-1$. This condition is vacuous when the ambient dimension is less than $q$. An exhaustion has compact closed sublevels. A space is strongly $q$-complete if it has a smooth strongly $q$-convex exhaustion. In particular $q=1$ means a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion. Levi forms below use $\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial $; multiplying by $1/(2\pi )$ changes none of their signs.

Lemma 9.49.1.DD53C11960A9 Smooth partitions of unity subordinate to locally finite relatively compact embedding patches exist on a complex space. They can be chosen with nonnegative ambient extensions near every point. A smooth function on a closed analytic subspace has a global smooth extension. A complex space has a nonnegative smooth exhaustion.

Strong $q$-convexity is independent of the embedding and is preserved by restriction to a complex submanifold or analytic subspace. If several Levi forms are positive on the same subspace of codimension at most $q-1$, every convex increasing combination has that property provided at least one positive coefficient is retained.

Proof. Local compactness and a countable basis give compact sets $K_ j\subset \operatorname {int}K_{j+1}$ with union the space: take finite unions of closures from a countable relatively compact basis and enlarge successively. Cover each compact annulus by finitely many smaller embedding patches inside the prescribed cover. The resulting family is locally finite. Ordinary nonnegative ambient smooth bumps, positive on a smaller cover and supported in these patches, restrict to functions $b_ j$. Divide by the positive locally finite sum $\sum _ jb_ j$. Near a point all finitely many summands have ambient extensions, the denominator stays positive after shrinking, and each numerator may be chosen nonnegative, for example as a square. This proves the partition assertion and its local extension property. Patch local extensions of a smooth function on the analytic subspace using such a partition and the additional open set complementary to the subspace; their restrictions all equal the given function. Choose smooth $0\leq \chi _ j\leq 1$ equal to one near $K_ j$, supported in $\operatorname {int}K_{j+1}$. Then $r=\sum _ j(1-\chi _ j)$ is a locally finite smooth sum, is nonnegative, and satisfies $r\geq N$ off $K_{N+1}$, so is an exhaustion.

For embedding independence, let $A\subset \Omega \subset \mathbb C^ N$ and $A'\subset \Omega '\subset \mathbb C^{N'}$ be two local models. The change of models and its inverse lift locally to holomorphic maps $H,H'$ on the ambient spaces, by the definition of their holomorphic structure sheaves. If $f$ is an extension in the first model, the function

\[ f(H'(z'))+|z'-H(H'(z'))|^2 \]

is the restriction, along the graph of $H'$, of $f(z)+|z'-H(z)|^2$. The latter is strongly $q$-convex: the holomorphic automorphism $(z,z')\mapsto (z,z'-H(z))$ identifies it with $f(z)+|z'|^2$. Restriction preserves the required number of positive directions, since a subspace of codimension at most $q-1$ intersects a $d$-dimensional tangent space in dimension at least $d-q+1$. This proves independence and restriction. Finally

\[ H\bigl (F(f_1,\ldots ,f_ l)\bigr ) =\sum _ j F_ jHf_ j+\sum _{j,k}F_{jk}\partial f_ j\otimes \overline{\partial f_ k}. \]

The second term is semipositive when $F$ is convex, and the first has the asserted positivity on any common positive subspace. The assertion deliberately does not say that arbitrary sums of strongly $q$-convex functions remain strongly $q$-convex. $\square$

Lemma 9.49.2.71E35F5F0916 Let $Y$ be a closed analytic subset of a complex space $X$. There is a nonnegative smooth function $R$ on $X$ with zero set $Y$ such that $v=\log R$, with value $-\infty $ on $Y$, is smooth on $X\setminus Y$ and locally admits an ambient extension which is plurisubharmonic plus a smooth function, or identically $-\infty $. Locally near $Y$,

\[ v=\log \sum _ j|g_ j|^2+O(1), \]

where the $g_ j$ generate its analytic ideal in an embedding. Identically $-\infty $ restrictions on components contained in $Y$ are allowed. On every compact embedded piece where the extension is nontrivial, its negative Levi part has a finite uniform bound.

Proof. The checked analytic ideal coherence theorem Cartan's coherence theorem for analytic ideals gives finitely many generators on a neighborhood of each relatively compact embedding patch. For a space embedded as $A\subset \Omega $, use generators of the ambient ideal of $Y\subset \Omega $; their restrictions generate $\mathcal I_ Y\subset \mathcal O_ A$, since its kernel is the ideal of $A$. This uses only coherent quotient algebra, not cohomological acyclicity. Take a locally finite such cover and a subordinate partition $(\theta _\lambda )$ as in lemma 9.49.1, and put

\[ R=\sum _\lambda \theta _\lambda |g_\lambda |^2. \]

The sum is smooth and its zero set is exactly $Y$.

Near a point choose $\lambda _0$ with $\theta _{\lambda _0}>0$ and use its embedding. Let $h=g_{\lambda _0}$ be the ambient ideal generators. Each of the finitely many other $g_\lambda $ can be written $A_\lambda h$ on $A$ after shrinking, since $h$ generates the ideal sheaf; the coefficient matrices lift holomorphically to the ambient patch. For $\lambda _0$ take $A_{\lambda _0}=I$. The nonnegative local extensions of the weights then give an extension

\[ R_{\mathrm{ext}}=h^*Hh,\qquad H=\sum _\lambda \theta _\lambda A_\lambda ^*A_\lambda \geq \theta _{\lambda _0}I>0. \]

Thus $H$ is a smooth positive Hermitian matrix and is bounded above and below on a smaller compact patch. This proves the logarithmic comparison.

Here is the required Levi bound, including its uniformity. At a fixed point a holomorphic linear change of frame makes $H=I$ and its holomorphic first derivatives zero: first take a constant square root, then cancel the first derivatives by a matrix affine in the coordinates. For any tangent vector $\xi $, differentiation gives

\[ H_\xi \log (h^*Hh)= \frac{|dh(\xi )|^2|h|^2-|\langle dh(\xi ),h\rangle |^2}{|h|^4} +\frac{\langle H_{\xi \bar\xi }h,h\rangle }{|h|^2} \geq -C|\xi |^2. \]

The last matrix, after this change of frame, is controlled by the second derivatives of $H$ and the quadratic products of its first derivatives, with factors $H^{-1}$. Its norm is therefore uniformly bounded on a compact patch where $H,H^{-1}$ and their first two derivatives are bounded. The first term is nonnegative by Cauchy–Schwarz. Hence $\log (h^*Hh)+C|z|^2$ is plurisubharmonic off $\{ h=0\} $. Its value tends to $-\infty $ at that set and it is locally bounded above. On each complex line not wholly in the zero set, zeros are isolated and the bounded-above subharmonic removal theorem extends it with exactly this value; on a line wholly in the zero set the restriction is $-\infty $. The line test proves the stated ambient plurisubharmonic extension. The identically zero ideal case has the same interpretation. The canonical representative is the displayed logarithm, not an arbitrary change on a null set. $\square$

Lemma 9.49.3.339FB068D13D A complex space $Y$ admits closed analytic subsets $\varnothing =Z_{-1}\subset Z_0\subset Z_1\subset \cdots $ with union $Y$, locally stationary, such that $Z_ k\setminus Z_{k-1}$ is a smooth $k$-dimensional manifold, possibly empty.

Proof. Use the proved local branch, singular-locus and global-component statements of analytic singular strata and Hausdorff dimension, global irreducible analytic components. These are intrinsic under changes of embeddings, so their local descriptions apply to spaces as well. Start with the irreducible components of $Y$; adjoin components of their singular sets and of finite intersections, and repeat. Only proper singular sets and intersections give new components, and each has strictly smaller dimension than an ancestor. This family is locally finite. Indeed, near a point the initial components have finitely many branches and bounded dimension $d$. In descending dimension, the new germs are components of singular sets or intersections of finitely many already listed germs, so there are finitely many at the next dimension; shrink the neighborhood to representatives. At most $d+1$ repetitions give a neighborhood meeting finitely many resulting components. The same argument works near every point, with its own finite $d$. Let $Z_ k$ be the union of members of dimension at most $k$. Local finiteness makes it closed analytic. It contains the singular set and all component intersections of its $k$-dimensional members in $Z_{k-1}$, and all smaller members are already there. Thus the difference is smooth of pure dimension $k$. Locally the process has bounded dimension, proving stationarity and the union. $\square$

Proposition 9.49.4.DBED5041C4BA Let $Y$ be a closed analytic subset of a complex space $X$, let $\psi $ be smooth strongly $q$-convex on $Y$, and let $\delta >0$ be continuous on $Y$. There are a neighborhood $V$ of $Y$ and a smooth strongly $q$-convex function $\varphi $ on $V$ such that

\[ \psi \leq \varphi |_ Y<\psi +\delta . \]

Proof. Take $R$ from lemma 9.49.2 and the stratification of lemma 9.49.3. Start with any global smooth extension $f_{-1}$ of $\psi $. We construct $f_ k$ smooth on $X$, strongly $q$-convex when restricted to $Y$, and strongly $q$-convex on an open neighborhood $G_ k$ of $Z_ k$. Choose also a closed neighborhood $C_ k$ of $Z_ k$ contained in $G_ k$, increasing with $k$, with $C_{-1}=G_{-1}=\varnothing $. Arrange

8FAB545D8919 9.49.5
\begin{equation} 0\leq (f_ k-f_{k-1})|_ Y<2^{-k-2}\delta , \qquad \operatorname {supp}(f_ k-f_{k-1}) \subset \{ R<2^{-k}\} \setminus C_{k-1}. \end{equation}

The closed set $E_ k=Z_ k\setminus G_{k-1}$ lies in the smooth stratum $Z_ k\setminus Z_{k-1}$. Cover it by locally finite relatively compact embedding patches, with supports disjoint from $C_{k-1}$ and inside $\{ R<2^{-k}\} $. In each ambient patch flatten $Z_ k$ to $w_\lambda =0$, with $w_\lambda $ the normal holomorphic coordinates. Choose nonnegative smooth $\theta _\lambda $ of compact support in these patches whose sum is one near $E_ k$. Set

6F7888367208 9.49.6
\begin{equation} f_ k=f_{k-1}+\sum _\lambda b_\lambda ,\qquad b_\lambda =\theta _\lambda \varepsilon _\lambda ^3 \log (1+\varepsilon _\lambda ^{-4}|w_\lambda |^2). \end{equation}

All weights have nonnegative ambient extensions, as above.

We give the estimates and the simultaneous choice of constants. On any fixed compact ambient overlap, all coordinate maps, weights and their first two derivatives are bounded. For $0<\varepsilon <1/2$,

\[ 0\leq \varepsilon ^3\log (1+\varepsilon ^{-4}|w|^2) \leq C\varepsilon ^3(1+|\log \varepsilon |), \qquad |d\log (1+\varepsilon ^{-4}|w|^2)|\leq C\varepsilon ^{-2}. \]

The logarithm is plurisubharmonic. Product differentiation therefore gives the uniform lower Levi bound

3ACE50D93223 9.49.7
\begin{equation} Hb_\lambda \geq -C\varepsilon _\lambda I. \end{equation}

Indeed the cutoff Hessian costs $O(\varepsilon ^3(1+|\log \varepsilon |))$ and its two first-derivative cross terms cost $O(\varepsilon )$; the remaining term is nonnegative. These bounds also hold after holomorphic changes to another embedding: extend the finitely many $w_\lambda $ holomorphically and choose nonnegative local extensions of the weights.

Along $Y$ take a strongly $q$-convex extension of $f_{k-1}|_ Y$. Its strict positive eigenvalue margin on a smaller compact piece is positive. Because $Y$ is closed, these pieces may be chosen locally finite in $X$, by adding $X\setminus Y$ to the covering. The error bound (9.49.7) preserves that margin when the finitely many relevant $\varepsilon _\lambda $ are small. This retains the required positivity along all of $Y$; the function is unchanged on a neighborhood of $C_{k-1}$ by the support condition. We do not require preservation on the whole open $G_{k-1}$, where a strict margin could tend to zero at its boundary. Nor do we assume positivity is stable under arbitrary $q$-convex sums.

At a point of $Z_ k$, all the normal functions involved vanish, so the logarithms and their first derivatives vanish. Consequently there is the exact ambient formula there

\[ Hf_ k=Hf_{k-1}+\sum _\lambda \theta _\lambda \varepsilon _\lambda ^{-1}(dw_\lambda )^*dw_\lambda . \]

Choose a patch $\mu $ with $\theta _\mu >0$ at a point of $E_ k$. The restriction of $Hf_{k-1}$ to $TZ_ k$ has a positive subspace $F$ of dimension $\max (0,k-q+1)$, by restriction from $Y$. The $\mu $ Gram form is positive definite on the normal directions and zero on $TZ_ k$. If there are no normal directions, the required ambient positivity already follows from this restriction, and no domination is necessary. Otherwise, on $F\oplus N$ write the first block's lower bound as $a>0$, the mixed-block norm as $b$, the normal lower bound as $-c$, and the Gram normal lower bound as $d>0$. Then

\[ (Hf_{k-1}+t(dw_\mu )^*dw_\mu )(x+y,x+y) \geq \tfrac a2|x|^2+(td-c-2b^2/a)|y|^2. \]

For $F=0$ only the normal bound $td-c$ is required. Taking $t=\theta _\mu /\varepsilon _\mu $ sufficiently large therefore gives $\max (0,k-q+1)+N_\mu -k\geq N_\mu -q+1$ positive directions. The other Gram forms are nonnegative. Compact smaller pieces where $\theta _\mu $ is bounded below give one sufficient upper bound on $\varepsilon _\mu $, and continuity extends positivity to their neighborhoods. At points of $Z_ k\cap G_{k-1}$ the exact Gram formula adds a semipositive form to an already strongly $q$-convex ambient extension, so positivity there persists without a small-error estimate.

All the smallness choices can be made simultaneously. Refine the above compact pieces and the preservation pieces to a locally finite family with compact closures. Each meets only finitely many bump supports, and each compact bump support meets only finitely many pieces. On a preservation piece choose the sum of the error bounds less than half its strict margin. On a new-stratum piece choose the designated $\varepsilon _\mu $ below its normal-domination bound. Each parameter is subject to finitely many positive upper bounds. For the value budget, enumerate the supports and additionally require on $Y\cap \operatorname {supp}\theta _\lambda $

\[ b_\lambda <2^{-\lambda -1}2^{-k-2}\delta . \]

The positive minimum of $\delta $ on this compact set and the uniform amplitude bound make this another positive upper bound; an empty intersection imposes none. Their sum proves (9.49.5). Thus $f_ k$ is strongly $q$-convex on an open set containing $C_{k-1}\cup Z_ k$. Shrink inside this set to a closed neighborhood $C_ k$ of $C_{k-1}\cup Z_ k$, and choose an open $G_ k$ containing $C_ k$ on which the positivity holds. Such shrinkings follow, for example, from the locally finite partition construction in lemma 9.49.1 applied to a closed set and its open neighborhood.

The sequence is stationary near every compact subset of $X$. For a compact $K$, local stationarity of the stratification puts $K\cap Y$ in the interior of some $C_ j$. The remaining compact set $K\setminus \operatorname {int}C_ j$ is disjoint from $Y$, so $R$ has a positive minimum $\eta $ there; if this set is empty use any $\eta >0$. For large $k$ the support condition in (9.49.5) excludes this compact set, and it excludes $C_ j$ as well. Local finiteness gives stationarity on a neighborhood of $K$: use the union of $\operatorname {int}C_ j$ and $\{ R>\eta /2\} $, and then take $2^{-k}<\eta /2$. Thus $f=\lim f_ k$ is smooth. Near every point of $Y$ it is eventually equal to a strongly $q$-convex $f_ k$, and hence is strongly $q$-convex on some neighborhood $V$ of $Y$. Summing the positive value budgets gives $\psi \leq f|_ Y<\psi +\delta $ (in fact the total increment is at most $\delta /2$). Restrict $f$ to $V$. $\square$

Theorem 9.49.8.9EBE244C8709 Let $Y$ be a closed strongly $q$-complete analytic subset of a complex space $X$. Every neighborhood $O$ of $Y$ contains an open strongly $q$-complete neighborhood $V$ of $Y$. More precisely, if $\psi $ is a smooth strongly $q$-convex exhaustion on $Y$ and $\delta >0$ is continuous, the neighborhood can carry a smooth strongly $q$-convex exhaustion $\widetilde\psi $ with

\[ \psi \leq \widetilde\psi |_ Y<\psi +\delta . \]

Proof. The empty case is immediate. Extend $\psi $ approximately by proposition 9.49.4, obtaining $\varphi $ strongly $q$-convex on $W_0$ and the displayed inequalities on $Y$. Adding the same constant to $\psi ,\varphi $ first, assume $\varphi |_ Y>2$. We first arrange properness on a closed neighborhood. Let $r\geq 0$ be a smooth exhaustion of $X$. For every integer $j$, $r$ is bounded on the compact set $Y\cap \{ \varphi \leq j\} $. Choose an increasing continuous $a$ such that $r<a(\varphi )$ on $Y$. There is an open neighborhood $W_1$ of $Y$ with

\[ \overline W_1\subset W_0\cap \{ \varphi >1,\ r<a(\varphi )\} . \]

The local partition/shrinking construction above supplies this closed neighborhood inclusion. On $\overline W_1$, each closed $\varphi $-sublevel is compact: it is closed in $X$, lies in $W_0$, and has $r\leq a(t)$ when $\varphi \leq t$. Choose a neighborhood $W$ with $\overline W\subset W_1\cap O$. Its boundary is disjoint from $Y$.

Use $v$ from lemma 9.49.2. We choose a smooth convex increasing $\chi $ so that, on $W\setminus Y$,

\[ u=v+\chi (\varphi ) \]

has a common positive subspace with $H\varphi $ in every required ambient patch, and $u>1$ on $\partial W$. Here are all bounds needed for this choice. The compact sets $\overline W\cap \{ \varphi \leq j\} $ have finite ambient covers on which $H\varphi $ is positive on a fixed subspace $F$ of codimension at most $q-1$, with lower bound $\lambda >0$, whereas $Hv\geq -CI$. After shrinking, the same $F$ works throughout each patch. Patches on which $v$ is identically $-\infty $ need no bound, since the final correction below is identically zero there. Thus $\chi '\geq (C+\lambda )/\lambda $ gives $Hu|_ F\geq \lambda I$, because the $\chi ''$ term is nonnegative. The compact portion of $\partial W$ in that sublevel has finite lower bound for $v$, since it is disjoint from $Y$. Thus a finite lower bound on $\chi $ makes $u>1$ there. Increase all these bounds to a positive nondecreasing sequence $B_ j$. There is a smooth nondecreasing function $b(t)$ on $[1,\infty )$ dominating $B_{j+2}+1$ on $[j,j+1]$: interpolate the larger values $B_{j+4}+1$ with a fixed smooth increasing step function flat at both ends. Set $\chi (t)=B_3+2+\int _1^ t b(s)\, ds$ on this interval and extend convexly and increasingly to $\mathbb R$. Then $\chi '$ has all the required derivative bounds, and the last unit integration interval (or the initial constant for $1\leq t\leq 2$) gives all value bounds. The extension to the left can use a constant positive derivative near1; the chosen interpolation may be constant there.

Choose a smooth convex nondecreasing $\zeta $ which vanishes for $s\leq 1/3$ and has derivative one for $s\geq 1$. For example integrate a smooth increasing function from zero to one on $[1/3,1]$. The function $\theta (t)=\zeta (-1/t)$, $t<0$, is nonnegative, convex and nondecreasing, vanishes for $t\leq -3$, and tends to $+\infty $ as $t\uparrow 0$. Put

\[ V=\{ x\in W:u(x)<0\} ,\qquad \widetilde\psi =\varphi +\theta (u). \]

At $Y$ use $u=-\infty $ and $\theta (u)=0$. Since $v\to -\infty $ locally at $Y$, this last term vanishes on a neighborhood of each point of $Y$. Thus $V$ is a neighborhood of $Y$ and $\widetilde\psi $ is smooth everywhere on it, with $\widetilde\psi |_ Y=\varphi |_ Y$. Off $Y$, the chain rule on the common subspace $F$ gives

\[ H\widetilde\psi |_ F =H\varphi |_ F+\theta '(u)Hu|_ F +\theta ''(u)|\partial u|_ F^2>0. \]

Thus it is strongly $q$-convex; no arbitrary sum rule is invoked.

Finally it is an exhaustion. A sequence in a closed sublevel $\widetilde\psi \leq M$ has $\varphi \leq M$ and hence a subsequence converging in the compact set $\overline W\cap \{ \varphi \leq M\} $. Its limit cannot lie on $\partial W$, where $u>1$. A limit in $Y$ lies inside $V$. Any other limit is in $W\setminus Y$; if $u$ tended to zero, $\theta (u)$ would tend to infinity, a contradiction. The limit therefore belongs to $V$ and its closed sublevel. This proves compactness. Subtract the initial constant if one was added. $\square$

Proposition 9.49.9.64118C3BCA67 Let $Y\subset X$ be closed analytic and $U\subset Y$ open. Suppose that for a given compact $L\subset U$ there are a smooth strongly $q$-convex exhaustion $\psi $ on $Y$ and a real $c$ with

\[ L\subset \{ \psi <c\} \Subset U. \]

There are a strongly $q$-complete open neighborhood $V$ of $Y$ in $X$, a smooth strongly $q$-convex exhaustion $\widetilde\psi $ on $V$, and a sublevel $V_ b=\{ \widetilde\psi <b\} $ such that

\[ L\subset Y\cap V_ b\Subset U. \]

The neighborhood may be required to lie in any prescribed neighborhood of $Y$. This applies, in particular, to each compact $L$ when $U$ is $q$-Runge complete in $Y$.

Proof. For nonempty $L$ choose $d>0$ with $\max _ L\psi <c-d$. For empty $L$ any $d>0$ suffices. Apply theorem 9.49.8 with constant error $d/2$. Then $\psi \leq \widetilde\psi |_ Y<\psi +d/2$, so with $b=c$ one has

\[ L\subset Y\cap V_ c\subset \{ \psi <c\} \Subset U. \]

Its closure in $Y$ is compact and contained in $U$, which is the asserted relative compactness. In particular $V$ contains all of $Y$; there is no assertion $Y\cap V=U$ for a proper open $U$. $\square$

Corollary 9.49.10.4D18CC58062C Let $A$ be closed analytic in an open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ N$, and $U\subset A$ a strongly $q$-complete open subset. Choose an open $\Omega _ U\subset \Omega $ with $A\cap \Omega _ U=U$. Every neighborhood of $U$ in $\Omega _ U$ contains a strongly $q$-complete open set $V$ with $A\cap V=U$. For $q=1$ the ambient $V$ carries a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion. If $U'\subset U$ is $q$-Runge complete, the exhaustion can additionally have $L\subset A\cap V_ b\Subset U'$ for any specified compact $L\subset U'$.

Proof. An open subset of the subspace $A$ has the form $A\cap \Omega _ U$, so this choice of ambient open set is possible. Now $U$ is closed analytic in $\Omega _ U$. Apply theorem 9.49.8 there, and, for the last assertion, proposition 9.49.9 with $Y=U$. Every resulting $V\subset \Omega _ U$ contains $U$, so $A\cap V=U$. This equality concerns this ambient restriction, not a neighborhood of all of the original $A$. $\square$
