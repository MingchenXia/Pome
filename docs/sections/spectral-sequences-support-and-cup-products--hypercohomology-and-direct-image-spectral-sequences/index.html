10.55 Hypercohomology and direct-image spectral sequences05C05EF1C41B

05C05EF1C41B

Let $L^\bullet $ be a bounded-below complex of sheaves of abelian groups, with differential $\delta $; say $L^ q=0$ for $q<q_0$. Use the exact functors and resolution from theorem 10.43.5, writing $C^ pF=G^{p+1}F$. Form

\[ K_ L^{p,q}=\Gamma (X,C^ pL^ q),\quad p\ge 0,\ q\ge q_0, \qquad d_ h=d_ G,\quad d_ v=(-1)^ pC^ p\delta . \]

Naturality gives $d_ hd_ v+d_ vd_ h=0$. Every total diagonal is finite. Define

\[ \mathbb H^ n(X,L^\bullet )=H^ n(\operatorname {Tot}K_ L,D), \qquad D=d_ G+(-1)^ pC^ p\delta . \]

Complex morphisms give natural maps of these groups.

Theorem 10.55.1.C081C1E12790 For a sheaf in degree zero, this construction is its existing Godement cohomology. For a nonnegative complex, $\mathbb H^0(X,L)=\Gamma (X,\ker \delta ^0)$. Every degreewise short exact sequence of bounded-below sheaf complexes gives a natural long exact hypercohomology sequence.

For the right shift $L\langle s\rangle ^ q=L^{q-s}$, with differential $(-1)^ s\delta $, one has

\[ \mathbb H^ n(X,L\langle s\rangle ) \simeq \mathbb H^{n-s}(X,L). \]

Proof. A sheaf in degree zero leaves precisely its Godement section complex. In total degree zero of a nonnegative complex, the two components of the cocycle equation say $d_ Gx=0$ and $\delta x=0$; the first says $x$ is an actual section of $L^0$. There are no negative-degree boundaries. Exactness of every $\Gamma (X,C^ p-)$ and finiteness of each diagonal make a short exact sheaf-complex sequence a degreewise short exact sequence of total complexes. Apply lemma 10.43.3, including its naturality. For shifts, multiply the component of Godement degree $p$ by $(-1)^{sp}$. This identifies the total complex of $K_{L\langle s\rangle }$ with the shifted total complex $(\operatorname {Tot}K_ L)\langle s\rangle $: on the horizontal map the multiplier changes by $(-1)^ s$, and on the vertical map the shift supplies the same factor. The shifted differential is $(-1)^ sD$, proving the cohomology assertion with its signs. $\square$

Theorem 10.55.2.D461C5DFED48 There are natural convergent spectral sequences

\[ E_2^{p,q}=H^ p(X,\mathcal H^ q(L)) \quad \Rightarrow \quad \mathbb H^{p+q}(X,L), \]

and

\[ \widetilde E_1^{p,q}=H^ q(X,L^ p),\qquad \widetilde E_2^{p,q}=H^ p\bigl (H^ q(X,L^\bullet ),\delta \bigr ) \quad \Rightarrow \quad \mathbb H^{p+q}(X,L). \]

Both filtrations are degreewise finite. In the second first page the actual differential under these representative identifications is $(-1)^ qH^ q(\delta )$; this fixed-row sign does not change its cohomology groups. A quasi-isomorphism of bounded-below sheaf complexes induces isomorphisms on all hypercohomology groups. Here quasi-isomorphism means an actual complex morphism inducing isomorphisms of all cohomology sheaves; abstract graded isomorphism types alone are not asserted sufficient.

Proof. Filter $K_ L$ first by Godement degree. Exactness of the functor $\Gamma (X,C^ p-)$ identifies vertical cohomology with $\Gamma (X,C^ p\mathcal H^ q(L))$. Indeed apply exactness to the kernel, image and quotient sequences defining each cohomology sheaf. The factor $(-1)^ p$ does not alter kernels or images. The next differential is $d_ G$, giving the first $E_2$ display. Filter instead by sheaf-complex degree. Horizontal cohomology is $H^ q(X,L^ p)$, and the induced other differential has exactly the factor $(-1)^ q$ from the original Godement degree. Multiplication of a whole row's differential by this unit preserves its kernel and image, giving the second $E_2$ display.

For total degree $n$, Godement degree ranges from $0$ to $n-q_0$, and sheaf-complex degree from $q_0$ to $n$. Thus both filtrations have explicit finite bounds. The page and convergence proofs apply unchanged, or after translating the lower sheaf index to zero. There is no unbounded-total convergence assertion. For a quasi-isomorphism the first $E_2$ maps are isomorphisms, so theorem 10.54.3 proves the last claim by an actual filtered morphism. $\square$

Corollary 10.55.3.D8E20B32F22B If $L^\bullet $ resolves $F$ in degree zero, its augmentation gives an isomorphism

\[ H^ n(X,F)\longrightarrow \mathbb H^ n(X,L). \]

If every $L^ q$ is acyclic on $X$, inclusion of global sections by their Godement germs gives an isomorphism

\[ H^ n(L^\bullet (X),\delta )\longrightarrow \mathbb H^ n(X,L). \]

More generally, if only $\mathcal H^{q_1}(L)$ is nonzero, the single-row identification gives

\[ \mathbb H^ n(X,L)\simeq H^{n-q_1}(X,\mathcal H^{q_1}(L)). \]

Proof. The first augmentation is a quasi-isomorphism from $F[0]$, so the theorem applies. For the second, the germ inclusion is a cochain map: $d_ G\eta =0$ and on Godement degree zero the other differential is $\delta $. Its map to the second spectral sequence is the identity on the only nonzero first-page row, since all positive sheaf cohomology of the terms vanishes. Apply finite comparison, or the proved one-row collapse. In the final assertion the first $E_2$ has just the indicated row, so its finite filtration has a single graded piece in each total degree. $\square$

The augmentational normalization here can be compared explicitly with the successive positive connecting-map normalization of theorem 10.43.8. Suppose $L$ is an acyclic resolution of $F$, and denote that earlier isomorphism by $\Psi _ n:H^ n(L(X))\to H^ n(X,F)$. If $j_ F$ and $j_ L$ are the two maps to hypercohomology in the corollary, then

9AECA85DAE24 10.55.4
\begin{equation} j_ F^{-1}j_ L=(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}\Psi _ n. \end{equation}

To check this, take a closed $z\in L^ n(X)$. Exactness of the Godement degree functors gives finite zigzag lifts $a_ j\in \Gamma (X,C^ jL^{n-1-j})$, $0\le j<n$, with $\delta a_0=\eta z$ and $\delta a_ j=d_ Ga_{j-1}$ for $j>0$. The positive connecting construction represents $\Psi _ n[z]$ by $d_ Ga_{n-1}\in \Gamma (X,C^ nF)$. In the total complex, $Da_ j=d_ Ga_ j+(-1)^ j\delta a_ j$. The first relation replaces $[\eta z]$ by $-[d_ Ga_0]$; each subsequent replacement multiplies by $(-1)^ j$ for $j=0,\ldots ,n-2$. The product is $(-1)^{1+(n-2)(n-1)/2}=(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}$. For $n=0$ both maps are the identity on the kernel sections. This proves the formula without silently changing either earlier connecting maps or the total differential.

10.55.5 Higher direct images and Leray convergenceCF90123DE41C

Let $f:X\to Y$ be continuous and $A$ a sheaf of abelian groups. Use the existing direct image $(f_*A)(V)=A(f^{-1}V)$. Direct image preserves flabby sheaves, because inverse images of inclusions of open sets are inclusions of open sets. Define

\[ R^ qf_*A=\mathcal H^ q(f_*C_ X^\bullet A). \]

Left exactness gives $R^0f_*A=f_*A$.

Theorem 10.55.6.2CFDCB1083B2 For every continuous map of topological spaces and every sheaf of abelian groups there is a natural spectral sequence

\[ E_2^{p,q}=H^ p(Y,R^ qf_*A) \quad \Rightarrow \quad H^{p+q}(X,A),\qquad p,q\ge 0. \]

In each total degree convergence means the associated graded of a finite filtration. One has the stalk description

\[ (R^ qf_*A)_ y=\varinjlim _{V\ni y}H^ q(f^{-1}V,A), \]

and every short exact sequence of coefficient sheaves gives the natural long exact sequence of higher-direct-image sheaves. If $R^ qf_*A=0$ for all $q>0$, the augmentation edge gives isomorphisms $H^ n(Y,f_*A)\to H^ n(X,A)$. This edge is the composite of inverse-image pullback and the coefficient map $f^{-1}f_*A\to A$. No identification of the displayed stalk with fiber cohomology is asserted without further hypotheses.

Proof. Filtered direct limits of modules are exact by the representative argument: an image which is zero becomes zero at some later index, where exactness gives a preimage. The same argument shows that cohomology of a filtered limit of complexes is the limit of its cohomology: a limiting cocycle becomes a cocycle at some later index, and a limiting boundary has a primitive at a later index. Consequently stalkwise cohomology of $f_*C_ X^\bullet A$ is the displayed limit, by the definition of stalk and $\Gamma (V,f_*C_ X^\bullet A)=\Gamma (f^{-1}V,C_ X^\bullet A)$. Apply the sheaf-cohomology long exact sequence on $f^{-1}V$ and take these exact stalk limits to obtain the higher-image sequence; its maps are compatible with restriction and hence are sheaf maps.

Put $L^\bullet =f_*C_ X^\bullet A$. Every term is flabby on $Y$, thus acyclic by theorem 10.43.7. Hence corollary 10.55.3 identifies

\[ \mathbb H^ n(Y,L)\simeq H^ n(L(Y)) =H^ n(C_ X^\bullet A(X))=H^ n(X,A). \]

The first hypercohomology spectral sequence for $L$ has the stated $E_2$, with finite first-quadrant convergence. All constructions are natural in $A$. Its bottom edge is the map induced by the inclusion $f_*A[0]\to L$: the spectral sequence of its source has just the bottom row, and naturality identifies its map to the stable bottom row and the final filtration inclusion with this edge. If all higher $R^ q$ vanish, this is also a quasi-isomorphism and the edge is an isomorphism. This proves the asserted edge statement within the given augmentational construction. $\square$

Here is a complete verification of the last edge identification, including the pullback construction. For a sheaf $B$ on $Y$ there is a natural map

\[ \lambda _ B:f^{-1}G_ YB\longrightarrow G_ Xf^{-1}B. \]

For a section $u$ on an open set of $X$, its image is the arbitrary assignment whose value at $x$ is $e_{f(x)}^ B(u_ x)\in B_{f(x)}=(f^{-1}B)_ x$. The evaluation is well defined on the germ of an assignment, as in the Godement construction. This description commutes with restriction and coefficient maps, and

\[ \lambda _ B f^{-1}\eta _ B=\eta _{f^{-1}B}. \]

Iterating gives $\lambda ^{(m)}:f^{-1}G_ Y^ mB\to G_ X^ mf^{-1}B$: take $\lambda ^{(1)}=\lambda _ B$ and compose $\lambda _{G_ Y^{m-1}B}$ with $G_ X\lambda ^{(m-1)}$. These iterates commute with every insertion of $\eta $. Indeed insertion in the first position is the displayed identity; insertion in a later position reduces, after the outer $G_ X$, to the preceding induction case by naturality of $\lambda $. Thus they commute with the alternating sums defining $d_ G$. Pulling back global sections and then applying these maps constructs the cochain map

\[ \Gamma (Y,C_ Y^\bullet B)\longrightarrow \Gamma (X,C_ X^\bullet f^{-1}B) \]

and hence $f^*:H^ n(Y,B)\to H^ n(X,f^{-1}B)$. In degree zero it is ordinary section pullback. Since inverse image and the Godement section functors are exact, these cochain maps give morphisms of the short exact complexes for every short exact coefficient sequence; their cohomology maps commute with the connecting maps.

We use the following elementary uniqueness argument. Natural maps from $H^ n(Y,B)$ to any coefficient functor with compatible connecting maps are determined by their degree-zero maps. To see this embed $B\to G_ YB$ and put $Q=G_ YB/B$. For $n=1$ the connecting map $H^0(Y,Q)\to H^1(Y,B)$ is onto, since $G_ YB$ is acyclic. For $n>1$ the map $H^{n-1}(Y,Q)\to H^ n(Y,B)$ is an isomorphism. Commutation with these maps and induction on $n$ prove uniqueness; no existence theorem for derived functors is being assumed.

Apply this to the exact coefficient functor $B\mapsto f^{-1}B$. The augmented sheaf complex on $Y$

\[ B[0]\longrightarrow L_ B^\bullet :=f_*C_ X^\bullet f^{-1}B \]

uses the adjunction unit followed by Godement augmentation. All terms of $L_ B$ are flabby. Its hypercohomology therefore identifies with $H^ n(X,f^{-1}B)$ by the global-section inclusion already proved. The augmentation gives maps $e_ B^ n:H^ n(Y,B)\to H^ n(X,f^{-1}B)$. They commute with connecting maps: a short exact coefficient sequence gives short exact complexes $L_ B$, because $\Gamma (f^{-1}V,C_ X^ p f^{-1}-)$ is exact for every open $V$; both augmentations and the global-section inclusions are morphisms of these short exact complexes. Naturality of the cochain long exact sequence proves the assertion. In degree zero $e_ B^0$ is section pullback. The uniqueness argument thus gives $e_ B^ n=f^*$ in every degree.

Finally take $B=f_*A$ and the counit $\epsilon :f^{-1}B\to A$. The composite

\[ B[0]\longrightarrow f_*C_ X^\bullet f^{-1}B \xrightarrow {f_*C_ X^\bullet \epsilon } f_*C_ X^\bullet A \]

is the original augmentation: on a section of $A(f^{-1}V)$, taking its inverse-image germs and then evaluating them in $A$ returns the same section. The naturality of hypercohomology and of its global-section comparison therefore identifies its map with $H^ n(X,\epsilon )\circ f^*$. The preceding proof identified that augmentation map with the Leray bottom edge. This proves the claimed edge identification and, when all higher direct images vanish, the isomorphism induced by this precise composite.
