10.44 Soft sheaves and paracompact comparisonDF8C0003C01A

DF8C0003C01A

A paracompact space will mean a Hausdorff space every open cover of which has a locally finite open refinement. A family is locally finite if every point has a neighborhood meeting only finitely many members. For a sheaf $F$ and a subspace $S$, write $F|_ S=i^{-1}F$, where $i:S\to X$ is inclusion, and $F(S)=\Gamma (S,F|_ S)$. Thus a section on $S$ assigns an ambient germ to each point, locally represented by an actual section on an ambient open set. It remembers germs, not merely values of continuous functions at the points of $S$. Use the sheaf and inverse-image constructions of proposition 10.43.1, proposition 10.43.2.

10.44.1 Closed refinements and neighborhood extension4D03862B1482

Lemma 10.44.2.B28AAC964119 A paracompact space is regular and normal. Every open cover $(U_ i)$ has a locally finite open refinement $(V_ j)$ with $\overline{V_ j}\subset U_{\rho (j)}$. Every locally finite open cover $(U_ i)$ has an open shrinking $(W_ i)$ with $\overline{W_ i}\subset U_ i$, still covering $X$. Closed subspaces are paracompact. Unions of any subfamily of a locally finite family of closed sets are closed, and closures preserve local finiteness.

Proof. An open neighborhood meeting a closure meets the original set. Thus closures preserve local finiteness. Near any point a locally finite union of closed sets is a finite union, proving the closed-union claim.

Let $x\notin A$ with $A$ closed. For every $a\in A$, Hausdorffness gives disjoint open neighborhoods $N_ a$ of $a$ and $M_ a$ of $x$. Refine the cover $\{ X\setminus A\} \cup \{ N_ a:a\in A\} $ by a locally finite open cover $(V_ j)$. Each $V_ j$ meeting $A$ is contained in some $N_ a$, so $x\notin \overline{V_ j}$. Consequently $O=X\setminus \bigcup _{V_ j\cap A\ne \varnothing }\overline{V_ j}$ is an open neighborhood of $x$. The open union of those $V_ j$ contains $A$ and is disjoint from $O$, so $\overline O\cap A=\varnothing $. This proves regularity.

For each point choose an open $O_ x$ with closure in some cover member $U_ i$, by regularity, and take a locally finite refinement $(V_ j)$ of $(O_ x)$. Then $\overline{V_ j}\subset U_{\rho (j)}$. To obtain the same-index shrinking, group the $V_ j$ with the same label $i$: $W_ i=\bigcup _{\rho (j)=i}V_ j$. The closed locally finite union $\bigcup _{\rho (j)=i}\overline{V_ j}$ contains $\overline{W_ i}$ and lies in $U_ i$. The grouped family is locally finite, since the original family is. For a closed set $A\subset U$ open, apply this construction to $\{ U,X\setminus A\} $. The grouped open set belonging to $U$ contains $A$ and has closure in $U$. This is normality. Finally extend any relative open cover of a closed $S$ to ambient open sets and add $X\setminus S$. Restricting a locally finite refinement to $S$, and deleting empty sets, proves its paracompactness. $\square$

Call $S\subset X$ strongly paracompact in $X$ if it is Hausdorff and every ambient open cover $(U_ i)$ of $S$ has an ambient open cover $(V_ j)$ of $S$, locally finite near $S$, and an open neighborhood $W$ of $S$ such that $W\cap \overline{V_ j}\subset U_{\rho (j)}$. Here local finiteness near $S$ is required only on some neighborhood of each of its points.

Lemma 10.44.3.A5B4BF17B65F A strongly paracompact subspace is paracompact. Each of the following conditions suffices for strong paracompactness:

  1. $X$ is paracompact and $S$ is closed;

  2. $S$ has a fundamental family of paracompact neighborhoods in $X$;

  3. $S$ is paracompact and has a product neighborhood in which it is a slice $S\times \{ t_0\} $.

Proof. Intersecting the defining refinement with $S$ proves the first assertion. In case(1), refine the cover together with $X\setminus S$ using lemma 10.44.2, and retain the sets assigned to the given cover members. They cover $S$ and one can take $W=X$.

In case(2), choose a paracompact neighborhood $P$ of $S$ inside the union of the given cover members, and an ambient open $N$ with $S\subset N\subset P$. On $P$ take a locally finite open refinement $(A_ j)$ with relative closures in the prescribed cover members. Write $A_ j=P\cap O_ j$, $O_ j$ ambient open, and set $V_ j=N\cap O_ j$. These sets are locally finite near $S$, and $N\cap \overline{V_ j}\subset \overline{A_ j}^{\, P}$. Thus $W=N$ works.

For case(3), work first in the product neighborhood. For every $s\in S$ choose a product $O_ s\times P_ s$ about $(s,t_0)$ inside a prescribed cover member. Choose a locally finite open refinement $(A_ j)$ on $S$ with $\overline{A_ j}\subset O_{s(j)}$. The product sets $A_ j\times P_{s(j)}$ are locally finite near the slice. Their closed bad parts, namely their closures minus the assigned cover members, miss the slice: at a slice point in such a closure, the first coordinate belongs to $O_{s(j)}$ and the second is $t_0\in P_{s(j)}$. Remove the union of these bad parts. This is a closed union near the slice by local finiteness, so its complement gives the required $W$. If the original product neighborhood is not open in $X$, first choose an ambient open neighborhood $N$ of the slice inside it, represent the product sets by ambient open sets, and intersect with $N$. Closures intersected with $N$ are controlled by the relative product closures, and the same removal on $N$ proves the assertion. $\square$

Theorem 10.44.4.79027F524A8F Every section of a sheaf $F$ on a strongly paracompact subspace $S$ extends to a section on an open neighborhood of $S$. In particular this holds for every closed subset of a paracompact space.

Proof. Represent the section by sections $f_ i\in F(U_ i)$ which agree with it on $S\cap U_ i$. Take $(V_ j)$ and $W$ as in the definition, and restrict $W$ further to an open neighborhood of $S$ on which the family $(V_ j)$ is locally finite. On $W\cap \overline{V_ j}\cap \overline{V_ k}$ the sections $f_{\rho (j)}$ and $f_{\rho (k)}$ are both defined. Their unequal-germ locus is closed there, because the equality locus of two sections is open. These bad sets form a locally finite family: locally only finitely many pairs of closures meet. Their union is therefore closed in $W$. Remove it and intersect with $\bigcup _ jV_ j$, obtaining an open set $\Omega $. It contains $S$, since all relevant germs there are the prescribed germ. The sections $f_{\rho (j)}$ agree on $\Omega \cap V_ j\cap V_ k$, so they glue on $\Omega $ to the desired extension. $\square$

We shall also use closed pasting of germ sections. If a subspace is covered by a locally finite family of relatively closed sets, sections on them agreeing on intersections glue to a section. Indeed, near a point avoid all the finitely many nearby closed sets not containing it. Represent the remaining sections by ambient sections near that point. Their germs there agree, hence after one further shrinking all these finitely many representatives agree. They represent the pasted section locally. This proves the assertion without requiring the space of germs to be Hausdorff.

10.44.5 Soft lifting, acyclicity and supported partitionsE7A914DA41A5

A sheaf $F$ of abelian groups is soft if $F(X)\to F(S)$ is onto for every closed $S\subset X$. On a paracompact space every flabby sheaf is soft: first extend a section to a neighborhood using theorem 10.44.4, and then use flabbiness to extend to $X$. Restriction of a soft sheaf to a closed subspace is soft, because a closed subset of that subspace is closed in $X$ and restriction of germs composes.

Proposition 10.44.6.8017BDB06160 On a paracompact space, $F$ is soft if and only if each point has a closed neighborhood $N$ such that $F|_ N$ is soft. In particular softness on an open neighborhood of every point suffices. No assertion that restriction to an arbitrary open subset preserves softness is needed.

Proof. The forward implication is the closed-restriction observation. For the converse choose a locally finite closed refinement $(K_ i)$ of the open cover by interiors of such $N$, with its interiors still covering $X$, using lemma 10.44.2. Each $F|_{K_ i}$ is soft. Given $f\in F(S)$ with $S$ closed, well-order the index set and extend it over successive closed sets $S\cup \bigcup _{j<i}K_ j$. At stage $i$, softness on $K_ i$ extends the already assigned section on its closed intersection with that set to all of $K_ i$; closed pasting attaches it. At a limit stage, the sections agree on their common pieces, and the family consisting of $S$ and the preceding $K_ j$ is locally finite and closed. Closed pasting therefore gives a section on their union. After all indices one has a global section extending $f$. If softness is known on open neighborhoods, take smaller closed neighborhoods inside them; closed restriction again gives the criterion. $\square$

Theorem 10.44.7.695AD5517AC4 Let $X$ be paracompact and $0\to A\xrightarrow {j}B\xrightarrow {p}Q\to 0$ an exact sequence. If $A$ is soft, then $B(S)\to Q(S)$ is onto for every closed $S$. If $A$ and $B$ are soft, then $Q$ is soft. Every soft sheaf is acyclic on $X$ and, after restriction, on every closed subspace.

Proof. First lift $q\in Q(X)$. There is an open cover $(U_ i)$ on which it has local lifts $b_ i$. Choose a locally finite closed refinement $(K_ i)$ with $K_ i\subset U_{\rho (i)}$ and interiors covering $X$. Well-order these sets. Suppose a lift $b$ is already defined on $C=\bigcup _{j<i}K_ j$, which is closed. On $C\cap K_ i$, $b-b_{\rho (i)}$ is the image of a section of $A$. This uses left exactness of sections on the subspace and exactness of inverse image, as proved in proposition 10.43.1, proposition 10.43.2. Softness extends that section from the closed $C\cap K_ i$ to an $a\in A(X)$. Replace the lift on $K_ i$ by $b_{\rho (i)}+j(a)$ and paste it to $b$. At limit stages closed local finiteness and the closed-pasting argument apply. The final lift is global. On a closed $S$, its space is paracompact and $A|_ S$ is soft, so the identical construction proves the asserted lifting. If also $B$ is soft, lift a section of $Q(S)$ to $B(S)$, extend it to $B(X)$, and apply $p$. This proves softness of $Q$.

For acyclicity, use the already constructed Godement resolution theorem 10.43.5. Its terms $C^ qA$ are flabby, hence soft. Put $Z^0=A$ and $Z^{q+1}=\ker (C^{q+1}A\to C^{q+2}A)$. In $0\to Z^ q\to C^ qA\to Z^{q+1}\to 0$, the preceding quotient and lifting assertions inductively show that every $Z^ q$ is soft and the sequence is exact on global sections. Hence the unaugmented Godement section complex is exact in positive degree, proving $H^ q(X,A)=0$ for $q>0$. Apply the same theorem on a closed paracompact subspace to the soft restriction. This concerns ordinary cohomology of that subspace; no compact-support assertion is made. $\square$

Theorem 10.44.8.848B74D63948 If $F$ is soft on a paracompact space and $(U_ i)$ is any open cover, every $f\in F(X)$ can be written $f=\sum _ if_ i$ with $f_ i\in F(X)$, $\operatorname {Supp}f_ i\subset U_ i$, and the family of supports locally finite. Support here is the closed support of nonzero germs.

Proof. Suppose first the cover is locally finite, and choose a shrinking $(W_ i)$, $K_ i=\overline{W_ i}\subset U_ i$, covering $X$. Well-order the indices. After choosing the previous sections put $C_ i=\bigcup _{j<i}K_ j$ and $r_ i=f-\sum _{j<i}f_ j$; the sum is defined because its supports lie in the locally finite family $(U_ j)$. Maintain $r_ i|_{C_ i}=0$. On the closed set

\[ (C_ i\cup K_ i)\cup (X\setminus U_ i) \]

prescribe $r_ i$ on the first part and zero on the second. These germ sections agree: $K_ i\subset U_ i$ and $r_ i=0$ on $C_ i$. Closed pasting and softness extend the prescription to an $f_ i$ on $X$. Its germs vanish on $X\setminus U_ i$, so its closed germ support lies in $U_ i$. It vanishes on $C_ i$ and equals $r_ i$ on $K_ i$, maintaining the invariant. At limit stages the locally finite sum exists and has the same invariant. At the end it equals $f$ on the covering closed sets $K_ i$, hence everywhere.

For a general cover take a locally finite refinement $(V_ j)$ with $V_ j\subset U_{\rho (j)}$. Apply the first case and group its sections by $\rho $: $f_ i=\sum _{\rho (j)=i}f'_ j$. Every grouped support is contained in the locally finite closed union of the corresponding supports, hence is a closed subset of $U_ i$. The grouped family of supports is locally finite, since any neighborhood meeting only finitely many original supports meets only finitely many groups. This proves the claim. $\square$

Corollary 10.44.9.BE8990AF4756 Let $R$ be a soft sheaf of commutative unital rings on a paracompact space. Every open cover has a subordinate locally finite partition of $1$ by sections of $R$, with supports inside the prescribed open sets. Every sheaf of unital $R$-modules is soft and acyclic. Its positive-degree Čech cohomology vanishes for every open cover.

Proof. Apply theorem 10.44.8 to $1\in R(X)$. For a section $s\in F(S)$ on a closed set, extend it to an open neighborhood $\Omega $ by theorem 10.44.4. The prescription $1$ on $S$ and $0$ on $X\setminus \Omega $ is a section of $R$ on their disjoint closed union. Extend it by softness to $\psi \in R(X)$. Thus its germs equal1 on $S$ and vanish on $X\setminus \Omega $; in particular its support lies inside $\Omega $. The product $\psi s$ on $\Omega $, extended by zero, is a section on $X$: near every point outside $\Omega $ the section $\psi $ is identically zero. It extends the original germ section on $S$. Soft acyclicity follows from theorem 10.44.7. The Čech contraction with the partition of1 is precisely lemma 10.43.14; no ordered positivity in $R$ is required. $\square$

For completeness the continuous-function example can be proved using only the normality established above. On a normal space, disjoint closed sets $A,B$ admit a continuous $h:X\to [0,1]$ with $h=0$ on $A$ and $h=1$ on $B$. Indeed choose open sets $O_ t$, for dyadic $0\le t\le 1$, with $A\subset O_0$, $O_1=X\setminus B$ and $\overline{O_ s}\subset O_ t$ for $s<t$. Start by shrinking around $A$ inside $X\setminus B$; insert the intermediate dyadics recursively using normality. Define $h(x)=\inf \{ t:x\in O_ t\} $, with the empty infimum equal1. The identities

\[ \{ h<a\} =\bigcup _{t<a}O_ t,\qquad \{ h>a\} =\bigcup _{t>a}(X\setminus \overline{O_ t}) \quad (0<a<1) \]

prove continuity and the stated closed-set values. Given closed $S\subset \Omega $ open, normality twice and this construction produce a continuous $\chi :X\to [0,1]$ equal1 on a neighborhood of $S$ and with closed support contained in $\Omega $: choose $S\subset V\subset \overline V\subset W\subset \overline W\subset \Omega $, and separate $\overline V$ from $X\setminus W$. A germ section of continuous functions along $S$ extends first to $\Omega $; multiplying by $\chi $ and extending by zero gives a global section with exactly the same germs. Thus the real or complex continuous-function sheaf is soft. It need not be flabby: on $X=\mathbb R$, the continuous section $1/x$ on $(0,1)$ cannot extend across0. Smooth-function softness and its module acyclicity continue to reuse the existing smooth partition construction and proposition 10.43.10.

10.44.10 Lifting after refinement and global Čech comparison3DFB0CD1D083

Use the full ordered-tuple Čech complex, including repetitions, and the refinement/prism and comparison maps already constructed in theorem 10.43.16.

Lemma 10.44.11.D088E9B20989 Let $B\to Q$ be a surjective morphism of sheaves of abelian groups on a paracompact space. For every $q\ge 0$, open cover $\mathcal U$ and cochain $c\in \check C^ q(\mathcal U,Q)$, there is a refinement $\mathcal V\to \mathcal U$ on which the restricted cochain has a componentwise lift to $B$.

Proof. First take a locally finite refinement of $\mathcal U$ and restrict $c$; it is enough to prove the result for that cover, again denoted $(U_ i)$. Take an open shrinking $(W_ i)$ with $\overline{W_ i}\subset U_ i$. At every $x$ choose a neighborhood $N_ x$ meeting only finitely many $U_ i$. There are only finitely many indices with $x\in U_ i$, and hence finitely many ordered $(q+1)$-tuples of such indices, including repetitions. Surjectivity of stalks gives a lift of each corresponding cochain component on some neighborhood of $x$. Shrink $N_ x$ to a common open $V_ x$ so that all these lifts are defined there, so that $V_ x\subset U_ i$ for each $U_ i\ni x$, and so that it avoids $\overline{W_ i}$ for every one of the finitely many nearby $U_ i$ not containing $x$. The latter is possible since $\overline{W_ i}\subset U_ i$. Sets not meeting the original $N_ x$ need no further avoidance. Finally choose $\rho (x)$ with $x\in W_{\rho (x)}$ and shrink $V_ x$ further inside this $W_{\rho (x)}$.

If $V_{x_0}\cap \cdots \cap V_{x_ q}\ne \varnothing $, then $V_{x_0}$ meets $W_{\rho (x_ j)}$ for every $j$. By construction this implies $x_0\in U_{\rho (x_ j)}$ for every $j$. Consequently the component $c_{\rho (x_0)\ldots \rho (x_ q)}$ already has a lift on $V_{x_0}$, which restricts to the common intersection. Choose these lifts componentwise. This proves that the refinement $(V_ x)$ has the claimed property. $\square$

Theorem 10.44.12.E83248AB1E9D For a sheaf of abelian groups $F$ on a paracompact space the canonical maps induce natural isomorphisms

\[ \check H^ q(X,F)\simeq H^ q(X,F)\qquad (q\ge 0). \]

For every short exact sequence of sheaves the Čech direct-limit groups have a natural long exact sequence, and these isomorphisms commute with its connecting maps and the Godement connecting maps. No acyclic cover or good cover is assumed to exist.

Proof. For $0\to A\to B\to Q\to 0$ let $\check C_ B^\bullet (\mathcal U,Q)$ be the image of $\check C^\bullet (\mathcal U,B)$ in $\check C^\bullet (\mathcal U,Q)$. This is a subcomplex and

\[ 0\to \check C^\bullet (\mathcal U,A) \to \check C^\bullet (\mathcal U,B) \to \check C_ B^\bullet (\mathcal U,Q)\to 0 \]

is degreewise exact. Refinement preserves the liftable subcomplex. The prism homotopy for two refinement functions preserves it too: each component is a finite sum of restrictions of liftable components, which one lifts by the same sum of restricted lifts. Thus the cohomology groups $\check H_ B^ q(\mathcal U,Q)$ have well-defined refinement maps independent of the chosen function, and their long exact sequences are natural under refinement.

The induced map

\[ \varinjlim _{\mathcal U}\check H_ B^ q(\mathcal U,Q) \longrightarrow \check H^ q(X,Q) \]

is onto: a cocycle becomes a liftable cochain after lemma 10.44.11, and remains a cocycle in the subcomplex. It is injective: if a liftable cocycle becomes a boundary after some refinement in the full $Q$-complex, refine again so that a chosen bounding $(q-1)$-cochain becomes liftable. It is then a boundary in the liftable complex. For $q=0$ there is no bounding cochain; a zero class is already the zero cochain after the indicated refinement. This proves the assertion in all degrees.

Filtered direct limits of these long exact group sequences are exact by an elementary representative argument: represent an element at a cover; if its image is zero in the limit, pass to a common refinement where it is zero, and use exactness at that cover to find a preimage. The same argument proves equality of kernel and image at every position. This constructs the asserted Čech long exact sequence, using cohomology groups, without assuming that all cochain refinement functions define one canonical directed system of complexes. Covers may be taken as sets of open subsets. If an intermediate construction repeats an open set, retaining one copy gives refinement maps in both directions; the refinement homotopy makes their induced maps inverse. Hence this set-sized cofinal convention changes no group.

We check compatibility with Godement connecting maps, not merely coefficient-map naturality. For a fixed cover use the double complexes $D_ F^{a,b}=\check C^ a(\mathcal U,C^ bF)$ and total differential $\delta +(-1)^ a d$, as before. By exactness of $\Gamma (U,C^ b-)$, proved in theorem 10.43.5, the sequence of total complexes for $A,B,Q$ is degreewise exact. The sheaf augmentations give a morphism from the short exact sequence of Čech complexes above into this short exact sequence of total complexes; for its last term use the inclusion of liftable cochains followed by $Q\to C^0Q$. Therefore the cochain connecting maps commute by lemma 10.43.3. The horizontal augmentations $\Gamma (X,C^\bullet F)\to \operatorname {Tot}D_ F$ give another morphism of short exact complexes, and are quasi-isomorphisms by lemma 10.43.14, lemma 10.43.15. Their natural cohomology identifications therefore also commute with connecting maps. Combining the two diagrams gives exactly the comparison already defined in theorem 10.43.16, and proves the desired compatibility before and after passage to the limit.

Finally embed any $F$ into $GF$ and put $Q=GF/F$. The flabby sheaf $GF$ has zero positive cohomology in both theories, by theorem 10.43.7, lemma 10.43.14. Both degree-zero groups are ordinary sections and the comparison is their identity. In degree one the two long exact sequences identify the groups with the same cokernel of $GF(X)\to Q(X)$; compatibility just proved identifies the comparisons. For $q\ge 2$ their connecting maps identify degree $q$ for $F$ with degree $q-1$ for $Q$. Induct simultaneously for all sheaves, using compatibility of these maps, to prove the isomorphism in every degree. Naturality follows from the constructed comparison and its refinement compatibility. These are ordinary section-cohomology groups; no comparison with singular cohomology or with a compact-support theory is being made. $\square$

10.44.13 Alternating cochains and cover order213915544C35

Proposition 10.44.14.54F5489B5EF9 For every sheaf of abelian groups and every open cover, the inclusion of alternating Čech cochains into all ordered-tuple cochains is a cochain homotopy equivalence. Alternating means zero on repeated indices and multiplication by the permutation sign on reordering.

Proof. Well-order the cover indices. Let $C_ q$ be the free abelian group on all ordered $(q+1)$-tuples, with omission boundary $\partial $, and let $A_ q$ be the subgroup spanned by strictly increasing tuples. The inclusion $i:A_\bullet \to C_\bullet $ is a chain map. Define $\pi :C_ q\to A_ q$ by sorting a tuple with its permutation sign, and by zero if an index repeats. It is a chain map: for distinct indices, reordering commutes with the signed omissions; when exactly one pair repeats, the only possibly surviving two omitted terms have opposite signed sorted values; with more repetitions every term vanishes. Also $\pi i=\mathrm{id}$.

Here is an explicit integral homotopy for $P=i\pi $. Put $H_0=0$; $P$ is the identity on vertices. For a tuple $\sigma $ of degree $q\ge 1$, assume $H$ constructed in smaller degrees and put

\[ e_\sigma =\sigma -P\sigma -H_{q-1}\partial \sigma , \qquad H_ q\sigma =\operatorname {cone}_{m(\sigma )}e_\sigma , \]

where $m(\sigma )$ is its smallest index and the cone prefixes that index to every tuple in the chain. The induction identity and $\partial P=P\partial $ give $\partial e_\sigma =0$. For positive-degree chains the omission formula gives $\partial \operatorname {cone}_ m b=b-\operatorname {cone}_ m\partial b$. Thus $\partial H_ q\sigma =e_\sigma $, proving $\partial H+H\partial =\mathrm{id}-P$. Every tuple occurring in $H\sigma $ uses only indices already in $\sigma $.

Dualize this construction to sheaf cochains: a tuple formula with integer coefficients is evaluated by restricting each of its finitely many terms to the common intersection of the original tuple. The last support property makes every such restriction defined. The restriction to increasing tuples is a retraction onto the alternating complex, and the dual $K$ of $H$ satisfies $\delta K+K\delta =\mathrm{id}-\mathrm{inclusion}\circ \mathrm{retraction}$. This proves the assertion for every cover and every abelian coefficient sheaf, without division by a factorial. $\square$

Corollary 10.44.15.FC813BFC2984 Let $X$ be paracompact, $F$ a sheaf of abelian groups and $n\ge 0$ an integer. Suppose either there are arbitrarily fine open covers with every $n+2$ distinct members having empty common intersection, or there is one such cover acyclic for $F$ on all its nonempty finite intersections. Then $H^ q(X,F)=0$ for every $q>n$.

Proof. The alternating cochain groups of any such cover vanish in degree $q>n$, since every tuple has either a repetition or an empty intersection. The proposition gives the same cohomology vanishing for its full complex. In the first case every direct-limit class restricts to zero on a sufficiently fine such cover; apply theorem 10.44.12. In the second case apply the acyclic-cover comparison theorem 10.43.16 to that cover. $\square$
