25.11 Perspectives and further readingBCAE26692B2F

BCAE26692B2F

The preceding history follows three recurring transitions: from functions to measures and currents, from bounded potentials to controlled singularities, and from local constructions to global geometric data. Each transition introduces a new compatibility question. Products need a domain of definition; capacities need a testing region; equations need a mass normalization and a singularity class; algebraic comparisons need a precise correspondence between ideals, valuations, and analytic potentials. These questions account for the organization of the reference and for many of its open problems.

A reader approaching the subject through analysis can follow harmonic and subharmonic functions through capacity and the bounded Bedford–Taylor operator, then proceed to non-pluripolar measures and weak equations. A geometric route begins with positive currents, multiplier ideals, and cohomological positivity, then moves to singularity envelopes and section growth. Energy and geodesics connect the two routes, while toric examples give explicit convex models. The Hermitian and locally conformally Kähler chapters examine which parts of these constructions persist when closedness or a global potential is unavailable.

For classical potential theory, Brelot and Helms provide complementary accounts of harmonic spaces and Euclidean potentials. For pluripotential theory, Klimek and Guedj–Zeriahi provide broad treatments; Demailly's analytic texts explain the current-theoretic and weighted analytic background. The papers cited in the preceding sections supply more specific starting points for non-pluripolar products, variational equations, relative singularities, metric geometry, and algebraic asymptotics. The bibliography collects these references, and Sources retains the original bibliographical notes and exact contributing revisions.

Klimek's 1991 monograph is a principal reference for affine extremals, capacities, local Green functions, and invariant distances. Demailly's homotopy and smoothing methods provide shared analytic tools. Cegrell's monograph on capacities gives another account of the local theory and its envelope constructions. These texts offer complementary routes through the subjects developed in the preceding historical sections.

The chapter on problems in pluripotential theory contains the formally stated conjectures and questions, including those formerly placed among historical comments. They retain their own permanent tags. Historical attribution identifies the origin and development of an idea; it neither replaces a proof nor asserts priority for the additional arguments in this reference. Where a contribution is a working research draft, the statement, its assumptions, and its recorded source should be consulted together.
