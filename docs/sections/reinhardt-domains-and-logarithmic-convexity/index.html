9.62 Reinhardt domains and logarithmic convexityC236D3977F38

9.62.1 Logarithmic convexity and complete Reinhardt domainsAEE4F058351A

An open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ is Reinhardt if it is invariant under separate rotations of all coordinates. Its logarithmic indicatrix is

\[ D=\{ (\log |z_1|,\ldots ,\log |z_ n|): z\in \Omega \cap (\mathbb C^*)^ n\} . \]

Here logarithms are unsquared; changing to squared logarithms dilates $D$ by two and changes none of the convexity assertions.

Proposition 9.62.2.B7A5F2850576 If $\Omega $ is pseudoconvex, every connected component of $D$ is convex. Conversely, if $D$ is open and convex, the set

\[ \Omega _ D^*=\{ z\in (\mathbb C^*)^ n:\log |z|\in D\} \]

is pseudoconvex, considered as an open subset of $\mathbb C^ n$. Convexity of $D$ alone does not imply pseudoconvexity of an arbitrary Reinhardt open set having indicatrix $D$.

Proof. The map $E(\zeta )=(e^{\zeta _1},\ldots ,e^{\zeta _ n})$ has inverse image $E^{-1}(\Omega )=D+\mathrm i\mathbb R^ n$. If $\rho \geq 0$ is a continuous PSH exhaustion of $\Omega $, then $|\zeta |^2+\rho (E(\zeta ))$ exhausts this inverse image. Indeed, a sublevel lies in a bounded $\zeta $-ball and its image lies in the compact sublevel $\{ \rho \leq c\} \subset \Omega $; a convergent sequence therefore has its limit in the same inverse image and sublevel. Thus the tube is pseudoconvex.

For completeness the tube criterion follows from exact earlier inputs. For an open $V\subset \mathbb R^ n$, the Euclidean distance to the complement of $V+\mathrm i\mathbb R^ n$ is $d_ V(x)$, $x=\Re \zeta $. Unless $V=\mathbb R^ n$, Oka's boundary-distance criterion makes $-\log d_ V(x)$ PSH on a pseudoconvex tube. Applying translation-invariant plurisubharmonic functions on tubes on small convex real balls shows that it is locally convex in $x$. Consequently $|x|^2+\max \{ 0,-\log d_ V(x)\} $ is a finite continuous locally convex exhaustion of $V$. Its restriction exhausts every component, since components are relatively closed. The exact connected-set criterion local convexity and convex exhaustion makes each component convex. The full-space case is immediate. Apply this to $V=D$.

If $D=\varnothing $, its converse assertion is immediate. Otherwise let $\chi $ be a nonnegative continuous convex exhaustion of $D$, supplied by local convexity and convex exhaustion. The function $\chi (\log |z|)$ is PSH by toric plurisubharmonic functions and logarithmic convexity, with the factor-two change of variables in its squared-log convention. It exhausts $\Omega _ D^*$: the inverse image of a compact subset of $D$ is compact, since every coordinate modulus is bounded above and bounded away from zero and the angular torus is compact.

For the last assertion take $\Omega =\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} $, $n\geq 2$. Its indicatrix is $\mathbb R^ n$. If it had a PSH exhaustion $\rho $, the discs $\zeta \mapsto (\zeta ,t,0,\ldots ,0)$, $|\zeta |\leq 1$, $0<|t|\leq 1$, would lie in $\Omega $. Their boundary union for $|t|\leq 1$ is compact in $\Omega $, so the maximum principle bounds $\rho (0,t,0,\ldots ,0)$ uniformly. These points approach the missing origin, contradicting compactness of that sublevel. $\square$

Lemma 9.62.3.9179B3F88EE7 Let $D\subset \mathbb R^ n$ be nonempty, open, convex and downward closed: $x\in D$, $y_ j\leq x_ j$ imply $y\in D$. Set

591389D2112E 9.62.4
\begin{equation} \widetilde\Omega _ D =\{ z\in \mathbb C^ n: |z_ j|\leq e^{x_ j}\ (1\leq j\leq n) \text{ for some }x\in D\} . \end{equation}

If $\chi :D\to [0,\infty )$ is finite, convex and coordinatewise nondecreasing, then $\chi (\log |z|)$ on the nonzero-coordinate locus has a unique continuous extension $u$ to $\widetilde\Omega _ D$. This extension is PSH. For a point with nonzero-coordinate set $J$ and zero-coordinate set $I$, its value is

D8AC5552CA80 9.62.5
\begin{equation} u(z)=\lim _{T\to \infty }\chi (\log |z_ J|,-T\mathbf1_ I). \end{equation}

The order of coordinates in this formula is the original one.

Proof. Openness of $D$ permits every witnessing $x$ in (9.62.4) to be increased slightly in all coordinates. Thus the same union is obtained using strict inequalities, and it is an open union of polydiscs through zero. It is invariant under all coordinate contractions. Its intersection with $(\mathbb C^*)^ n$ is exactly $\Omega _ D^*$, by downward closure.

Write $D_ J$ for the coordinate projection of $D$ to $\mathbb R^ J$. It is open and convex. A point with nonzero coordinates exactly $J$ belongs to $\widetilde\Omega _ D$ if and only if $x_ J=\log |z_ J|$ belongs to $D_ J$. To verify the reverse direction, lift $x_ J$ to a point of $D$ and then increase all coordinates slightly. The resulting radii strictly dominate the given point. The forward direction follows from downward closure after decreasing the nonzero coordinates to their logarithms.

For every compact real box $K\subset D_ J$, all $(x_ J,-T\mathbf1_ I)$ with $x_ J\in K$ belong to $D$ when $T$ is sufficiently large. In fact, each point has a local lift; a finite subcover of $K$ and downward closure give one common $T$. The functions $f_ T(x_ J)=\chi (x_ J,-T\mathbf1_ I)$ are continuous and convex there, decrease in $T$ and are bounded below by zero. Their finite limit $f_\infty $ is convex on $D_ J$. By local regularity of finite convex functions, a finite convex function on an open convex set is continuous. Hence convergence is uniform on compact boxes. One can see this last assertion directly: for each positive tolerance, the open sets where $f_ T-f_\infty $ is below that tolerance increase to cover the compact box; a finite subcover gives a single $T$. This also proves the case $J=\varnothing $, where the box is a point.

If $x_ i\leq -T$ for every $i\in I$, monotonicity gives the sandwich

\[ f_\infty (x_ J)\leq \chi (x_ J,x_ I)\leq f_ T(x_ J). \]

Together with the compact-uniform limit, this proves that the prescribed values (9.62.5) extend the original function continuously near every coordinate stratum. It proves joint continuity when the nonzero coordinates vary as well. The same sandwich persists after taking limits in any coordinates that are already zero, so this also proves continuity between different strata. The dense nonzero-coordinate locus makes the extension unique.

We verify PSH without differentiating a singular logarithm. On any sufficiently small neighborhood in $\widetilde\Omega _ D$, for all small $\varepsilon >0$ the vector

\[ l_\varepsilon (z)= \bigl (\tfrac 12\log (|z_1|^2+\varepsilon ^2),\ldots , \tfrac 12\log (|z_ n|^2+\varepsilon ^2)\bigr ) \]

lies in $D$. Indeed the neighborhood and all its small padded moduli lie in one of the strictly larger polydiscs just constructed. Each component of $l_\varepsilon $ is PSH. The continuous function $u_\varepsilon =\chi (l_\varepsilon )$ is PSH: on a small complex-line circle the coordinate submean inequalities, monotonicity of $\chi $ and finite-dimensional convex Jensen give

\[ \chi (l_\varepsilon (z_0)) \leq \chi \left(\mathop{\mathrm{avg}}_\theta l_\varepsilon (z_0+re^{\mathrm i\theta }a)\right) \leq \mathop{\mathrm{avg}}_\theta \chi (l_\varepsilon (z_0+re^{\mathrm i\theta }a)). \]

All vectors and their averages lie in $D$, by convexity and downward closure. As $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, these functions decrease to the continuous extension $u$: the same sandwich handles unequal negative logarithms in the zero coordinates. They are nonnegative and locally uniformly bounded above by monotonicity and a fixed larger radius vector. Dominated convergence therefore passes every circle submean to $u$. Continuity and the line definition of PSH finish the proof. $\square$

Theorem 9.62.6.2A0C2847FC1E For every nonempty open convex downward-closed $D\subset \mathbb R^ n$, the set $\widetilde\Omega _ D$ of (9.62.4) is pseudoconvex and contains zero. In particular all its coordinate-hyperplane strata are included in the conclusion.

Proof. If $D=\mathbb R^ n$, the set is $\mathbb C^ n$, exhausted by $|z|^2$. Otherwise let $d_ D(x)=\operatorname {dist}(x,\mathbb R^ n\setminus D)$. The ball argument in local convexity and convex exhaustion makes $d_ D$ concave. Downward closure gives $d_ D(x-h)\geq d_ D(x)$ for $h\geq 0$, since translating an inner ball by $-h$ leaves it in $D$. Thus

\[ \chi (x)=\sum _{j=1}^ n e^{2x_ j}+\max \{ 0,-\log d_ D(x)\} \]

is finite, nonnegative, convex and coordinatewise nondecreasing. It blows up at every finite boundary point of $D$. The previous lemma gives a continuous PSH extension $u$ on $\widetilde\Omega _ D$, with $u(z)\geq |z|^2$. We prove that this is an exhaustion even at boundary points where some coordinates vanish.

Suppose a bounded sequence $z_\nu \in \widetilde\Omega _ D$ converges to $b\in \partial \widetilde\Omega _ D$. Let $J=\{ j:b_ j\ne 0\} $. This set is nonempty, since zero is an interior point. The vector $x^ b_ J=\log |b_ J|$ is in $\partial D_ J$: it is in the closure by projection from the approaching points, but cannot belong to $D_ J$ by the exact stratum criterion above. In particular $D_ J$ is proper. A ball in $D$ projects to a ball of the same radius in $D_ J$, so

\[ d_ D(x)\leq d_{D_ J}(x_ J)\qquad (x\in D). \]

For points with every coordinate nonzero this gives $u(z)\geq \max \{ 0,-\log d_{D_ J}(\log |z_ J|)\} $. By continuity it holds also when other coordinates vanish, as long as $z_ J$ stays nonzero. Its right side tends to infinity along $z_\nu $. Thus no bounded sublevel of $u$ accumulates at any boundary stratum. Since $u\geq |z|^2$, each sublevel is bounded and, by continuity and this boundary exclusion, closed in $\mathbb C^ n$. It is compact. This proves the full asserted pseudoconvexity. $\square$
