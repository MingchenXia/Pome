9.56 Refined bounds for simultaneous vanishing orders8C202BD7724D

8C202BD7724D

Write $\mathcal O(d)=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^ n}(d)$ and give it the tensor power of the Fubini–Study metric. In this section $\omega =\mathrm i\Theta (\mathcal O(1))$ is the unscaled positive curvature form; thus $\omega =2\pi \omega _{\rm FS}$ in the normalized $dd^ c$ convention. Orders at projective points are orders in local holomorphic line frames.

Definition 9.56.1.145E38D5855C For a finite set $S\subset \mathbb P^ n$, $n\geq 1$, and an integer $t\geq 1$, let

\[ \omega _ t(S)=\min \{ d\geq 0:\ 0\ne P\in H^0(\mathbb P^ n,\mathcal O(d)), \ \operatorname {ord}_ sP\geq t\ (s\in S)\} . \]

The sections here are homogeneous polynomials of degree $d$. Set $\omega _0=0$ and

\[ \Omega (S)=\inf _{t\geq 1}\frac{\omega _ t(S)}t =\lim _{t\to \infty }\frac{\omega _ t(S)}t. \]

For the empty set both quantities are zero. For nonempty $S$, $\omega _ t(S)\geq t$. These definitions are independent of any choice of affine chart. The sequence $\omega _ t$ is nondecreasing and subadditive.

Here are the reductions to the existing affine definition and limit. A hyperplane missing all the finitely many points exists, since its coefficients need only avoid finitely many proper linear subspaces. Dehomogenization in its affine chart, and homogenization to a specified degree, preserve the local orders at those points. Thus the minimum is exactly the affine minimum in corollary 9.55.8, whose full subadditivity argument proves the displayed limit. The polynomial identification is the $p=0$ case of proposition 10.51.5. Finiteness also follows directly by taking powers of a product of hyperplanes through the individual points. The degree bounds the order of a nonzero polynomial at any point of its affine chart. Monotonicity follows by inclusion of the conditions, and multiplication gives subadditivity.

Lemma 9.56.2.C0DCD035F833 Let $n\geq 2$ and let

\[ \Delta =\sum _{j=1}^ N r_ j[Z_ j],\qquad 0\leq r_ j<1, \]

where $Z_ j=\{ P_ j=0\} $ for distinct irreducible homogeneous polynomials $P_ j$. Use their reduced local equations, including all local branches of a given $Z_ j$. Its nonintegrability locus $A$ consists of the points where, for local reduced equations $p_ j$, the function $\prod _ j|p_ j|^{-2r_ j}$ is not locally integrable. This is a well-defined analytic set of dimension at most $n-2$. Put

\[ a=\begin{cases} \dim A,& A\ne \varnothing ,\\ 0,& A=\varnothing .\end{cases} \]

For any nonzero homogeneous polynomial $Q$, there is a linear subspace $Y\simeq \mathbb P^{n-a-1}$, cut out by $a+1$ independent linear forms, such that $Y\cap A=\varnothing $ and $Q|_ Y\not\equiv 0$. There is a neighborhood $V$ of $Y$ with compact closure disjoint from $A$ on which the preceding density is integrable, in any smooth projective metric.

Proof. Changing a reduced equation multiplies the density by a smooth positive factor bounded above and below locally. The local PSH weight $\sum _ j r_ j\log |p_ j|^2$ has a coherent multiplier ideal by coherence and weighted generators of multiplier ideals. Its stalk contains a unit exactly where the density is locally integrable: a unit is bounded away from zero on a smaller neighborhood. In a finite generating family, the stalk contains a unit exactly when some generator has nonzero value. Hence $A$ is its common zero set and is analytic.

We justify the reduced-equation and codimension claims without a projective algebraicity theorem. Polynomial rings over $\mathbb C$ are unique factorization domains: inductively, primitive polynomials over a unique factorization domain have primitive product, since reduction modulo any prime is a product in a domain. Factoring over the fraction field, a Euclidean polynomial ring, and clearing contents therefore gives existence and uniqueness of factorizations over the original ring. This starts with the field $\mathbb C$. On any affine chart, the nonconstant dehomogenizations of the $P_ j$ are distinct irreducible polynomials. Indeed homogenizing any proposed factorization would factor $P_ j$; the chart coordinate is the only factor that becomes a unit, and it cannot divide another irreducible factor. Their product $G$ is consequently squarefree.

Make a linear affine coordinate change so its highest homogeneous part is nonzero in the last direction. After multiplication by a constant, $G(z',T)$ is monic in $T$. Every one of its irreducible factors has nonzero constant leading coefficient in this direction. In characteristic zero it is coprime to $\partial _ TG$ over $\mathbb C(z')[T]$: each irreducible factor remains irreducible there by the primitive-product argument, and its derivative has smaller degree. The Euclidean algorithm and clearing denominators give

\[ h(z')=A(z',T)G+B(z',T)\partial _ TG,\qquad h\ne 0. \]

Thus $E=\{ G=\partial _ TG=0\} $ projects into $\{ h=0\} $ and has finite fibers, since $G$ is monic. Every analytic branch of $E$ has dimension at most $n-2$. To see this, take its regular locus and a point of maximal projection rank. The holomorphic constant rank theorem the holomorphic constant rank theorem says that a rank smaller than its dimension gives positive-dimensional fibers, which is impossible. The image of a constant-rank neighborhood is therefore a submanifold of that dimension inside $\{ h=0\} $. The proper-subset dimension bound gives dimension at most $n-2$. This also proves that no local hypersurface branch can be repeated in one factor, or shared by two factors: such a branch would be contained in $E$ and have dimension $n-1$. Hence the original dehomogenizations are the asserted reduced local equations. Off $E$, the equation $G=0$ has exactly one smooth simple factor; its density has a single exponent $r_ j<1$ and is integrable by polar coordinates and Fubini. Thus $A\subset E$ locally, proving the required dimension bound in every chart.

Choose $y$ outside $A\cup \{ Q=0\} $. To construct $Y$ through $y$, start with $Y_0=\mathbb P^ n$. At each stage choose a hyperplane of $Y_ j$ through $y$ containing no irreducible component of $A\cap Y_ j$, and call it $Y_{j+1}$. There are only finitely many such components: their local finiteness in global irreducible analytic components and compactness give finiteness. Each component has a point different from $y$, so containing it is a proper linear condition on a hyperplane through $y$. Finitely many such conditions can be avoided. The proper-subset dimension drop of purity and dimension of irreducible analytic sets gives $\dim (A\cap Y_ j)\leq a-j$ until the intersection becomes empty. For $A=\varnothing $ choose any hyperplane through $y$. After $a+1$ stages the intersection is empty; the successive proper hyperplanes have independent defining equations. Since $y\in Y$ and $Q(y)\ne 0$, the restriction of $Q$ is nonzero.

Every point of the compact set $Y$ has a neighborhood on which the density is integrable. Choose finitely many smaller neighborhoods with compact closures in these neighborhoods and disjoint from $A$. Their union contains a neighborhood $V$ as asserted. Bounded changes of frame and smooth volume only change the finite integral by a constant on these compact closures. $\square$

Lemma 9.56.3.18D5586E479D Let $P\ne 0$ be homogeneous of degree $D\geq 1$ on $\mathbb P^ n$, let $\alpha >0$, and let $Y$ be cut out by $p$ independent linear forms, $1\leq p\leq n$. For an integer $k$ suppose $k-\alpha D>p$. If a holomorphic section $f$ of $K_{\mathbb P^ n}\otimes \mathcal O(k)$ is defined on a neighborhood of $Y$ and

\[ \int _ V |f|^2|P|_{\rm FS}^{-2\alpha }\, dV_\omega <\infty \]

on some neighborhood $V$ of $Y$, then there is a global holomorphic section $F$ of the same bundle, equal to $f$ on the reduced $Y$, with

\[ \int _{\mathbb P^ n\setminus \{ P=0\} } |F|^2|P|_{\rm FS}^{-2\alpha }\, dV_\omega <\infty . \]

The norms of top-form-valued sections use the fixed metric $\omega $ and the indicated line metric. In particular $F\ne 0$ if $f|_ Y\ne 0$.

Proof. Put $s=|P|_{\rm FS}^2$. For $0<\delta \leq 1$, set $\varphi _\delta =\alpha \log (s+\delta )$, a global smooth function. In a frame of $\mathcal O(D)$ with squared norm $e^{-D\psi }$, where $\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial \psi =\omega $, one has

\[ \log (s+\delta )+D\psi =\log \bigl (|p|^2+\delta e^{D\psi }\bigr ). \]

The right side is PSH. For example regularize $\log |p|^2$ by $\log (|p|^2+\eta )$ and apply the chain rule to $\log (e^ u+e^ v)$: its first partial derivatives are nonnegative and its Hessian is the nonnegative variance matrix. Then let $\eta \downarrow 0$. Since the displayed function is smooth, this also gives its pointwise nonnegative Hessian. Consequently

\[ \mathrm i\Theta (\mathcal O(k),h_{\rm FS}^ k e^{-\varphi _\delta }) \geq (k-\alpha D)\omega . \]

Choose $0<\epsilon <k-\alpha D-p$, independently of $\delta $. The tuple of the defining linear forms is a section $\sigma $ of $E=\mathcal O(1)^{\oplus p}$ with curvature $\omega I$. Rescale this tuple so that $U=\{ |\sigma |^2<1\} $ has compact closure in $V$. Indeed its unscaled squared norm has a strictly positive minimum on the compact complement of any smaller neighborhood of $Y$. Write $t=|\sigma |^2$. Since $\langle \mathrm i\Theta (E)\sigma ,\sigma \rangle =t\omega $, the curvature condition in theorem 6.31.1 follows from

\[ \left(p+\frac{\epsilon t}{1+t}\right)\omega \leq (p+\epsilon )\omega <(k-\alpha D)\omega . \]

Projective space is compact Kähler, hence weakly pseudoconvex with constant smooth PSH exhaustion. The preceding theorem therefore applies to the smooth metric for every $\delta $. Its data integral is at most $I=\int _ U|f|^2s^{-\alpha }\, dV_\omega <\infty $, because $(s+\delta )^{-\alpha }\leq s^{-\alpha }$ off the null hypersurface. It produces global $F_\delta $ equal to $f$ on $Y$ with

\[ \int _{\mathbb P^ n} \frac{|F_\delta |^2(s+\delta )^{-\alpha }}{(1+t)^{p+\epsilon }} \, dV_\omega \leq \left(1+\frac{(p+1)^2}{\epsilon }\right)I. \]

Both $s$ and $t$ are bounded on the compact space. Thus the output density has a positive lower bound independent of $\delta $, and the $F_\delta $ have a uniform ordinary $L^2$ bound.

For completeness, $K_{\mathbb P^ n}\simeq \mathcal O(-n-1)$: the determinant of the affine coordinate change from $Z_ i=1$ to $Z_ j=1$ is, up to the constant sign fixed by wedge order, $(Z_ j/Z_ i)^{-n-1}$. These are exactly the canonical-bundle transition factors. The $p=0$ case of proposition 10.51.5 identifies the fixed section space with homogeneous polynomials of degree $k-n-1$, or with zero if that degree is negative. In particular it is finite dimensional. All norms on it are equivalent: on the coefficient unit sphere an ordinary $L^2$ norm has a positive minimum by compactness and the identity theorem. Hence a sequence $\delta \downarrow 0$ has a coefficientwise convergent subsequence, converging in every smooth norm to a global section $F$. Equality on $Y$ passes to the limit. Fatou on $\{ P\ne 0\} $ gives the displayed finite singular integral with its denominator $(1+t)^{p+\epsilon }$; boundedness of this denominator removes it. This proves the assertion without applying a smooth-metric theorem directly to a singular metric. $\square$

Theorem 9.56.4.D5C665BE2BA4 Let $S\subset \mathbb P^ n$ be finite and nonempty, $n\geq 2$, and let $t_1,t_2\geq 1$ be integers. Let $P\ne 0$ be any homogeneous polynomial of degree $D$ with $\operatorname {ord}_ sP\geq t_2$ for every $s\in S$. Factor it as $P=c\prod _ j P_ j^{k_ j}$ with distinct irreducible homogeneous $P_ j$ and positive integers $k_ j$. Put

\[ \alpha =\frac{t_1+n-1}{t_2},\quad b_ j=\lfloor \alpha k_ j\rfloor ,\quad r_ j=\alpha k_ j-b_ j,\quad \Delta =\sum _ jr_ j[Z_ j],\quad Z_ j=\{ P_ j=0\} , \]

and let $a$ be the dimension of the nonintegrability locus of $\Delta $, with $a=0$ when this locus is empty. Then

B104C3AB0116 9.56.5
\begin{equation} \omega _{t_1}(S)+n-a-1\leq \alpha D. \end{equation}

In particular, for every degree-minimizing $P$ at order $t_2$, with its own exceptional dimension $a$,

\[ \frac{\omega _{t_1}(S)+n-a-1}{t_1+n-1} \leq \frac{\omega _{t_2}(S)}{t_2}. \]

Proof. Write $d_ j=\deg P_ j$ and set

\[ Q=\prod _ jP_ j^{b_ j},\qquad q=\deg Q=\sum _ jb_ jd_ j,\qquad \beta =\sum _ jr_ jd_ j=\alpha D-q. \]

Scalar constants in $P$ have no effect on integrability or on its exceptional locus. The preceding geometric lemma gives $0\leq a\leq n-2$ and a linear subspace $Y$ of codimension $p=a+1$, disjoint from that locus, with $Q|_ Y\not\equiv 0$.

First suppose $\beta \geq n-a-1$. Choose

\[ k=\lfloor \alpha D\rfloor +a+2,\qquad \ell =k-n-1=\lfloor \alpha D\rfloor +a+1-n. \]

Then $k-\alpha D=p+1-\{ \alpha D\} >p$ and

\[ \ell -q=\lfloor \beta \rfloor +a+1-n\geq 0. \]

Choose a homogeneous polynomial $H$ of degree $\ell -q$ nonzero at a point $y\in Y$ with $Q(y)\ne 0$; a power of any linear form nonzero at $y$ suffices, including the constant when the degree is zero. Via $K_{\mathbb P^ n}\otimes \mathcal O(k)\simeq \mathcal O(\ell )$, let $f=QH$. Its restriction to $Y$ is nonzero. On a neighborhood of $Y$ the density $|f|^2|P|_{\rm FS}^{-2\alpha }$ differs by smooth bounded factors and the locally bounded $|H|^2$ from $\prod _ j|p_ j|^{-2r_ j}$. Compactness and the geometric lemma give its finite integral on a smaller neighborhood. Apply lemma 9.56.3 to obtain a nonzero homogeneous $F$ of degree $\ell $ with

\[ \int _{\mathbb P^ n\setminus \{ P=0\} }|F|^2|P|_{\rm FS}^{-2\alpha } \, dV_\omega <\infty . \]

At $s\in S$, choose local coordinates centered at $s$ and smooth line frames. Taylor's formula gives $|P(z)|\leq C|z|^{t_2}$. Thus the last integrability implies integrability of $|F(z)|^2|z|^{-2\alpha t_2}$. If the order of $F$ there is $b$, its first nonzero homogeneous term and spherical orthogonality give a lower radial integral

\[ c\int _0^ r u^{2b-2\alpha t_2+2n-1}\, du,\qquad c>0. \]

Since $\alpha t_2=t_1+n-1$, convergence requires $b>t_1-1$, that is, $b\geq t_1$. Consequently $\omega _{t_1}(S)\leq \ell \leq \alpha D+a+1-n$.

Now suppose $\beta <n-a-1$. At every $s\in S$, the degree bound $\operatorname {ord}_ sP_ j\leq d_ j$ gives

\[ \operatorname {ord}_ sQ =\alpha \operatorname {ord}_ sP-\sum _ jr_ j\operatorname {ord}_ sP_ j \geq t_1+n-1-\beta . \]

Taking the ceiling gives $\operatorname {ord}_ sQ\geq t_1+e$, where $e=n-1-\lfloor \beta \rfloor \geq 1$. Since $S$ is nonempty, $q\geq t_1+e$, so some homogeneous partial derivative of $Q$ of order $e$ is nonzero. Every such derivative lowers the order at every projective point by at most $e$: in an affine line frame $Z_ i=1$, differentiating a degree-$q$ homogeneous polynomial in $Z_ j$, $j\ne i$, gives $\partial _{z_ j}$ on its coefficient, while differentiation in $Z_ i$ gives $q-\sum _{j\ne i}z_ j\partial _{z_ j}$. Each lowers order by at most one, and iteration proves the assertion. A nonzero chosen derivative therefore vanishes at order at least $t_1$ on $S$ and has degree

\[ q-e=\alpha D-\beta -n+1+\lfloor \beta \rfloor =\lfloor \alpha D\rfloor -n+1\leq \alpha D-n+1. \]

This is at most $\alpha D+a+1-n$, since $a\geq 0$. Both cases prove (9.56.5). Finally choose $D=\omega _{t_2}(S)$ to obtain the last assertion. $\square$

Corollary 9.56.6.A99D1189F3F3 For finite nonempty $S\subset \mathbb P^ n$, $n\geq 2$, and all integers $t,u\geq 1$,

\[ \frac{\omega _ t(S)+1}{t+n-1} \leq \Omega (S)\leq \frac{\omega _ u(S)}u. \]

If, for a fixed $t$, there is an unbounded sequence of integers $u$ admitting degree-minimizing polynomials whose fractional divisors formed with $\alpha =(t+n-1)/u$ have nonintegrability locus of dimension at most $a_0$, using zero for an empty locus, then

\[ \frac{\omega _ t(S)+n-a_0-1}{t+n-1}\leq \Omega (S). \]

In particular a zero-dimensional or empty locus along such a sequence gives the numerator $\omega _ t(S)+n-1$. For $n=1$, $\omega _ t(S)=t\, \# S$ and $\Omega (S)=\# S$.

Proof. In the theorem always $a\leq n-2$. Let $t_2\to \infty $ through all positive integers and use the defining limit, giving the first lower bound. The upper bound is the infimum property. Along the specified unbounded sequence, use $a\leq a_0$ before taking the same limit. On $\mathbb P^1$, vanishing at a point to order $t$ means divisibility of a homogeneous binary polynomial by the corresponding linear form to that power. The distinct factors for the points of $S$ force degree at least $t\# S$, and their product attains it. This proves the final assertion. $\square$
