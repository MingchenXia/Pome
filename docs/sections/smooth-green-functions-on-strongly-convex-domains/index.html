9.24 Smooth Green functions on strongly convex domainsD1E419621675

D1E419621675

We use the complex bilinear pairing $g\cdot v=\sum g_ jv_ j$ for covectors, and write $r_ z$ for the complex differential of a real defining function. The positive stationary multiplier is normalized by $|g(0)|=1$ when parameters are needed. Sobolev norms below are angular norms on the unit circle. Write $\mathcal H$ for the Hardy subspace of $L^2$ with nonnegative Fourier coefficients, and $H^ s$ for angular Sobolev spaces. Smooth means $C^\infty $. Strong real convexity includes convexity of the domain and positivity of $D^2r$ on the real boundary tangent spaces for $r<0$ inside.

Lemma 9.24.1.EEFBD40AC261 Let $M$ be a smooth totally real submanifold of maximal real dimension in a complex manifold. A holomorphic map on a half-disc, which is $C^{1/2}$ up to its straight boundary interval and maps that interval into $M$, is smooth locally up to that interval.

Proof. Work in a complex coordinate chart and parametrize $M$ near a point by a smooth map $\varphi :V\subset \mathbb R^ d\to \mathbb C^ d$. Total reality says that its derivative has an invertible complex linear extension. For an integer $N$ define, locally,

\[ \Phi _ N(x+iy)=\sum _{|\beta |\leq N} \frac{(iy)^\beta }{\beta !}\partial _ x^\beta \varphi (x). \]

Terms of degrees below $N$ cancel in $\bar\partial \Phi _ N$. On compact coordinate patches, $D^ j\bar\partial \Phi _ N=O(|y|^{N-j})$ for $j\leq N$. Along $y=0$ its real derivative is the invertible complexification of $D\varphi $, so it has a smooth real inverse $\Psi _ N$ near the chosen point. Differentiating $\Phi _ N\circ \Psi _ N=I$ gives

\[ (\Psi _ N)_{\bar w} =-((\Phi _ N)_ z\circ \Psi _ N)^{-1} ((\Phi _ N)_{\bar z}\circ \Psi _ N) \overline{(\Psi _ N)_ w}. \]

The inverse and its derivatives are bounded on smaller patches; distances to $M$ and to $\mathbb R^ d$ are comparable there. It follows, also after differentiating, that

\[ D^ j(\Psi _ N)_{\bar w}(w) =O(\operatorname {dist}(w,M)^{N-j}),\qquad j\leq N. \]

Let $f$ be the given map, $z=x+iy$ with $y>0$. Its boundary Hölder estimate gives $\operatorname {dist}(f(z),M)\leq Cy^{1/2}$. Cauchy's formula on the disc of radius $y/2$, after subtracting $f(x)$, gives $|D^ j f(z)|\leq C_ j y^{1/2-j}$ for $j\geq 1$. Put $h=\Psi _ N\circ f$. It is real on the boundary interval and

\[ \bar\partial h=((\Psi _ N)_{\bar w}\circ f)\overline{f'}. \]

In a derivative of order $j$, a term with $\ell $ derivatives of $(\Psi _ N)_{\bar w}$ has $\ell +1$ factors from derivatives of $f$, with total derivative order $j+1$. The preceding estimates give

\[ D^ j\bar\partial h=O(y^{(N-1)/2-j}). \]

For any specified integer $k$, choose $N>2k+3$. Reflect $h$ by $h(z)=\overline{h(\bar z)}$ into the lower half-disc. Its reflected $\bar\partial $ has a $C^ k$ extension, with all derivatives through order $k$ equal to zero on the real interval. This follows directly from the displayed vanishing bounds on both sides, successively integrating the normal derivatives. The reflected $h$ is continuous. Integration by parts above and below height $\epsilon $, followed by $\epsilon \downarrow 0$, shows that its distributional $\bar\partial $ is this extended function: the two boundary terms cancel by continuity. The derivatives of $h$ are locally integrable by the same estimates and the chain rule.

Multiply the extended right hand side by a compactly supported smooth cutoff equal to one near the point. Its Cauchy transform solves the $\bar\partial $ equation and is $C^ k$: transfer each of the first $k$ derivatives to the compactly supported $C^ k$ datum in the convolution with the locally integrable kernel $1/(\pi z)$. Continuity follows from translation continuity of that kernel on bounded sets. The difference from the reflected $h$ is distributionally holomorphic, hence holomorphic: convolution with small smooth kernels gives holomorphic functions converging locally uniformly to this continuous difference. Thus $h$, and then $f=\Phi _ N\circ h$, is $C^ k$. Since $k$ was arbitrary, $f$ is smooth. A separate finite Taylor extension is enough for each $k$; no infinite almost-analytic extension theorem is assumed. $\square$

Lemma 9.24.2.CB9F3D62BE1C Let $D$ be bounded and strongly real convex with smooth boundary. Each convex complex geodesic and its positive stationary lift are smooth on the closed disc. The lift is nowhere zero, and after writing it as $g=\zeta \rho r_ z(f)$ on the circle, one has

\[ g\cdot f'=\alpha >0 \]

throughout the disc, for a positive constant $\alpha $.

Proof. Theorem 9.20.5 and Proposition 9.20.4 give $f,g,\rho $ of class $C^{1/2}$, with $\rho >0$. Replacing the unit normal by $r_ z$ merely multiplies $\rho $ by a positive smooth boundary factor. The stationary left-inverse construction in Proposition 9.18.2 shows that $g$ has no interior zero: a zero at $a$ would make $(z-f(\zeta ))\cdot g(\zeta )$ vanish at $a$ for every $z$, so its unique zero, the left inverse of $f$, would be constant. On the circle nonvanishing follows from $\rho >0$ and $dr\ne 0$.

Consider the projectivized conormal graph

\[ M=\{ (z,[r_ z(z)]):z\in \partial D\} . \]

It has real dimension $2n-1$ in a complex manifold of dimension $2n-1$. It is totally real. Indeed, if a tangent vector over $v$ and its multiple by $i$ both belong to its tangent space, then $r_ z\cdot v=0$ and $r_{z\bar z}\bar v$ is proportional to $r_ z$, by subtracting the two derivatives of the projective normal. Pairing with $v$ gives $v^ T r_{z\bar z}\bar v=0$. Strong convexity implies strict positivity of this Levi form on nonzero complex tangent vectors, so $v=0$ and the graph tangent vector is zero. The map $(f,[g])$ is holomorphic, $C^{1/2}$ on the closed disc and attached to $M$. The preceding lemma in projective coordinate charts makes $f$ and $[g]$ smooth up to the circle.

To recover the scalar size of the lift, choose a component with $r_{z_1}(f)\ne 0$ on a short arc. The smooth nonzero function $q=\zeta r_{z_1}(f)$ has a smooth argument there. Extend this argument smoothly to the circle and use its Fourier series to find a holomorphic smooth $A$ with that imaginary part. Then $g_1e^{-A}$ has positive real boundary values on the arc. The reflection argument for a continuous holomorphic function with real boundary values makes it analytic across the arc. Hence

\[ \rho =(g_1e^{-A})e^{\operatorname {Re}A}/|q| \]

is smooth there. Finitely many arcs give smooth $\rho $; the boundary relation and holomorphic Fourier series give smooth $g$.

Differentiating $r(f(e^{i\theta }))=0$ shows that $g\cdot f'$ is real on the circle. It is holomorphic and therefore constant. It is positive. To see the strict sign without a transversality assumption, choose the defining function with positive real Hessian near the boundary. One obtains it from any defining function by adding a sufficiently large positive multiple of its square; positivity on tangent spaces and compactness give full positivity after shrinking the collar. On an annulus near the circle, $r\circ f<0$ is subharmonic, zero on the outer circle, and bounded above by a negative constant on the inner circle. Comparison with a positive multiple of $\log |\zeta |$ gives a strictly positive outward radial derivative on the circle. Since this derivative is $2\operatorname {Re}(\zeta r_ z(f)\cdot f')$ and $\rho >0$, the constant is positive, as claimed. $\square$

Lemma 9.24.3.106A14BC1AFE Let $X$ be a closed real subspace of $L^2(S^1,\mathbb R^ d)$, invariant under translations and their inverses. Let $P_ X$ be its orthogonal projection, and let $W$ be a smooth symmetric matrix with $W\geq cI$, $c>0$. Then $A=P_ XW|_ X$ is invertible. Its inverse is bounded on $X\cap H^ s$ for every nonnegative integer $s$.

Proof. Coercivity gives $\| Au\| _2\geq c\| u\| _2$, closed range and, by self-adjointness and zero kernel, dense range. Thus it is onto. The orthogonal projection commutes with translations. If $Au=v$, translation and subtraction give

\[ A\delta _ hu=\delta _ hv-P_ X((\delta _ hW)\tau _ hu). \]

For $v\in H^1$, the right hand side is uniformly bounded in $L^2$. Parseval and Fatou give $u\in H^1$, as in the elementary difference-quotient proof: the multiplier $(e^{ijh}-1)/h$ tends to $ij$ and is bounded by $|j|$. Differentiate the equation in distributions and repeat. At order $s$, all additional terms are products of derivatives of $W$ and lower derivatives of $u$. Induction proves the estimate $\| u\| _{H^ s}\leq C_ s\| v\| _{H^ s}$, with constants depending on $c$ and finitely many derivatives of $W$. The same proof applies to complex Hardy components viewed as real Hilbert spaces, and to finite components represented by constant real functions. $\square$

Proposition 9.24.4.495B56CEC1A8 For a fixed smooth strongly real convex $D$, a smooth stationary disc with lift $(f,g,\rho )$ is, locally, a smooth function of $a=f(0)$ and $b=g(0)$. Smoothness may be taken with values in any fixed angular $H^ s$ space, $s\geq 2$ an integer, near that disc. The boundary multiplier stays positive for sufficiently small parameter changes.

Proof. We prove the invertibility needed for a local contraction, including its boundary regularity. For unknown holomorphic variations $v,h$ and a real variation $\lambda $, the linearized equations with arbitrary prescribed data $\phi ,\xi ,a_1,b_1$ are

\[ \begin{split} 2\operatorname {Re}(r_ z(f)\cdot v)& =\phi ,\qquad v(0)=a_1,\quad h(0)=b_1,\\ h/\zeta -\lambda r_ z(f) -\rho \{ r_{zz}(f)v+r_{z\bar z}(f)\bar v\} & =\xi . \end{split} \]

All boundary coefficients are smooth. First work in $L^2$, with holomorphic boundary values identified with their nonnegative Fourier coefficients. Put $S v=2\operatorname {Re}(r_ z(f)\cdot v)$. On holomorphic $v$ with $v(0)=0$, this real operator has a bounded right inverse on every $H^ s$. For a real $\psi $, let $Q$ be the holomorphic Fourier extension with $\operatorname {Re}Q=\rho \psi /2$ and real constant coefficient. Explicitly its coefficients are the constant coefficient and twice the positive coefficients of $\rho \psi /2$. Then

\[ R\psi =\zeta f' Q/\alpha \]

has zero constant coefficient and $S R\psi =\psi $. Choose the particular $v_0=a_1+R(\phi -Sa_1)$.

Let $K$ be the real Hilbert subspace of holomorphic $v$ with $v(0)=0$ and $S v=0$. Define the real symmetric form

\[ B(v,w)=\operatorname {Re}\int \rho \{ r_{zz}(f)v+r_{z\bar z}(f)\bar v\} \cdot w\, dm. \]

For $v\in K$, twice its integrand with $w=v$ is $\rho D^2r(f)[v,v]$. Since $v$ is a real tangent vector and the boundary is strongly real convex, this is at least $c|v|^2$, uniformly. Hence $B$ is coercive on $K$. The closed-range Hilbert argument for its positive self-adjoint operator gives a unique $u\in K$ such that, for every $w\in K$,

\[ B(u,w)=\operatorname {Re}(b_1\cdot w'(0)) -B(v_0,w)-\operatorname {Re}\int \xi \cdot w\, dm. \]

Put $v=v_0+u$. The functional

\[ L(w)=\operatorname {Re}(b_1\cdot w'(0)) -B(v,w)-\operatorname {Re}\int \xi \cdot w\, dm \]

on zero-constant holomorphic $w$ vanishes on $\ker S$. Since $S$ has the right inverse $R$, $L(w)=L(RSw)$. The real Hilbert representation of $L\circ R$ therefore supplies a unique real $\lambda \in L^2$ such that $L(w)=\operatorname {Re}\int \lambda r_ z(f)\cdot w\, dm$. The factor of two is absorbed in $\lambda $, since $S w=2\operatorname {Re}(r_ z(f)\cdot w)$. Set

\[ h=\zeta [\lambda r_ z(f)+\rho \{ r_{zz}(f)v+ r_{z\bar z}(f)\bar v\} +\xi ]. \]

The orthogonality just proved says $\operatorname {Re}\int (h-b_1)\cdot w/\zeta \, dm=0$ for every zero-constant holomorphic $w$. Both $w$ and $iw$ are allowed, so all nonpositive Fourier coefficients of $h-b_1$ vanish. Thus $h$ is holomorphic with constant coefficient $b_1$. The equations are solved in $L^2$, with a bounded solution operator. Uniqueness follows from coercivity for $u$ and surjectivity of $S$ for $\lambda $.

Here are the regularity details for that solution operator. The holomorphic projection $\Pi =I-f'g^ T/\alpha $ has range $\ker g^ T$ and smooth boundary values. This bundle has a holomorphic smooth frame $N$: solve $U'=[\Pi ',\Pi ]U$, $U(0)=I$, and apply $U$ to a basis of $\operatorname {ran}\Pi (0)$. The identity $\Pi U=U\Pi (0)$ follows by differentiation using $\Pi ^2=\Pi $. The holomorphic ODE is solved by successive integrals from zero; if its coefficient matrix is $C$, its $j$th iterated integral is bounded by $\| C\| _\infty ^ j/j!$ on the closed disc. The uniformly convergent series solves the equation. The analogous equation $V'=-VC$, $V(0)=I$, gives $VU=I$ by differentiation. The equation then bounds all derivatives up to the circle, so the frame and its inverse are smooth there. Together with $f'/\alpha $ its columns form a uniformly invertible holomorphic smooth matrix.

For $v\in K$, the holomorphic function $g\cdot v/\zeta $ has zero real boundary part, hence is $ic$ for a real constant $c$. Consequently the unique parametrization of $K$ is

\[ v=\zeta (icf'/\alpha +Nk), \qquad c\in \mathbb R,\quad k\in \mathcal H^{n-1}. \]

It is an isomorphism for $L^2$ and each angular $H^ s$ norm. The form $B$ becomes a pointwise smooth uniformly positive real matrix form on the values of $(c,k)$, compressed to the fixed translation-invariant subspace consisting of a real constant component and complex Hardy components. The preceding projected form lemma gives its inverse bounded on every integer $H^ s$. The forcing vector of its Hilbert equation is obtained by smooth multiplications, Fourier projections and the finite functional $w'(0)$; it is in $H^ s$ whenever the data are. Thus $v\in H^ s$. The representing function of $L\circ R$ has the same regularity, since the adjoints of the displayed smooth multiplications and Fourier projections also preserve $H^ s$. This gives $\lambda \in H^ s$, and then $h\in H^ s$ by its formula. In particular the full linearized operator is a bounded isomorphism on the asserted Sobolev spaces.

For completeness the nonlinear local step requires no additional existence theorem. Angular $H^ s$, $s\geq 2$, is a Banach algebra: Fourier convolution, the inequality $(1+|j+k|)^ s\leq C_ s((1+|j|)^ s+(1+|k|)^ s)$ and Cauchy–Schwarz imply the product estimate, because $\sum (1+|j|)^{-2s}<\infty $. The same Fourier estimate gives the continuous embedding in continuous functions. Smooth composition is smooth on these spaces. One checks this first in $H^1$ by the ordinary chain rule and the product estimate, and then inducts using $(F\circ u)'=(DF\circ u)u'$; Taylor's integral remainder and the product bound give the Fréchet derivatives $(D^ jF\circ u)\prod h_ i$ at every order. Smooth approximation proves the same identities for Sobolev functions.

Apply these facts to the boundary map

\[ (f,g,\rho )\longmapsto (r(f),\ g/\zeta -\rho r_ z(f),\ f(0),\ g(0)). \]

It is a smooth map between the indicated real Banach spaces and its derivative is the isomorphism just proved. Multiply by its inverse at the base point. In a small ball the equation is equivalent to a fixed-point map with derivative norm less than $1/2$. For parameters near the base it maps that ball into itself, so successive iterates converge to its unique solution. Differentiating the fixed-point equation and using the convergent Neumann series for its derivative gives smooth parameter dependence, inductively at every order. Positivity of $\rho $ persists by the continuous embedding. The boundary values of $f$ remain on $\partial D$, and the holomorphic maximum principle for real supporting linear functionals puts $f(\Delta )$ in $D$ when $f(0)\in D$. Thus these are genuine stationary discs. $\square$

Theorem 9.24.5.A2E9734AB1F5 Let $n\geq 2$ and let $D\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be a bounded convex domain, with smooth boundary and strictly positive real second fundamental form. Then its isolated pole Green function is smooth on $(D\times D)\setminus \{ (a,a):a\in D\} $. For fixed $a$ it is the unique negative PSH function that is locally bounded and maximal off $a$, has zero boundary limits, and has singularity $\log |z-a|+O(1)$; off $a$ it solves the classical homogeneous complex Monge–Ampère equation.

Proof. By Theorem 9.20.5, each $a\ne z$ lies on a stationary disc $f$ with $f(0)=a$ and $f(t)=z$, $0<t<1$. Normalize its lift by $|g(0)|=1$. The previous lemmas make it smooth and give local smooth dependence on $(a,b)$, $b=g(0)$. Consider the finite-dimensional map

\[ (a,b,t)\longmapsto (a,f_{a,b}(t)),\qquad |b|=1. \]

Both domain and target have real dimension $4n$. We show that its differential is injective. A kernel variation has $\dot a=0$ and $v(t)+f'(t)\dot t=0$. Tangency gives $g\cdot v/\zeta =ic$ for a real constant $c$. Pairing the last equation with $g(t)$ yields

\[ ict+\alpha \dot t=0. \]

Thus $c=\dot t=0$ and $v(t)=0$.

The holomorphic function $h\cdot v/\zeta $ is smooth on the closed disc, since $v(0)=0$. Integrate its real part against the positive Poisson kernel based at $t$. Its integral is its real value at $t$, which is zero. In the linearized stationary equation the real multiplier term vanishes by tangency, so this same integral is the positive weighted integral of $\rho D^2r(f)[v,v]/2$. Strong convexity forces $v=0$ on the circle and hence everywhere. Now $h=(\lambda /\rho )g$ on the circle. The holomorphic wedge $h\wedge g$ vanishes there and hence inside. Since $g$ is nowhere zero, the ratios glue to a holomorphic scalar with real boundary values; it is a real constant $s$. Hence $h=sg$ and $\dot b=sb$. The normalization $|b|=1$ gives $0=\operatorname {Re}(\bar b\cdot \dot b)=s$, so the entire kernel variation is zero. The finite-dimensional inverse function theorem, or the same contraction proof, gives a smooth local inverse $(a,z)\mapsto (b,t)$.

The local discs are convex complex geodesics by their stationary left inverses. Therefore the Green identity in Theorem 9.20.5 gives $g_ D(f_{a,b}(t),a)=\log t$. Evaluation at interior $t$ is smooth for these Sobolev-valued families: its derivatives follow from the uniformly convergent holomorphic series on compact subdiscs. Hence $g_ D(z,a)$ is smooth near every off-diagonal pair. No smoothness at the pole is asserted.

A smooth strongly convex domain is hyperconvex: a negative convex defining function near its boundary, patched to a negative constant inside by a maximum, gives a negative PSH exhaustion. Propositions 7.17.4 and 7.17.7 therefore give zero boundary limits and uniqueness. The two ball comparisons in Proposition 7.17.1 give its logarithmic singularity. Maximality and the bounded Bedford–Taylor criterion away from the pole give zero Monge–Ampère measure there. Since the function is now smooth, the canonical smooth determinant formula makes this the classical homogeneous equation. $\square$
