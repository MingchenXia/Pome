25.5 Cohomology, compact curves, and complex tori62EE456A4F3B

62EE456A4F3B

Sheaf resolutions, supported products and spectral sequences organize the cohomological part of Demailly's Complex Analytic and Differential Geometry. The de Rham comparison connects this language to differential forms. Integral orientation and cap duality require local coefficient systems and retain torsion information which real differential forms do not detect. Hatcher's Algebraic Topology supplies a complementary account of the integral singular-topological theory. Compact-support duality, ordinary duality and their coefficient hypotheses are distinguished throughout these comparisons. Nuclear Fréchet tensor products and analytic direct images form a separate topological development: closed-range cohomology, actual balanced quotients and algebraic Tor groups keep their respective hypotheses.

For compact complex curves the analytic and topological theories meet in Riemann–Roch, Serre duality, periods and Jacobians. Picard and Albanese tori extend the period construction, while polarizations relate integral forms to projective embeddings. These topics are presented together in Demailly's book; their organization here follows the common cohomological and geometric structures.
