2.13 Nuclear spaces and completed tensor products54CD186EFBFD

54CD186EFBFD

All spaces are Hausdorff and complex. For continuous seminorms $p,q$ on $E,F$, respectively, put

\[ \pi _{p,q}(t)=\inf _{t=\sum x_ j\otimes y_ j}\sum _ jp(x_ j)q(y_ j), \qquad \varepsilon _{p,q}(t)= \sup _{|\xi |\leq p,\, |\eta |\leq q}|(\xi \otimes \eta )(t)|. \]

The completions for these families are denoted $E\widehat\otimes _\pi F$ and $E\widehat\otimes _\varepsilon F$. Dominated linear extension, already proved in section 2.12, gives the crossnorm identity $\varepsilon _{p,q}(x\otimes y)=\pi _{p,q}(x\otimes y)=p(x)q(y)$ and $\varepsilon _{p,q}\leq \pi _{p,q}$. The tensor topologies are Hausdorff: on the finite-dimensional spans in a nonzero tensor, coordinate functionals extend continuously and a suitable product functional has nonzero value. The continuous dual of the projective completion is precisely the jointly continuous bilinear forms. Indeed, $|b(x,y)|\leq C p(x)q(y)$ is equivalent to the corresponding linear functional being bounded by $C\pi _{p,q}$; continuity of a bilinear form gives such a bound by scaling zero neighborhoods. Hahn–Banach applied to the projective seminorm also shows that $\pi _{p,q}$ is the supremum of these normalized bilinear tests. Consequently it is unchanged on passing to the Banach completions $E_ p,F_ q$ of the seminorm quotients. The analogous assertion for $\varepsilon $ follows by extending the dominated linear tests. Continuous maps induce maps on both tensor completions by these same bounds. For Fréchet factors the completions are Fréchet, since a countable defining family gives a metrizable completion.

Lemma 2.13.1.67761F6CDA64 A nuclear map between complete locally convex spaces is a map

\[ A x=\sum _ j\lambda _ j\xi _ j(x)y_ j, \quad \sum _ j|\lambda _ j|<\infty , \]

where $(\xi _ j)$ is equicontinuous and $(y_ j)$ bounded. Equivalently, it factors through a nuclear map of Banach spaces. Such maps form a two-sided ideal under continuous compositions and are compact on some source zero neighborhood.

For Banach spaces its nuclear norm is the infimum of $\sum _ j|\lambda _ j|$ over representations with $\| \xi _ j\| ,\| y_ j\| \leq 1$. The nuclear operator space is the quotient of $E'\widehat\otimes _\pi F$ by the kernel of its map to operators; injectivity of that map is not required. Every Banach nuclear map factors as two bounded maps through a Hilbert space.

Proof. Equicontinuity gives $|\xi _ j(x)|\leq p(x)$ after rescaling one continuous seminorm. The map $B:\ell ^1\to F$, $B(a)=\sum _ ja_ jy_ j$, is well defined: every continuous seminorm bounds its tails by a constant times the corresponding $\ell ^1$ tails, and completeness supplies the limit. It is continuous. Let $F_1=\ell ^1/\ker B$, a Banach space, and let $v:F_1\to F$ be the induced continuous injection. The map

\[ E_ p\longrightarrow F_1,\qquad x\longmapsto \sum _ j\lambda _ j\xi _ j(x)[e_ j] \]

is Banach nuclear and gives the stated factorization. Conversely compose a Banach nuclear representation with the two continuous outer maps. The functionals remain equicontinuous and the vectors bounded. The ideal property follows by the same composition.

A Banach nuclear series is uniformly approximated on the unit ball by its finite-rank heads, with tail operator norm at most $\sum _{j>N}|\lambda _ j|$. The heads have relatively compact bounded images; finite nets and completeness give a compact closure for the full image. In the factorization above a source neighborhood maps into a Banach unit ball, so its image in $F$ has compact closure as well.

Every element of a completed projective tensor is a sum of simple tensors with summable products of norms: approximate it by algebraic tensors with geometrically small successive errors, then decompose each error with cost arbitrarily close to its projective norm. This proves surjectivity onto the nuclear operators and the asserted infimum/quotient norm. The kernel is closed since operator norm is bounded by projective norm. No assertion identifying operators injectively with tensors is used.

For a Banach representation with normalized factors put

\[ u(x)=(|\lambda _ j|^{1/2}\xi _ j(x))_ j, \qquad v(a)=\sum _ j|\lambda _ j|^{1/2} \operatorname {sgn}(\lambda _ j)a_ jy_ j. \]

Here $\operatorname {sgn}(\lambda )=\lambda /|\lambda |$ when $\lambda \neq 0$, and zero otherwise. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\| u\| ,\| v\| \leq (\sum _ j|\lambda _ j|)^{1/2}$ and the series for $v$ converges in norm. Thus $A=vu$ through $\ell ^2$. $\square$

Proposition 2.13.2.D02C4838DC99 If $A:E\to F$ is Banach nuclear, then for every Banach space $G$

\[ A\otimes \mathrm{Id}: E\widehat\otimes _\varepsilon G \longrightarrow F\widehat\otimes _\pi G \]

is continuous with norm at most $\| A\| _\nu $.

Proof. For a normalized nuclear representation, contraction of an algebraic tensor $t$ by $\xi _ j$ has norm at most $\| t\| _\varepsilon $: test that contraction by every unit functional on $G$ and use dominated linear extension. Thus

\[ (A\otimes \mathrm{Id})t =\sum _ j\lambda _ j y_ j\otimes (\xi _ j\otimes \mathrm{Id})t, \qquad \| (A\otimes \mathrm{Id})t\| _\pi \leq \sum _ j|\lambda _ j|\, \| t\| _\varepsilon . \]

Take the infimum and extend by completeness. $\square$

A Fréchet space is nuclear if it has increasing defining seminorms $p_ j$ for which every canonical map $E_{p_{j+1}}\to E_{p_ j}$ is Banach nuclear. Inverse-limit notation here uses the Banach completions, not an assertion that the uncompleted seminorm quotients are Banach. For any Fréchet space these completions recover $E$ as their countable inverse limit. Given compatible vectors $x_ j\in E_{p_ j}$, choose $e_ j\in E$ with $\| e_ j-x_ j\| _{p_ j}<2^{-j}$. For fixed $r$, the images of $e_ j$, $j\geq r$, converge to $x_ r$ in $E_{p_ r}$. Thus $(e_ j)$ is Cauchy in every defining seminorm, has a limit in $E$, and that limit has the prescribed images. Injectivity and the equality of topologies follow from the defining seminorms.

Theorem 2.13.3.A46D09531259 The preceding definition is equivalent to admitting increasing Hilbertian defining seminorms with nuclear Hilbert-space links. Closed subspaces, closed-subspace quotients, countable products and countable inverse limits of nuclear Fréchet spaces are nuclear. A completed tensor product of two nuclear Fréchet spaces is nuclear. If one Fréchet factor is nuclear, its completed projective and injective tensor products with any Hausdorff locally convex space coincide topologically.

Proof. Factor $E_{p_{j+1}}\to E_{p_ j}$ through a Hilbert space by the lemma. The Hilbertian seminorm $h_ j$ obtained from the first map, multiplied by the norm of the second, satisfies $p_ j\leq h_ j\leq C_ jp_{j+1}$. Zero links cause no exception. Put $q_ j=(\sum _{r\leq j}h_ r^2)^{1/2}$. These are increasing Hilbertian defining seminorms with $p_ j\leq q_ j\leq C'_ jp_{j+1}$. The map $E_{q_{j+2}}\to E_{q_ j}$ factors through the nuclear link $E_{p_{j+2}}\to E_{p_{j+1}}$, with bounded outer maps. Taking the even subsequence proves the Hilbertian criterion. The converse is immediate from the definition.

Let $S\subset E$ be closed, use this Hilbertian family, and write $H_ j=E_{q_ j}$, $K_ j=\overline{S}^{\, H_ j}$. The completion of $S$ at $q_ j$ is $K_ j$, and the completion of $E/S$ at the quotient seminorm is $H_ j/K_ j$. The latter identity follows because the infimum over $S$ is the distance to $K_ j$. The canonical nuclear link $A_ j:H_{j+1}\to H_ j$ sends $K_{j+1}$ into $K_ j$. Its restriction into $K_ j$ equals its composition with the orthogonal projection onto $K_ j$. Its quotient link is, after identifying Hilbert quotients with orthogonal complements, a restriction followed by the projection onto $K_ j^\perp $. Both are nuclear by the ideal property. Orthogonal projections onto closed Hilbert subspaces follow by minimizing distance: the parallelogram identity makes a minimizing sequence Cauchy, and variation at its limit gives orthogonality. The subspace is Fréchet by closedness and the quotient is Fréchet by proposition 2.12.1.

For a countable product $\prod _ kE^ k$, choose Hilbertian nuclear families $q_ j^ k$ and use

\[ P_ j(x)^2=\sum _{k\leq j}q_ j^ k(x_ k)^2. \]

Its Hilbert completion is the finite orthogonal sum of the corresponding completions. A link is the finite sum of their nuclear links and is zero on the new coordinate; its nuclear norm is at most the sum of theirs. The product is Fréchet by coordinatewise completeness. An inverse limit with continuous bonding maps is the closed compatibility subspace in this countable product, proving the inverse-limit assertion.

For nuclear Banach maps $A,B$, tensoring their nuclear series gives a double series of rank-one operators, with nuclear norm at most $\| A\| _\nu \| B\| _\nu $ for the projective tensor norms: the source functional is $\xi _ j\otimes \eta _ k$, and the target vector is $y_ j\otimes z_ k$. Apply this to defining links in the two Fréchet spaces. Their projective tensor completions at each defining seminorm are precisely the completed Banach projective tensors, by the norm-completion facts above. Thus the completed projective tensor is nuclear.

Finally suppose $E$ nuclear. The preceding proposition gives, for every continuous seminorm $q$ on the other factor,

\[ \pi _{p_ j,q}(t)\leq C_ j\varepsilon _{p_{j+1},q}(t). \]

Together with $\varepsilon \leq \pi $ this proves equality of the two algebraic tensor topologies and hence of their Hausdorff completions. This proof uses a nuclear link and a shifted source seminorm; it does not identify the two norms at the same index. The nuclear tensor assertion for the injective completion now follows from the projective one. $\square$

For completeness, the nuclear ideal has the following additional range and quotient properties. A composition $B A$ of two Banach nuclear maps is nuclear into any closed target subspace containing its range, and on any quotient by a closed source subspace in its kernel. For the range statement factor $B=vu$ through a Hilbert space $H$, put $H_1=v^{-1}(T)$, and use the orthogonal projection onto $H_1$ after the nuclear map $uA$. For the quotient statement factor $A=vu$, put $H_1=\overline{u(S)}$, and use the bounded map $E/S\to H/H_1=H_1^\perp $ followed by $v$ and the nuclear map $B$. Continuity makes $Bv$ zero on $H_1$, so this factorization is well defined.

If $E$ is nuclear Fréchet, a continuous linear map $f:E\to F$ into a complete locally convex space is nuclear exactly when it factors by continuous linear maps through a Banach space. One direction was proved in the first lemma. For the other, a continuous map from $E$ into a Banach space is bounded by some $p_ j$, so insert the nuclear link $E_{p_{j+1}}\to E_{p_ j}$. In particular such a nuclear map remains nuclear when its range is regarded in a closed target subspace: in a Banach factorization restrict the intermediate space to the closed inverse image of that subspace. It also descends nuclearly through a closed kernel subspace $S$: quotient the intermediate Banach space by the closure of the image of $S$, then use nuclearity of $E/S$ just proved. No restriction theorem for an arbitrary single Banach nuclear map is asserted here.

Theorem 2.13.4.E8F18F08D2C8 For every complex manifold $X$ with a countable basis, $\mathcal O(X)$ with its compact-open topology is nuclear.

Proof. First let $D$ be a polydisc and choose concentric polydiscs $D_ j\Subset D_{j+1}$ increasing to $D$. The Hilbertian seminorms $\| f\| _{L^2(D_ j)}$ generate the compact-open topology: the upper estimate is the volume times the squared sup norm, and the reverse local estimate follows from the mean inequality for $|f|^2$ on balls compactly inside a larger polydisc. These are the Cauchy/submean estimates already used in singular values of Bergman restriction. They also show that an $L^2$-Cauchy sequence of holomorphic functions is locally uniformly Cauchy; its holomorphic limit agrees with its $L^2$ limit. Thus the Bergman spaces used below are complete.

The completion at this seminorm is $A^2(D_ j)$. Indeed, the normalized monomials are a complete orthonormal system there: expand a holomorphic function in its existing Taylor series, integrate squared moduli on concentric tori, and integrate the resulting nonnegative coefficient sum in the radial variables. Orthogonality and monotone convergence give Parseval and convergence of Taylor truncations in $L^2$. Polynomials belong to $\mathcal O(D)$, proving the asserted completion. For radii $r_ i<R_ i$ the restriction of normalized monomials from $A^2(D_ R)$ to $A^2(D_ r)$ is diagonal with factors

\[ a_\alpha =\prod _{i=1}^ n(r_ i/R_ i)^{\alpha _ i+1},\qquad \sum _{\alpha \in \mathbb N^ n}a_\alpha =\prod _ i\frac{r_ i/R_ i}{1-r_ i/R_ i}<\infty . \]

The coefficient functionals have norm one, so this is a nuclear representation of each restriction link. It proves nuclearity of $\mathcal O(D)$ by the Hilbertian criterion.

The original sup-norm version has the same correct direction. On functions holomorphic in $D_ R$ and continuous on its closure, Cauchy's inequalities give coefficient functionals $f\mapsto R^\alpha a_\alpha (f)$ of norm at most one. The monomials $z^\alpha /r^\alpha $ have sup norm one on $D_ r$, and the coefficients $(r/R)^\alpha $ sum to $\prod _ i(1-r_ i/R_ i)^{-1}$. Thus restriction from the larger polydisc algebra to the smaller one is nuclear. The completion of $\mathcal O(D)$ at a smaller closed-polydisc sup norm is that polydisc algebra: radial dilations converge uniformly on the closure, and Taylor polynomials approximate each dilation.

Now choose a countable coordinate-polydisc cover $(U_ a)$ of $X$. Restriction embeds $\mathcal O(X)$ into $\prod _ a\mathcal O(U_ a)$ as the families agreeing on overlaps. This image is closed: agreement at each overlap point is the kernel of a continuous difference of evaluations. Compatible families glue to a holomorphic function, and the induced topology is compact-open, because a compact set is covered by finitely many smaller coordinate neighborhoods compact in the $U_ a$. Countable products and closed-subspace permanence therefore finish the proof. Empty $X$ gives the zero space; in dimension zero the space is a countable product of copies of $\mathbb C$, which is covered by the same permanence result. $\square$

Theorem 2.13.5.7434BE36F417 Let $0\to E_1\to E_2\overset {Q}{\to }E_3\to 0$ be a strict short exact sequence of Fréchet spaces: $E_1$ is identified with a closed subspace and $E_3$ with the quotient topology. Let $F$ be Fréchet. If $E_2$ or $F$ is nuclear, then

\[ 0\longrightarrow E_1\widehat\otimes F \longrightarrow E_2\widehat\otimes F \longrightarrow E_3\widehat\otimes F\longrightarrow 0 \]

is strict exact. The tensor products may use either topology.

Proof. First the injective completion preserves a closed embedding. A continuous seminorm $p$ on $E_1$ has a continuous seminorm extension to $E_2$: choose $q$ on $E_2$ with $p\leq q$ on $E_1$, and set

\[ \widetilde p(x)=\inf _{a\in E_1}\{ p(a)+q(x-a)\} . \]

This is a seminorm bounded by $q$ and equals $p$ on $E_1$. Dominated linear extension gives equality of the injective tensor seminorms on the subspace. Hence the completed inclusion is a topological embedding; its image is closed, since its complete Fréchet source is complete for the induced uniform topology.

Next the projective completion preserves a Fréchet quotient surjection. Use increasing source seminorms $p_ j$ on $E_2$ and the quotient seminorms $\bar p_ j$ on $E_3$. An algebraic tensor of $\pi _{\bar p_ j,q}$-size less than $r$ lifts to one of $\pi _{p_ j,q}$-size less than $r$: in a finite decomposition, choose each coefficient lift arbitrarily close to its quotient seminorm infimum. Thus the closure of the image of every zero neighborhood in the completed source contains a zero neighborhood in the target. More precisely use any smaller radius and density of algebraic tensors in the completion. The successive-correction part of the proof of proposition 2.12.1 now proves surjectivity and openness: it needs just these neighborhoods in closures, not the preliminary Baire step that obtained them there. All completed spaces here are Fréchet.

If $E_2$ is nuclear then so are $E_1,E_3$; if $F$ is nuclear it is already the common nuclear factor. Thus projective and injective completions coincide for all three terms. The first map consequently has closed image $L$ and the last is open and surjective. To prove equality of $L$ with the kernel, suppose a vector in the kernel were outside $L$. Continuous linear separation of the Hausdorff quotient gives a functional $\ell $ vanishing on $L$ but not on that vector. Its bilinear form $b$ on $E_2\times F$ vanishes on $E_1\times F$ and obeys $|b(x,y)|\leq C p_ j(x)q(y)$ for suitable seminorms. It descends to a bilinear form on $E_3\times F$ with bound $C\bar p_ j([x])q(y)$, by taking the infimum over $E_1$. The descended functional on the projective completion therefore pulls back to $\ell $, contradicting its nonzero value on the kernel. This proves exactness and, with the already established closed embedding/open surjection, strictness. $\square$
