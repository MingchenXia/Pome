10.72 The dualizing complex and cohomological duality89386B6A23D6

89386B6A23D6

Proposition 10.72.1.9E818639205F Let $X$ be locally compact Hausdorff and paracompact, let $A$ be a sheaf of $R$-modules, and suppose $\operatorname {cd}X\le n$. Choose a soft resolution $A\to L^\bullet $ of length $n$ and an injective resolution $0\to M\to I^0\xrightarrow {\delta _ I}I^1\to 0$ over a PID. For $0\le p\le 1$ and $0\le q\le n$ put

\[ F^{p,q}(U)=\operatorname {Hom}_ R(\Gamma _ c(U,L^{n-q}),I^ p), \qquad Df=\delta _ If+(-1)^ p f d_ L. \]

These presheaves are flabby sheaves, and their total complex has a natural short exact sequence

\[ 0\to \operatorname {Ext}(H_ c^{n-q+1}(X,A),M) \to H^ q(\Gamma (X,F^\bullet )) \to \operatorname {Hom}(H_ c^{n-q}(X,A),M)\to 0. \]

It splits noncanonically. The assertions hold on every open subset with the restricted resolution and compact supports there.

Proof. We prove the section gluing premise. For a soft sheaf $L$ and two opens $U,V$, extension by zero gives the exact sequence

\[ 0\to \Gamma _ c(U\cap V,L)\to \Gamma _ c(U,L)\oplus \Gamma _ c(V,L) \to \Gamma _ c(U\cup V,L)\to 0. \]

Only surjectivity needs proof. Extend a compact section $s$ of the union by zero to $X$, and choose a relatively compact open $W$ containing its support. On the closed union $(X\setminus V)\cup (X\setminus U)\cup (X\setminus W)$ prescribe $s$ on the first set and zero on the latter two. These germ sections agree on the intersections, since $s$ is supported in $U\cup V$ and in $W$. Closed pasting and softness extend them to a global section $s_ U$. Its support is contained in $U\cap W$ and is compact; $s-s_ U$ has compact support in $V$. The kernel assertion follows because two opposite extended sections have support in the intersection.

For a finite cover, induction gives the cosheaf coequalizer: if $\sum _{i=1}^ rs_ i=0$, the last term lies in the intersection of its open set with the union of the preceding ones. Decompose it there by the just proved finite-cover surjectivity and subtract the resulting pairwise-overlap relations; then apply induction to the first $r-1$ terms. For an arbitrary cover, a compact support has a finite subcover, and an element of the direct sum uses only finitely many terms. Thus

\[ \bigoplus _{i,j}\Gamma _ c(U_ i\cap U_ j,L)\longrightarrow \bigoplus _ i\Gamma _ c(U_ i,L)\longrightarrow \Gamma _ c(U,L)\to 0 \]

is a coequalizer. Applying $\operatorname {Hom}(-,I)$ gives exactly the sheaf equalizer axiom. Extension by zero from one open to a larger one is injective, so injectivity of $I$ makes restriction of this Hom sheaf onto. It is flabby.

Restrictions of a globally soft sheaf to an open set are acyclic for compact supports. Indeed, for $V\Subset U$ the compact closed set $K=\overline V$ has soft restriction $L|_ K$. Extension by zero from the open subset $V$ of $K$ remains soft by proposition 10.56.6. Its positive cohomology on the compact Hausdorff space $K$ vanishes. The actual compact-neighborhood limit of theorem 10.56.7, applied to $U$, therefore gives $H_ c^ j(U,L|_ U)=0$ for $j>0$. This argument does not assume that an arbitrary open subset of a paracompact space is paracompact. Hence $\Gamma _ c(U,L^\bullet )$ computes $H_ c^\bullet (U,A)$ by theorem 10.56.3. Reverse indices to form the homological complex $K_ q=\Gamma _ c(U,L^{n-q})$, with boundary $d_ L$. The displayed differential is precisely that in theorem 10.68.6; in particular its two mixed terms have opposite signs, so $D^2=0$. That theorem gives the natural exact sequence and its separately chosen splitting, without finite-type assumptions. All restriction maps are duals of open extension, as required. $\square$

Proposition 10.72.2.13DBDC23AA55 On an $n$-manifold and for a local system $A$ of stalk $L$, the preceding complex has cohomology sheaves

\[ \mathcal H^0(F)=\tau _ X\otimes \mathcal Hom_ R(A,\underline M), \qquad \mathcal H^1(F)=\tau _ X\otimes \mathcal Ext_ R^1(A,\underline M), \qquad \mathcal H^ q(F)=0\quad (q\ne 0,1). \]

Here the second sheaf is the local system with fiber $\operatorname {Ext}_ R(L,M)$ and its contravariant coefficient transport. In particular

\[ \tau _ X(U)=\operatorname {Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(H_ c^ n(U,\mathbb Z),\mathbb Z). \]

The orientation system is constant exactly when $X$ is orientable; a choice of its unit section is the choice of an orientation. If $\operatorname {Ext}_ R(L,M)=0$, the complex $F$ is a flabby resolution of $\tau _ X\otimes \mathcal Hom_ R(A,\underline M)$.

Proof. Manifolds have sheaf dimension at most $n$ by the coordinate and local-dimension results used above, so the bounded soft resolution exists. On a ball trivializing $A$, compact cohomology is $L$ in degree $n$ and zero elsewhere. Its Hom sequence therefore gives $H^0(F(U))=\operatorname {Hom}(L,M)$ and $H^1(F(U))=\operatorname {Ext}(L,M)$, with all other groups zero. For nested coordinate balls these identifications are compatible: cap duality identifies the compact top-degree extension with the map on $H_0$ of the balls, carrying the chosen oriented coefficient to the same coefficient. It is an isomorphism. This proves the restriction assertion using open extension, without a deformation retraction onto a proper open subset.

Filtered stalk limits are exact, so the preceding calculations compute the cohomology sheaves. Changing a local orientation multiplies them by its sign, and transporting $A$ composes a Hom or Ext class with the inverse coefficient transport. These are exactly the displayed local systems. For $A=M=\mathbb Z$ the Ext fiber vanishes and the degree-zero sheaf is the already constructed $\tau _ X$. Since the complex begins in degree zero, sections of its degree-zero cohomology sheaf are its global zero-cocycles. The preceding natural sequence in degree zero has no Ext term, because $H_ c^{n+1}(U,\mathbb Z)=0$. It gives the displayed identity on every open $U$. A constant unit section chooses compatible local generators, which is precisely orientability. If the Ext fiber is zero, stalkwise exactness makes the augmented complex a flabby resolution, proving the last assertion. $\square$

Theorem 10.72.3.3968AEF4C8FE Let $A$ have stalk $L$, let $M$ be a module over a PID, and assume $\operatorname {Ext}_ R(L,M)=0$. There is a natural short exact sequence

\[ 0\to \operatorname {Ext}(H_ c^{n-q+1}(X,A),M) \to H^ q(X,\tau _ X\otimes \mathcal Hom_ R(A,\underline M)) \xrightarrow {\rho }\operatorname {Hom}(H_ c^{n-q}(X,A),M)\to 0. \]

It splits as modules, without a natural splitting. With cap normalization, $\rho $ is evaluation after the isomorphism $D_{X,A}$. For an oriented connected $X$ this is the compact-first cup product and orientation trace, followed by the coefficient evaluation. If $A=\underline R$ and $M=R$, the twist disappears when $X$ is oriented or when $R$ has characteristic two.

Proof. The preceding two propositions already construct such a sequence from a bounded soft resolution. To specify its product normalization and give a second explicit description, set $C_ j=C_ j^{\mathrm{sing}}(X,\tau _ X\otimes A)$. It is a direct sum of modules isomorphic to $L$; the choices of fiber identifications need not be canonical. Taking direct sums of length-one free resolutions and then Hom shows $\operatorname {Ext}(\bigoplus L,M)=\prod \operatorname {Ext}(L,M)=0$. Products are exact, so the augmentation

\[ \operatorname {Hom}(C_\bullet ,M)\longrightarrow \operatorname {Tot}\operatorname {Hom}(C_\bullet ,I^\bullet ) \]

is a quasi-isomorphism: in each chain degree its coefficient Hom complex has only degree-zero cohomology. This is an actual two-row first-quadrant comparison, not an assumed ordinary Hom exactness.

The left complex is canonically the singular cochain complex of $\tau _ X\otimes \mathcal Hom_ R(A,\underline M)$. At a simplex this identifies $\operatorname {Hom}(\tau _ x\otimes A_ x,M)$ with $\tau _ x\otimes \operatorname {Hom}(A_ x,M)$ using $\tau _ x^2=\mathbb Z$; precomposing the chain boundary gives precisely the previously specified transported cochain differential. Apply singular/sheaf comparison, the natural derived Hom sequence of theorem 10.68.6, and the actual isomorphisms $H_ q(C)=H_ c^{n-q}(X,A)$ supplied by cap duality. This yields the stated sequence with $\rho $ as asserted. Its splitting is the module splitting in that algebraic theorem.

Finally equation 10.71.4 identifies evaluation on $D_{X,A}(u)$ with evaluation of the coefficient-contracted cup product $u\smile v$ on the compact fundamental class. This is the orientation trace proved explicitly below. The local coefficient contraction is $A\otimes \mathcal Hom(A,M)\to M$; the two orientation factors cancel. Thus the indicated cup convention gives the stated map. The twist is trivial under the final hypotheses because its transition scalars are $\pm 1$. $\square$

Corollary 10.72.4.9C9125F4F2EE For any local system $A$ on a connected manifold,

\[ H_ c^ n(X,A)=H_0(X,\tau _ X\otimes A) \]

is the module of coinvariants of the transported fiber $\tau _ x\otimes A_ x$. In particular, for any constant module $L$,

\[ H_ c^ n(X,\tau _ X\otimes \underline L)\simeq L, \qquad H_ c^ n(X,\underline L)\simeq L/2L \quad \hbox{if $X$ is nonorientable}. \]

For an oriented connected $X$, the first identity gives the trace $\operatorname {tr}_ X:H_ c^ n(X,\underline L)\to L$. On a disconnected manifold compact top cohomology is the direct sum of its component groups; trace is the sum of the finitely many nonzero component traces. Ordinary cohomology is a product over components.

Proof. Cap duality in degree $n$ gives the first equality. In singular $H_0$, the boundary of an edge identifies a coefficient at its first vertex with its transport to the last. Choose paths from a basepoint to all vertices: these relations identify every class with one in the base fiber, and the remaining relations are exactly $\rho (\gamma )a-a$ for based loops. Conversely transport to the coinvariant module kills every edge boundary, proving the asserted presentation. For $\tau \otimes L$, the additional orientation twist squares to the trivial system, so the coinvariants are $L$. For constant $L$ on a nonorientable connected manifold, the orientation character has image $\{ 1,-1\} $, giving precisely the quotient by $2L$. The identification for $\tau \otimes L$ is canonical without an orientation choice. An orientation trivializes $\tau $ in the untwisted case and defines the trace. On a ball it sends the positive compact generator with coefficient $l$ to $l$, and open extension preserves it by cap naturality.

Components of a manifold are open. A compact support meets only finitely many of them, whereas an unrestricted section can be chosen on every component. The Godement complexes are consequently the direct sum and the product, respectively, of the component complexes. Both operations are exact for module families, proving the final assertions and the sum trace. $\square$

Proposition 10.72.5.B7AF8FB473E5 For oriented $X$ and any modules $L,M$, with no Ext hypothesis,

\[ H_ c^{n-q}(X,\underline L)\times H^ q(X,\underline M) \longrightarrow L\otimes M, \qquad (u,v)\longmapsto \operatorname {tr}_ X(u\smile v) \]

is a natural bilinear pairing. When the cohomological duality hypothesis $\operatorname {Ext}(L,L\otimes M)=0$ holds, it is its map induced by $M\to \operatorname {Hom}(L,L\otimes M)$ in cap normalization. Reversing the order of the factors multiplies the pairing by $(-1)^{q(n-q)}$ with the corresponding tensor flip.

Proof. The supported cup theorem constructs the product with the indicated coefficient and support, and the preceding trace contracts it. The cap evaluation identity proves the comparison under the stated Ext hypothesis. Graded commutativity in theorem 10.58.3 gives the final sign. Thus the pairing exists even when the Ext hypothesis needed for a short exact duality sequence does not hold.

One can also check the signs directly in the bounded dualizing resolution. This records a normalization that an unsigned comparison would leave ambiguous. Assume first $X$ connected and take $L=R$. Let $B^\bullet =C_ X^\bullet \underline M$ and $L^ j=C_ X^ j\underline R$ for $j<n$, with $L^ n$ the kernel of its next differential. Embed $E=\Gamma _ c(X,B^ n)/d\Gamma _ c(X,B^{n-1})$ in an injective $I^0$. Its cycle submodule is $H_ c^ n(X,M)=M$ by trace. Hence the induced map $d:E\to \Gamma _ c(X,B^{n+1})$ identifies $E/M$ with its image. The module $I^1=I^0/M$ is injective over a PID, since it is divisible. Extend the inclusion $E/M\to I^1$ from this image to a map $\pi :\Gamma _ c(X,B^{n+1})\to I^1$ by injectivity. Set $\varepsilon _ q=(-1)^{q(q+1)/2}$. A cochain $f\in B^ q(U)$ maps to the following pair of functionals in $F^ q(U)$:

\[ f\longmapsto \left( g\longmapsto \varepsilon _ q[f\smile g],\quad h\longmapsto (-1)^ q\varepsilon _ q\pi (f\smile h)\right), \]

where $g\in \Gamma _ c(U,L^{n-q})$ and $h\in \Gamma _ c(U,L^{n-q+1})$; a term outside the bounded resolution is zero. All products extend compactly to $X$ before taking the quotient. Truncate $B$ at its degree-$n$ cycles as well.

The first-component chain identity follows from $[df\smile g]=(-1)^{q+1}[f\smile dg]$ in the quotient $E$ and $\varepsilon _{q+1}=(-1)^{q+1}\varepsilon _ q$. For the second component, the relation $\delta _ I[f\smile h]=\pi (d(f\smile h))$ and Leibniz give

\[ \varepsilon _ q\pi (df\smile h) +\bigl ((-1)^ q\varepsilon _ q-(-1)^ q\varepsilon _ q\bigr ) \pi (f\smile dh). \]

This is the required second component in the next degree because $(-1)^{q+1}\varepsilon _{q+1}=\varepsilon _ q$. Thus this is an actual morphism of differential sheaves for the specified $D$. In degree zero it induces the identity on $M$ by the chosen trace, so it is the identity-augmenting resolution comparison. Its edge functional is $\varepsilon _ q\operatorname {tr}_ X(f\smile g)$, or $\varepsilon _ q(-1)^{q(n-q)}\operatorname {tr}_ X(g\smile f)$. This explains explicitly the unit signs relating that dualizing complex convention to the compact-first cap normalization; changing a convention does not change the short exact sequence or its nonnatural splitting. Componentwise construction gives the same conclusion without connectedness. $\square$
