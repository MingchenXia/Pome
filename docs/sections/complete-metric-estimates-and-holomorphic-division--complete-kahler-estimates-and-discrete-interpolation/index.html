6.26 Complete Kähler estimates and discrete interpolation7957A1D91471

7957A1D91471

Manifolds in this section are Hausdorff, second countable and without boundary. Completeness of a Riemannian metric means completeness of its length distance on each connected component. We use the metrics, unscaled curvature and formal adjoints of section 10.33; in particular $\mathrm dV_\omega =\omega ^ n/n!$ and $\mathrm i\Theta (L)=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial \phi $ for a line frame of squared norm $\mathrm e^{-\phi }$. Write $\mathcal H(f)=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial f$.

Lemma 6.26.1.87F36288392E A complete smooth Riemannian manifold admits smooth compactly supported functions $0\leq \chi _ R\leq 1$, $R\geq 1$, eventually equal to one near every compact set, with

917B532008FD 6.26.2
\begin{equation} |\mathrm d\chi _ R|_ g\leq C/R \end{equation}

for one constant $C$ independent of $R$.

Proof. On a connected component let $r_0$ be distance from a fixed point. It is $1$-Lipschitz, and its closed sublevels are compact by lemma 9.28.1. In the disconnected case enumerate the components and add their indices to the respective distance functions. There are at most countably many components, and the resulting $r_0\geq 0$ is still proper and is $1$-Lipschitz within each component.

Choose a countable locally finite cover by nested coordinate balls $U_ i\Subset W_ i$ and a smooth nonnegative partition of unity $\theta _ i$ with compact support in $U_ i$, as in manifold cutoffs, partitions and compact-support Stokes. Make the balls small enough that the matrix of $g$ on $W_ i$ lies between half and twice one fixed positive matrix $g_ i$. Take $W_ i$ to be a coordinate Euclidean ball. Straight segments show that $r_0$ in these coordinates is Lipschitz with constant $\sqrt2$ for the norm $g_ i$.

Let $A_ i=\sup |\mathrm d\theta _ i|_ g<\infty $ and choose $0<e_ i\leq \min \{ 1,2^{-i}/(1+A_ i)\} $. Convolve $r_0$ on $W_ i$ with a nonnegative smooth kernel of sufficiently small radius to obtain a smooth $r_ i$ near $\overline U_ i$ such that

\[ |r_ i-r_0|\leq e_ i,\qquad |\mathrm dr_ i|_ g\leq 2. \]

For clarity, the gradient estimate does not require differentiating the distance function: the difference of two convolution values along a coordinate vector $v$ is bounded by $\sqrt2|v|_{g_ i}$ times the increment. Taking its derivative and comparing the dual norms of $g_ i$ and $g$ gives the factor2. The approximation error follows from the same Lipschitz estimate times the convolution radius.

Set $r=\sum _ i\theta _ i r_ i$. This is smooth and $|r-r_0|\leq 1$. At each point its differential satisfies

\[ \mathrm dr=\sum _ i\theta _ i\mathrm dr_ i+ \sum _ i(r_ i-r_0)\mathrm d\theta _ i, \qquad |\mathrm dr|_ g\leq 2+\sum _ i e_ iA_ i\leq 3. \]

Here $\sum _ i\mathrm d\theta _ i=0$ cancels the unbounded value $r_0$ after differentiating the smooth summands; $r_0$ itself has not been differentiated. The function $r$ is proper and $r\geq -1$. Choose a smooth $\eta :\mathbb R\to [0,1]$ equal to one on $(-\infty ,1]$ and zero on $[2,\infty )$, and put $\chi _ R=\eta ((r+2)/R)$. These functions have the stated support and eventual equality properties, and $|\mathrm d\chi _ R|_ g\leq 3\| \eta '\| _\infty /R$. $\square$

Corollary 6.26.3.B586BBD14EA1 For a smooth Riemannian manifold the following are equivalent: completeness; existence of a smooth exhaustion $s$ with $|\mathrm ds|_ g\leq 1$; and existence of compact sets $K_\nu \subset \operatorname {Int}K_{\nu +1}$ exhausting the manifold and smooth $0\leq \psi _\nu \leq 1$ with $\psi _\nu =1$ near $K_\nu $, $\operatorname {Supp}\psi _\nu \subset \operatorname {Int}K_{\nu +1}$ and $|\mathrm d\psi _\nu |_ g\leq 2^{-\nu }$.

Proof. Completeness gives $s=(r+2)/3$ from the preceding construction. Conversely, a finite continuous exhaustion is bounded below and may be shifted to be nonnegative. Its bounded gradient makes it Lipschitz for the length distance. A Cauchy sequence therefore lies in a compact sublevel; a convergent subsequence and the metric topology give its convergence, proving completeness.

Given $s\geq 0$ with gradient at most one, choose a smooth decreasing profile $\eta $ equal to one up to1.1 and zero from1.9 onwards. Take $A\geq \max \{ 1,\| \eta '\| _\infty \} $, set $K_\nu =\{ s\leq A2^\nu \} $ and $\psi _\nu =\eta (s/(A2^\nu ))$. The strict transition margins give the asserted support and neighborhood conditions and the derivative bound follows by the chain rule. Finally, given those compact sets and cutoffs, the sum $s=\sum _{\nu \geq 1}2^\nu (1-\psi _\nu )$ is locally finite and is a smooth nonnegative exhaustion: outside $K_{N+1}$ its first $N$ terms sum to $\sum _{\nu =1}^ N2^\nu $. The support of $\mathrm d\psi _\nu $ lies in $\operatorname {Int}K_{\nu +1}\setminus K_\nu $; these sets are pairwise disjoint. At most one differentiated term contributes at any point, so $|\mathrm ds|_ g\leq 1$. $\square$

Lemma 6.26.4.3A311E8B52D5 Let $(X,\omega )$ be complete Hermitian and $(E,h)$ a smooth Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle. In every bidegree the maximal distributional operator $D''$ is closed and densely defined. Its Hilbert adjoint is exactly the maximal distributional formal adjoint $\delta ''$. Compactly supported smooth forms are dense in each of their graph domains and in their joint graph domain.

Proof. Convergence in the global $L^2$ norms implies local distributional convergence, proving closedness. Chart cutoffs and coefficient mollification give density of compactly supported smooth forms in $L^2$, hence dense operator domains.

Use lemma 6.26.1. The product rules are

\[ D''(\chi _ Rv)=\chi _ RD''v+\bar\partial \chi _ R\wedge v, \qquad \delta ''(\chi _ Rv)=\chi _ R\delta ''v- (\bar\partial \chi _ R\wedge )^*v. \]

Wedge and contraction by a covector have operator norm at most its norm, in every degree and bundle rank, by a unitary-coframe calculation. Thus each error is bounded in $L^2$ by $C\| v\| /R$. Dominated convergence reduces each maximal graph approximation, simultaneously when required, to compact support. On that support use a finite frame cover and partition of unity. The complete smooth-matrix first-order commutator proof in graph-norm density by cutoffs and mollification applies to the joint vector of operators $(D'',\delta '')$ in any bidegree and rank. It gives compactly supported smooth approximation in the joint graph norm; using either operator alone gives its individual assertion.

If $v$ is in the Hilbert adjoint domain, testing on compact supports identifies its adjoint distribution as $\delta ''v$. Conversely, if $v,\delta ''v$ are $L^2$, approximate any $u$ in the maximal $D''$ domain in its graph norm by compactly supported smooth forms. The distributional identity $\langle D''u_ j,v\rangle = \langle u_ j,\delta ''v\rangle $ passes to the limit. This is precisely the defining Hilbert adjoint identity. This proves domain equality by graph approximation. $\square$

For a positive semidefinite Hermitian endomorphism $B$ of a coefficient fiber use the dual energy $|g|_{B^{-1}}^2=\sup _{v\ne 0}|\langle g,v\rangle |^2/\langle Bv,v\rangle $, with $0/0=0$ and a positive numerator over zero equal to infinity. The finite-dimensional facts checked in section 6.25 apply in smooth local frames; in particular this is measurable and has the usual Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. This notation does not assert invertibility of $B$.

Theorem 6.26.5.84AFC5445E6E Let $(X,\omega )$ be complete Kähler, of dimension $n\geq 1$, and let $(E,h)$ be a smooth Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle. Fix $0\leq p\leq n$, $1\leq q\leq n$, and suppose $B=[\mathrm i\Theta (E),\Lambda _\omega ]\geq 0$ in bidegree $(p,q)$. If $g\in L^2_{p,q}(X,E)$ satisfies $D''g=0$ distributionally and

\[ M=\int _ X|g|_{B^{-1}}^2\, \mathrm dV_\omega <\infty , \]

there is $u\in L^2_{p,q-1}(X,E)$ with

FDA2D94CB453 6.26.6
\begin{equation} D''u=g,\qquad \| u\| ^2\leq M. \end{equation}

Its unique solution of smallest norm satisfies $\delta ''u=0$ distributionally and is smooth in $X$ when $g$ is smooth.

Proof. For compactly supported smooth $v$, the Kähler identity theorem 10.33.2 gives

\[ \int _ X\langle Bv,v\rangle \, \mathrm dV_\omega \leq \| D''v\| ^2+\| \delta ''v\| ^2. \]

Set $T=D''$ from degree $(p,q-1)$ to $(p,q)$ and $S=D''$ to degree $(p,q+1)$ with maximal domains. For $q=n$ the latter space is zero and $S=0$. The preceding lemma supplies closedness, dense domains, $T^*=\delta ''$ and joint graph density; $ST=0$ follows from $(D'')^2=0$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[ |\langle g,v\rangle |^2 \leq M\bigl (\| T^*v\| ^2+\| Sv\| ^2\bigr ). \]

Since $g$ is globally $L^2$, both sides are continuous in the joint graph norm, so this holds on the full domains. Apply Hilbert-complex solvability from the dual energy estimate to obtain (6.26.6). Its minimum-norm solution is orthogonal to the closed kernel of $T$. The preceding maximal $D''$ operator has range in that kernel; the adjoint definition gives $\delta ''u=0$. When $q=1$ this equation is automatic.

We justify the claimed local regularity. In local coordinates and smooth frames the principal symbol of $\Delta ''$ is $|\xi |_\omega ^2\operatorname {Id}/2$, as explicitly computed in equation 10.33.13. For a smooth compactly supported coefficient vector $w$ in a fixed relatively compact coordinate ball, the same integration-by-parts calculation gives

8AF50F0EE8AA 6.26.7
\begin{equation} \| w\| _{H^1}^2\leq C\bigl ( \| D''w\| ^2+\| \delta ''w\| ^2+\| w\| ^2\bigr ). \end{equation}

Indeed its principal form is $\tfrac 12\int g^{ij}G_{AB}\partial _ jw_ A \overline{\partial _ iw_ B}\rho $, where $G$ is the positive coefficient metric and $\rho $ the volume density. It is uniformly positive on the entire matrix of first derivatives on this compact set. The remaining terms have absolute value at most $C\| w\| \| w\| _{H^1}$ and are absorbed by Young's inequality. This is a local use of the proved calculation; compactness of $X$ is not assumed.

Now $D''u=g$ and $\delta ''u=0$ are locally $L^2$. Localize by a compactly supported chart cutoff and use the same matrix commutator approximation as above. The smooth approximants are bounded in $H^1$ by (6.26.7). Weak compactness of their derivatives identifies the weak limit as the distributional derivatives of the localized $u$, so $u\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$. The equation $\Delta ''u=\delta ''g$ has smooth right-hand side. Its divergence-form principal coefficients are $\tfrac 12g^{ij}G_{AB}\rho $ and its remaining coefficients are smooth first- and zeroth-order matrices. Apply the full finite-vector theorem interior regularity for elliptic systems in divergence form on successively smaller balls, and then the local embedding in Sobolev spaces, duality and local embedding, to obtain smoothness. No global Laplacian self-adjointness or harmonic decomposition is used. $\square$

In degree $(n,q)$, proposition 10.33.6 describes $B$ by the Nakano form on the tensors $(v_{jS})_ j$. Thus a lower curvature bound $\varepsilon (x)$ on tensors of rank at most $\min (n-q+1,\operatorname {rank}E)$ gives $B\geq q\varepsilon (x)$. For a line bundle with semipositive curvature eigenvalues $\lambda _1\leq \cdots \leq \lambda _ n$, the inverse energy is bounded by $|g|^2/(\lambda _1+\cdots +\lambda _ q)$. These statements retain the global $L^2$ hypothesis of the theorem; no noncomplete-metric estimate has been inferred from them.

Lemma 6.26.8.4BD00ED77586 If a Kähler manifold $(X,\omega _0)$ has a smooth PSH exhaustion $\rho \geq 0$, then $\omega _0+\mathcal H(\mathrm e^\rho )$ is complete Kähler. If $\rho $ is strictly PSH, then $\omega =\mathcal H(\mathrm e^\rho )$ itself is complete Kähler, without a prior Kähler metric.

Proof. The chain rule gives

\[ \mathcal H(\mathrm e^\rho )=\mathrm e^\rho (\mathcal H(\rho )+\mathrm i\partial \rho \wedge \bar\partial \rho ). \]

Each stated form is closed and positive definite. In its induced real metric, $|\mathrm d\rho |^2\leq 2\mathrm e^{-\rho }\leq 2$, by the displayed rank-one lower bound and the relation $|\mathrm d\rho |^2=2|\partial \rho |^2$. Consequently a Cauchy sequence for the length distance has bounded $\rho $-values and lies in one compact sublevel. It has a convergent subsequence in the manifold topology, which is the length topology by the local metric comparison proved before lemma 9.28.1. Cauchyness makes the whole sequence converge. This proves completeness on each component. $\square$

We will use a simple fast-weight construction on a manifold with a smooth nonnegative strict-PSH exhaustion $\rho $. Put $\beta =\mathcal H(\rho )>0$. If $A$ is any continuous positive $(1,1)$-form, $C\geq 0$ is continuous, and $F\geq 0$ is locally integrable, then there is a smooth convex increasing $\chi $ such that

B9439CD62E8F 6.26.9
\begin{equation} \mathcal H(\chi \circ \rho )\geq C\beta +A, \qquad \int _ X F\mathrm e^{-\chi (\rho )}\, \mathrm dV_\omega <\infty . \end{equation}

Here $\omega $ may be any fixed smooth Hermitian form. To check the construction explicitly, bound $C\beta +A$ relative to $\beta $ on each compact sublevel and let $a_ k=\int _{\{ k\leq \rho \leq k+1\} }F\, \mathrm dV_\omega <\infty $. Choose nondecreasing $M_ k\geq 1$ larger than the relative-form bound on $\{ \rho \leq k+2\} $ and than $\log (2^{k+1}(1+a_{k+1}))$. For a smooth nondecreasing step $\zeta $ equal to zero on $(-\infty ,0]$ and one on $[1,\infty )$ set

\[ b(t)=M_0+\sum _{k\geq 0}(M_{k+1}-M_ k)\zeta (t-k), \qquad \chi (t)=\int _0^ t b(s)\, \mathrm ds. \]

These sums are locally finite. On $[k,k+1]$, $\chi '\geq M_ k$; the chain rule proves the form bound. For $k\geq 1$, $\chi (k)\geq M_{k-1}\geq \log (2^ k(1+a_ k))$, so the integral on that band is at most $2^{-k}$. The initial compact band has finite integral. This proves (6.26.9).

Corollary 6.26.10.E5505B695336 Let $X$ have a smooth PSH exhaustion, and let $(E,h)$ be an $m$-positive holomorphic vector bundle of rank $r$, with $m\geq 1$. For $1\leq q\leq n$ and $m\geq \min (n-q+1,r)$, every distributionally closed locally $L^2$ $E$-valued $(n,q)$-form has a locally $L^2$ primitive. For smooth data a smooth primitive exists; in particular the smooth analytic Dolbeault group $H^{n,q}_{\bar\partial }(X,E)$ is zero.

Proof. Positivity on rank-one tensors implies $\omega _0=\mathrm i\operatorname {Tr}\Theta (E)>0$: evaluate on a nonzero base vector and sum its strictly positive pairings over an orthonormal fiber basis. This is a closed form, since the Chern matrix formula gives $\operatorname {Tr}\Theta (E)=\bar\partial \partial \log \det H$ in a holomorphic frame. Thus $X$ is Kähler. Shift its PSH exhaustion $\rho $ to be nonnegative and use lemma 6.26.8 to make a complete Kähler form $\widehat\omega $.

By proposition 10.33.6, the curvature operator $B$ on $(n,q)$ is positive definite. Its least eigenvalue $\lambda (x)>0$ is continuous. The bandwise step construction just used for (6.26.9), without its strict-Hessian domination requirement, chooses a smooth convex increasing $\chi $ such that

\[ \int _ X(1+\lambda ^{-1})|g|^2 \mathrm e^{-\chi (\rho )}\, \mathrm dV_{\widehat\omega } <\infty . \]

Only the finite integrals on compact exhaustion bands are needed. For $h_\chi =h\mathrm e^{-\chi (\rho )}$ the curvature increases by the semipositive scalar form $\mathcal H(\chi (\rho ))$; in holomorphic top degree its operator therefore increases as well. The displayed integral bounds both the ordinary $L^2$ norm and inverse-curvature energy for $(E,h_\chi ,\widehat\omega )$. Apply theorem 6.26.5. Its solution is locally $L^2$, and is smooth for smooth data by the proved minimum-norm regularity. This establishes the analytic quotient conclusion without a sheaf-cohomology identification. $\square$
