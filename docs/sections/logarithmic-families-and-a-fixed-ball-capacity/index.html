16.31 Logarithmic families and a fixed-ball capacity7526AE0AA6AA

7526AE0AA6AA

Use the canonical logarithmic growth class $\mathcal L$ and the raw extremals $V_ E$; use the existing Josefson theorem with upper growth $\log ^+|z|$. Thus a set is pluripolar exactly when it is contained in the pole set of a member of $\mathcal L$. This equivalence and its countable-union assertion keep their existing entries.

Theorem 16.31.1.62CEBBF93786 Let $\mathcal U\subset \mathcal L$ be nonempty and put $F=\sup _{u\in \mathcal U}u$. If $\{ F<\infty \} $ is non-pluripolar, then $\mathcal U$ is locally uniformly bounded above and $F^*\in \mathcal L$. For any $E\subset \mathbb C^ n$, either $E$ is pluripolar and $V_ E^*\equiv +\infty $, or $V_ E^*\in \mathcal L$.

Proof. If local uniform bounds fail, choose a closed ball $B=\overline{B(a,r)}$ and $u_ j\in \mathcal U$ with $M_ j=\sup _ Bu_ j\geq 2^ j$. The norm-ball formula gives

\[ w_ j=u_ j-M_ j\leq p:=\log ^+(|z-a|/r)\quad \hbox{on }\mathbb C^ n. \]

There is $z_0$ with $\limsup _ jw_ j(z_0)>-\infty $. Otherwise Hartogs' lemma, using the common local upper bound $p$, would give $\sup _ Bw_ j\to -\infty $, contrary to its value zero. Pass to a subsequence with $w_ j(z_0)\geq -C$, retaining $M_ j\geq 2^ j$ after relabeling. The sum $w=\sum _{j\geq 1}2^{-j}w_ j$ is PSH on the whole space and finite at $z_0$: on each ball subtract a constant from every $w_ j$ to make the series decrease, then use its finite value at $z_0$ on a larger connected ball. It satisfies $w\leq p$ and hence belongs to $\mathcal L$. At any point where $F(z)<\infty $,

\[ \sum _{j=1}^ N2^{-j}w_ j(z) \leq \Bigl (\sum _{j=1}^ N2^{-j}\Bigr )F(z)-N\longrightarrow -\infty . \]

The case $F(z)=-\infty $ is immediate as well. Thus $\{ F<\infty \} $ is pluripolar, a contradiction. Once local bounds hold, a common bound $M$ on one ball gives $F\leq M+p$ by the same formula. The upper-envelope theorem proves $F^*\in \mathcal L$.

If $E$ is non-pluripolar, the extremal family is finite (indeed zero) on $E$, so the first assertion applies. If $E$ is pluripolar, let $b\in \mathcal L$ have poles on $E$. Every $b+C$ is a competitor; their supremum is infinite wherever $b$ is finite, hence almost everywhere. Its regularization is identically infinite. $\square$

Proposition 16.31.2.A76B9D558430 For bounded $E$ and pluripolar $P$, $V_{E\cup P}^*=V_ E^*$. If $E_ j\uparrow E$ with bounded union, then $V_{E_ j}^*\downarrow V_ E^*$ pointwise. For arbitrary $E$, the points $a\in E$ at which $V_ E$ is not continuous form a pluripolar set.

Proof. If $E$ is pluripolar, so is $E\cup P$, and both regularizations are infinite. Otherwise choose a logarithmic pole $b$ on $P$, subtracting a constant so that $b\leq 0$ on the bounded set $E$. For every competitor $u$ of $V_ E$,

\[ u_\epsilon =\frac{u+\epsilon b}{1+\epsilon } \]

remains in $\mathcal L$ and is nonpositive on $E\cup P$. It tends to $u$ almost everywhere, giving $u\leq V_{E\cup P}^*$ everywhere by canonical PSH representatives. Supremum and regularization give one inequality; monotonicity gives the other. Applying this to $E\setminus P$ also proves invariance on deleting a pluripolar subset of a bounded obstacle. No unnormalized slope-$(1+\epsilon )$ sum is used.

For increasing sets, if all $E_ j$ are pluripolar, so is their union and all values are infinite. Otherwise discard finitely many indices so every $E_ j$ is non-pluripolar. Enclose $E$ in a closed ball $B$. The decreasing sequence $V_{E_ j}^*$ is bounded below by $V_ B$ and above by $V_{E_1}^*$. Its limit $v$ is PSH in $\mathcal L$. For each $j$, the canonical envelope discrepancy theorem makes $E_ j\cap \{ V_{E_ j}^*>0\} $ pluripolar. Off their countable union $P$, we have $v\leq 0$ on $E$. Polar obstacle invariance therefore gives $v\leq V_{E\setminus P}^*=V_ E^*$; monotonicity gives equality.

Finally, if arbitrary $E$ is pluripolar, any subset of it is pluripolar. Otherwise the family theorem gives $V_ E^*\in \mathcal L$, and $\{ V_ E<V_ E^*\} $ is pluripolar by the canonical discrepancy theorem. At $a\in E$ outside this set we have $0=V_ E(a)=V_ E^*(a)$. Since $0\leq V_ E\leq V_ E^*$, upper semicontinuity of the last function proves continuity of the raw envelope at $a$. $\square$

Theorem 16.31.3.E215D05E52EC Fix a closed Euclidean ball $B$ of positive radius. For arbitrary $E$ put

\[ \alpha _ B(E)=\exp \bigl (-\sup _ B V_ E^*\bigr ),\qquad e^{-\infty }=0. \]

Then $0\leq \alpha _ B\leq 1$, its zero sets are exactly the pluripolar sets, and it is monotone. It is continuous on increasing sequences with bounded union and on decreasing sequences of compact sets. Consequently its restriction to the subsets of any bounded open set is a generalized capacity with these three continuity/monotonicity axioms.

Proof. The bounds, zero sets and monotonicity follow from the family theorem and $V_ E\geq 0$. For $E_ j\uparrow E$ with bounded union, the preceding proposition gives decreasing upper semicontinuous functions on the compact test ball. Their suprema decrease to the supremum of their limit: if the limit were smaller, points of maximum have a convergent subsequence and upper semicontinuity of each fixed earlier function gives a contradiction. If the union is non-pluripolar, countable-union polarity permits discarding finitely many infinite terms; otherwise every capacity is zero. Exponentiation proves increasing continuity.

Let $K_ j\downarrow K$ be compact and set $u_ j=V_{K_ j}^*$. If any $K_ j$ is pluripolar then $K$ is pluripolar and all later capacities vanish. Otherwise the $u_ j$ are nonnegative finite PSH functions increasing with $j$. The raw compact-limit theorem gives $V_{K_ j}\uparrow V_ K$, whence $(\sup _ j u_ j)^*=V_ K^*$, with the value $+\infty $ allowed.

Write $B^\circ $ for the open test ball. For any PSH function $u$ defined near $B$, $\sup _ Bu=\operatorname *{ess\, sup}_{B^\circ }u$. The submean inequality proves the assertion at interior points. At a boundary point, small balls have an asymptotically positive fraction inside $B^\circ $; if $u$ exceeded the interior supremum there, upper semicontinuity bounds the remaining fraction by $u$'s value plus an arbitrarily small error, contradicting the submean inequality.

If $\sup _ j\sup _ Bu_ j$ is infinite, then so is $\sup _ BV_ K^*$ by monotonicity, and the desired limit follows. If it is finite, the increasing family is uniformly bounded on $B^\circ $. There its regularized supremum equals $V_ K^*$ and agrees with the pointwise supremum almost everywhere by the local upper-envelope theorem. This local bound on $V_ K^*$, followed by the global norm-ball majorant for its raw competitors, makes $V_ K^*$ a finite PSH function on a neighborhood of $B$. The interior/boundary supremum identity therefore applies to it as well. Thus

\[ \sup _ BV_ K^* =\operatorname *{ess\, sup}_{B^\circ }\sup _ j u_ j =\sup _ j\operatorname *{ess\, sup}_{B^\circ }u_ j =\sup _ j\sup _ Bu_ j. \]

Exponentiation now proves decreasing compact continuity, including the case of a pluripolar intersection. $\square$

Proposition 16.31.4.4146861A3795 Define

\[ \gamma (E)=\limsup _{|z|\to \infty }(V_ E^*(z)-\log |z|), \qquad c(E)=e^{-\gamma (E)}. \]

Use $e^{-\infty }=0$ and $e^{+\infty }=+\infty $. Then $c$ is monotone, vanishes exactly on pluripolar sets, and is continuous on increasing sequences with bounded union. For the unit test ball, $\alpha _{\overline B(0,1)}(E)\leq c(E)$.

Proof. If $E$ is non-pluripolar, the growth bound on $V_ E^*$ makes $\gamma (E)$ bounded above, so $c(E)>0$; the polar case has $V_ E^*\equiv +\infty $ and $c(E)=0$. Monotonicity is immediate from the obstacle inequalities.

For non-pluripolar $E$, put $\chi =\log (1+|z|^2)$ and use the canonical correspondence to extend $p_ E=V_ E^*-\chi /2$ to a $\tfrac 12\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$-PSH function on $\mathbb P^ n$. For the hyperplane at infinity $H$, canonical extension and compactness give $\gamma (E)=\sup _ Hp_ E$: $\chi /2-\log |z|\to 0$ at infinity, every sequence approaching $H$ obeys the upper bound by upper semicontinuity, and the canonical limsup realizes every boundary value.

Let $E_ j\uparrow E$ have bounded union. If all $E_ j$ are pluripolar, all capacities are zero. Otherwise discard finitely many indices and use the preceding extremal continuity. The corresponding $p_{E_ j}$ decrease on $\mathbb P^ n$, and are bounded below by the bounded projective potential of one enclosing norm ball. Their limit is therefore a proper PSH potential, equals $p_ E$ on the affine chart, and hence equals its canonical extension everywhere. Suprema on the compact $H$ decrease to $\sup _ Hp_ E$ by the same compact upper semicontinuity argument as above. This proves the asserted continuity.

Finally, for non-pluripolar $E$, set $M=\sup _{\overline B(0,1)}V_ E^*$. The global norm-ball formula gives $V_ E^*\leq M+\log ^+|z|$. Thus $\gamma (E)\leq M$ and the claimed inequality follows; for pluripolar $E$ both sides are zero. $\square$
