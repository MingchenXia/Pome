3.7 Polynomial approximation and a separate-subharmonic example7AF53FD9F731

7AF53FD9F731

Proposition 3.7.1.6D285B395FD0 Let $K\subset \mathbb C$ be compact with connected complement. Every function holomorphic on a neighborhood of $K$ is a uniform limit on $K$ of polynomials.

Proof. The empty set is immediate. For nonempty $K$, choose a sufficiently fine finite grid of squares in the neighborhood, including an extra layer around the squares meeting $K$. Their union contains $K$ in its interior and has boundary disjoint from $K$. Summing the Cauchy formula over the squares cancels interior edges and gives an integral representation of $f(z)$ for $z\in K$ over the remaining oriented edges, whose winding number is one there. On this boundary times $K$ the Cauchy integrand is uniformly continuous. Its Riemann sums therefore approximate $f$ uniformly by rational functions with poles outside $K$.

Let $A$ be the uniform closure of polynomials in $C(K)$; it is a closed algebra. The set of $a\in \mathbb C\setminus K$ for which $(z-a)^{-1}\in A$ is relatively closed by uniform continuity of the resolvent in $a$. It is relatively open by the convergent geometric expansion about such an $a$ for $|b-a|<\operatorname {dist}(a,K)$. It contains all sufficiently large $a$, by expansion in $z/a$. Connectedness makes it the whole complement. Products then put every higher resolvent power in $A$, so all rational approximants above belong to $A$. This proves the assertion. $\square$

There is a finite nonnegative function on $\mathbb C^2$ that is subharmonic in each complex variable separately and is not jointly subharmonic. Use the argument branch $0<\arg z\leq 2\pi $ and set

\[ a_ j=j^{-1}e^{i/(j+1)},\qquad K_ j=\{ z:|z|\leq j,\ 1/j\leq \arg z\leq 2\pi \} \cup \{ 0\} . \]

These are compact, $a_ j\notin K_ j$, and their complements are connected: the missing open sector connects every complementary point to the exterior of the disc. Removing the additional point $a_ j$ preserves connectedness, by detouring a path around it in a small disc. Approximate the locally constant holomorphic function zero near $K_ j$ and $j+1$ near $a_ j$ on $K_ j\cup \{ a_ j\} $ by a polynomial $Q_ j$ with error $\eta _ j<1/8$. After the constant correction $P_ j=Q_ j+j+1-Q_ j(a_ j)$, we have $P_ j(a_ j)=j+1$ and $\| P_ j\| _{K_ j}<1/4<1/2$.

Put $b_ j=\max (|P_ j|-1,0)$ and

\[ F(z,w)=\sum _{j\geq 1}b_ j(z)b_ j(w). \]

Each $b_ j$ is continuous nonnegative subharmonic. Every fixed $z$ belongs to $K_ j$ for all sufficiently large $j$, including zero and the positive real ray with argument $2\pi $. Thus $b_ j(z)=0$ eventually and $F$ is finite. At fixed $w$ its sum has only finitely many nonzero coefficients, so it is subharmonic in $z$, and likewise in $w$. But $F(0,0)=0$, whereas $F(a_ j,a_ j)\geq j^2$ and $a_ j\to 0$. Joint upper semicontinuity fails. The total two-variable function has the single name $F$; an index $j$ on it would confuse the summation index with a free parameter.
