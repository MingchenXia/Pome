17.18 Partial Legendre transforms and Monge–Ampère mass12D395D414C1

12D395D414C1

Use $dd^ c=(i/2\pi )\partial \bar\partial $ and put $y=\log |\tau |^2$, so that $d^ cy=d\arg \tau /(2\pi )$ on $\mathbb C^*$. The angular measure has total mass one.

The partial transforms are plurisubharmonic by the Kiselman minimum principle on a product tube, applied in logarithmic coordinates after subtracting the affine function $\lambda y$. Local boundedness on the entire closed parameter interval is a hypothesis in the following lemma.

Lemma 17.18.1.C12669D1EC3D Let $U\subset \mathbb C^ n$ and let $u(z,\tau )$ be psh on $U\times \mathbb C^*$, invariant under rotation of $\tau $. Let $a<b$. Suppose its partial Legendre transforms

\[ \widehat u_\lambda (z)=\inf _{y\in \mathbb R}\bigl (u(z,e^{y/2})-\lambda y\bigr ) \]

are locally bounded psh functions for $\lambda \in [a,b]$ and are identically $-\infty $ outside this interval. Then

\[ (\pi _ U)_*(dd^ cu)^{n+1} =(n+1)\int _ a^ b(dd^ c\widehat u_\lambda )^ n\, d\lambda . \]

For a smooth closed background $\theta $ on a compact complex $n$-fold, the corresponding identity holds for non-pluripolar products if all the transforms are locally bounded off one common proper analytic set.

Proof. Absorb a local potential for $\theta $ when a background is present. First suppose the functions are smooth and $u_{yy}>0$. At the unique minimizer $y=y(z,\lambda )$, $\lambda =u_ y$ and the complex Hessian of $\widehat u_\lambda $ in the $z$ variables is the Schur complement

\[ (\widehat u_\lambda )_{i\bar j} =u_{i\bar j}-\frac{u_{iy}u_{y\bar j}}{u_{yy}}. \]

The block-determinant identity consequently expresses the top product as

\[ (dd^ cu)^{n+1} =(n+1)u_{yy}\, dy\wedge \frac{d\arg \tau }{2\pi } \wedge (dd^ c_ z\widehat u_\lambda )^ n. \]

Integration in $y$ and the substitution $d\lambda =u_{yy}\, dy$ give the formula, including its coefficient $n+1$.

Here is a bounded approximation argument that also controls the noncompact fiber. By an affine rescaling assume $[a,b]=[0,1]$. For the moment consider an affine concave segment with a small strictly concave correction,

\[ v_ s=(1-s)\varphi +s\psi -\delta s^2,\qquad 0\leq s\leq 1, \quad \delta >0, \]

where $\varphi ,\psi $ are locally bounded psh. For smooth $\varphi ,\psi $ the Legendre supremum is explicit. Its maximizing parameter is $s=(\psi -\varphi +y)/(2\delta )$ when this lies in $(0,1)$, and is an endpoint otherwise. The supremum is $C^{1,1}$; on an endpoint region it is affine in $y$ and its top product is zero. On the intervening region the preceding determinant calculation applies. There is no hypersurface term at the interfaces, because the first derivatives agree there. This proves the identity for the segment. Convolve $\varphi ,\psi $ on a slightly larger coordinate set and decrease the convolution radius. Their suprema decrease, are locally uniformly bounded, and the transition region in $y$ stays in a fixed bounded interval over each compact base set. Bedford–Taylor monotone continuity and the local mixed-mass bound therefore give the same identity for locally bounded $\varphi ,\psi $.

For a general concave family $\widehat u_\lambda $, interpolate its values at the dyadic mesh $j2^{-N}$ linearly, and subtract $\lambda ^2/N$. Denote this family by $v^ N_\lambda $ and its Legendre supremum by $u_ N$. It is concave in $\lambda $ and psh in $z$. Concavity and local boundedness at the endpoints and at two interior parameters give upper and lower bounds for the whole family on compact base sets. These bounds justify upper semicontinuity of the supremum by uniform finite-grid approximation. Thus $u_ N$ is psh.

For fixed $N$, convolve all the finitely many mesh values using the same kernel. This preserves their concavity inequalities. With smooth mesh values, the maximizing parameter is unique, piecewise in one mesh interval; the supremum is $C^{1,1}$. On a region where the maximizer is a mesh node it is affine in $y$ and has zero top product. On the other regions the affine-segment calculation applies. Summing those identities gives

\[ (\pi _ U)_*(dd^ cu_ N)^{n+1} =(n+1)\int _0^1(dd^ cv^ N_\lambda )^ n\, d\lambda . \]

Removing the simultaneous convolution is legitimate by the same bounded monotone-continuity argument; for this fixed finite mesh all relevant $y$-intervals remain bounded over a fixed compact base set.

The dyadic interpolants, and the corrections $-\lambda ^2/N$, increase to the original family at every interior parameter. Hence $u_ N$ increases almost everywhere to $u$. To pass to pushforwards one must also prevent mass escaping to $|y|=\infty $. Fix compact coordinate sets $K\Subset K'\Subset U$. The locally uniform bounds just obtained give, by the local mixed-mass estimate, a common bound for $\int _ K(dd^ cv^ N_\lambda )^ n$ for all $N,\lambda $. Concavity bounds the parameter slopes on $[\varepsilon ,1-\varepsilon ]$ by $C_{K'}/\varepsilon $. Thus, outside $|y|\leq C_{K'}/\varepsilon $, the maximizing parameters lie in endpoint intervals of total length at most $2\varepsilon $. The already proved finite-mesh identity bounds the top mass there by $C_ K\varepsilon $, uniformly in $N$. This is the required tail bound. Bounded monotone continuity on compact cylinders, followed by this tail bound, permits passage to the pushforward. On the right, monotone continuity for each interior parameter and the uniform local mass bound permit integration in $\lambda $. Endpoints have Lebesgue measure zero. This proves the local identity.

Off a common analytic set the preceding argument applies to local potentials of a smooth closed background. Both non-pluripolar products give zero mass to that analytic set and its product with $\mathbb C^*$. The local identities consequently glue and extend by zero, proving the last assertion. $\square$
