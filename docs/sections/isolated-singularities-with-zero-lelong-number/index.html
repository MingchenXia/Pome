6.50 Isolated singularities with zero Lelong number281E2F71EBD4

281E2F71EBD4

All products at an isolated pole in this section are the classical products of the theorem on classical products with compact unbounded loci. They include any mass at the pole. Write $\mathbb D^2$ for the unit bidisc and $\| \zeta \| _\infty =\max \{ |z|,|w|\} $ for $\zeta =(z,w)$. Let $F_\varepsilon $, $\varepsilon _ j$, $H_ j$ and $p_ j$ be the maps, parameters, iterates and weights of definition 6.49.3. In particular, $p_ j<0$ on $\overline{\mathbb D^2}$, its only pole is zero, and $e^{p_ j}$ is continuous there. Put

E147430C1211 6.50.1
\begin{equation} M_ j=2\cdot 4^ j,\qquad W_ j=\max \{ p_ j,-M_ j\} ,\qquad K_ j=\{ \zeta \in \overline{\mathbb D^2}:p_ j(\zeta )\leq -M_ j\} . \end{equation}

Lemma 6.50.2.9D91DC8AF289 For $0<\varepsilon \leq 1/4$ and $\| \zeta \| _\infty \leq 1$,

\[ \| F_\varepsilon (\zeta )\| _\infty \geq \frac18\| \zeta \| _\infty ^4. \]

Consequently

2BCC4F872AFE 6.50.3
\begin{equation} p_ j(\zeta )\geq 2^{j+1}\log \| \zeta \| _\infty -2(\log 2)(2^ j-2^{-j}). \end{equation}

Each $K_ j$ contains a neighborhood of zero, is compactly contained in $\mathbb D^2$, and satisfies

E8FFA55A2EB8 6.50.4
\begin{equation} K_ j\subset \{ \| \zeta \| _\infty \leq 2e^{-2^ j}\} . \end{equation}

Proof. Set $r=\max \{ |z|,|w|\} $. If $|w|\geq r^2/2$, the first component of $F_\varepsilon $ has modulus at least $r^4/8$. If $|w|<r^2/2$, then $r>0$ and $r=|z|$, since $r\leq 1$. The second component has modulus at least

\[ \frac12r^2-\varepsilon |w|> \frac12r^2-\frac18r^2=\frac38r^2\geq \frac18r^4. \]

This proves the estimate; at zero it holds directly. Every iterate stays in the closed bidisc. Iterating the estimate therefore gives

\[ \| H_ j(\zeta )\| _\infty \geq 8^{-(1+4+\cdots +4^{j-1})}\| \zeta \| _\infty ^{4^ j} =2^{1-4^ j}\| \zeta \| _\infty ^{4^ j}. \]

Taking $2^{-j}$ times the logarithm of its square proves (6.50.3).

If $\zeta \in K_ j\setminus \{ 0\} $, this bound implies

\[ \log \| \zeta \| _\infty \leq -2^ j+(\log 2)(1-4^{-j})\leq -2^ j+\log 2, \]

which proves (6.50.4), including zero. The function $e^{p_ j}$ is continuous on the closed bidisc and vanishes at zero. Its sublevel at the positive number $e^{-M_ j}$ is closed and contains a neighborhood of zero. Finally, $2e^{-2}<1$, so the displayed radius bound places this closed set inside a compact subset of the open bidisc. $\square$

Lemma 6.50.5.EA0EF7A5BB47 For every $j\geq 1$,

E3DD353DBBC8 6.50.6
\begin{equation} -2^{1-j}\log 2\leq p_{j+1}-p_ j\leq 0 \quad \hbox{on }\{ p_ j\geq -M_ j\} . \end{equation}

On the whole bidisc, $p_{j+1}\leq W_ j$ and $W_{j+1}\leq W_ j$.

Proof. Fix a nonzero point and write $H_ j(\zeta )=(z,w)$ and $r=\max \{ |z|,|w|\} >0$. Directly from the definition,

0394B9F53F55 6.50.7
\begin{equation} p_{j+1}(\zeta )-p_ j(\zeta ) =2^{-j}\log \frac{\| F_{\varepsilon _{j+1}}(z,w)\| _\infty }{r^2}. \end{equation}

On the indicated set, $r\geq e^{-8^ j}$ and hence $\varepsilon _{j+1}=e^{-8^ j}/4\leq r/4$. The two components of $F_{\varepsilon _{j+1}}(z,w)$ have moduli at most $r^2/2$ and $3r^2/4$. If $r=|w|$, the first component has modulus $r^2/2$; if $r=|z|$, the second has modulus at least $r^2/2-\varepsilon _{j+1}r\geq r^2/4$. Thus the ratio in (6.50.7) belongs to $[1/4,1]$. This proves (6.50.6), and in particular $p_{j+1}\leq W_ j$ there.

If $p_ j(\zeta )<-M_ j$, put $R_ j=e^{-8^ j}$ and $r=R_ jx$, where $0<x<1$. Then

\[ \| F_{\varepsilon _{j+1}}(z,w)\| _\infty \leq R_ j^2\left(\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{x}{4}\right) <R_ j^2. \]

It follows that $p_{j+1}(\zeta )<-2\cdot 4^ j=-M_ j=W_ j(\zeta )$. At zero, $p_{j+1}(0)=-\infty $, so the global assertion holds there as well. Finally $-M_{j+1}\leq -M_ j\leq W_ j$; taking the maximum with $p_{j+1}$ proves $W_{j+1}\leq W_ j$. $\square$

Proposition 6.50.8.4058F9C6D2AC The bounded negative plurisubharmonic functions $W_ j$ decrease to a negative plurisubharmonic function $U$ on $\mathbb D^2$. Its pole set is $\{ 0\} $, it is continuous outside zero, and $e^ U$ is continuous on the whole bidisc. Both $p_ j$ and $W_ j$ converge locally uniformly to $U$ on the punctured bidisc.

Proof. Each $W_ j$ is bounded and plurisubharmonic by closure under finite maxima for plurisubharmonic functions, and is strictly negative. The preceding lemma proves decreasing convergence to the extended-valued function $U=\inf _ jW_ j$. Let $L$ be a compact subset of the punctured bidisc. The radius bound (6.50.4) supplies $N$ such that $L\cap K_ k=\varnothing $ for every $k\geq N$. For $\ell >k\geq N$, the visible comparison gives

\[ 0\leq p_ k-p_\ell \leq \sum _{s=k}^{\ell -1}2^{1-s}\log 2 \leq 2^{2-k}\log 2\qquad \hbox{on }L. \]

Thus the finite continuous functions $p_ j$ are uniformly Cauchy on $L$. For $j\geq N$, $W_ j=p_ j$ there. Their common limit is finite and continuous on $L$.

The decreasing-limit theorem for plurisubharmonic functions therefore applies with the identically $-\infty $ alternative excluded. Thus $U$ is plurisubharmonic and $U\leq W_1<0$. Since $W_ j(0)=-M_ j\to -\infty $, its pole set is exactly zero. For any $A>0$, choose $j$ with $M_ j>A$. The set $K_ j$ contains a neighborhood of zero, on which $U\leq W_ j=-M_ j<-A$. Therefore $U(\zeta )\to -\infty $ as $\zeta \to 0$, proving continuity of $e^ U$ at zero and hence on the whole bidisc. $\square$

Lemma 6.50.9.1D1AC8862EEA If $p_ j(\zeta )>-M_ j$, then $p_ k(\zeta )>-M_ k$ for every $k\geq j$ and

EA1C01079C02 6.50.10
\begin{equation} 0\leq p_ j(\zeta )-U(\zeta )\leq 2^{2-j}\log 2. \end{equation}

Proof. The visible comparison implies

\[ p_{k+1}(\zeta )>-M_ k-2^{1-k}\log 2>-M_{k+1} \]

whenever $p_ k(\zeta )>-M_ k$. The last inequality holds for $k\geq 1$, since $M_{k+1}-M_ k=6\cdot 4^ k$ and $2^{1-k}\log 2\leq \log 2$. Induction proves that every later cutoff is inactive. Summing (6.50.6) from $j$ to $m-1$ and letting $m\to \infty $ proves (6.50.10). $\square$

Theorem 6.50.11.367D36FB36A0 The explicit function

\[ U(\zeta )=\lim _{j\to \infty } \max \{ 2^{-j}\log \| H_ j(\zeta )\| _\infty ^2,-2\cdot 4^ j\} \]

is negative and plurisubharmonic on $\mathbb D^2$ and satisfies

FC2BE4EE5D1B 6.50.12
\begin{equation} U^{-1}(-\infty )=\{ 0\} ,\qquad e^ U\in C^0(\mathbb D^2),\qquad \nu (U,0)=0,\qquad (dd^ cU)^2=\delta _0. \end{equation}

Proof. The assertions about its sign, pole set and continuity follow from proposition 6.50.8.

The mass. By proposition 6.49.4, $(dd^ cp_ j)^2=\delta _0$. On the boundary of the bidisc, (6.50.3) gives

\[ p_ j\geq -2(\log 2)(2^ j-2^{-j})>-M_ j. \]

Since $p_ j$ is continuous near that compact boundary, choose $s_ j$ strictly between $-M_ j$ and the boundary minimum. Then $\{ p_ j<s_ j\} $ is relatively compact in $\mathbb D^2$. The weight $p_ j$ is extended-continuous, with its only pole at zero. The bidisc admits the smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion $-\log (1-|z|^2)-\log (1-|w|^2)$. Apply the swept boundary measure formula at level $-M_ j$. Its restriction term $\mathbf1_{\{ p_ j\geq -M_ j\} }(dd^ cp_ j)^2$ vanishes. Hence $(dd^ cW_ j)^2$ is a probability measure supported on $\{ p_ j=-M_ j\} \subset K_ j$. For every $\chi \in C_ c(\mathbb D^2)$,

\[ \left|\int \chi (dd^ cW_ j)^2-\chi (0)\right| \leq \sup _{K_ j}|\chi -\chi (0)|\longrightarrow 0, \]

by (6.50.4). The classical continuity theorem cited at the start applies to $W_ j\downarrow U$: every point admits a coordinate ball whose boundary avoids the isolated pole, and all these functions are locally bounded on its boundary collar. It follows that $(dd^ cW_ j)^2$ also converges to $(dd^ cU)^2$. The two weak limits give $(dd^ cU)^2=\delta _0$.

The Lelong number. Let $e_2=(0,1)$ and

\[ c_ j=4^{-j}\exp \left(-\frac{8^ j-1}{7}\right),\qquad L_ j=-\log c_ j=j\log 4+\frac{8^ j-1}{7},\qquad \zeta _ j=c_ je_2. \]

The positive-ray estimate for the iterates gives $\| H_ j(\zeta _ j)\| _\infty \geq c_ j^2$. Thus $B_ j=-p_ j(\zeta _ j)$ satisfies

\[ 0\leq B_ j\leq 2^{2-j}L_ j, \qquad \frac{2^{2-j}L_ j}{M_ j}\longrightarrow \frac27<1. \]

For all sufficiently large $j$ the point $\zeta _ j$ is therefore in the visible set $\{ p_ j>-M_ j\} $. The tail estimate gives

\[ U(\zeta _ j)\geq -B_ j-2^{2-j}\log 2. \]

Since $U\leq 0$ and $\log \| \zeta _ j\| _\infty ^2=-2L_ j<0$,

\[ 0\leq \frac{U(\zeta _ j)}{\log \| \zeta _ j\| _\infty ^2} \leq 2^{1-j}+\frac{2^{1-j}\log 2}{L_ j}\longrightarrow 0. \]

Finally $c_ j\to 0$. The logarithmic growth characterization of the Lelong number identifies $\nu (U,0)$ with the lower limit of the quotient in the last display. Indeed, its spherical-supremum formulation gives this lower limit by choosing a point attaining the supremum on each small sphere. Replacing the Euclidean norm by the maximum norm changes its logarithm by a bounded amount. The resulting nonnegative lower limit is zero along $\zeta _ j$, so $\nu (U,0)=0$. $\square$

6.50.13 Isolated poles in higher dimensionB8EC48BF7AB0

Corollary 6.50.14.ECF3A574075A For every $n\geq 2$ there is a negative plurisubharmonic function $U_ n$ on the unit polydisc $\mathbb D^ n$ whose unbounded locus is $\{ 0\} $, such that $e^{U_ n}$ is continuous,

\[ \nu (U_ n,0)=0, \qquad (dd^ cU_ n)^ n=\delta _0. \]

The Monge–Ampère measure here is the classical measure at the isolated pole.

Proof. Let $U$ be the two-dimensional function of theorem 6.50.11. This proves the case $n=2$. For $n>2$ put $m=n-2$, write coordinates as $(z,w)$, and set

\[ V(w)=\log \| w\| _\infty ^2=\max _{1\leq j\leq m}\log |w_ j|^2, \qquad U_ n(z,w)=\max \{ U(z),V(w)\} . \]

The function $V$ is negative plurisubharmonic with its only pole at zero, and $(dd^ cV)^ m=\delta _0$ by the anisotropic logarithmic-pole formula. Moreover,

\[ e^{U_ n(z,w)}=\max \{ e^{U(z)},\| w\| _\infty ^2\} . \]

This is continuous and vanishes only at $(0,0)$; consequently $U_ n$ is finite and locally bounded away from that point. It is negative and plurisubharmonic by holomorphic pullback and the maximum operation. If $a=\nu (U_ n,0)$, the attained logarithmic growth bound restricts on $w=0$ to $U(z)\leq a\log |z|^2+O(1)$. The same growth characterization gives $0=\nu (U,0)\geq a\geq 0$.

Fix $0<r<1$ and work on $\Omega _1=r\mathbb D^2$ and $\Omega _2=r\mathbb D^ m$. Both potentials are continuous and finite on neighborhoods of their respective boundaries, so their sufficiently low closed sublevels are compact in those domains. For all sufficiently large $T$ put

\[ f_ T=\max \{ U,-2T\} +2T, \qquad g_ T=\max \{ V,-2T\} +2T. \]

The boundary sweeping formula applies to these extended-continuous weights on the polydiscs, which admit the smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustions $-\sum _ j\log (r^2-|z_ j|^2)$ and $-\sum _ j\log (r^2-|w_ j|^2)$. Since the original classical measures are unit point masses at their poles, it gives probability measures

\[ \begin{gathered} (dd^ cf_ T)^2\text{ supported on }\{ U=-2T\} \subset \{ f_ T=0\} , \\ (dd^ cg_ T)^ m\text{ supported on }\{ V=-2T\} \subset \{ g_ T=0\} . \end{gathered} \]

The preceding theorem therefore gives

\[ \bigl (dd^ c\max \{ U_ n,-2T\} \bigr )^ n =(dd^ cf_ T)^2\otimes (dd^ cg_ T)^ m, \]

because $\max \{ f_ T,g_ T\} =\max \{ U_ n,-2T\} +2T$. These product probability measures have supports contained in $\{ U\leq -2T\} \times \{ V\leq -2T\} $, which shrink to $(0,0)$. Indeed, on any compact set separated from the respective origin, the continuous functions $e^ U$ and $e^ V$ have a positive minimum; the low sublevels already lie in a fixed compact subset of each domain. The product measures consequently tend weakly to $\delta _0$.

Finally, $\max \{ U_ n,-2T\} \downarrow U_ n$. The classical decreasing-continuity theorem applies in every dimension: near the isolated unbounded point one chooses a coordinate ball whose boundary avoids it, and outside that point the potentials are locally bounded. It identifies the last weak limit with $(dd^ cU_ n)^ n$ on $r\mathbb D^ n$. As $r<1$ was arbitrary, this proves the formula on $\mathbb D^ n$. $\square$
