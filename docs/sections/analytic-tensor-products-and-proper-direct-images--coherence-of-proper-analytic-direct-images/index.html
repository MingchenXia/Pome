9.47 Coherence of proper analytic direct images0D3949EEEF52

0D3949EEEF52

Lemma 9.47.1.81A3D17F4D37 Let $X$ be a Hausdorff analytic space, possibly nonreduced, and let finitely many open patches $U_ i$ be closed analytic subspaces of polydiscs $\Omega _ i$. Their intersection $U_ I$ is a closed analytic subspace of $\prod _{i\in I}\Omega _ i$ under the map giving all its coordinate embeddings. If $g:X\to Y\subset W\subset \mathbb C^ m$ is analytic and $D\subset W$ is open, the graph of $g$ on $U_ I\cap g^{-1}D$ is closed analytic in $(\prod _{i\in I}\Omega _ i)\times D$. The direct image of any coherent sheaf under these closed embeddings is coherent, and its section spaces and their intrinsic topologies are unchanged.

Proof. A limit in the indicated product of a net of diagonal points has, in every coordinate patch, a limit point $x_ i\in U_ i$, because each $U_ i$ is closed in $\Omega _ i$. The inverse coordinate maps are continuous, so the same net in $X$ converges to every $x_ i$. Hausdorffness makes all these points equal; their common point lies in $U_ I$ and gives the product limit. Thus the diagonal image is closed. At a point of that image choose one patch as coordinates $z$ and extend the other coordinate maps locally holomorphically as $H_ i(z)$. If $\mathcal J(z)$ is the full defining ideal of this first patch, the nonreduced diagonal is locally defined by

\[ \mathcal J(z),\qquad w_ i-H_ i(z)\quad (i\ne i_0). \]

The quotient is the original structure ring. Indeed substitution removes the $w_ i$'s, and the kernel of substitution is their displayed graph ideal: for a convergent germ $F(z,w)$, subtract $F(z,H(z))$ and integrate its derivatives along the segment from $H(z)$ to $w$ in a sufficiently small polydisc. The difference is a sum of $(w_ i-H_ i(z))$ times convergent germs. This argument keeps $\mathcal J$ itself, not its radical. It proves the local analytic closed-embedding assertion.

The graph assertion follows by adjoining target coordinates $\zeta -g(z)$ with the same substitution calculation. Closedness follows from the already closed diagonal and continuity of $g$: any limit $(x,\zeta )$ has $\zeta =g(x)\in D$. Each graph ideal is locally finitely generated, hence coherent by Oka. The nonreduced coherent direct-image argument of the local resolution theorem lifts relation generators and adjoins the defining ideal in every coordinate. It proves coherence of the pushed sheaf. Sections under a closed embedding are identical; the intrinsic coherent-section theorem identifies their topologies, including all nilpotent module germs. $\square$

Theorem 9.47.2 (Proper analytic direct images).1A9A12704621 Let $g:X\to Y$ be a proper analytic morphism of Hausdorff, countable-basis analytic spaces, possibly nonreduced. For every coherent $\mathcal O_ X$-module $\mathcal S$ and every $q\geq 0$, the analytic higher direct image $R^ qg_*\mathcal S$ is a coherent $\mathcal O_ Y$-module.

Proof. Fix $y_0\in Y$ and restrict to an open neighborhood of $y_0$ embedded as a closed analytic subspace of $W\subset \mathbb C^ m$. Properness is preserved by this open restriction: the preimage of each compact subset of the restricted target is the same compact set as before. Choose polydiscs $D\Subset D_0\Subset W$ centered at $y_0$. The set

\[ K=g^{-1}(Y\cap \overline{D_0}) \]

is compact because the target intersection is compact and $g$ is proper. If it is empty, all these preimages and the resulting cohomology sheaves are zero near $y_0$, so the assertion holds. Otherwise choose finitely many patches $U_ a^0\subset g^{-1}(Y\cap W)$ closed analytic in polydiscs $\Omega _ a^0$, and concentric smaller polydiscs

\[ \Omega '_ a\Subset \Omega _ a\Subset \Omega _ a^0, \qquad U_ a=U_ a^0\cap \Omega _ a,\quad U'_ a=U_ a^0\cap \Omega '_ a, \]

such that the $U'_ a$ cover $K$. To obtain them, choose three nested sufficiently small coordinate polydiscs around each point of $K$ and take a finite subcover of the smallest patches. Thus both larger and smaller families cover $g^{-1}(Y\cap B)$ for every open $B\subset D_0$. This fixed compact preimage and its fixed finite cover will be used for every subsequent shrinking of the base.

For each nonempty increasing tuple $I$ of patch indices put $\Omega _ I^0=\prod _{a\in I}\Omega _ a^0$ and similarly $\Omega _ I,\Omega '_ I$. Put $U_ I^0=\bigcap _{a\in I}U_ a^0$. The preceding lemma embeds $U_ I^0\cap g^{-1}(D_0)$ by its diagonal coordinates and the graph of $g$ as a closed analytic subspace of $\Omega _ I^0\times D_0$. Push $\mathcal S$ restricted to this graph forward and call the coherent sheaf $\mathcal S_ I$. For every open $B\subset D_0$ the actual section identifications are

6DBD55D2F0A7 9.47.3
\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} \mathcal S(U_ I\cap g^{-1}B)& =\mathcal S_ I(\Omega _ I\times B),\\ \mathcal S(U'_ I\cap g^{-1}B)& =\mathcal S_ I(\Omega '_ I\times B). \end{aligned} \end{equation}

They are topological module identifications, and restriction between them is ordinary restriction in the $\Omega _ I$ variable. There is no assertion that an arbitrary ambient intersection of polydiscs is pseudoconvex; the overlap is instead the explicitly proved closed diagonal subspace of a product of polydiscs.

If $B$ is a polydisc, each intersection on the left is closed analytic in the strict-PSH space $\Omega _ I\times B$ or $\Omega '_ I\times B$ through its graph. Restrict the sum of nonnegative strict-PSH exhaustions to this closed analytic subspace. It remains proper and strongly1-complete, including at singular and nonreduced points by the analytic ambient convention. The coherent vanishing theorem therefore makes both finite covers acyclic, with all finite intersections acyclic.

Let $A=\mathcal O(D)$ and $D_ t$ denote the concentric polydisc of radius $t$ times that of $D$. Form the alternating Čech complexes

\[ E^ p=\bigoplus _{|I|=p+1}\mathcal S_ I(\Omega _ I\times D), \qquad F^ p=\bigoplus _{|I|=p+1}\mathcal S_ I(\Omega '_ I\times D). \]

There are finitely many increasing tuples. These complexes are zero in negative degrees and above one less than the number of patches. The checked integral alternating/full Čech homotopy identifies their cohomology with that of the ordinary acyclic-cover complexes, naturally and $A$-linearly. Consequently the restriction chain map $r:E\to F$ induces the canonical identity on $H^ q(g^{-1}(Y\cap D),\mathcal S)$ in every degree. All cochain restrictions and differentials are continuous.

By proposition 9.46.6, applied with the first factor $D_0$ and the second $\Omega _ I^0$, each term of $E,F$ is a fully $A$-transverse nuclear module. Its $t$th base change is exactly the corresponding section over $\Omega _ I\times D_ t$ or $\Omega '_ I\times D_ t$. Finite sums preserve these facts. By proposition 9.46.1, $r^ p$ is $A$-subnuclear: each of its finitely many components restricts across $\Omega '_ I\Subset \Omega _ I\Subset \Omega _ I^0$, with $D\Subset D_0$. The fully nuclear finite-free-model theorem 9.46.7 gives, for any fixed $0<t<1$, a bounded-above complex $L^\bullet $ of finite-rank free $\mathcal O(D_ t)$-modules and a chain map $h:L\to E_ t$ inducing all cohomology isomorphisms. No Hausdorffness of the cohomology of $E$ or $F$ has been assumed to apply this theorem.

The matrices of $L$ are holomorphic on $D_ t$, so define a complex $\mathcal L^\bullet $ of finite free analytic sheaves there. Let $B\Subset D_ t$ be any smaller polydisc, not necessarily concentric. The analytic restriction transversality theorem makes $\mathcal O(B)$ transverse over $\mathcal O(D_ t)$ to every term of $E_ t$, by (9.47.3) and its larger $D_0\times \Omega _ I^0$ presentation. It is transverse to the free terms of $L$ as well. Apply the corrected cone transversality theorem to $h$ in every degree. Its base change remains a quasi-isomorphism and is exactly

\[ \mathcal L^\bullet (B)\longrightarrow AC^\bullet ((U_ a\cap g^{-1}B),\mathcal S). \]

The right cover is acyclic as already proved; hence this map gives $H^ q(\mathcal L^\bullet (B))\simeq H^ q(g^{-1}(Y\cap B),\mathcal S)$ for every $q$. These isomorphisms commute with further small-polydisc restriction: they arise from base change of the single map $h$, the functorial free-factor/analytic restriction identifications and natural acyclic-cover comparison. Associativity here has Hausdorff transverse intermediate modules, exactly as in the proved module calculus.

Sheafify on this polydisc basis. Filtered stalk limits commute with kernels, images and cohomology, so the left side becomes $\mathcal H^ q(\mathcal L^\bullet )$. The direct-image stalk proposition 9.43.2 identifies the right side with $R^ qg_*\mathcal S$, viewed on $D_ t$ by the closed embedding of $Y\cap D_ t$. This last embedding has exact direct image on stalks, so its direct image commutes with cohomology of the Godement direct-image complex; no composition spectral sequence is required. Thus this direct image is locally the cohomology of a finite-rank free analytic complex. Coherent-sheaf algebra and Oka make each such cohomology sheaf coherent, without any global closed-range assertion about its section complex.

Finally the full defining ideal $\mathcal J_ Y$ annihilates the right cochain sections: its pullback under the morphism to the nonreduced space $Y$ is zero. It therefore annihilates the identified cohomology sheaf. A coherent ambient module annihilated by $\mathcal J_ Y$ is coherent over $\mathcal O_ W/\mathcal J_ Y$. Indeed its finite local generators still generate over that quotient; the relations of any finite family over the quotient are the images of the coherent ambient relation module, so are locally finitely generated. This proves coherence as an $\mathcal O_ Y$-module, retaining its nilpotent action. Since $y_0$ was arbitrary, the theorem follows. $\square$
