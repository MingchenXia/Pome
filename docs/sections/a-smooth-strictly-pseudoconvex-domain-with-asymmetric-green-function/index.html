7.22 A smooth strictly pseudoconvex domain with asymmetric Green functionA0F714BDFC5E

A0F714BDFC5E

Proposition 7.22.1.8B924AA3ED4A There is a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain in $\mathbb C^2$ with real analytic boundary whose pluricomplex Green function is asymmetric. On some such domain and at some pole, the total symmetric-envelope slice mass is strictly greater than $2^{-2}$, possibly infinite.

Proof. Fix $a=1/2$ and choose $\epsilon >0$ sufficiently small. Put

\[ P(z,w)=w^2-z^2(z-a),\qquad u(z,w)=\max \left\{ \tfrac 12\log \frac{|P(z,w)|}{\epsilon ^2}, \log |z|\right\} ,\qquad D=\{ u<0\} . \]

This is bounded, since $|z|<1$ and $|P|<\epsilon ^2$ bound $w$. It is connected: the proper finite holomorphic map $(z,w)\mapsto (z,P/\epsilon ^2)$ maps it onto $\Delta ^2$, and every component maps openly and properly, hence onto that connected target. Each component would therefore contain a preimage of $(0,0)$, whose only preimage is $(0,0)$. There can be only one component.

The maximum $u$ is continuous off $(0,0)$, zero at the boundary, and $u=\log |(z,w)|+O(1)$ at zero. The last assertion follows from $|P|\leq C|(z,w)|^2$ near zero and $|w|^2\leq |P|+C|z|^2$. It is maximal off zero: if one holomorphic factor is zero, its logarithm is inactive locally; otherwise take their local holomorphic logarithms, subtract one pluriharmonic term, and express the maximum as a convex function of the real part of one holomorphic scalar function. Its smooth approximations have complex Hessian rank at most one, so their top products in dimension two vanish. Bounded continuity passes this to $u$. Also $\max (u,-1)$ is a continuous negative PSH exhaustion of $D$. Green uniqueness therefore gives $u=g_ D(\cdot ,0)$, in particular $g_ D((a,0),0)=\log a$.

Let $L=\log (1/\epsilon )$ and $c_\epsilon =1/2+L^{-1/2}<1$. The corrected competitor for the other pole is

\[ v(z,w)= \begin{cases} \log (|w|/2),& |z-a|\leq \epsilon ,\\ \max \{ \log (|w|/2),c_\epsilon \log |(z-a)/(1-az)|\} , & |z-a|\geq \epsilon . \end{cases} \]

On the gluing circle, points of $D$ satisfy the actual lower estimate

\[ |w|^2\geq (a-\epsilon )^2\epsilon -\epsilon ^2\geq C_0\epsilon \]

for a fixed $C_0>0$ and small $\epsilon $. Therefore the first branch is at least $-L/2-C_1$. On this circle $\log |(z-a)/(1-az)|=-L+O(1)$ uniformly, so the other branch is at most $-L/2-\sqrt L+C_2$. For small $\epsilon $ the first strictly dominates, proving PSH gluing. Also $|w|<2$ on $D$, so both branches are negative. Near $(a,0)$ the expression is $\log (|w|/2)$, at most the required logarithmic pole in the full Euclidean norm. It is thus a Green competitor, and

\[ g_ D(0,(a,0))\geq v(0,0)=c_\epsilon \log a >\log a=g_ D((a,0),0). \]

To obtain smooth strictly pseudoconvex domains explicitly, let

\[ Q_ j(z,w)=|z|^{4j}+|P(z,w)/\epsilon ^2|^{2j} +\delta _ j(|z|^2+|w|^2), \qquad \delta _ j\downarrow 0, \]

and choose regular values $b_ j\uparrow 1$ in $(1/2,1)$. Proposition 16.37.1 provides them. Each $Q_ j$ is a strictly PSH real polynomial. Its sublevel $Q_ j<b_ j$ is relatively compact in $D$, with strongly pseudoconvex real analytic boundary. Let $D_ j$ be its component containing zero. On $D$, $Q_{j+1}\leq Q_ j$ and $b_{j+1}>b_ j$, so these components increase, with compact containment. They exhaust $D$: on every compact joining path in $D$, $Q_ j\to 0$ uniformly. If every $g_{D_ j}$ were symmetric, the domain-exhaustion limit $g_{D_ j}\downarrow g_ D$ would be symmetric, contradicting the strict inequality above. One $D_ j$ has the stated asymmetry. On that domain $W\ne g$ at some pair because $W$ is symmetric. The preceding mass criterion gives strictly greater total mass at a corresponding pole. $\square$
