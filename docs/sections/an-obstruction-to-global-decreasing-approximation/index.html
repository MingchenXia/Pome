6.20 An obstruction to global decreasing approximation8141EAE679FF

8141EAE679FF

Example 6.20.1.4BA55C74028C There is a bounded connected domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^2$ and a bounded plurisubharmonic function $u$ on $\Omega $ that is not the pointwise limit of any decreasing sequence of continuous plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega $.

Proof. For $j\geq 2$ put

\[ a_ j=\frac{2^{-j}}{\log j},\qquad \varepsilon _ j=\exp (-8^ j),\qquad A=\bigcup _{j\geq 2}D(1/j,\varepsilon _ j). \]

All these discs lie in $D(0,1)$. They are pairwise disjoint: $8^ j>4j(j+1)$ for $j\geq 2$, by induction, so $\varepsilon _ j<1/(4j(j+1))$. The sum of the bounds for neighboring radii is less than $1/(j(j+1))$, the distance between their centers. The ordering of these disjoint intervals on the real axis then also separates every nonneighboring pair of discs. Define

\[ \Omega =\bigl ((D(0,2)\setminus \{ |z|=1\} )\times D(0,1)\bigr ) \ \cup \ \bigl (\{ |z|=1\} \times A\bigr ). \]

This set is open: at a point of its added circles the second coordinate lies in the open set $A$, so a full product neighborhood is present. The inner and outer products are connected, and each added circle has such a neighborhood meeting both. Thus $\Omega $ is connected.

On $\mathbb C$ consider

\[ \psi (w)=\sum _{j\geq 2} a_ j\log |w-1/j|. \]

This defines a subharmonic function, with $\psi (0)=-\sum _{j\geq 2}2^{-j}=-1/2$. For completeness, on $D(0,R)$ with $R>1$, subtract $C_ R=\log (R+1)$ from every logarithm. The resulting finite sums are decreasing subharmonic functions and their values at zero have a finite limit, since $\sum a_ j<\infty $. The decreasing-limit theorem therefore applies. Adding back the convergent constant $C_ R\sum a_ j$ gives the displayed series; these local definitions agree.

For $w\in D(1/j,\varepsilon _ j)$ the $j$th term is less than $-4^ j/\log j<-1$, and every other term is negative because $|w-1/k|<1$. Hence $\psi <-1$ on $A$. Put

\[ u(z,w)= \begin{cases} \max \{ \psi (w),-1\} ,& |z|<1,\\ -1,& |z|\geq 1, \end{cases}\qquad (z,w)\in \Omega . \]

Both pieces are plurisubharmonic. Near each bridge point they agree with the constant $-1$, so they define a plurisubharmonic function on $\Omega $. It is bounded below by $-1$ and above because $\psi (w)\leq \log (3/2)\sum a_ j$ for $|w|<1$. In particular

\[ u(0,0)=-1/2,\qquad u(z,0)=-1\quad (|z|=3/2). \]

Every continuous plurisubharmonic $v$ on $\Omega $ satisfies

\[ v(0,0)\leq \sup _{|z|=3/2}v(z,0). \]

Indeed the entire disc $D(0,2)\times \{ 1/j\} $ lies in $\Omega $, and its subharmonic maximum principle gives $v(0,1/j)\leq \sup _{|z|=3/2}v(z,1/j)$. As $j\to \infty $, continuity gives convergence on the left and uniform convergence on the right: the latter follows from uniform continuity on the compact product of the outer circle with a small closed $w$-disc, which is contained in $\Omega $.

Suppose now that continuous plurisubharmonic functions $v_ k$ decrease pointwise to $u$. On the compact outer circle, $v_ k(\cdot ,0)\downarrow -1$ implies $\sup _{|z|=3/2}v_ k(z,0)\downarrow -1$. To see this directly, the increasing open sets $\{ z:v_ k(z,0)<-1+\eta \} $ cover that circle for every $\eta >0$; a finite subcover and monotonicity give the uniform upper bound. The preceding inequality for $v_ k$ would then give $-1/2=u(0,0)\leq -1$, a contradiction. $\square$
