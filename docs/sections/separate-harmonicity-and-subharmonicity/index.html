3.6 Separate harmonicity and subharmonicityB27F5ECEDD9B

B27F5ECEDD9B

Theorem 3.6.1.58AAF98CC15E Let $U\subset \mathbb R^ p$ and $V\subset \mathbb R^ q$ be domains. Suppose $u:U\times V\to [-\infty ,\infty )$ is subharmonic in each group of variables separately, allowing identically $-\infty $ slices. If $u$ has a locally integrable pointwise majorant, it is subharmonic on $U\times V$. In particular, it is enough to assume directly that $u$ is locally bounded above.

Proof. We first derive a local upper bound from the majorant $M$. For small fixed radii $r,s$ and $(x,y)$ in a smaller product of balls, the two successive submean inequalities give

\[ u(x,y)\leq \frac1{|B_ r|}\int _{B(x,r)}u(t,y)\, dt \leq \frac1{|B_ r||B_ s|} \int _{B(x,r)}\int _{B(y,s)}M^+(t,w)\, dw\, dt. \]

For the second inequality, first apply the $y$-submean inequality at each $t$ for which the slice of $M^+$ is integrable, then integrate the measurable function $u(\cdot ,y)$. Exceptional $t$ form a null set by Fubini applied to $M^+$. All balls lie in a fixed compact product, so its $L^1$ norm bounds the last expression uniformly. This step does not assume joint measurability of $u$.

We now assume a local upper bound and prove joint upper semicontinuity. Let $(x_ j,y_ j)\to (x,y)$. The functions $v_ j=u(x_ j,\cdot )$ are locally uniformly bounded above on a ball about $y$, and for each fixed $w$ separate upper semicontinuity gives $\limsup _ jv_ j(w)\leq u(x,w)$. If a subsequence contradicted the desired upper bound at $y_ j$, subharmonic compactness would give a further subsequence converging in $L^1$ to a subharmonic $v$; the alternative of locally uniform convergence to $-\infty $ cannot contradict the bound. A further almost-everywhere subsequence gives $v\leq u(x,\cdot )$ almost everywhere and hence everywhere by canonical subharmonic representatives. Hartogs' lemma for the moving points $y_ j$ then gives $\limsup _ jv_ j(y_ j)\leq v(y)\leq u(x,y)$, a contradiction. If $u(x,\cdot )\equiv -\infty $, the nondegenerate $L^1$ alternative is impossible by the same almost-everywhere inequality. Thus $u$ is jointly upper semicontinuous, in particular Borel measurable.

Either $u\equiv -\infty $, or every open product of balls contains a point where $u$ is finite: if it vanished to $-\infty $ on an open product, first the $x$-slice nondegeneracy theorem and then its $y$ counterpart would propagate this to the whole connected product. Fix a smaller product and choose such a point $(a,b)$ sufficiently near its center. Choose an $x$-ball $B(a,R)$ containing its smaller $x$-projection and still in the larger product. With an upper bound $C\geq 0$ there, the $x$-submean inequality implies, for almost every $y$ in the smaller $y$-ball,

\[ \int _{B(a,R)}u^-(t,y)\, dt \leq |B_ R|\bigl (C+u^-(a,y)\bigr ). \]

The slice $u(a,\cdot )$ is nondegenerate because $u(a,b)$ is finite, so it is locally integrable. Integrating the displayed bound proves local integrability of $u$ on the product. This argument works about every point by taking sufficiently small nested product balls.

Fubini and the slice distribution inequalities now give $\Delta _ xu\geq 0$ and $\Delta _ yu\geq 0$, hence $\Delta u\geq 0$. Convolve on smaller products with the product of the two canonical nonnegative radial mollifiers. The resulting smooth functions are jointly subharmonic. Successive submeans give $u_\epsilon \geq u$; joint upper semicontinuity gives $u_\epsilon \to u$ pointwise. The one-variable radial convolution monotonicity in each factor shows that these approximants decrease as both radii decrease. The canonical decreasing-limit theorem proves joint subharmonicity, including the identically $-\infty $ case. $\square$

We need a precise real-trace consequence of pluripolar discrepancy. Let $Q\subset \mathbb R^ m\subset \mathbb C^ m$ be a closed cube in a complex ball $B$. Its explicitly computed global extremal is continuous and zero on $Q$. The proved global-relative comparison therefore gives $h_{Q,B}^*=-1$ on $Q$. Deleting a pluripolar subset does not change this relative regularization. Consequently, if a PSH function $w$ on $B$ is at most $A$ on $Q\setminus E$, where $E$ is pluripolar, then $w\leq A$ on $Q$. Indeed, take a smaller ball containing $Q$, an upper bound $C>A$ for $w$ there, and use $(w-A)/(C-A)-1$ as a relative competitor. The assertion is immediate if $w\leq A$ already on the ball.

It follows that if $v_ j$ are PSH on a complex neighborhood of a real open set, locally uniformly bounded above, and $\limsup _ jv_ j(x)\leq A$ at every real point, then for every compact real cube $K$ in that set and $\epsilon >0$,

\[ v_ j(x)\leq A+\epsilon \quad (x\in K,\ j\geq j_0). \]

To see this, set $w_ k=(\sup _{j\geq k}v_ j)^*$. Canonical envelope discrepancy and countable pluripolar unions give $w=\lim _ kw_ k=\limsup _ jv_ j$ off a pluripolar set. Locally $w$ is PSH or identically $-\infty $. The preceding real-cube observation makes $w\leq A$ on each larger real cube. On a smaller compact cube, the decreasing closed sets $\{ w_ k\geq A+\epsilon \} $ have empty intersection and hence are eventually empty. If some $v_ j$ is identically $-\infty $, it can simply be omitted.

Theorem 3.6.2.95AFE3B73B3D A real-valued function on an open subset of $\mathbb R^ p\times \mathbb R^ q$ that is harmonic in each group separately is jointly harmonic. The assertion extends to finitely many groups.

Proof. It suffices to work on a product of balls. If one group has dimension one, harmonicity in its interval means affinity there; two fixed values determine the affine coefficients, which are harmonic in the other group. Assume therefore $p,q\geq 2$.

We first prove propagation from a bounded strip. Suppose $f$ is jointly bounded on compact subsets of $X\times B(P,\rho )$, where $X$ is an $x$-ball, $P\in V$ and $\rho >0$. The preceding theorem makes $f$ jointly subharmonic there. It is locally integrable, and Fubini gives $\Delta f=0$ because both slice Laplacians vanish. Weyl's lemma gives a harmonic representative. Its partial Laplacians also vanish, since it represents the same distribution; its slices are therefore harmonic. It agrees with $f$ everywhere: equality almost everywhere and harmonic slice uniqueness first give equality for all $x$ at almost every $y$, and then for all $y$ at every fixed $x$.

For each fixed $x$, expand the harmonic function in the other group about $P$:

\[ f(x,P+r\theta )=\sum _{s\geq 0}a_ s(x,\theta )r^ s, \qquad |\theta |=1. \]

The coefficients are homogeneous harmonic polynomials in $r\theta $. They are harmonic in $x$, by the joint harmonicity on the original strip and the Poisson coefficient formula on a small $y$-sphere. If $R=\operatorname {dist}(P,\partial V)$, that same formula on any radius $r<R$ gives, for each fixed real $x$,

\[ \limsup _{s\to \infty }\frac1s \log \sup _{|\theta |=1}|a_ s(x,\theta )|\leq -\log R. \]

Here the Poisson kernel expansion has coefficients bounded by a polynomial in $s$ times $r^{-s}$: expand $(1-2t\cos \gamma +t^2)^{-q/2}$; its absolute coefficients are bounded by the coefficients of $(1-t)^{-q}$, namely $\binom {s+q-1}{q-1}$, and the numerator $1-t^2$ adds only two terms. Taking $s$-th roots and then $r\uparrow R$ proves the bound.

On any smaller $x$-ball the original bounded strip gives uniform geometric bounds for all $a_ s(x,\theta )$. Harmonic functions on a fixed real ball extend holomorphically to a fixed smaller complex ball, with a uniform supremum estimate. This follows directly by complexifying its Poisson kernel: for a unit sphere the denominator $\sum _ j(\xi _ j-z_ j)^2$ stays in a disc about one avoiding zero when $|z|$ is sufficiently small, uniformly in $\xi $; choose its branch there and integrate. The numerator and denominator are bounded uniformly on a smaller complex ball, so the extension norm is at most a fixed constant times the real boundary supremum. The extension is unique on overlaps by the holomorphic identity theorem on a real open set, obtained by applying the one-variable identity theorem successively to the coordinates.

Thus the holomorphic extensions $a_ s(z,\theta )$ have a common complex neighborhood and locally uniform geometric bounds. The functions

\[ v_ s(z)=\max \left\{ \frac1s\log \sup _{|\theta |=1} |a_ s(z,\theta )|,-s\right\} \quad (s\geq 1) \]

are PSH there: the coefficients form a finite holomorphic tuple, and the supremum over the real sphere is a complex norm on its finite-dimensional space of homogeneous harmonic polynomials. Use the canonical complex-norm logarithm and maxima. The real-trace estimate just proved gives, uniformly for real $x$ on a smaller cube, $\sup _\theta |a_ s(x,\theta )|\leq (e^\epsilon /R)^ s$ for large $s$. The harmonic series therefore converges absolutely and uniformly on its product with every $B(P,r)$, $r<R$, after choosing $\epsilon >0$ so that $re^\epsilon <R$. This bounds $f$ jointly there. The preceding local boundedness, distribution and Weyl argument now proves joint harmonicity on $X\times B(P,R)$.

To start a strip, take any closed $x$-ball $\overline X$ inside the given $x$-domain and a closed $y$-ball $\overline Y$ inside $V$. The closed sets

\[ E_ j=\{ y\in \overline Y: |f(x,y)|\leq j \text{ for all }x\in \overline X\} \]

cover $\overline Y$, since each $x$-slice is continuous on the compact ball. Baire's theorem gives a $y$-ball inside some $E_ j$. This is the required bounded strip on $X$. The propagation step extends it from its center $P$ to $B(P,\operatorname {dist}(P,\partial V))$. Along a compact path in the connected domain $V$, its distance from the boundary has a positive lower bound. Repeatedly choose the next center inside the preceding ball and move by less than half that lower bound. Finitely many propagation steps reach any specified point of $V$. Hence $f$ is harmonic on $X\times V$. The ball $X$ was arbitrary, proving the two-group theorem. Local induction combines any finite number of groups. $\square$
