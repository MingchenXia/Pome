7.20 Green level measures and boundary representation88620089FFCA

88620089FFCA

Use $d^ c=\mathrm i(\bar\partial -\partial )/(4\pi )$, so that $dd^ c=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial /(2\pi )$, and put $m_ n=2^{-n}$.

Proposition 7.20.1.1359A4B6C1D8 Let $u$ be $C^2$, PSH and maximal on an open subset of $\mathbb C^ n$. If $c$ is a regular value and $S=\{ u=c\} $ is compact, then

\[ (dd^ c\max (u,c))^ n=[S]\wedge d^ cu\wedge (dd^ cu)^{n-1}, \]

where $S$ is oriented as the boundary of $\{ u<c\} $. In particular the right side is a positive measure.

Proof. Off $S$ the asserted measure is zero, by locality and maximality. Choose smooth convex increasing $F_\epsilon $ decreasing to $\max (t,c)$, with derivative zero below $c-\epsilon $, one above $c+\epsilon $, and between zero and one elsewhere. Such functions are the even one-dimensional convolutions of the maximum. The chain rule and $(dd^ cu)^ n=0$ give

\[ (dd^ cF_\epsilon (u))^ n =n(F_\epsilon '(u))^{n-1}F_\epsilon ''(u) du\wedge d^ cu\wedge (dd^ cu)^{n-1}. \]

This identity holds for $C^2$ potentials by the determinant formula. In regular-level coordinates, integration of a continuous compactly supported test $h$ on the right is integration in $t$ of

\[ n(F_\epsilon '(t))^{n-1}F_\epsilon ''(t) \int _{\{ u=t\} }h\, d^ cu\wedge (dd^ cu)^{n-1}. \]

The level integral is continuous near $c$, by the local flow of a vector field transverse to the level and the continuity of the coefficients. The nonnegative scalar coefficient is the derivative of $(F_\epsilon ')^ n$, has integral one and is supported near $c$. It therefore converges to evaluation at $c$. Bounded decreasing Bedford–Taylor continuity identifies the left limit. The orientation is the outward one because the transverse coordinate is $u$. $\square$

Let $D$ be a bounded hyperconvex domain, $a\in D$, $g=g_ D(\cdot ,a)$, $g_ r=\max (g,r)$ and $B(r)=\{ g<r\} $ for $r<0$. The measures $\beta _ r=(dd^ cg_ r)^ n$ are supported on $\{ g=r\} $ and have total mass $m_ n$. Indeed locality/maximality gives the support; the compact-change cutoff argument for the classical point measure gives mass preservation under truncation, and $M_ g=m_ n\delta _ a$.

The domain $B(r)$ is connected. A component not containing $a$ would be relatively compact away from $a$ and have boundary values $r$ for the continuous maximal function $g$; comparison with the constant $r$ would contradict $g<r$ there. Its negative continuous PSH exhaustion $\max (g-r,-1)$ proves hyperconvexity. Zero-boundary logarithmic uniqueness therefore gives

\[ g_{B(r)}(z,a)=g_ D(z,a)-r\qquad (z\in B(r)). \]

Proposition 7.20.2.351C8FCB17FF For $v\in \operatorname {PSH}(D)\cap C(D)$ there is a positive classical mixed measure $\nu =dd^ cv\wedge (dd^ cg)^{n-1}$, defined by decreasing truncations, and

\[ \int _ D v\, d\beta _ r-m_ n v(a) =\int _{B(r)}(r-g)\, d\nu \qquad (r<0). \]

In particular all integrals on the right are finite and $r\mapsto \int v\, d\beta _ r$ is increasing.

Proof. For $s<0$ put $\nu _ s=dd^ cv\wedge (dd^ cg_ s)^{n-1}$. The bounded polynomial expansion of $(dd^ c(g_ s+t v))^ n$ has positive mixed coefficients. For any fixed $t\geq 0$, $g_ s+t v$ decreases to a PSH function locally bounded off $a$. The proved one-point classical continuity theorem gives a limit for its top measure. Evaluating at $n+1$ distinct nonnegative $t$ and inverting the Vandermonde matrix therefore gives weak limits for every mixed coefficient, still positive. The coefficient of $t$ is $n\nu _ s$, defining the asserted $\nu $. For $n=1$ it is simply $dd^ cv$. On $D\setminus \{ a\} $ this agrees with the ordinary bounded mixed product by locality.

For $s<t<0$, we claim

\[ \nu _ s(B(t))=\nu (B(t)). \]

For $n=1$ the claim is immediate. For $n\geq 2$ and $s'<s$, the difference $\nu _ s-\nu _{s'}$ is $dd^ c$ of a bounded mixed current supported in the compact set $\{ g\leq s\} \subset B(t)$: use the telescoping sum with factor $g_ s-g_{s'}$ and the closed factors $dd^ cv$, $dd^ cg_ s$, $dd^ cg_{s'}$. A smooth cutoff equal to one on a neighborhood of that compact set integrates this difference to zero. Let $s'\to -\infty $ with this same cutoff. Locality shows that $\nu _ s-\nu $ is still supported there, and its cutoff integral is zero. This proves the claim without assuming that $\{ g=t\} $ has zero measure in a weak-limit argument.

Write $F(r)=\int v\, d\beta _ r$. For $s<r<0$, bounded compactly supported integration by parts and the telescoping product give

\[ F(r)-F(s) =\int _ D(g_ r-g_ s)\, dd^ cv\wedge \sum _{k=0}^{n-1}(dd^ cg_ r)^ k\wedge (dd^ cg_ s)^{n-1-k} =\int _ D(r-g_ s)_+\, d\nu _ s. \]

To justify the first equality, insert a cutoff equal to one on the compact difference set. Smooth all the continuous bounded potentials on a slightly larger relatively compact set, use Stokes there, and pass to the limit by bounded weighted Bedford–Taylor continuity. The smooth differences are still supported in one fixed compact set, so no outer boundary term occurs. In the second equality every $k\geq 1$ term vanishes where its weight is positive, because $g_ r$ is constant on $\{ g<r\} $; on the remaining level the continuous weight is zero. Layer cake and the preceding claim give

\[ F(r)-F(s)=\int _ s^ r\nu _ s(B(t))\, dt =\int _ s^ r\nu (B(t))\, dt. \]

As $s\to -\infty $, the supports of $\beta _ s$ shrink to $a$ and their common mass is $m_ n$, so continuity of $v$ gives $F(s)\to m_ n v(a)$. Tonelli's theorem now gives the claimed identity. Its finite left side also proves the weighted integrability, rather than presupposing a product with the unbounded weight $g$. Positivity proves monotonicity. $\square$

Proposition 7.20.3.EC62871F80C4 The measures $\beta _ r$, extended by zero outside $D$, converge weakly as $r\uparrow 0$ to a positive measure $\mu _{D,a}$ on $\partial D$ of mass $m_ n$. For $v\in \operatorname {PSH}(D)\cap C(\overline D)$,

\[ v(a)=2^ n\int _{\partial D}v\, d\mu _{D,a} +2^ n\int _ Dg\, dd^ cv\wedge (dd^ cg)^{n-1}. \]

For pluriharmonic $v$ the second term vanishes.

Proof. For a real smooth function $\varphi $ near $\overline D$, choose $C$ so large that both $\varphi +C|z|^2$ and $C|z|^2$ are PSH there. Their $\beta _ r$ integrals are increasing by the preceding proposition and bounded by their suprema on $\overline D$ times $m_ n$. Their difference therefore has a limit. Smooth functions near a compact subset of Euclidean space are uniformly dense in its continuous functions: use a finite small-ball cover and normalized smooth bumps to approximate by convex combinations of values at the centers. The common mass bound extends the limit to all continuous tests on $\overline D$. The positive functional representation gives a unique weak limit of mass $m_ n$. Every compact subset of $D$ eventually misses $\{ g=r\} $, so this limit is supported on the boundary.

For the stated $v$, let $r\uparrow 0$ in the point Jensen identity. The left integral converges by the continuous boundary test. The nonnegative weights $(r-g)_+$ increase to $-g$, so monotone convergence gives

\[ \int _{\partial D}v\, d\mu _{D,a} =m_ n v(a)+\int _ D(-g)\, dd^ cv\wedge (dd^ cg)^{n-1}. \]

Rearrangement gives the formula, including its finiteness. For a real pluriharmonic function $dd^ cv=0$; the same statement for complex-valued pluriharmonic functions follows by real and imaginary parts. $\square$

Proposition 7.20.4.438C4583CB39 Let $d\sigma $ be normalized Euclidean surface probability on the unit sphere of $\mathbb C^ n$. For the unit ball,

\[ d\mu _{B,a}(\zeta )=m_ n \left(\frac{1-|a|^2}{|1-\langle \zeta ,a\rangle |^2}\right)^ n d\sigma (\zeta ). \]

For the unit polydisc the measure is supported on its distinguished boundary and

\[ d\mu _{\Delta ^ n,a}(\zeta )=m_ n\prod _{j=1}^ n \frac{1-|a_ j|^2}{|\zeta _ j-a_ j|^2}\frac{d\theta _ j}{2\pi }, \qquad \zeta _ j=e^{\mathrm i\theta _ j}. \]

Proof. For the ball and pole zero, the canonical spherical-product calculation gives $\beta _ r=m_ n\sigma _{e^ r}$, hence $\mu _{B,0}=m_ n\sigma $. Let $A_ a$ be the ball automorphism interchanging $a$ and zero. Then $g_ B(z,a)=\log |A_ a(z)|$. Near the sphere,

\[ 1-|A_ a(z)|^2=\lambda (z)(1-|z|^2),\qquad \lambda (z)=\frac{1-|a|^2}{|1-\langle z,a\rangle |^2}. \]

Restricting differentials to the sphere gives $d^ cg_ a=\lambda d^ cg_0$ and $dd^ cg_ a=d\lambda \wedge d^ cg_0+\lambda dd^ cg_0$. The regular-level flux form consequently changes by $\lambda ^ n$: every term with $d\lambda \wedge d^ cg_0$ has a repeated $d^ cg_0$. Pass the regular levels to the sphere, or equivalently use the smooth automorphism and bounded-product invariance. This proves the first kernel. The radial measure also gives the ordinary mean formula for real harmonic functions at the center. At other poles the represented class in complex dimension greater than one is pluriharmonic.

For the polydisc and pole zero, $g(z)=\max _ j\log |z_ j|$. We show that $\beta _ r$ is supported where every $|z_ j|=e^ r$. Below level $r$ the truncation is constant. Above that level, take local holomorphic logarithms of the nonzero active coordinates. Their real linear functions all have one common affine direction with slope one. Subtract its pluriharmonic coordinate: the remaining maximum depends on at most $n-1$ holomorphic variables. Its top bounded product is zero, as follows directly by smoothing in those variables and bounded-product continuity. At level $r$, if some coordinate is strictly inactive, the truncated maximum is locally independent of that coordinate and has the same zero top product. This proves the asserted torus support. Independent rotations preserve the measure. Averaging any continuous test over these rotations and using Fubini identifies it as $m_ n$ times the product of uniform angular probabilities. Let $r\uparrow 0$. For other poles apply the coordinate disc automorphisms. Their circle Jacobian is $(1-|a_ j|^2)/|\zeta _ j-a_ j|^2$; bounded-product invariance then gives the displayed product kernel. $\square$
