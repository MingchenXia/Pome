20.8 Divisorial restricted volumes30E77D3CC0BE

30E77D3CC0BE

For a smooth closed background $\theta $ on a $d$-fold, write $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)=\langle (\theta +dd^ cv)^ d\rangle $. Restricted volumes below are classical restricted volumes outside the non-Kähler locus, identified with masses of restricted minimal currents in the next lemma.

Lemma 20.8.1.7E82D2724E2F Fix a smooth submanifold $V$ of dimension $q>0$ meeting the Kähler locus of a big class $a$. The classical restricted volume is continuous at $a$ on the open set of such classes. In particular it is continuous under a sufficiently small perturbation in any fixed direction. It equals the mass of the restriction of a current with minimal singularities. For finitely many such submanifolds the latter masses can be approached simultaneously by one analytic current in the exact class $a$.

Proof. First prove the maximal-mass assertion without using continuity. Let $U$ be any positive current in $a$ whose potential restricts to $V$, and let $T_0\in a$ be a Kähler current with $T_0\geq c\omega $ and no pole containing $V$. For $\delta >0$, $W_\delta =(1-\delta )U+\delta T_0$ dominates $\delta c\omega $. Apply gentle analytic regularization to this current with background $\theta -\delta c\omega $. Once its positivity loss is less than $\delta c\omega /2$, the resulting current is positive in the exact class $a$, less singular than $W_\delta $, and has no pole containing $V$. Restricted monotonicity and positivity of the mixed terms give its restricted mass at least $(1-\delta )^ q\int _ V\langle (U|_ V)^ q\rangle $. Letting $\delta \downarrow 0$ bounds the mass of every such $U$ by the defining restricted supremum. For a minimal current the opposite inequality is restricted monotonicity, proving equality. For finitely many $V$, choose $T_0$ with no pole containing any of them: take a finite maximum of normalized strict potentials smooth at their respective general points and then regularize with a smaller strict margin. The same regularization of $W_\delta $ gives all the preceding lower bounds simultaneously. This proves the last assertion as well.

Choose a Kähler current $T_0\in a$, smooth at a general point of $V$, with $T_0\geq c\omega $. In a fixed finite-dimensional space of smooth representatives, a perturbation $e$ has representative $\xi $ with $-C\| e\| \omega \leq \xi \leq C\| e\| \omega $. Choose $s=O(\| e\| )>0$ so that both

\[ sT_0+\xi \geq 0,\qquad sT_0-(1-s)\xi \geq 0. \]

For any positive current $T\in a$ with the permitted singularities, $(1-s)T+sT_0+\xi $ is positive in $a+e$ and has no pole containing $V$. Its restricted top product has mass at least $(1-s)^ q$ times that of $T$, by expansion of positive products. Thus $\operatorname {Vol}_{X\mid V}(a+e)\geq (1-s)^ q\operatorname {Vol}_{X\mid V}(a)$. Conversely, for a competitor $S\in a+e$ use $(1-s)S+sT_0-(1-s)\xi \in a$ to obtain the reverse inequality with the same factor. These mixed currents need not themselves have analytic singularities: the maximal-mass assertion just proved bounds their restricted masses by the corresponding defining suprema. These two inequalities prove continuity, and also show that $V$ keeps meeting the Kähler locus for small $e$. $\square$

For a zero-dimensional connected submanifold the restricted mass is one whenever it meets the Kähler locus. This convention is constant, so the corresponding continuity assertion needs no limiting argument.

Lemma 20.8.2.D201C3DEDDE1 Let $\theta $ represent a big class $\beta $ on a compact Kähler manifold, and let $u=P_\theta (0)$. For every $\varepsilon >0$ there are a modification $\pi :Z\to X$, a $\theta +\varepsilon \eta $-psh function $\psi \geq u-C$, and a smooth Kähler form $\kappa $ such that

\[ \pi ^*(\theta +\varepsilon \eta )+dd^ c(\psi \circ \pi ) =\kappa +[G],\qquad G\geq 0. \]

The function $\psi $ is smooth away from $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\beta )$, and $G$ is a divisor with support in its inverse image.

Proof. Regularize the minimal current $\theta +dd^ cu$, with a positivity loss smaller than $\varepsilon \eta /2$, using its analytic approximants which are less singular than $u$ and smooth off $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\beta )$. Resolve the resulting ideal, with all centers over its zero set. Its pullback potential is $\psi _0\circ \pi =\sum a_ i\log |s_ i|^2+\rho $, with $\rho $ smooth, and its current dominates $\varepsilon \pi ^*\eta /2$. Every exceptional component has $a_ i>0$.

The elementary Kähler blowup construction gives an effective exceptional real divisor $F=\sum f_ iE_ i$ and a metric for it such that $\eta _ Z=\pi ^*\eta -\Theta _ F$ is Kähler. First replace $\psi _0$ by $(1-s)\psi _0$, with $s>0$ small enough that the current still dominates $\varepsilon \pi ^*\eta /4$. This scaling changes the lower bound only by a fixed smooth multiple of $s\pi ^*\eta $. Then add $t\log |s_ F|^2$ upstairs, with $0<t<\varepsilon /4$ and $tf_ i<sa_ i$ for every exceptional component. The smooth remainder is bounded below by

\[ (\varepsilon /4)\pi ^*\eta -t\Theta _ F =(\varepsilon /4-t)\pi ^*\eta +t\eta _ Z>0. \]

The divisor coefficients remain positive, while

\[ (1-s)\psi _0\circ \pi +t\log |s_ F|^2 \geq \psi _0\circ \pi -C\geq u\circ \pi -C. \]

This positive current is in the pullback class $\pi ^*(\beta +\varepsilon [\eta ])$. The correspondence of potentials under a modification of a smooth target therefore makes it the pullback of its positive pushforward. Its potential descends up to a constant, giving the required $\psi $. Only the resolved divisorial description is needed. $\square$

Theorem 20.8.3.8DC7DAC31FB7 Let $X$ be a compact Kähler $n$-fold, let $\alpha $ be big, and let $Y$ be a smooth prime divisor meeting its Kähler locus. Then

\[ \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha +t\{ Y\} ) =n\operatorname {Vol}_{X\mid Y}(\alpha ). \]

Proof. If $n=1$, a big class on the compact curve has positive degree, its volume equals that degree, and the restricted mass of the point $Y$ is one. The formula is therefore immediate. Assume $n\geq 2$ below. Choose $c>0$ so small that $Y$ still meets the Kähler locus of $\alpha -t\{ Y\} $ for $0\leq t\leq c$. This follows by subtracting $t$ times a smooth curvature representative from a fixed Kähler current in $\alpha $ smooth near a general point of $Y$. Choose $b>c+1$. Let

\[ \mu :\mathcal X=\operatorname {Bl}_{Y\times \{ 0\} }(X\times \mathbb P^1) \longrightarrow X\times \mathbb P^1, \qquad \beta =p^*\alpha +b\{ \mathcal X_0\} -c\{ E\} . \]

Write $X'$ for the strict transform of $X\times \{ 0\} $, so that $X'\simeq X$, $\mathcal X_0=X'+E$, and $E\simeq \mathbb P(N_{Y/X}\oplus \mathbb C)$. The class $\beta $ is big and its Kähler locus meets $X'$, $E$ and every nonzero fiber. To check the assertion on $E$, choose $\epsilon >0$ so small that $\epsilon <b-c-1$ and a Kähler current in $\alpha -c\{ Y\} $ still has a strict margin after subtracting $\epsilon \omega $. For $0<\delta <c$ sufficiently small the blowup metric construction gives the Kähler class

\[ \gamma =\epsilon (p^*[\omega ]+\{ \mathcal X_0\} )-\delta \{ E\} . \]

Now

\[ \beta -\gamma =p^*(\alpha -\epsilon [\omega ]) +(b-\epsilon -c+\delta )\{ \mathcal X_0\} +(c-\delta )\{ X'\} . \]

Choose a gently analytic positive current in $\alpha -\epsilon [\omega ]$ smooth near a general point of $Y$. Its pullback, the displayed positive base multiple, and $(c-\delta )[X']$ form a positive remainder smooth at general points of $E$ and of every nonzero fiber. Adding a Kähler representative of $\gamma $ proves the assertions there. For $X'$, write $\mathcal D$ for the strict transform of $Y\times \mathbb P^1$ and use $p^*\{ Y\} =\{ \mathcal D\} +\{ E\} $:

\[ \beta -\gamma =p^*(\alpha -(c-\delta )\{ Y\} -\epsilon [\omega ]) +(b-\epsilon )\{ \mathcal X_0\} +(c-\delta )\{ \mathcal D\} . \]

A positive gently analytic current in the first class exists by the strict margin already chosen. This remainder is smooth near a general point of $X'$. Adding $\gamma $ proves the assertion on $X'$. All coefficients remain positive when $b$ is replaced by $b-1$, so the same proof applies to $\beta -\{ \mathcal X_0\} $.

Volume formulas. Choose an invariant metric on $\mathcal O(E)$ and a smooth invariant representative $\widetilde\theta $ of $\beta $. Put $\widetilde u=P_{\widetilde\theta }(0)$. On $X\times \mathbb C^*$, after adding the two smooth local potentials, the corresponding envelope is

\[ u=\sup \{ v\in \operatorname {PSH}(p^*\theta ): v\leq b\log (1+|\tau |^2)+c\log |s_ E|^2\} . \]

It satisfies $\widetilde u=u-b\log (1+|\tau |^2)-c\log |s_ E|^2$. One inequality follows from the envelope definitions, and the other from removable singularities, since the difference is bounded above across the deleted divisors.

For $0\leq t\leq c$, set $v_ t=P_\theta (t\log |s_ Y|^2)$. Subtracting $t\log |s_ Y|^2$ identifies $v_ t$ with a minimal potential of $\alpha -t\{ Y\} $. The partial Legendre transforms of $u$ are $-\infty $ outside $[0,b]$ and satisfy

\[ \widehat u_\lambda =v_{\max (c-\lambda ,0)}+O(1). \]

To verify both comparisons, a candidate $\widehat u_\lambda +\lambda \log |\tau |^2$ lying below the obstacle has generic Lelong number at least $c$ along $Y\times \{ 0\} $. Thus its $Y$-Lelong number is at least $\max (c-\lambda ,0)$. Conversely $v_{\max (c-\lambda ,0)}+\lambda \log |\tau |^2$ has that Lelong bound, is below the obstacle up to a constant near the zero fiber, and has the required growth at infinity. Subtracting that constant gives a competitor. These statements can equally be checked on the blowup, using Siu decomposition along its divisor $E$.

Lemma 17.18.1, invariance of non-pluripolar mass under bounded changes, and the restricted-mass formula of lemma 20.8.1 give

419BFB5D9AD7
\begin{align} \operatorname {Vol}(\beta ) & =(n+1)\left((b-c)\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha ) +\int _0^ c\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha -t\{ Y\} )\, dt\right), \tag{20.8.4}\\ \operatorname {Vol}_{\mathcal X\mid \mathcal X_\tau }(\beta ) & =\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha )\quad (\tau \ne 0), \tag{20.8.5}\\ \operatorname {Vol}_{\mathcal X\mid X'}(\beta ) & =\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha -c\{ Y\} ), \tag{20.8.6}\\ \operatorname {Vol}_{\mathcal X\mid E}(\beta ) & =n\int _0^ c\operatorname {Vol}_{X\mid Y}(\alpha -t\{ Y\} )\, dt. \tag{20.8.7} \end{align}

Here are details of the two central restrictions. At $X'$, taking $\tau \to 0$ in the Legendre supremum selects $\widehat u_0$. The transforms are closed concave in the parameter, so their values approach $\widehat u_0$ at that endpoint; local boundedness off a common analytic set justifies the supremum limit. After subtracting $c\log |s_ Y|^2$, this has the minimal type of $\alpha -c\{ Y\} $. Near $E$, use coordinates $z_1=\tau \zeta $, with $Y=\{ z_1=0\} $. Terms with $\lambda >c$ tend to $-\infty $. For $\lambda \in [0,c]$, subtracting the prescribed $Y$-logarithm gives a psh potential on $Y$, and the remaining fiber term is $(c-\lambda )\log |\zeta |^2$. Here the restriction of the supremum is justified on a whole blowup chart, rather than by exchanging two arbitrary limits. Over $\operatorname {Amp}(\alpha -c\{ Y\} )\cap Y$ and away from the zero and infinity sections, each extended summand

\[ \phi _\lambda =\widehat u_\lambda +\lambda \log |\tau |^2 -b\log (1+|\tau |^2)-c\log |s_ E|^2 \]

is quasi-psh with a common smooth lower background and is concave in $\lambda $. The residual endpoint potentials at $\lambda =0,c$ are locally bounded there, and so are two interior parameters. Concavity bounds the slopes towards the remaining endpoints from the appropriate side. The finite-grid argument used in the Legendre proof consequently shows that $\sup _{0\leq \lambda \leq b}\phi _\lambda $ is upper semicontinuous on that whole chart: its supremum is within $O(N^{-1})$ of the supremum on a parameter mesh, locally uniformly. Thus its quasi-psh extension agrees with $\widetilde u$ across $E$. Restricting each summand now gives exactly

\[ \widetilde u|_ E =\sup _{0\leq \lambda \leq c} \bigl (\psi _\lambda +(c-\lambda )\log |\zeta |^2\bigr ) +\text{smooth metric potential}, \]

where $\psi _\lambda $ is the restriction of $\widehat u_\lambda -(c-\lambda )\log |z_1|^2$. Its residual mass on $Y$ equals the classical restricted volume of $\alpha -(c-\lambda )\{ Y\} $ by bounded-type comparison with $v_{c-\lambda }$ and lemma 20.8.1. The same Legendre mass identity in dimension $n-1$ now gives (20.8.7). The zero and infinity sections and the common analytic pole sets have zero non-pluripolar mass, so deleting them loses no integral. For a nonzero fiber, the transform at $\lambda =b$ gives a lower comparison with the minimal type in $\alpha $; the reverse comparison is minimality. This proves (20.8.5).

Conservation at the central fiber. We next prove

93C6922E83B2 20.8.8
\begin{equation} \operatorname {Vol}_{\mathcal X\mid X'}(\beta ) +\operatorname {Vol}_{\mathcal X\mid E}(\beta ) =\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha ). \end{equation}

For the inequality $\leq $, restrict one ambient gently analytic current to nearby fibers and use local bounded-product continuity away from its poles and $X'\cap E$. Its fiber mass is bounded by $\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha )$. Apply the exact-class simultaneous approximation assertion of lemma 20.8.1 to $X'$ and $E$. A single strict mixture of a minimal current, gently regularized with less than its strict positivity margin, has both restricted masses at least $(1-\delta )^ n$ times their respective suprema. This proves the inequality on letting $\delta \downarrow 0$.

For the converse choose a smooth convex function $\chi $ with $\chi (s)=s$ for $s\geq 0$ and $\chi (s)=e^ s-1$ for $s\leq -1$, and define, on the base,

\[ g_ k=\chi (\log |\tau |^2+k)-k-\log (1+|\tau |^2),\quad \omega _ k=\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+dd^ cg_ k. \]

The forms $\omega _ k$ are probability measures obtained from one fixed smooth measure, positive at zero, by the scaling $\tau \mapsto e^{-k/2}\tau $. Let $u_ k=P_{\widetilde\theta }(g_ k)$ and $D_ k=\{ u_ k=g_ k\} $. Their total masses equal $\operatorname {Vol}(\beta )$, since each obstacle is smooth and bounded. The smooth contact-set formula applies. Let $g_\infty =\log |s_{\mathcal X_0}|^2$, using the metric whose curvature is the pullback of $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$. The obstacles decrease to $g_\infty $, and their envelope limit is exactly $P_{\widetilde\theta }(g_\infty )$: the limit is an admissible competitor, and each competitor for the limiting obstacle lies below every $u_ k$. Siu decomposition and removal across the fiber give

\[ P_{\widetilde\theta }(g_\infty )-g_\infty =P_{\widetilde\theta -\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}(0). \]

This potential is finite because $\beta -\{ \mathcal X_0\} $ is big. Outside every fixed disk about zero, $g_ k=g_\infty $ for large $k$. There the contact sets decrease precisely to the contact set of this residual envelope. The contact formula and dominated convergence of the fixed smooth contact density consequently show that the limiting mass away from zero is bounded by $\operatorname {Vol}(\beta -\{ \mathcal X_0\} )$. Applying (20.8.4) with $b$ and with $b-1$ gives

\[ \operatorname {Vol}(\beta )- \operatorname {Vol}(\beta -\{ \mathcal X_0\} ) =(n+1)\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha ). \]

Hence at least this much $(n+1)$-dimensional mass concentrates near zero. On $D_ k$, positivity and the rank-one base form give

\[ \mathrm{MA}_{\widetilde\theta }(u_ k) \leq \mathbf1_{D_ k}\widetilde\theta ^{n+1} +(n+1)\mathbf1_{D_ k}\widetilde\theta ^ n\wedge \omega _ k. \]

The integral of the first term over a shrinking base disk tends to zero, because it is a fixed smooth top form. It follows that

\[ \liminf _ k\int _{\mathbb P^1} \left(\int _{\mathcal X_\tau \cap D_ k}\widetilde\theta ^ n\right) \omega _ k\geq \operatorname {Vol}(\alpha ). \]

Each inner integral lies between zero and $\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha )$ by the contact-set identity on the fiber and (20.8.5). The fixed rescaled measure gives positive mass to every disk about zero. A diagonal choice consequently gives $\tau _ k$ such that

\begin{equation} e^{k/2}|\tau _ k|\longrightarrow 0,\qquad \int _{\mathcal X_{\tau _ k}\cap D_ k}\widetilde\theta ^ n \longrightarrow \operatorname {Vol}(\alpha ). \end{equation}

Fix $\varepsilon >0$ and choose the resolved majorant $\psi $ of lemma 20.8.2 for $\beta $. Define the relative envelopes

\[ v_ k=\sup \{ v\leq g_ k:v\in \operatorname {PSH} (\mathcal X,\widetilde\theta +\varepsilon \eta ),\ v\preceq \psi \} . \]

On the resolution these equal $P_\kappa (g_ k\circ \pi -\psi \circ \pi )+\psi \circ \pi $. The correspondence follows from Siu decomposition of the prescribed divisorial singularities. The envelopes on the resolution are bounded, so $v_ k$ has precisely the type of $\psi $ for each fixed $k$. Because $u_ k\preceq \widetilde u\preceq \psi $, one has $u_ k\leq v_ k$; in particular $D_ k\subset \{ v_ k=g_ k\} $.

On either central component, $v_ k+k$ increases to a limit $v_0$: the obstacles $g_ k+k$ increase and equal $-1$ there. On compact sets away from the poles these limits are bounded. The trace bound of lemma 16.44.1, applied on the fixed resolution, gives on a coordinate product $U\times \{ |\tau |\leq e^{-k/2}\} $ away from the poles

\[ |v_ k+k|\leq C_ U,\qquad |\Delta v_ k|\leq C_ Ue^ k. \]

Indeed the upper trace of $dd^ cg_ k$ is $O(e^ k)$ globally, whereas $g_ k-\psi -\inf (g_ k-\psi )$ is bounded on that product independently of $k$. Restrict to a complex vertical line and rescale $\tau =e^{-k/2}w$. Its potential and Laplacian are uniformly bounded on the unit disk; the Poisson formula, or the local gradient estimate for its Newton potential and harmonic remainder, yields

\begin{equation} |v_ k(z,\tau )-v_ k(z,0)|\leq C_ Ue^{k/2}|\tau | \quad (|\tau |\leq e^{-k/2}/2). \end{equation}

Take a subsequence of (20.8.9) such that $\delta _ l=e^{k_ l/2}|\tau _{k_ l}|$ decreases and $\sum _ l\delta _ l<\infty $. In fixed local fiber coordinates add smooth potentials for the varying backgrounds. These vary smoothly with $\tau $, so their errors also satisfy (20.8.10). For the resulting local psh functions $w_ l=v_{k_ l}(\cdot ,\tau _{k_ l})+k_ l+\text{background potential}$, monotonicity on the central component gives $w_{l+1}-w_ l\geq -2C_ U\delta _ l$. Therefore

\[ w_ l-2C_ U\sum _{j\geq l}\delta _ j \]

is increasing and has the same limit as $v_{k_ l}(\cdot ,0)+k_ l$, with its fixed background potential added. Bounded monotone continuity proves convergence of their local Monge–Ampère measures on the two central components away from the poles and their intersection.

Each central limit has restricted mass at most that of $\psi $. In fact the sequence on a fixed central component has the same mass as $\psi $ at every finite stage, by invariance under bounded changes. Local monotone continuity and exhaustion off the pole set give the upper bound for the limit's mass; the pole set is pluripolar and receives no non-pluripolar mass. Thus this mass is at most the corresponding restricted volume of $\beta +\varepsilon [\eta ]$.

Finally, the contact identity on a moving fiber gives

\[ \mathrm{MA}_{(\widetilde\theta +\varepsilon \eta )|\mathcal X_\tau } (v_ k|\mathcal X_\tau ) \geq \mathbf1_{D_ k\cap \mathcal X_\tau }\widetilde\theta ^ n. \]

At contact points $\widetilde\theta |\mathcal X_\tau $ is semipositive almost everywhere; adding $\varepsilon \eta $ only increases its top product. A neighborhood of the poles and $X'\cap E$ contributes arbitrarily little to the right side, uniformly for $\tau \to 0$. To see this, dominate the absolute value of the fixed smooth fiber form by a fixed Kähler fiber volume; the fiber integration currents converge to $[X']+[E]$, and the excluded analytic set has measure zero on both components. Apply local convergence and (20.8.9) outside that neighborhood. We obtain

\[ \operatorname {Vol}_{\mathcal X\mid X'}(\beta +\varepsilon [\eta ]) +\operatorname {Vol}_{\mathcal X\mid E}(\beta +\varepsilon [\eta ]) \geq \operatorname {Vol}(\alpha ). \]

Restricted volumes are continuous where the component meets the Kähler locus, so $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ proves the opposite inequality in (20.8.8).

Combining (20.8.8) with the two central volume formulas yields

\[ \operatorname {Vol}(\alpha )-\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha -c\{ Y\} ) =n\int _0^ c\operatorname {Vol}_{X\mid Y}(\alpha -t\{ Y\} )\, dt. \]

The integrand is continuous at zero, since $Y$ meets the Kähler locus throughout this interval. Dividing by $c$ and letting $c\downarrow 0$ gives the left derivative. Applying the same identity at $\alpha +s\{ Y\} $ and using the same continuity gives the right derivative and the asserted formula. $\square$

Use the numerical restricted volume $\langle \alpha ^{n-1}\rangle _{X\mid D}$, defined using currents with analytic singularities, for a prime divisor $D$ and a big class $\alpha $. One may restrict the defining numerical suprema to gentle approximants, which are smooth off their analytic pole sets and have smooth remainders on a log resolution. Indeed, gently regularize a competitor with arbitrarily small extra positivity loss. The approximant is less singular, so its restricted mass, after that extra positive perturbation, is at least the original mass. The outer one-sided perturbation limit absorbs the loss. Conversely gentle approximants are admissible analytic competitors. On them the restricted non-pluripolar mass is precisely the integral of the smooth, hence absolutely continuous, top power off the pole set. Only smoothness after resolution is used in these approximations.

Lemma 20.8.11.90BA432FB977 Let $\mu :Z\to X$ be a composition of smooth-center blowups, and let $\widetilde D$ be the strict transform of a prime divisor $D$. Assume that $\mu $ is an isomorphism near a general point of $D$. Then

\[ \langle (\mu ^*\alpha )^{n-1}\rangle _{Z\mid \widetilde D} =\langle \alpha ^{n-1}\rangle _{X\mid D}. \]

Proof. The generic minimal Lelong number along the two divisors is the same. One inequality follows by pullback. For the other, push forward a positive current in $\mu ^*\alpha $ and use the isomorphism near the generic point. For a big class the generic minimal Lelong number is the infimum over its positive currents. Thus either both divisors lie in the respective non-nef loci, in which case both sides are zero by definition, or neither does. Work in the latter case.

Fix Kähler forms $\omega $ on $X$ and $\eta $ on $Z$. There is $C>0$ with $\mu ^*\omega \leq C\eta $. If $T\in \alpha $ has analytic singularities, $T\geq -\varepsilon \omega $, and no pole containing $D$, then

\[ \mu ^*T+C\varepsilon \eta \geq \mu ^*(T+\varepsilon \omega )\geq 0. \]

Off the exceptional set, pullback preserves the restricted integral; that set has zero measure on the strict transform. Taking the suprema and then $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ gives the inequality from left to right with $\geq $.

For the reverse inequality we can choose

\[ [\eta ]=\mu ^*(\lambda [\omega ])-\{ F\} ,\qquad F\geq 0 \text{ exceptional}, \]

with $\eta $ Kähler, by successive blowup metrics and a sufficiently large $\lambda $. Let $S\in \mu ^*\alpha $ be a numerical competitor with $S\geq -\varepsilon \eta $ and no pole containing $\widetilde D$, and set $R=S+\varepsilon \eta $. The positive current $U=\mu _*R$ has class $\alpha +\varepsilon \lambda [\omega ]$. Moreover

EB71078BBAFF 20.8.12
\begin{equation} \mu ^*U=R+\varepsilon [F]. \end{equation}

Indeed $R+\varepsilon [F]$ is positive in the pullback class $\mu ^*(\alpha +\varepsilon \lambda [\omega ])$. By the correspondence of potentials under a modification of a smooth target, it equals the pullback of its pushforward. That pushforward is $U$, since an exceptional divisor pushes forward to zero. This proves the formula.

Gently regularize $U$ by less singular analytic currents $U_ j$ with positivity loss $\delta _ j\omega \to 0$. Its generic Lelong number along $D$ is zero, so the approximants have no pole containing $D$. After adding $2\delta _ j\omega $, pullback and the restricted monotonicity theorem, applied to (20.8.12), give

\[ \operatorname {Vol}_{X\mid D} (\alpha +(\varepsilon \lambda +2\delta _ j)[\omega ]) \geq \int _{\widetilde D} \left\langle (R|_{\widetilde D})^{n-1}\right\rangle . \]

Here one may first keep the extra $\delta _ j\mu ^*\omega $ on the right; positivity only enlarges that mass. The equality of the base restricted integral with its pullback is computed on the regular isomorphism locus. All omitted sets are analytic and receive no non-pluripolar mass. Classical restricted volume on the left is well defined: $U_ j+2\delta _ j\omega \geq \delta _ j\omega $ is a Kähler current smooth at a general point of $D$.

Let $j\to \infty $ and then $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, or choose a diagonal $\delta _ j=o(\varepsilon )$. Monotonicity of the defining one-sided numerical suprema makes the left side converge to $\langle \alpha ^{n-1}\rangle _{X\mid D}$; the right-side suprema converge to the numerical restricted volume upstairs. This proves the opposite inequality and the lemma. $\square$

Lemma 20.8.13.DA69AEE4E88A Let $\mu :Y\to X$ be a modification and let $F$ be an effective $\mu $-exceptional real divisor. For a big class $\alpha $,

\[ \operatorname {Vol}(\mu ^*\alpha +t\{ F\} )=\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha ) \quad (t\geq 0), \qquad \langle (\mu ^*\alpha )^{n-1}\rangle \cdot \{ F\} =0. \]

Proof. Pulling back a positive current in $\alpha $ and adding $t[F]$ proves the inequality $\geq $: the absolutely continuous top mass is unchanged over the common isomorphism locus. Conversely, pushing forward any positive current in $\mu ^*\alpha +t\{ F\} $ gives a positive current in $\alpha $, with the same absolutely continuous top mass on that locus. The exceptional analytic sets have Lebesgue measure zero, so the reverse inequality follows from the defining supremum of volume. This also proves birational invariance of volume when $t=0$. The derivative formula in theorem 10.99.8 on the big cone, applied on $Y$, identifies the right derivative at $t=0$ with $n\langle (\mu ^*\alpha )^{n-1}\rangle \cdot \{ F\} $. The displayed constant-volume identity makes that derivative zero. $\square$

Lemma 20.8.14.B50F0B700160 For a big class $\beta $ and its divisorial negative part $N(\beta )$,

\[ \langle \beta ^{n-1}\rangle \cdot \{ N(\beta )\} =0. \]

Proof. Let $T_{\min }\in \beta $ have minimal singularities. Its generic divisor coefficients are those of $N(\beta )$, so Siu decomposition gives $T_{\min }-s[N(\beta )]\geq 0$ for $0\leq s\leq 1$. Only finitely many divisors occur: their support is contained in that of any gently analytic Kähler current in the big class. Removing their integration currents does not change the non-pluripolar top mass, because the currents agree off those divisors and all the products give the divisors zero mass. The maximal-mass characterization of volume thus gives

\[ \operatorname {Vol}(\beta -s\{ N(\beta )\} ) \geq \int _ X\langle (T_{\min }-s[N(\beta )])^ n\rangle =\operatorname {Vol}(\beta ). \]

The reverse inequality is pseudoeffective monotonicity of volume. These classes are big since their volume is positive. Differentiate the constant-volume identity at $s=0$ using theorem 10.99.8. The derivative is $-n\langle \beta ^{n-1}\rangle \cdot \{ N(\beta )\} $, proving the claim. $\square$

Lemma 20.8.15.41D346BEA9CE For a big class $\alpha $ and a prime divisor $D\subset E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$,

\[ \langle \alpha ^{n-1}\rangle \cdot \{ D\} =0. \]

Proof. For every sufficiently small $\varepsilon >0$, the class $\beta _\varepsilon =\alpha -\varepsilon [\omega ]$ is big. Its minimal generic Lelong number along $D$ is strictly positive. Indeed, if it were zero, Demailly's gentle analytic regularization of a current with minimal singularities in $\beta _\varepsilon $ would give a current $T_ j\in \beta _\varepsilon $ with $T_ j\geq -\frac12\varepsilon \omega $, analytic singularities, and zero generic Lelong number along $D$. A gentle approximant is smooth off its analytic pole set, and zero generic Lelong number means that this pole set does not contain $D$. Hence it is smooth near a general point of $D$, and $T_ j+\varepsilon \omega $ would be a Kähler current in $\alpha $ smooth at such a point, contrary to $D\subset E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$.

Thus $D$ occurs with a positive coefficient in $N(\beta _\varepsilon )$. The preceding lemma 20.8.14 gives

\[ \langle \beta _\varepsilon ^{n-1}\rangle \cdot N(\beta _\varepsilon )=0. \]

Each summand in this pairing is nonnegative: the positive-product class is a limit of pushforwards of nef powers, and its pairing with any effective divisor is nonnegative. The positive coefficient of $D$ therefore forces $\langle \beta _\varepsilon ^{n-1}\rangle \cdot \{ D\} =0$. The continuous full volume-gradient formula now allows $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, proving the assertion. $\square$

Proposition 20.8.16.7ED04B77FCC6 Let $\alpha $ be big on a compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$. For every prime divisor $D$,

\[ \langle \alpha ^{n-1}\rangle _{X\mid D} =\langle \alpha ^{n-1}\rangle \cdot \{ D\} , \qquad \langle \alpha ^{n-1}\rangle _{X\mid D}=0 \ \Longleftrightarrow \ D\subset E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha ). \]

Proof. For $n=1$, work on the connected component containing the point $D$. The top real cohomology is one dimensional, and a big class has positive degree, so it is a positive multiple of a Kähler class. Its non-Kähler locus is empty, its positive product of order zero is $1$, and the zero-dimensional restricted mass of $D$ is $1$. Both assertions follow. Hence assume $n\geq 2$.

First suppose $D\not\subset E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )$. For each $\varepsilon >0$, regularization of a minimal current in $\alpha $ with zero generic Lelong number along $D$, followed by addition of $\varepsilon \omega $, gives a Kähler current in $\alpha _\varepsilon =\alpha +\varepsilon [\omega ]$ smooth near a general point of $D$. Thus $D$ meets its Kähler locus.

Resolve $D$ by a modification $\mu :Z\to X$ which is an isomorphism near a general point of $D$, and write $\mu ^*D=\widetilde D+F$, with $F\geq 0$ exceptional and $\widetilde D$ smooth. The strict transform meets the Kähler locus of $\mu ^*\alpha _\varepsilon $. For completeness, a Kähler current smooth generically on $D$ pulls back to a current dominating $\delta \mu ^*\omega $; the standard exceptional logarithmic correction on the resolution replaces this by a Kähler lower bound, adding only exceptional poles. No pole contains $\widetilde D$.

Apply theorem 20.8.3 on $Z$ and the full volume-gradient formula theorem 10.99.8 there. Birational invariance of restricted volume lemma 20.8.11, then lemma 20.8.13, give

\[ \langle \alpha _\varepsilon ^{n-1}\rangle _{X\mid D} =\langle (\mu ^*\alpha _\varepsilon )^{n-1}\rangle \cdot \{ \widetilde D\} =\langle (\mu ^*\alpha _\varepsilon )^{n-1}\rangle \cdot \mu ^*\{ D\} . \]

Birational invariance of volume for all small perturbations in the direction $\{ D\} $ and its differentiability identify the last expression with $\langle \alpha _\varepsilon ^{n-1}\rangle \cdot \{ D\} $.

The defining one-sided limit for numerical restricted volume gives

\[ \lim _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \langle \alpha _\varepsilon ^{n-1}\rangle _{X\mid D} =\langle \alpha ^{n-1}\rangle _{X\mid D}. \]

Indeed, if $F(s)$ is the defining supremum over positive currents with analytic singularities in $\alpha +s[\omega ]$ whose poles do not contain $D$, then $F$ is increasing and the two sides are respectively $\lim _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0}\lim _{s\downarrow \varepsilon }F(s)$ and $\lim _{s\downarrow 0}F(s)$. They agree by monotonicity. Continuity of the full volume gradient proves the desired equality.

If $D\subset E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )$, the numerical restricted volume is zero by definition. The coefficient of $D$ in $N(\alpha )$ is positive, so lemma 20.8.14 and the nonnegativity of the separate divisor pairings give $\langle \alpha ^{n-1}\rangle \cdot \{ D\} =0$ as well.

It remains to identify the zero locus. Containment in the non-Kähler locus gives zero positive-product pairing by lemma 20.8.15, and therefore zero restricted volume by the equality already proved. Otherwise a Kähler current $T\in \alpha $, with the permitted analytic singularities and no pole containing $D$, satisfies $T\geq \delta \omega $ for some $\delta >0$. It is an admissible competitor, and its restricted integral is at least $\delta ^{n-1}\int _ D\omega ^{n-1}>0$. This proves the converse. $\square$
