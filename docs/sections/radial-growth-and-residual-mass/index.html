6.51 Radial growth and residual mass677C7F11615C

677C7F11615C

Use $dd^ c=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial /(2\pi )$ and $\ell (z)=\log |z|^2$, so $(dd^ c\ell )^ N=\delta _0$ on $\mathbb C^ N$. All products below are the classical products, retaining the pole mass. An isolated unbounded locus satisfies the common-boundary hypothesis in the theorem on classical products with compact unbounded loci. In particular all mixed products used below are locally finite and their decreasing bounded truncations converge, including at the origin.

Lemma 6.51.1.BE4AE95DA940 Let $N\geq 2$ and let $a,b$ be plurisubharmonic near $0\in \mathbb C^ N$, locally bounded away from zero. Set

\[ m_ j=\bigl ((dd^ ca)^ j\wedge (dd^ cb)^{N-j}\bigr )(\{ 0\} ), \qquad 0\leq j\leq N. \]

Then $m_ j$ are finite and nonnegative, and

\[ m_ j^2\leq m_{j-1}m_{j+1},\qquad 1\leq j<N. \]

Proof. We first explain the boundary reduction. After a coordinate dilation, choose two concentric spheres inside a ball on which both germs are defined. A radial function $A\ell $ with $A>0$ sufficiently large and a suitable additive shift of $a$ satisfy $A\ell <a$ on a neighborhood of the inner sphere and $A\ell >a$ on a neighborhood of the outer sphere. Indeed the two compact annuli have finite upper and lower bounds for $a$, and the difference of the two radial values grows linearly with $A$. Retain $a$ inside the inner sphere, use $\max \{ a,A\ell \} $ between the spheres, and retain $A\ell $ outside the outer sphere. Strict inequalities on the overlap collars make this a plurisubharmonic function on a neighborhood of the closed unit ball. Subtracting the boundary constant, if necessary, makes it equal to $A\ell $ near the unit sphere. Do this separately for $b$. The new functions have the same germs up to constants and coordinate dilation, which do not change their residual mixed masses. Write them again as $a,b$. They are negative on the unit ball and vanish at its boundary.

Here is the required weighted Cauchy–Schwarz argument, with its boundary and approximation details. Suppose first that $a,b,h$ are smooth plurisubharmonic functions on the closed unit ball, vanish at its boundary, and $h\leq 0$. For a closed positive smooth current $T$ of bidegree $(N-2,N-2)$, define

\[ B_ T(f,g)=\int _{B_1}(-h)\, dd^ cf\wedge dd^ cg\wedge T \]

for smooth real $f,g$ vanishing at the boundary. Two integrations by parts, whose boundary terms contain $h$ or $f$, give

\[ B_ T(f,f)=\int _{B_1}(-f)\, dd^ ch\wedge dd^ cf\wedge T =\int _{B_1}df\wedge d^ cf\wedge dd^ ch\wedge T\geq 0. \]

Thus the real symmetric bilinear form $B_ T$ is positive semidefinite. The discriminant of $B_ T(a+tb,a+tb)\geq 0$ gives

BE95E4FF4E8F 6.51.2
\begin{equation} \left(\int (-h)\, dd^ ca\wedge dd^ cb\wedge T\right)^2 \leq \left(\int (-h)(dd^ ca)^2\wedge T\right) \left(\int (-h)(dd^ cb)^2\wedge T\right). \end{equation}

For $N=2$ take $T=1$. For $N>2$, take $T=(dd^ ca)^{j-1}\wedge (dd^ cb)^{N-j-1}$.

We justify this argument for the bounded truncations of our patched germs. Put $a_ k=\max \{ a,-k\} $ and $b_ k=\max \{ b,-k\} $, for all sufficiently large $k$; both have the fixed radial collars above. For an integer $l\geq 1$, put

\[ h_ l=\max \{ \ell /l,-1\} . \]

All three bounded functions extend plurisubharmonically beyond the closed unit ball. Convolve with a radial nonnegative smooth kernel of radius $\varepsilon $. Near the unit sphere the convolutions are radial, hence constant on that sphere. Subtract that constant from each convolution. This produces smooth plurisubharmonic functions with zero boundary values, so the preceding inequality applies. The constants tend to zero. Convolution before this constant shift decreases to each bounded potential as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$; the shifts do not change any Hessian factor. The weight $h_ l$ is continuous, so its convolutions and boundary shifts converge uniformly. By the decreasing-continuity theorem for bounded Bedford–Taylor products, all integrals therefore pass to the limit on compact subsets.

There is no lost boundary contribution in this passage. On a fixed outer collar all Hessians of the convolutions are uniformly bounded. Stokes, applied to their smooth mixed top products, expresses the total mixed mass by a boundary integral of their first and second derivatives, which are uniformly bounded on that collar. Hence these total masses are uniformly bounded. Since the weights converge uniformly to $h_ l$, which vanishes on the boundary, their integrals on a sufficiently thin boundary collar are uniformly small. This passes (6.51.2) to $a_ k,b_ k,h_ l$.

Now let $k\to \infty $. Classical mixed-product continuity applies by the theorem on classical products with compact unbounded loci. The same fixed radial collars give a uniform bound for the total mixed masses, independently of $k$, by the preceding smooth Stokes argument. Thus the continuous weight $h_ l$, zero at the boundary, permits the same passage to the limit for the whole-ball integrals. Finally $-h_ l$ is bounded by one and tends pointwise to $1_{\{ 0\} }$. Dominated convergence for each finite mixed measure proves the stated residual inequality. $\square$

Definition 6.51.3.F85BD40ACE22 For a plurisubharmonic function $v$ near zero, its logarithmic truncation threshold, with the squared-logarithm convention, is

\[ \operatorname {lt}_2(v,0) =\liminf _{r\downarrow 0}\ \lim _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \sup _{r-\varepsilon <|z|<r+\varepsilon } \frac{v(z)}{\log |z|^2}. \]

Only radii below one and $\varepsilon <r$ are used. The inner limit exists in the extended reals because the annuli decrease. Addition of a constant does not change the threshold. The convention with denominator $\log |z|$ is twice this one; in particular finiteness and infinitude do not depend on this normalization.

Lemma 6.51.4.30125A1CC71C Let $v$ be plurisubharmonic near $0\in \mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 2$, locally bounded away from zero, with $\nu (v,0)=0$. For $\gamma >0$, for almost every complex linear map $A:\mathbb C^ n\to \mathbb C$,

\[ \bigl (dd^ c\max \{ v(z),\gamma \log |Az|^2\} \bigr )^ n(\{ 0\} )=0. \]

All functions are considered on a sufficiently small neighborhood of zero; for $A=0$ the logarithmic branch is identically minus infinity.

Proof. On a product neighborhood of zero in $\mathbb C^{n+1}$ set

\[ \Phi (z,w)=\max \{ v(z),\gamma \log |w|^2\} . \]

Its unbounded locus is contained in the origin. The liminf description of the ordinary Lelong number, tested on $(z,0)$, gives $0\leq \nu (\Phi ,0)\leq \nu (v,0)=0$. Write $N=n+1$ and

\[ e_ j=\bigl ((dd^ c\Phi )^ j\wedge (dd^ c\ell )^{N-j}\bigr )(\{ 0\} ). \]

The classical radial-pole identity identifies $e_1$ with $\nu (\Phi ,0)$; see the classical weighted pole-mass identity. All $e_ j$ are finite. The preceding log-convexity lemma, with $a=\Phi ,b=\ell $, implies successively $e_2=\cdots =e_{N-1}=0$. In particular $e_ n=0$.

We spell out the Crofton limiting argument, including its domination. Take radial convolutions $\Phi _ k\downarrow \Phi $ on a smaller ambient ball; they are smooth plurisubharmonic and satisfy $\Phi _ k\geq \Phi $. Fix a compact ball $K$ of linear maps $A$ in the graph chart $S_ A=\{ (z,Az)\} $ of $G(n,n+1)$. Choose $a>0$ so that these graphs above $|z|\leq 2a$ lie in the convolution domain. On the fixed annulus $a/2\leq |z|\leq 2a$, the functions $f_{k,A}(z)=\Phi _ k(z,Az)$ have uniform upper and lower bounds, independent of $k,A\in K$: their upper bound follows on a common ambient compact set, and their lower bound follows from $f_{k,A}\geq \Phi (z,Az)\geq v(z)$.

Choose a smooth cutoff $\eta =1$ on $B_ a$, supported in $B_{2a}$, whose derivatives are supported in this annulus. Stokes gives

\[ \int \eta (dd^ cf_{k,A})^ n =\int f_{k,A}\, dd^ c\eta \wedge (dd^ cf_{k,A})^{n-1}. \]

Applying the bounded Chern–Levine–Nirenberg estimate on a finite cover of the fixed annulus bounds the right side uniformly. Consequently

47C2B29CFB70 6.51.5
\begin{equation} \int _{B_ a}(dd^ cf_{k,A})^ n\leq C_ K. \end{equation}

This is an annular estimate; it assumes no bound for $v$ at its pole.

Let $0\leq \chi _ r\leq 1$ be smooth radial cutoffs on the ambient ball, equal to one near zero, supported in $B_{2r}$, decreasing pointwise to $1_{\{ 0\} }$ as $r\downarrow 0$. For $r$ sufficiently small, the complex Crofton formula gives

\[ \int \chi _ r(dd^ c\Phi _ k)^ n\wedge dd^ c\ell \geq \int _ K\left(\int \chi _ r(z,Az)(dd^ cf_{k,A})^ n\right)\, d\sigma (A), \]

where $d\sigma $ is the smooth positive Grassmannian volume in this chart. For each fixed $A$, $f_{k,A}$ decreases to $f_ A=\max \{ v,\gamma \log |Az|^2\} $, whose unbounded locus is contained in zero. Classical continuity passes its top products to the limit. The bound (6.51.5) and the finite volume of $K$ justify dominated convergence on the right. On the left the same classical continuity applies to the $n$ factors $\Phi _ k$ and the fixed pole $\ell $. Thus the inequality holds with $\Phi $ and $f_ A$. Let $r\downarrow 0$. The left side tends to $e_ n=0$; dominated convergence, or monotone convergence of these nonnegative integrals, gives

\[ \int _ K(dd^ cf_ A)^ n(\{ 0\} )\, d\sigma (A)=0. \]

Hence its nonnegative integrand vanishes almost everywhere on $K$. A countable exhaustion of the graph chart proves the assertion. $\square$

Theorem 6.51.6.E8283D7F161B Let $v$ be plurisubharmonic near $0\in \mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 2$, locally bounded away from zero. If $\nu (v,0)=0$ and $\operatorname {lt}_2(v,0)<\infty $, then

\[ (dd^ cv)^ n(\{ 0\} )=0. \]

Proof. Choose $\gamma >\gamma '>\operatorname {lt}_2(v,0)$, with $\gamma '>0$. The threshold definition supplies radii $r_ k\downarrow 0$ and open annular neighborhoods of their spheres on which $v\geq \gamma '\log |z|^2$. Choose one linear map $A$ supplied by the preceding lemma. Since $|Az|\leq C_ A|z|$ and $\gamma >\gamma '$, on all sufficiently small such annuli we have

\[ \gamma \log |Az|^2 \leq \gamma \log |z|^2+\gamma \log \max \{ C_ A^2,1\} \leq \gamma '\log |z|^2\leq v(z). \]

Thus $f_ A=v$ on collars of these spheres.

For completeness, equality on a boundary collar fixes the total classical mass inside. For the bounded truncations $v_ j=\max \{ v,-j\} $ and $g_ j=\max \{ f_ A,-j\} $, equality holds on the same collar. The identity

\[ (dd^ cv_ j)^ n-(dd^ cg_ j)^ n =dd^ c\left((v_ j-g_ j)\sum _{q=0}^{n-1} (dd^ cv_ j)^ q\wedge (dd^ cg_ j)^{n-1-q}\right) \]

and a cutoff constant on the inner ball show that the integral of the difference there is zero. Its primitive has compact support away from the outer boundary, so no boundary term remains. Bounded-product approximation proves the identity as currents. Passing $j\to \infty $ uses classical continuity; collar contributions coincide and cancel. It follows that $(dd^ cv)^ n(B_{r_ k})=(dd^ cf_ A)^ n(B_{r_ k})$. Continuity from above for these finite measures gives equality of their origin atoms. The latter atom is zero by the choice of $A$. $\square$

Proposition 6.51.7.A7C29032BBE0 Suppose $v$ is plurisubharmonic near zero and locally bounded away from zero. If its distributional radial derivative $r\partial _ rv$ is essentially bounded on a punctured ball, then

\[ v(z)\geq C\log |z|-C'\quad \text{near zero} \]

for some finite $C,C'$. In particular $\operatorname {lt}_2(v,0)<\infty $.

Proof. In polar coordinates put $t=\log r$. Fubini for distributions and the one-dimensional fundamental theorem for a bounded weak derivative show that, for almost every angular direction, $v(e^ t\theta )$ has a locally absolutely continuous representative with derivative bounded in absolute value by one common constant $C$. Fix a compact starting annulus. Local boundedness of $v$ there gives a common lower bound; choose a starting time in that annulus for which the radial representatives agree almost everywhere with $v$. Integration down the rays gives $v(z)\geq C\log |z|-C'$ almost everywhere. Upper semicontinuity passes this inequality to every nonzero point by approaching it through the full-measure set. The threshold assertion follows on dividing by the negative number $\log |z|^2$. $\square$

6.51.8 Asymmetry of an isolated pole9815D2DE80DF

Corollary 6.51.9.2B4173DC2F80 The explicit potential $U$ of theorem 6.50.11 is not invariant under simultaneous circular rotations. It is not uniformly directional Lipschitz near zero, in the sense that $r\partial _ rU$ cannot be essentially bounded on any punctured neighborhood. Its logarithmic truncation threshold is infinite.

Proof. The last assertion follows from theorem 6.51.6: this potential has zero Lelong number and positive residual mass. The radial proposition then excludes uniform directional Lipschitz continuity.

There is also a direct numerical witness for the failure of circular invariance. Use the iterates and $p_ j$ of definition 6.49.3. Put

\[ \varepsilon _1=\frac1{4e},\qquad \zeta =\left(\mathrm i\sqrt{\varepsilon _1/16},\frac1{32}\right). \]

Both $\zeta $ and $\mathrm i\zeta $ lie in the unit bidisc. Direct substitution in $H_1=F_{\varepsilon _1}$ gives

\[ \| H_1(\zeta )\| _\infty =\frac1{2048},\qquad \| H_1(\mathrm i\zeta )\| _\infty =\frac{\sqrt2\varepsilon _1}{32}. \]

The ratio of the latter to the former is $16\sqrt2/e>4$. Moreover $p_1(\zeta )=-11\log 2>-8$, and the rotated point has a larger $p_1$ value. The visible-tail estimate lemma 6.50.9, at $j=1$, therefore gives

\[ U(\mathrm i\zeta )\geq p_1(\mathrm i\zeta )-2\log 2 >p_1(\zeta )\geq U(\zeta ). \]

This proves noninvariance directly from the construction, without using a symmetry-case zero-mass theorem. $\square$
