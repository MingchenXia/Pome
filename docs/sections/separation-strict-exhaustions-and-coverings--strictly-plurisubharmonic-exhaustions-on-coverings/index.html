9.28 Strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustions on coverings5FA7A48903C4

5FA7A48903C4

The manifolds below are Hausdorff and have a countable basis. A finite continuous exhaustion has compact closed sublevels. Strong pseudoconvexity means the existence of a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion, as in section 9.26. A holomorphic unramified covering is a covering map whose restrictions to its local sheets are biholomorphisms.

We first supply the metric compactness needed in the construction. For a Riemannian metric $g$, the length distance $d_ g$ is the infimum of the lengths of piecewise smooth connecting paths. On a connected manifold these distances are finite: the points accessible from one point by finitely many coordinate segments form an open and closed set. The distance induces the manifold topology. Indeed, on a compact coordinate ball the metric has fixed positive lower and upper Euclidean bounds; a path leaving a smaller concentric ball has a fixed positive length before its first exit, whereas a coordinate segment gives the local upper distance bound.

Lemma 9.28.1.9DBBB1BAE0CF In a complete, locally compact length metric space, every closed bounded ball is compact. In particular, this holds for the length distance of a complete Riemannian metric.

Proof. Fix a center $o$. Local compactness gives a small closed ball about $o$ contained in a compact neighborhood, hence compact. Let $R$ be the supremum of the radii of compact closed balls about $o$. All closed balls of radius $r<R$ are compact, since each is a closed subset of a compact ball of a larger radius. Suppose $R<\infty $.

The closed ball of radius $R$ is totally bounded. For an arbitrary $\varepsilon >0$, choose $r<R$ with $R-r<\varepsilon /4$. A point $y$ of that ball can be joined to $o$ by a path of length less than $d(o,y)+\varepsilon /4$. If its length exceeds $r$, cut it after length $r$; the cut point lies in the compact ball of radius $r$ and its distance to $y$ is less than $\varepsilon /2$. If its length does not exceed $r$, then $y$ already lies in that ball. A finite $\varepsilon /2$-net of the smaller ball is thus a finite $\varepsilon $-net of the ball of radius $R$. The latter ball is complete, being closed in a complete space, and therefore compact. Here completeness and total boundedness imply compactness by the finite-net and Cauchy-subsequence argument used in the Arzelà–Ascoli compactness theorem.

For each point of this compact ball choose $a_ p>0$ such that the closed metric ball $\overline B(p,3a_ p)$ is compact. Finitely many $B(p_ i,a_ i)$ cover it. Put $a=\min _ i a_ i>0$. Any point $y$ with $d(o,y)\leq R+a/2$ has a connecting path of length less than $R+3a/4$. If that length exceeds $R$, its cut point after length $R$ belongs to $B(p_ i,a_ i)$ for some $i$, and then $d(y,p_ i)<a_ i+3a/4<2a_ i$. If its length is at most $R$, then $y$ itself lies in one of the $B(p_ i,a_ i)$. Thus the closed ball of radius $R+a/2$ is contained in the finite union of the compact closed balls $\overline B(p_ i,3a_ i)$. Being closed, it is compact, contradicting the definition of $R$. Hence $R=\infty $. $\square$

Proposition 9.28.2.E200BE9CFD9E Let $\pi :\widetilde X\to X$ be an unramified covering of connected complex manifolds. If $g$ is a Riemannian metric on $X$ complete for its length distance, then $\widetilde g=\pi ^*g$ is complete. For every $y_0\in \widetilde X$,

\[ \delta _0(y)=d_{\widetilde g}(y,y_0) \]

is a nonnegative continuous exhaustion and is $1$-Lipschitz.

Proof. Projection preserves the length of every piecewise smooth path, so $d_ g(\pi y,\pi z)\leq d_{\widetilde g}(y,z)$. Let $(y_ j)$ be Cauchy upstairs. Its projections converge to some $x\in X$. Choose an evenly covered neighborhood $V$ of $x$ and $a>0$ so that $B_ g(x,4a)\subset V$. For all sufficiently large indices, $\pi y_ j\in B_ g(x,a)$ and $d_{\widetilde g}(y_ j,y_ k)<a/2$. A path joining $y_ j$ to $y_ k$ with length less than $a$ projects entirely into $B_ g(x,2a)\subset V$. The path upstairs therefore lies in one sheet of $\pi ^{-1}(V)$. Fixing one sufficiently large $j$ shows that the whole tail belongs to that same sheet. On it the inverse of $\pi $ is continuous, so $y_ k$ converges to the inverse image of $x$ in the sheet. The length distance induces the manifold topology, proving completeness.

The metric is locally compact because the manifold is locally Euclidean. The preceding lemma makes its closed balls compact. These balls are precisely the nonnegative closed sublevels of $\delta _0$. The triangle inequality proves its continuity and $1$-Lipschitz bound. $\square$

Proposition 9.28.3.7808CCD76C79 Under the same hypotheses there is a smooth real exhaustion $\delta $ on $\widetilde X$ with $|\delta -\delta _0|\leq 1$. For each compact subset of a coordinate open set in $X$ and each positive derivative order, the coordinate derivatives of $\delta $ are bounded there uniformly over all sheets of the covering. There is a smooth positive Hermitian form $\gamma $ on $X$ such that

\[ H_\delta \geq -\pi ^*\gamma . \]

Proof. Choose a countable locally finite cover by relatively compact holomorphic coordinate balls $U_\alpha $, each compactly contained in a larger coordinate ball $W_\alpha $ that is evenly covered. Take a smooth nonnegative partition of unity $\theta _\alpha $ with compact support in $U_\alpha $. These choices use only the coordinate-ball and partition construction in manifold cutoffs, partitions and compact-support Stokes; no intersection condition on different balls is needed.

In the coordinates on $W_\alpha $ there is $L_\alpha <\infty $ such that the $g$-length of a straight segment is at most $L_\alpha $ times its Euclidean length. This follows by bounding the metric above on the compact closure of a slightly larger coordinate ball; choose the balls initially with these nested compact closures. For every inverse sheet $s:W_\alpha \to \widetilde X$, the function $f_{\alpha ,s}=\delta _0\circ s$ is therefore Euclidean $L_\alpha $-Lipschitz: lift the straight segment and use the $1$-Lipschitz bound for $\delta _0$.

Use the nonnegative compactly supported radial mollifier already constructed in convolution and mollifier conventions. Choose $\varepsilon _\alpha >0$ smaller than the coordinate distance from $\overline U_\alpha $ to $\partial W_\alpha $, and so small that $L_\alpha \varepsilon _\alpha \leq 1$. Put

\[ f^\varepsilon _{\alpha ,s}(z) =\int \rho _{\varepsilon _\alpha }(t) f_{\alpha ,s}(z-t)\, \mathrm dt, \qquad z\in U_\alpha . \]

The width is independent of the sheet, and

2037D5BC099D 9.28.4
\begin{equation} |f^\varepsilon _{\alpha ,s}-f_{\alpha ,s}|\leq 1, \qquad |D^\nu f^\varepsilon _{\alpha ,s}| \leq C_{\rho ,\nu }L_\alpha \varepsilon _\alpha ^{1-|\nu |} \quad (|\nu |\geq 1). \end{equation}

For the second estimate, differentiate the kernel and subtract $f_{\alpha ,s}(z)$ inside the integral, using $\int D^\nu \rho _{\varepsilon _\alpha }=0$. The Lipschitz bound gives $|f_{\alpha ,s}(z-t)-f_{\alpha ,s}(z)|\leq L_\alpha |t|$. The integral after scaling has exactly the displayed power of $\varepsilon _\alpha $. Smoothness and differentiation of the convolution are the existing local mollifier argument; the integration stays in $W_\alpha $.

On each sheet set $\delta _\alpha =f^\varepsilon _{\alpha ,s}\circ \pi $, and form

\[ \delta =\sum _\alpha (\theta _\alpha \circ \pi )\delta _\alpha . \]

Each summand extends smoothly by zero outside the preimage of $U_\alpha $, because its base cutoff has compact support there. The sum is locally finite. The first estimate and $\sum _\alpha \theta _\alpha =1$ give $|\delta -\delta _0|\leq 1$. Thus $\delta \geq -1$, and every closed sublevel is a closed subset of a compact sublevel of $\delta _0$; $\delta $ is a smooth exhaustion.

We check the derivative bound without bounding the unbounded sheetwise constants. Fix a compact coordinate set $C$ in the base. Only finitely many supports $\operatorname {Supp}\theta _\alpha $ meet $C$. The coordinate changes and their derivatives are bounded on the compact intersections $C\cap \operatorname {Supp}\theta _\alpha $ in the respective coordinate overlaps. In the product rule for a derivative of order $|\nu |\geq 1$, all terms with a positive derivative on $\delta _\alpha $ are uniformly bounded by (9.28.4) and the chain rule. The remaining terms, at a point $y$ over $C$, equal

\[ \sum _\alpha D^\nu \theta _\alpha (\pi y)\delta _\alpha (y) =\sum _\alpha D^\nu \theta _\alpha (\pi y) (\delta _\alpha (y)-\delta _0(y)), \]

since $\sum _\alpha D^\nu \theta _\alpha =0$. Their absolute values are bounded by the finite sum of $|D^\nu \theta _\alpha |$. This is a pointwise cancellation after differentiating smooth summands; it does not differentiate the merely Lipschitz function $\delta _0$. The resulting bounds are uniform on every sheet over $C$. Only positive orders are asserted; the values themselves must become unbounded along an infinite covering fiber.

Choose a smooth positive Hermitian form $h$ on $X$ by patching coordinate Euclidean forms with a partition of unity. Cover $X$ by locally finite relatively compact coordinate open sets $V_ j$ and take a subordinate partition of unity $\eta _ j$. The second-order bound on $\overline V_ j$, compared with the positive minimum eigenvalue of $h$ there, gives $C_ j\geq 0$ such that $H_\delta \geq -C_ j\pi ^*h$ over $V_ j$, on every sheet. Set

\[ \gamma =\left(\sum _ j\eta _ j(C_ j+1)\right)h. \]

It is smooth and positive. At each point the inequalities for the indices with $\eta _ j\neq 0$, multiplied by their nonnegative weights and added, give $H_\delta \geq -\pi ^*\gamma $. $\square$

Theorem 9.28.5.73159A8CF2BB Every connected unramified holomorphic covering of a connected strongly pseudoconvex complex manifold is strongly pseudoconvex.

Proof. In complex dimension zero both connected manifolds are points, so the assertion and all the constructions above are immediate. Assume positive dimension. Let $\psi $ be a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion on $X$. It is bounded below: otherwise points with $\psi $ tending to $-\infty $ would lie in one compact sublevel, contradicting continuity there. Shift it so that $\psi \geq 0$.

The needed complete metric does not require a metric existence theorem. Start with any smooth Riemannian metric $g_0$, obtained by a coordinate partition of unity, and put

\[ g=g_0+\mathrm d\psi \otimes \mathrm d\psi . \]

For every connecting path, the difference of its endpoint $\psi $-values is at most its $g$-length. Hence a $d_ g$-Cauchy sequence has bounded $\psi $-values and lies in a compact sublevel. It has a convergent subsequence, and the metric topology is the manifold topology. Cauchyness then gives convergence of the whole sequence. Thus $g$ is complete. The preceding propositions produce $\delta $ and $\gamma $ for this metric.

Since $H_\psi $ is positive definite, for $t\geq 0$ there is a finite nondecreasing bound $b(t)$ such that

\[ \gamma _ x\leq b(t)(H_\psi )_ x\quad \text{when }\psi (x)\leq t. \]

Take the maximum eigenvalue of $\gamma $ relative to $H_\psi $ on the compact sublevel, with value zero if it is empty. Choose a nondecreasing sequence $M_ k\geq 2+b(k+2)$, $k\geq 0$, and a smooth nondecreasing function $\zeta $ equal to zero on $(-\infty ,0]$ and one on $[1,\infty )$. The locally finite sum

\[ a(t)=M_0+\sum _{k\geq 0}(M_{k+1}-M_ k)\zeta (t-k), \qquad \chi (t)=\int _0^ t a(r)\, \mathrm dr \]

defines smooth functions on $\mathbb R$. Such a $\zeta $ is obtained by integrating a nonnegative smooth bump on $(0,1)$ with integral1. We have $\chi '>0$, $\chi ''\geq 0$, and $\chi (t)\geq 0$ for $t\geq 0$. For $t\in [k,k+1]$, $a(t)\geq M_ k\geq 2+b(t)$.

The complex chain rule therefore gives, for $F=\delta +\chi (\psi \circ \pi )$,

\[ H_ F\geq -\pi ^*\gamma +\chi '(\psi \circ \pi )\pi ^*H_\psi +\chi ''(\psi \circ \pi ) \partial (\psi \circ \pi )\otimes \overline{\partial (\psi \circ \pi )} \geq 2\pi ^*H_\psi >0. \]

The last form is positive definite because $\pi $ is locally biholomorphic. Thus $F$ is smooth and strictly plurisubharmonic by the smooth Hessian criterion the Levi criterion and decreasing convolution. Finally $\chi (\psi \circ \pi )\geq 0$ and $\delta \geq \delta _0-1$, so $F\geq \delta _0-1$. Every closed sublevel of $F$ is consequently compact. This proves the required strict exhaustion. $\square$
