25.3 Nonlinear products, capacity, and massE66D40EC5904

E66D40EC5904

The complex Monge–Ampère operator is nonlinear: a distributional complex Hessian cannot generally be multiplied by itself. The decisive construction of Bedford–Taylor (1976) and Bedford–Taylor (1982) defines mixed products for locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions by integration by parts. Continuity under monotone limits makes this definition useful beyond the smooth case. The chapter on local Monge–Ampère theory and capacity develops this calculus before the global theory uses it.

The Bedford–Taylor calculus is also the starting point for the classical unbounded products treated in Demailly's exposition. Compactness or dimension conditions on the unbounded loci permit further products by controlling their mass. Such classical products can retain mass on a pole set and therefore differ from the non-pluripolar construction, which removes that mass. Weighted boundary measures and Jensen identities belong to the same classical current framework, developed in Demailly’s memoir and work on pluriharmonic measures; their normalizations and pole terms are retained in the statements.

The associated capacity measures sets by testing them against bounded plurisubharmonic potentials. It supports quasi-continuity, comparison principles, and continuity of products under suitable convergence in capacity. This convergence must be read with its domain quantifier. On an open set, monotone convergence of plurisubharmonic functions gives convergence on each compact subset; exceptional sets can escape toward the boundary with capacity bounded away from zero on the entire open set. On a compact manifold a finite covering converts the local conclusion into a global one. This distinction is the nonlinear counterpart of the escape-of-mass issue in ordinary weak convergence.

Unbounded potentials require a further choice: what mass should be retained at their poles? Truncation produces bounded products, and restriction away from the truncation loci yields the non-pluripolar product. Bedford–Taylor's work on pluripolar sets, Guedj–Zeriahi, and Boucksom–Eyssidieux–Guedj–Zeriahi form successive stages of this development. The resulting measure does not charge pluripolar sets. Consequently, even in a fixed cohomology class, its total mass can decrease when the potential becomes more singular. Full mass is therefore a substantive condition.

The chapter on non-pluripolar measures and mass studies the resulting order and continuity questions. Bounded changes of singularities preserve mixed masses, while comparisons between different singularity types require monotonicity theorems. Work of Witt Nyström and Darvas–Di Nezza–Lu established the mass comparisons used in the modern relative theory. These results connect local truncation arguments with global intersection numbers, and explain why assumptions about singularities must accompany statements about mass.

On a general Hermitian manifold the background form need not be closed. Bedford–Taylor products remain available locally after a smooth correction, but global integration by parts now produces torsion terms. The total mass can vary even among bounded potentials. This is the point at which the Kähler and Hermitian developments separate: the local nonlinear calculus continues to be useful, while global mass, comparison, and normalization need new estimates. The next sections follow the geometric and equation-theoretic consequences of that separation.

Several local extensions explain why the domain of the operator matters. Klimek reports the obstruction of Shiffman and Taylor to a positive multilinear extension on the full plurisubharmonic cone. The regularizations attributed to Cegrell are discussed by Błocki. Generalized-function products provide another setting: Klimek credits Kiselman and Oberguggenberger, while the elementary Colombeau definitions used here are recalled by Nigsch. These constructions have different compatibility and convergence requirements. The boundary measures associated with logarithmic poles belong to the Lelong–Jensen theory developed by Demailly.

Symmetry and total mass also depend on the domain geometry. Cegrell studies symmetric Green envelopes, and Bedford–Demailly give a smooth-domain asymmetry example. The smooth-boundary Fatou–Bieberbach construction of Stensønes supplies the domain used here to test total-mass comparison without properness. The comparison associated with Taylor is retained with a proper-exhaustion hypothesis; the unrestricted nonproper assertion discussed in Klimek does not survive that test.
