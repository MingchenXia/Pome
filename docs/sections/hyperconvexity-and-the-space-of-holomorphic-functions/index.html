9.21 Hyperconvexity and the space of holomorphic functionsA59EB75E698B

A59EB75E698B

Let $\Delta ^ n$ denote the unit polydisc. The space $\mathcal O(D)$ has its compact-open Fréchet topology. Isomorphisms below are continuous complex-linear bijections with continuous inverses; they need not preserve multiplication.

Lemma 9.21.1.EA48C877147E Let $D\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be bounded and hyperconvex. Put $H=A^2(D)$, with its usual Lebesgue $L^2$ norm, and let $B_ H$ be its closed unit ball. If $p$ is the $L^2$ norm on any relatively compact open subset of $D$, there are another compact-open continuous norm $q$ and $C>0$ such that, for every $r>0$,

\[ \{ f\in \mathcal O(D):q(f)\leq 1\} \subset CrB_ H+r^{-1}\{ f\in \mathcal O(D):p(f)\leq 1\} . \]

In particular $H$ is dense in $\mathcal O(D)$.

Proof. Take a continuous negative PSH exhaustion $\rho $. The function $-\log (-\rho )$ is a PSH exhaustion, so $D$ is pseudoconvex. Choose $a<0$ so that the compact set used for $p$ lies in $\{ \rho <a\} $. Choose a smooth cutoff $\chi $, equal to one on $\{ \rho \leq a/2\} $ and supported in $\{ \rho <a/4\} $. These sets are compactly nested, so the ordinary cutoff construction applies. Take a relatively compact open set $D_ m$ containing its support and the compact set for $p$, and set $q(f)=\| f\| _{L^2(D_ m)}$.

For $L\geq 0$ put $P_ L=L\max \{ 0,\rho -a\} $. It is PSH, $0\leq P_ L\leq -aL$, vanishes on $\{ \rho \leq a\} $ and is at least $-aL/2$ on $\operatorname {supp}\bar\partial \chi $. Apply the weighted $L^2$ existence theorem with weight $P_ L+|z|^2$ to the closed form $f\bar\partial \chi $. Boundedness of $D$ and the fixed cutoff give a solution $v$ with

\[ \bar\partial v=f\bar\partial \chi ,\qquad \int _ D|v|^2e^{-P_ L}\, dV\leq C_0e^{aL/2}q(f)^2. \]

The constant is independent of $L$ and $f$; the smooth part $|z|^2$ has Hessian equal to the identity and its exponential is bounded above and below on $D$. Thus

\[ p(v)\leq C_1e^{aL/4}q(f),\qquad \| v\| _{L^2(D)}\leq C_1e^{-aL/4}q(f). \]

The distribution $g=\chi f-v$ is holomorphic on $D$ and belongs to $H$. On the set for $p$ the residual $f-g$ equals $v$. With $R=e^{-aL/4}\geq 1$ this gives $\| g\| _ H\leq C_2Rq(f)$ and $p(f-g)\leq C_1R^{-1}q(f)$. Replace $R$ by a fixed multiple of $r$ to make the second coefficient $r^{-1}$; enlarge the first constant. For the remaining bounded range of $r$, use $g=0$ and $p(f)\leq q(f)$, or the estimate with $R=1$, and enlarge the constant once more. This proves the inclusion for all $r>0$. These $L^2$ norms generate the compact-open topology by local submean estimates. Letting $r\to \infty $ proves density. $\square$

Lemma 9.21.2.8BD46FA4016F Let $D\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be a bounded domain and let $P\Subset D$ be a polydisc, with a concentric larger polydisc still compact in $D$. Let $H=A^2(D)$ and let $H_0$ be the closure of its restrictions in $L^2(P)$. The restriction $R:H\to H_0$ is injective and compact. After a fixed rescaling of the $H_0$ norm it has singular values $0<a_ j<1$ and orthonormal bases $(e_ j)$ of $H$ and $(b_ j)$ of $H_0$ with $Re_ j=a_ jb_ j$. There are constants $c,C>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $j$,

\[ c j^{1/n}\leq -\log a_ j\leq C j^{1/n}. \]

Proof. Restriction is bounded and injective by the identity theorem. Local submean and Cauchy estimates give uniform bounds and equicontinuity on compact subsets for the unit ball of $H$. Compactness of restriction follows from finite coverings and the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem.

We recall the elementary Hilbert argument which supplies its singular system. The compact positive operator $R^*R$ attains its largest Rayleigh quotient on the unit sphere. Indeed take a maximizing sequence, use weak Hilbert compactness and compactness of the operator to pass to the limit, and normalize the limit if necessary. Variation in directions orthogonal to a maximizing unit vector makes it an eigenvector. Repeat in its orthogonal complement. The positive eigenvalues tend to zero, since the images of an orthonormal sequence under a compact operator cannot stay separated. On the remaining orthogonal complement the operator vanishes; otherwise the same maximization would give another positive eigenvector; its eigenvalue would be bounded above by all the preceding eigenvalues, which tend to zero. Injectivity removes this kernel. Taking square roots of the eigenvalues gives $a_ j$ and $e_ j$; $b_ j=a_ j^{-1}Re_ j$ is orthonormal and complete because $R$ has dense range in $H_0$. Rescale the target norm so that $\| R\| <1$.

Let $N_ m=\binom {m+n}{n}$, the dimension of polynomials of total degree at most $m$. Taylor truncation on the concentric polydiscs, with Cauchy estimates on the larger one, gives an operator of rank at most $N_ m$ approximating $R$ with error at most $C_0\theta ^ m$, where $0<\theta <1$. To be explicit, the coefficient bound is $C_1s^{-|\alpha |}\| f\| _ H$ on a larger polydisc of radius $s$; the tail on a smaller radius $t<s$ is bounded by $C_1\sum _{k>m}\binom {k+n-1}{n-1}(t/s)^ k\| f\| _ H$. This is at most $C_0\theta ^ m\| f\| _ H$ after slightly increasing $\theta $. The truncated polynomials lie in $H$, hence in the target closure. The singular system shows $a_{N_ m+1}\leq C_0\theta ^ m$: any rank-$N_ m$ operator has a kernel vector in the span of the first $N_ m+1$ singular vectors.

Conversely enclose $D$ in a polydisc of radius $T$. Orthogonality of monomials on $P$ gives, for every polynomial $p$ of degree at most $m$, a bound $\| p\| _ H\leq C_2C_3^ m\| p\| _{H_0}$. Indeed the squared norm of $z^\alpha $ on a polydisc of radius $t$ is $\pi ^ n\prod _{k=1}^ nt^{2\alpha _ k+2}/(\alpha _ k+1)$; apply Cauchy–Schwarz to its finite Taylor sum on the enclosing polydisc. The resulting polynomial factors in $m$ are absorbed by increasing $C_3>1$. Translation of the polydiscs and the fixed norm rescaling only change the constants. In the $N_ m$-dimensional polynomial subspace there is a unit vector orthogonal to $e_1,\ldots ,e_{N_ m-1}$. For that vector the singular expansion bounds the restricted norm above by $a_{N_ m}$, so $a_{N_ m}\geq C_2^{-1}C_3^{-m}$. Since $N_ m$ is comparable to $m^ n$, the two bounds prove the claim. In particular $\sum _ ja_ j^ t<\infty $ for every $t>0$. $\square$

Proposition 9.21.3.35087713FD64 For a bounded hyperconvex domain $D\subset \mathbb C^ n$, $\mathcal O(D)$ is isomorphic to $\mathcal O(\Delta ^ n)$ as a Fréchet space. It admits a basis which is orthonormal in $A^2(D)$.

Proof. Put $E=\mathcal O(D)$ and use the Hilbert spaces and singular system of lemma 9.21.2. Density from lemma 9.21.1 identifies $H_0$ also as the closure of restrictions of $E$. Its norm $p_0$ is a continuous norm on $E$. Choose increasing relatively compact open sets exhausting $D$, containing $P$, and let $p_ k$ be their $L^2$ norms, scaled if necessary so that $p_ k\geq p_0$. These generate the topology of $E$.

First, for every $k$ there are $C_ k>0$ and $0<\lambda _ k<1$ such that, for $f\in H$,

\[ p_ k(f)\leq C_ kp_0(f)^{\lambda _ k}\| f\| _ H^{1-\lambda _ k}. \]

Here is the propagation argument. Local submean estimates bound $f$ on a small ball in $P$ by $Cp_0(f)$, and on every fixed larger ball compact in $D$ by $C\| f\| _ H$. On three concentric balls the maximum-modulus bound is logarithmically convex in the logarithm of the radius: restrict to each complex line through the center and apply the subharmonic maximum principle to $\log |f|$ minus the harmonic interpolant in $\log |\zeta |$ on a planar annulus. Connect the initial ball to a finite covering of the compact set for $p_ k$ by finitely many overlapping balls, all with their fixed larger balls in $D$. Repeated three-radius interpolation propagates the initial estimate with an exponent which is a finite product of positive numbers less than one. This proves the inequality; take the smallest of the finitely many exponents, using $p_0\leq \| \cdot \| _ H$. Consequently $p_ k(e_ j)\leq C_ ka_ j^{\lambda _ k}$.

Define $\ell _ j\in E'$ by $\ell _ j(f)=a_ j^{-1}\langle f|_ P,b_ j\rangle _{H_0}$. Its dual norm for $p_0$ is $a_ j^{-1}$ and its norm on $H$ is one. For a functional $\ell $ write $p_ k^*(\ell )$ for its supremum on the $p_ k$ unit ball and $p_ H^*(\ell )$ for its supremum on $B_ H$. The splitting lemma and optimization in $r>0$ give, for some $m=m(k)$,

\[ p_ m^*(\ell )\leq C_ k' p_ H^*(\ell )^{1/2}p_ k^*(\ell )^{1/2} \]

whenever the quantities on the right are finite. Starting with $p_0$ and iterating gives, for every integer $s\geq 1$, an index $k_ s$ and a constant $A_ s$ independent of $j$ such that $p_{k_ s}^*(\ell _ j)\leq A_ sa_ j^{-2^{-s}}$. Thus, for every $t>0$, choose $s$ with $2^{-s}<t$ to obtain

\[ \sum _ j|\ell _ j(f)|a_ j^ t \leq A_ sp_{k_ s}(f)\sum _ ja_ j^{t-2^{-s}}<\infty . \]

Conversely, if a sequence $(c_ j)$ satisfies $\sum _ j|c_ j|a_ j^ t<\infty $ for every $t>0$, then $\sum _ jc_ je_ j$ converges absolutely for every $p_ k$, by the primal interpolation bound. Completeness of $E$ gives its sum in $E$. The functionals satisfy $\ell _ j(e_ i)=\delta _{ij}$. For any $f\in E$, the series with $c_ j=\ell _ j(f)$ equals $f$: it converges in $E$ and in $H_0$, and its $H_0$ sum is precisely the orthonormal expansion in $(b_ j)$. The identity theorem identifies the functions on all of $D$. These estimates in both directions prove the topological isomorphism

\[ E\simeq \{ (c_ j):\sum _ j|c_ j|a_ j^ t<\infty \text{ for every }t>0\} . \]

The basis $(e_ j)$ is also an orthonormal basis of $H$.

Finally $-\log a_ j$ is comparable to $j^{1/n}$. Ordering the monomials of the unit polydisc by total degree gives the same sequence space: their degrees are comparable to $j^{1/n}$ after finitely many indices, and the Cauchy estimates identify compact-open convergence of Taylor series with $\sum _ j|c_ j|r^{\deg \alpha _ j}<\infty $ for every $r<1$. Changing the positive constants multiplying $j^{1/n}$ just changes the parameter $t>0$. This proves the asserted isomorphism. $\square$

Proposition 9.21.4.9028D1B15EA2 Let $D\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be a bounded domain of holomorphy. If $\mathcal O(D)\simeq \mathcal O(\Delta ^ n)$ as Fréchet spaces, then $D$ has a negative PSH exhaustion and is hyperconvex.

Proof. Let $T:\mathcal O(\Delta ^ n)\to \mathcal O(D)$ be the isomorphism and put $f_\alpha =T(z^\alpha )$. For $\alpha \ne 0$ set $v_\alpha =|\alpha |^{-1}\log |f_\alpha |$. On every compact subset $K\Subset D$, continuity of $T$ gives constants $C_ K>0$, $r_ K<1$ such that $\sup _ K|f_\alpha |\leq C_ Kr_ K^{|\alpha |}$. The regularized tail envelopes

\[ u_ m=\left(\sup _{|\alpha |\geq m}v_\alpha \right)^*,\qquad \rho =\lim _{m\to \infty }u_ m \]

are PSH and decrease; their limit is PSH or identically $-\infty $. The local uniform bound just obtained gives $\rho <0$ everywhere, and $\rho (z)\geq \limsup _{|\alpha |\to \infty }v_\alpha (z)$.

Fix $0<r<1$ and choose $r<r_1<r_2<1$. If $\rho (z)\leq \log r$, there is a finite constant $A_ z$ such that $|f_\alpha (z)|\leq A_ zr_1^{|\alpha |}$ for every multi-index, including $\alpha =0$ after enlarging $A_ z$. For $g\in \mathcal O(D)$, expand $T^{-1}g=\sum c_\alpha z^\alpha $. Cauchy's coefficient estimate on the polydisc of radius $r_2$ gives

\[ |g(z)|\leq A_ z\sum _\alpha |c_\alpha |r_1^{|\alpha |} \leq A_ zC_{r_1,r_2}\| T^{-1}g\| _{r_2\overline{\Delta }^ n}. \]

The Taylor expansion may be evaluated after $T$ because it converges in the compact-open topology and $T$ is continuous. Continuity of $T^{-1}$ gives one compact $L\Subset D$ and $C'>0$, depending on $r_2$ but not on $z$ or $g$, such that the last norm is at most $C'\| g\| _ L$.

Apply this bound to $g^ k$, where $k$ is an arbitrary positive integer. Taking $k$th roots and letting $k\to \infty $ eliminates the finite constant $A_ zC_{r_1,r_2}C'$ and proves $|g(z)|\leq \| g\| _ L$. Although $T$ need not preserve multiplication, it applies to every $g^ k$ with the same estimate. Hence

\[ \{ \rho \leq \log r\} \subset \widehat L_{\mathcal O(D)}\Subset D. \]

The last compactness is holomorphic convexity of a domain of holomorphy. If $\rho $ were identically $-\infty $, this inclusion would put the whole noncompact open domain $D$ in one compact subset of $D$, which is impossible. Thus $\rho $ is a negative PSH exhaustion. Apply proposition 7.25.1 to obtain a continuous negative PSH exhaustion, proving hyperconvexity. $\square$

Theorem 9.21.5.EA836E82DAC5 For a bounded domain $D\subset \mathbb C^ n$ the following are equivalent:

  1. $D$ is hyperconvex;

  2. $D$ is pseudoconvex and $\mathcal O(D)$ is isomorphic to $\mathcal O(\Delta ^ n)$ as a Fréchet space;

  3. for some $a\in D$, $g_ D(z,a)\to 0$ at every point of $\partial D$;

  4. the same boundary limit holds for every $a\in D$.

Proof. A hyperconvex domain is pseudoconvex, and the Bergman sequence-space proposition proves the required linear isomorphism. Conversely the theorem 9.13.2 makes a pseudoconvex domain a domain of holomorphy, so the preceding proposition proves hyperconvexity. The last two equivalences are theorem 7.25.2. $\square$

Sources. Aytuna, On Stein manifolds $M$ for which $\mathcal O(M)$ is isomorphic to $\mathcal O(\Delta ^ n)$ as Fréchet spaces [Aytuna1988] (1988), Theorems 2 and 9; Vogt, Eine Charakterisierung der Potenzreihenräume von endlichem Typ und ihre Folgerungen [Vogt1982] (1982), the Hilbert-scale argument of Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 7.5; Klimek, p. 225. The bounded-domain proof above supplies the spectral and Taylor-size arguments directly, and uses the existing weighted $L^2$ and Levi theorems. No general classification of nuclear Fréchet spaces is assumed. The general Stein-manifold classification is not asserted here.
