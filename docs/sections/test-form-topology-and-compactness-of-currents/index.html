2.10 Test-form topology and compactness of currentsB52B8F8A65A9

2.10.1 Topologies on test forms and weak compactness of currentsABDBA827C9B4

Let $M$ be a smooth Hausdorff real manifold of dimension $m$ with a countable basis, and let $0\leq p\leq m$. Write $\mathcal E^ p_ s(M)=C^ s(M,\Lambda ^ pT^*M\otimes \mathbb C)$ for $s\in \mathbb N\cup \{ \infty \} $, with the topology of uniform convergence on compact coordinate sets of all coefficient derivatives of order at most $s$. For a compact set $K\subset M$, let $\mathcal D^ p_{K,s}$ be the closed-support subspace of forms supported in $K$. Put $\mathcal D^ p(M)=\bigcup _ K\mathcal D^ p_{K,\infty }$. The notation $\mathcal D'_ p(M)$ means the complex-linear functionals whose restriction to every $\mathcal D^ p_{K,\infty }$ is continuous. Neither the topology of forms nor these functionals require an orientation. Pairings with differential-form coefficients use an orientation when appropriate.

Proposition 2.10.2.BC76137F21A7 Every $\mathcal E^ p_ s(M)$ is a Fréchet space. Each $\mathcal D^ p_{K,\infty }$ is Fréchet, whereas $\mathcal D^ p_{K,s}$ is Banach for finite $s$, with a norm obtained from finitely many compact coordinate sets covering $K$ and all coefficient derivatives of order at most $s$.

Proof. Choose countably many charts covering $M$, and in each chart choose countably many compact coordinate cubes whose interiors cover the chart. The coefficient seminorms on these cubes, with the permitted derivative orders, form a countable family. They generate the stated topology: given any other compact coordinate set, finitely many interiors of the chosen cubes cover it, and the chain rule on their compact intersections bounds its coefficient derivatives by finitely many of the chosen seminorms. After replacing a countable family $(q_ j)$ by its successive maxima, a compatible translation-invariant metric is

\[ d(u,v)=\sum _{j\geq 1}2^{-j}\min \{ 1,q_ j(u-v)\} . \]

The seminorms separate forms, so this is a metric.

A Cauchy sequence has uniform limits for each coefficient and each permitted derivative on every compact cube. The limits are consistent on overlaps, by the coordinate-change formula and its derivatives. On a smaller coordinate rectangle the identity

\[ f_\nu (x+te_ a)-f_\nu (x) =\int _0^ t\partial _ a f_\nu (x+re_ a)\, \mathrm dr \]

passes to the limit whenever the first derivatives are among the permitted ones. Thus the coefficient limit is differentiable with the prescribed first derivatives. Applying the same argument to successive derivatives proves $C^ s$ regularity, or smoothness if $s=\infty $. For $s=0$ only uniform convergence and continuity are needed. This proves completeness for the metric above and hence the Fréchet assertion.

The support condition is closed, since convergence of values outside $K$ preserves their vanishing. Thus $\mathcal D^ p_{K,\infty }$ is a closed Fréchet subspace. For finite $s$, choose compact coordinate sets $L_1,\ldots ,L_ N$ whose interiors cover $K$, and set

\[ P_{K,s}(u)=\max _{1\leq j\leq N}\ \max _{|I|=p,\, |\alpha |\leq s} \sup _{L_ j}|\partial ^\alpha u_{j,I}|. \]

This is a norm on $\mathcal D^ p_{K,s}$. Outside $K$ every coefficient and permitted derivative vanishes. Inside $K$, coordinate changes on the compact intersections with the $L_ j$ show that every defining seminorm is bounded by a constant times $P_{K,s}$. Consequently the norm induces the subspace topology. A norm-Cauchy sequence is Cauchy in $\mathcal E^ p_ s(M)$, its limit remains supported in $K$, and convergence on the finitely many $L_ j$ gives convergence in the norm. This proves the Banach assertion. Empty spaces and $K=\varnothing $ give the zero space, with its unique topology. $\square$

Give $\mathcal D^ p(M)$ the locally convex topology generated by all seminorms whose restriction to every $\mathcal D^ p_{K,\infty }$ is continuous. This extends the scalar test-function topology of the topology of test functions and distributions, rather than changing it. It is the finest locally convex topology making all these inclusions continuous. Indeed, the continuous seminorms of any such topology belong to the displayed family. A linear functional on $\mathcal D^ p(M)$ is continuous exactly when all its fixed-support restrictions are continuous: in the reverse direction its absolute value is itself an allowed seminorm. Thus its continuous dual is precisely $\mathcal D'_ p(M)$.

Proposition 2.10.3.257A408A0F68 The topology just defined induces the usual smooth topology on every $\mathcal D^ p_{K,\infty }$. A family of test forms is bounded for this topology if and only if its supports are contained in one compact set and all coefficient derivatives are uniformly bounded on compact coordinate sets. A sequence converges in this topology if and only if its terms and limit have support in one compact set and converge there in every smooth coefficient seminorm.

If $M$ is noncompact, $\mathcal D^ p(M)$ is dense and proper in $\mathcal E^ p_\infty (M)$ and is incomplete for the topology induced from that space. Its locally convex test-form topology is strictly finer and is not Fréchet. If $M$ is compact, the two spaces and their topologies coincide and are Fréchet.

Proof. Every smooth coefficient seminorm restricts continuously to every fixed-support space, so the inclusion $\mathcal D^ p(M)\longrightarrow \mathcal E^ p_\infty (M)$ is continuous. Conversely, each allowed seminorm is continuous on a fixed-support space by definition. These two observations prove the first assertion.

Choose compact sets $K_ j\subset \operatorname {Int}K_{j+1}$ whose interiors exhaust $M$. Such an exhaustion follows from a countable cover by relatively compact coordinate balls: at each step include the first $j$ closed balls and finitely many more whose interiors cover the preceding compact set. Suppose a bounded family $B$ of test forms has no common compact support. For every $j$ choose $u_ j\in B$ and $x_ j\notin K_ j$ with $u_ j(x_ j)\neq 0$. The points $x_ j$ leave every compact set. Choose a complex-linear functional $\ell _ j$ on the finite-dimensional fiber at $x_ j$ with $\ell _ j(u_ j(x_ j))\neq 0$, and choose $c_ j>0$ so that $c_ j|\ell _ j(u_ j(x_ j))|\geq j$. Then

\[ q(u)=\sup _ j c_ j|\ell _ j(u(x_ j))| \]

is finite for every compactly supported $u$. On every fixed-support space only finitely many terms can be nonzero; they are continuous point-evaluation seminorms. Hence $q$ is an allowed continuous seminorm, whereas $\sup _{u\in B}q(u)=\infty $, a contradiction. This proves the common-support assertion. The remaining boundedness characterization follows from the first assertion: on a fixed-support space a continuous seminorm is bounded by a constant times finitely many coefficient seminorms. Here boundedness in a locally convex space is equivalently boundedness for every continuous seminorm.

A convergent sequence together with its limit is bounded, because each of its continuous seminorms is bounded. It therefore has common compact support, and convergence is smooth by the continuous inclusion into $\mathcal E^ p_\infty (M)$. Conversely, convergence in a fixed-support space implies convergence in $\mathcal D^ p(M)$ by continuity of its inclusion. This proves the sequence assertion; no corresponding common-support assertion for arbitrary convergent nets is made.

Smooth compact cutoffs $\chi _ j=1$ near $K_ j$ give $\chi _ j u\longrightarrow u$ in $\mathcal E^ p_\infty (M)$ for every smooth form $u$, proving density. The real-coordinate cutoff construction preceding Stokes' formula on compact domains with corners supplies these cutoffs. If $M$ is noncompact, choose points escaping every compact set and pairwise disjoint, locally finite, relatively compact coordinate neighborhoods of them. They can be chosen recursively outside an exhaustion enlarged to contain the previously chosen closures. Nonzero smooth $p$-forms supported in these neighborhoods have a locally finite sum with noncompact support. Call this form $u$. The sequence $\chi _ j u$ is Cauchy in the induced smooth topology and has no limit there in $\mathcal D^ p(M)$, since its unique smooth limit is $u$. This proves properness and incompleteness. Choose instead one nonzero test form $v_ j$ supported outside $K_ j$ and normalize it to have value $1$ under a fiber functional at a point outside $K_ j$. Then $v_ j\to 0$ in the induced smooth topology, whereas the seminorm constructed above with weights $c_ j=j$ prevents convergence in the test-form topology. Thus that topology is strictly finer.

Each $\mathcal D^ p_{K_ j,\infty }$ is a closed proper linear subspace of $\mathcal D^ p(M)$: it is the common kernel of the point evaluations outside $K_ j$, and a nonzero bump form outside $K_ j$ proves properness. A proper linear subspace has empty interior, since a neighborhood of zero absorbs every vector. These spaces cover $\mathcal D^ p(M)$. It therefore cannot be Fréchet, by the Baire property of a complete metric space. For completeness, if a complete metric space were a countable union of closed sets with empty interior, one could choose nested closed balls of positive radii at most $2^{-j}$, each contained in the interior of its predecessor and disjoint from the $j$th closed set. Their centers are Cauchy and their common limit belongs to none of the sets, a contradiction. Finally, if $M$ is compact then $\mathcal D^ p(M)=\mathcal D^ p_{M,\infty }=\mathcal E^ p_\infty (M)$; the fixed-support assertion proves equality of topologies. $\square$

Proposition 2.10.4.5481DD68A367 Restriction identifies the continuous dual of $\mathcal E^ p_\infty (M)$ with the currents in $\mathcal D'_ p(M)$ having compact support. A compactly supported current acts on an arbitrary smooth form by inserting any compact cutoff equal to one on a neighborhood of its support.

Proof. Continuity of a linear functional $L$ on $\mathcal E^ p_\infty (M)$ gives a bound by a constant times finitely many compact coordinate seminorms. If $C$ is the union of those compact sets, this bound shows that the current obtained by restriction vanishes on tests supported in $M\setminus C$. Thus its support is contained in $C$. Conversely let $T$ have compact support $C$, and choose a compactly supported smooth $\chi $ equal to one near $C$. Set

\[ L(u)=T(\chi u),\qquad u\in \mathcal E^ p_\infty (M). \]

If $\chi '$ is another such cutoff, $(\chi -\chi ')u$ has compact support and vanishes near $C$, so $T((\chi -\chi ')u)=0$. Indeed, the support definition and a finite partition of unity show that $T$ annihilates every test form supported outside $C$. This proves independence and also shows that $L$ restricts to $T$.

On the fixed-support space with support $\operatorname {Supp}\chi $, continuity of $T$ bounds it by finitely many coefficient seminorms of some finite order. The Leibniz rule bounds those seminorms of $\chi u$ by a constant times finitely many compact coefficient seminorms of $u$. Hence $L$ is continuous on $\mathcal E^ p_\infty (M)$. Finally, two continuous extensions agree on the dense test-form subspace, so they agree everywhere. This proves the identification. $\square$

Theorem 2.10.5.423C948D346B Let $A\subset \mathcal D'_ p(M)$ satisfy

\[ \sup _{T\in A}|T(u)|<\infty \quad \text{for every }u\in \mathcal D^ p(M). \]

Then its closure for pointwise convergence on test forms is compact and consists of currents. In particular, every weakly closed, pointwise bounded family of currents is weakly compact.

Proof. The empty family needs no argument. Fix a compact set $K$, put $E_ K=\mathcal D^ p_{K,\infty }$, and consider the closed sets

\[ F_ n=\{ u\in E_ K: |T(u)|\leq n\text{ for every }T\in A\} , \qquad n\geq 1. \]

Pointwise boundedness gives $E_ K=\bigcup _ n F_ n$. The space $E_ K$ is complete by proposition 2.10.2, so the Baire argument above gives $u_0+U\subset F_ n$ for some neighborhood $U$ of zero. Shrink $U$ if necessary so that $0\in U$. For $v\in U$,

\[ |T(v)|\leq |T(u_0+v)|+|T(u_0)|\leq 2n \quad (T\in A). \]

There are $r\in \mathbb N$ and $\varepsilon >0$ such that $\{ P_{K,r}<\varepsilon \} \subset U$, using the finite-coordinate norms from proposition 2.10.2. Scaling $v$ now yields

73EE691E4241 2.10.6
\begin{equation} |T(v)|\leq C_ K P_{K,r}(v) \quad (v\in E_ K,\ T\in A),\qquad C_ K=4n/\varepsilon . \end{equation}

The zero space causes no exception. Crucially, $r$ and $C_ K$ may depend on $K$ but not on $T$.

For every test form put $M_ u=\sup _{T\in A}|T(u)|$ and let $I_ u=\{ z\in \mathbb C:|z|\leq M_ u\} $. The product $P=\prod _{u\in \mathcal D^ p(M)}I_ u$ is compact by compactness of products. Embed $A$ into $P$ by evaluation and take its closure there. Every point $a=(a_ u)$ in this closure satisfies $a_{u+v}=a_ u+a_ v$ and $a_{\lambda u}=\lambda a_ u$ for all tests and all complex $\lambda $, since these are closed equations involving finitely many coordinates. The bounds (2.10.6) also pass to the closure. Consequently $u\mapsto a_ u$ is linear and continuous on every $E_ K$; it is a current, rather than merely an algebraic functional.

The evaluation map from $\mathcal D'_ p(M)$ into the scalar product space is injective and is a homeomorphism onto its image for the weak topology, by the definition of that topology. The compact closure just obtained is therefore exactly the weak closure of $A$. If $A$ is already weakly closed, it equals that closure. This proves actual topological compactness, without replacing it by a sequence-only criterion or assuming a common order or a common support for $A$. $\square$
