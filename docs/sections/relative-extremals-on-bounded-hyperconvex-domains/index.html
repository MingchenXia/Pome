7.16 Relative extremals on bounded hyperconvex domains554C8622D718

554C8622D718

Let $n\geq 1$. For an open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ and $E\subset \Omega $, write

\[ h_{E,\Omega }=\sup \{ v\in \operatorname {PSH}(\Omega ):v\leq 0, \ v|_ E\leq -1\} , \qquad H_{E,\Omega }=h_{E,\Omega }^*. \]

This extends the existing ball notation. Taking the maximum with $-1$ does not change the supremum, so all competitors may be restricted to $[-1,0]$. We use the existing Bedford–Taylor products and their capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_\Omega $ on Borel sets, and outer capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_\Omega ^*$ on arbitrary sets. We do not extend inner capacity subadditivity to arbitrary sets. All capacities may be infinite.

Proposition 7.16.1.11F76749E371 The following properties hold.

  1. Set inclusion decreases $h$ and $H$; enlarging the ambient open set also decreases them wherever the smaller-domain function is defined. If $u\in \operatorname {PSH}(\Omega )$, $u\leq M$ on $\Omega $ and $u\leq m$ on $E$, for real numbers $m<M$, then

    \[ u\leq M+(M-m)h_{E,\Omega }. \]

  2. If compact sets $K_ j\subset \Omega $ decrease to $K$, then $h_{K_ j,\Omega }\uparrow h_{K,\Omega }$ pointwise.

  3. Suppose $\Omega $ is bounded, connected and hyperconvex: it has a continuous negative PSH exhaustion $\rho $. For $E\Subset \Omega $, $h_{E,\Omega }$ and $H_{E,\Omega }$ tend to $0$ at every boundary point. If $K\Subset \Omega $ is compact and $H_{K,\Omega }=-1$ on $K$, then $h_{K,\Omega }=H_{K,\Omega }$ is continuous. In particular this holds if $K$ is a union of closed balls of positive radius. For any compact $K$, the raw extremal $h_{K,\Omega }$ is lower semicontinuous, and the continuous extremals of $K_\delta =\{ \operatorname {dist}(z,K)\leq \delta \} $ increase pointwise to it as $\delta \downarrow 0$ through sufficiently small radii.

  4. On an arbitrary open $\Omega $, $H_{K,\Omega }$ is maximal on $\Omega \setminus K$ for every compact $K\Subset \Omega $.

Proof. The first assertions follow by inclusion of competitor families, and the two-constants inequality follows by applying the definition to $(u-M)/(M-m)$. Every competitor can be made strictly smaller than $-1$ on $K$ by subtracting a positive constant. If $v<-1$ on $K$, the open set $\{ v<-1\} $ contains all $K_ j$ for sufficiently large $j$: otherwise the decreasing compact sets $K_ j\setminus \{ v<-1\} $ would have nonempty intersection. Thus $v\leq h_{K_ j,\Omega }$ eventually. Take the supremum over these strict competitors to prove the second assertion.

For the boundary claim choose $A>0$ with $A\rho \leq -1$ on $\overline E$. Then $A\rho \leq h_{E,\Omega }\leq H_{E,\Omega }\leq 0$. The exhaustion property implies $\rho \to 0$ at the boundary: for every $t<0$ its $t$-sublevel is relatively compact. This also gives the uniform boundary estimate needed below.

If $H_ K=-1$ on $K$, the bounded PSH function $H_ K$ itself is a competitor, so $h_ K=H_ K$. Rescale $\rho $ to be at most $-1$ on $K$. Fix $\varepsilon >0$. For sufficiently small $\eta >0$, $K\subset \Omega _\eta $ and $\rho >-\varepsilon /2$ on $\Omega \setminus \Omega _\eta $, where $\Omega _\eta =\{ \operatorname {dist}(z,\partial \Omega )>\eta \} $. The radial convolutions $(H_ K)_ s$ are continuous PSH on $\Omega _\eta $ for $s<\eta $. They decrease to $-1$ on $K$; their continuous restrictions to the compact set $K$ therefore converge uniformly there by Dini. Choose $s$ with $(H_ K)_ s-\varepsilon \leq -1$ on $K$. The function

\[ v_\varepsilon = \begin{cases} \max \{ \rho ,(H_ K)_ s-\varepsilon \} ,& \Omega _\eta ,\\ \rho ,& \Omega \setminus \Omega _\eta \end{cases} \]

is continuous PSH: near the new interface the second branch is at most $-\varepsilon $ and $\rho >-\varepsilon /2$. It is a competitor and $H_ K-\varepsilon \leq v_\varepsilon \leq H_ K$, also outside $\Omega _\eta $. Thus $H_ K$ is continuous.

We record the local ball barrier used for regularity of unions of balls. If $N$ is any complex norm, $0<r<R$, and $D=\{ N(z-a)<R\} $, the extremal of the closed inner norm ball is

\[ \max \{ -1,\log (N(z-a)/R)/\log (R/r)\} . \]

Indeed $\log N$ is PSH. To verify this without an additional norm theorem, take a real supporting functional $L$ of $N$ at a nonzero point and put $\ell (z)=L(z)-iL(iz)$. Then $\ell $ is complex linear and $|\ell (z)|=\sup _{|\lambda |=1}L(\lambda z)\leq N(z)$; equality holds at the supporting point. Hence $\log N$ is the supremum of the PSH functions $\log |\ell |$ and is already upper semicontinuous, including its value $-\infty $ at $0$. The displayed candidate is therefore PSH and continuous. On each line $a+tb$, $N(b)=1$, any competitor is bounded above by it by harmonic comparison on $r<|t|<R$. This proves the formula.

If $b\in \partial K$ belongs to a closed ball $\overline{B(a,r)}\subset K$, choose $R>r$ with $\overline{B(a,R)}\subset \Omega $. Such a choice is possible because the inner closed ball is compact in $\Omega $. Monotonicity bounds $h_ K$ above by the preceding local continuous extremal, which equals $-1$ at $b$. Consequently $H_ K=-1$ on $K$, and the continuity criterion applies. Each sufficiently small $K_\delta $ is a compact union of closed balls contained in $\Omega $. Its continuous extremals increase to $h_ K$ by the second assertion, proving lower semicontinuity.

Finally, choose increasing bounded competitors $v_ j$ tending almost everywhere to $H_ K$ by the existing Choquet selection. On a ball $D\Subset \Omega \setminus K$ replace $v_ j$ by its homogeneous PSH balayage. The replacements still equal $v_ j$ on $K$ and are nonpositive by the maximum principle. They remain competitors, increase, and lie between $v_ j$ and $H_ K$, so they tend almost everywhere to $H_ K$. Increasing BT continuity shows that its Monge–Ampere measure vanishes on $D$. The existing bounded maximality theorem proves the assertion. $\square$

Proposition 7.16.2.0667BDD8ADC8 Let $\Omega $ be bounded and hyperconvex, let $K\Subset \Omega $ be compact, and let open subsets $\Omega _ j$ increase to $\Omega $, with $K\subset \Omega _1$. Then $h_{K,\Omega _ j}\downarrow h_{K,\Omega }$ pointwise.

Proof. Choose a continuous negative PSH exhaustion $\psi $ with $\psi <-1$ on $K$. For $0<\varepsilon <1$, the closure of $\{ \psi <-\varepsilon \} $ lies in $\Omega _ j$ for all large $j$. If $v$ is a competitor on such $\Omega _ j$, glue $\max \{ v-\varepsilon ,\psi \} $ on this sublevel to $\psi $ elsewhere. The boundary inequality follows from $v\leq 0$. The result is a competitor on $\Omega $. At a point in the sublevel it is at least $v-\varepsilon $; outside, $\psi \geq -\varepsilon \geq v-\varepsilon $ wherever $v$ is defined. Consequently $h_{K,\Omega _ j}-\varepsilon \leq h_{K,\Omega }$ on $\Omega _ j$. Monotonicity gives the opposite inequality without $\varepsilon $. Let $j\to \infty $, then $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. $\square$

Proposition 7.16.3.86AFECC63967 Let $\Omega $ be a bounded connected open subset of $\mathbb C^ n$. For $E\subset \Omega $ the following conditions are equivalent: $H_{E,\Omega }=0$; $E$ is pluripolar; $\operatorname {Cap}_\Omega ^*(E)=0$; and $E$ is contained in the pole set of one negative PSH function on $\Omega $. Countable unions of sets with zero extremal again have zero extremal. If $E_ j$ increase to $E$, then $H_{E_ j,\Omega }\downarrow H_{E,\Omega }$ pointwise.

Proof. If $H_ E=0$, the raw supremum is zero almost everywhere by the existing envelope theorem. Choose $a$ where $h_ E(a)=0$, and competitors $v_ j\in [-1,0]$ with $v_ j(a)>-2^{-j}$. Their sum has decreasing partial sums and is finite at $a$; subtracting $1$ from its limit gives a proper strictly negative PSH function which is $-\infty $ on $E$. Conversely, if such a function $v$ exists, $tv$ is a competitor for every $t>0$. Thus $h_ E=0$ almost everywhere and $H_ E=0$. A negative global pole function proves local pluripolarity. For the reverse implication use the existing Josefson theorem on $\mathbb C^ n$, after the existing locality theorem extends pluripolarity from $\Omega $ to $\mathbb C^ n$. The resulting global PSH function is bounded above on $\overline\Omega $; subtract a larger constant to make it negative on $\Omega $. Its restriction is not identically $-\infty $ because it is locally integrable.

The existing local pole-capacity theorem and a compact exhaustion give outer capacity zero in $\Omega $ for every pluripolar set. Conversely, if that capacity is zero, fix a ball $B\Subset \Omega $. On compact subsets of $B$, the existing local comparability of capacities bounds $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*$ by a constant times $\operatorname {Cap}_\Omega ^*$. Exhaust $B$ by such compact sets to see that $E\cap B$ has zero outer capacity in $B$. The existing ball zero-capacity theorem makes it pluripolar there. This proves all four equivalences. Countable unions follow from the existing countable-union theorem for pluripolar sets.

For increasing $E_ j$, the bounded PSH functions $H_{E_ j}$ decrease to a bounded PSH function $H\geq H_ E$. Each set $E_ j\cap \{ H_{E_ j}>-1\} $ is pluripolar by the existing envelope discrepancy theorem. Outside their countable union $F$, the limit equals $-1$ on $E$. Choose a negative PSH function $v$ with poles on $F$ by the equivalences just proved. Then $H+tv$ is a competitor for $E$ for every $t>0$. Let $t\downarrow 0$ at the almost-everywhere finite points of $v$ and use uniqueness of canonical PSH representatives to get $H\leq H_ E$ everywhere. The same assertions about increasing sets hold componentwise on a disconnected bounded open set. $\square$

Proposition 7.16.4.CF1B00103D5B Let $\Omega '\subset \mathbb C^ m$ be open and $E\subset \Omega '$. If $f:\Omega \to \Omega '$ is holomorphic, then $h_{E,\Omega '}\circ f\leq h_{f^{-1}(E),\Omega }$. If it has full target rank generically on every component of $\Omega $, then also $H_{E,\Omega '}\circ f\leq H_{f^{-1}(E),\Omega }$. For a proper surjective holomorphic map between open subsets of the same $\mathbb C^ n$, both inequalities are equalities.

Proof. Pull back bounded competitors to prove the raw inequality. On the full-rank locus $f$ is locally open by the holomorphic submersion normal form, so taking upper regularizations commutes there with composition. The two regularizations are bounded PSH functions and the exceptional analytic set is null. Their equality there extends everywhere by uniqueness of canonical representatives, proving the regularized inequality.

A proper map as in the last assertion has finite fibers: they are compact analytic subsets of a Euclidean domain. It is open by the existing finite-map theorem. For a bounded PSH competitor $v$ upstairs the fiber maximum $w(y)=\max _{f(x)=y}v(x)$ is PSH. To recall the checked argument, off the proper analytic critical image it is the maximum of finitely many inverse-branch pullbacks. For continuous $v$ it is continuous by properness and openness, so the existing bounded analytic-removal theorem extends this PSH function. For general $v$, on a relatively compact target neighborhood convolve $v$ decreasingly on a neighborhood of its compact inverse image. The continuous fiber maxima decrease to $w$, since each fiber is finite; hence $w$ is PSH. These local arguments agree on overlaps. It is a downstairs competitor and $v\leq w\circ f$, proving the reverse raw inequality. Openness also makes upper regularization commute with composition at every point, giving the regularized equality. $\square$

Theorem 7.16.5.08DA18E923C1 Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ be bounded, connected and hyperconvex. For every $E\Subset \Omega $,

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_\Omega ^*(E) =\int _\Omega (dd^ cH_{E,\Omega })^ n<\infty . \]

For compact $K\Subset \Omega $ the measure is carried by $K\cap \{ H_ K=-1\} $ and $\operatorname {Cap}_\Omega (K)=\operatorname {Cap}_\Omega ^*(K)$. In particular $H_ K>-1$ everywhere on $K$ implies capacity zero. Outer capacity is continuous on decreasing compact sets and on increasing sequences of arbitrary subsets of $\Omega $, with extended values allowed.

Proof. For compact $K$, Proposition 7.16.1 proves that the measure of $H_ K$ is carried by $K$. Its subset $K\cap \{ H_ K>-1\} $ lies in the exceptional set $\{ h_ K<H_ K\} $, whose outer capacity is zero by the existing envelope theorem, valid on every bounded open set. The competitor $H_ K+1$ shows that this subset also has zero measure for $(dd^ cH_ K)^ n$. Thus the support assertion holds.

The existing ball proof of the compact capacity formula now applies with the barrier $A\rho $ in place of the quadratic ball barrier. Here are its two inequalities. Since $H_ K+1$ is a capacity competitor, $\int (dd^ cH_ K)^ n\leq \operatorname {Cap}_\Omega (K)$. For a capacity competitor $0\leq v\leq 1$, let $w=(1-2\varepsilon )(v-1)-\varepsilon $, $0<\varepsilon <1/2$. Then $-1+\varepsilon \leq w\leq -\varepsilon $, the set $K\cap \{ H_ K\geq w\} $ is null for $(dd^ cw)^ n$, and the boundary comparison between $H_ K$ and $w$ gives

\[ (1-2\varepsilon )^ n\int _ K(dd^ cv)^ n \leq \int _{\{ H_ K<w\} }(dd^ cH_ K)^ n \leq \int _\Omega (dd^ cH_ K)^ n. \]

Let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ and take the supremum over $v$.

For $K_ j\downarrow K$, raw extremal convergence implies $H_{K_ j}\uparrow H_ K$ almost everywhere. Increasing BT continuity and the fixed compact support $K_1$ give convergence of their total masses, and hence of their capacities. Apply this to $K_\delta \downarrow K$: $\operatorname {Cap}^*(K)\leq \operatorname {Cap}(\operatorname {Int}K_\delta ) \leq \operatorname {Cap}(K_\delta )$ proves equality of inner and outer capacity on $K$. All these numbers are finite by local CLN.

For an open $G\Subset \Omega $, exhaust $G$ by compact sets $K_ j$ with $K_ j\subset \operatorname {Int}K_{j+1}$. Then $H_{K_ j}\downarrow h_ G=H_ G$: the limit is PSH, equals $-1$ on $G$, and the two competitor inequalities identify it. Decreasing BT continuity, common compact support $\overline G$ and the existing Borel capacity continuity from below give

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_\Omega (G)=\int _\Omega (dd^ cH_ G)^ n. \]

For arbitrary $E\Subset \Omega $, choose a relatively compact open neighborhood $U$ of $\overline E$. Monotonicity gives $H_ U\leq H_ E$. If $E\ne \varnothing $ we may assume $U$ nonempty; then $H_ U<0$ on the connected domain by the maximum principle, since it is $-1$ on $U$. Thus $(1+\varepsilon )H_ U<H_ E$ everywhere. Both extremals tend to zero on the boundary, so comparison gives

\[ \int _\Omega (dd^ cH_ E)^ n \leq (1+\varepsilon )^ n\int _\Omega (dd^ cH_ U)^ n =(1+\varepsilon )^ n\operatorname {Cap}_\Omega (U). \]

The same argument works for any relatively compact open neighborhood of $E$. Let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ and take the infimum. Such neighborhoods suffice in the outer-capacity definition, by intersecting with one fixed relatively compact neighborhood of $\overline E$.

For the reverse inequality, choose bounded competitors $v_ j\uparrow H_ E$ almost everywhere, and replace them by $\max \{ v_ j,A\rho \} $, where $A\rho \leq -1$ on $\overline E$. They still have the same limit and boundary value zero. Put $G_ j=\{ (1+1/j)v_ j<-1\} $. These open sets decrease, contain $E$, and have closures in the fixed compact sublevel $\{ A\rho \leq -1/2\} \Subset \Omega $, since $v_ j\geq v_1\geq A\rho $. Their extremals increase, and

\[ (1+1/j)v_ j\leq H_{G_ j}\leq H_ E \]

identifies their limit almost everywhere with $H_ E$. All their measures are supported in one compact subset of $\Omega $; increasing BT continuity therefore gives

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_\Omega ^*(E)\leq \lim _ j\operatorname {Cap}_\Omega (G_ j) =\lim _ j\int _\Omega (dd^ cH_{G_ j})^ n =\int _\Omega (dd^ cH_ E)^ n. \]

For $E=\varnothing $, all functions and capacities are zero, so the formula also holds.

We finish with increasing arbitrary sets. First suppose each $E_ j$ is relatively compact, without assuming a common compact neighborhood, and let $E=\bigcup _ jE_ j$. The exceptional sets $E_ j\cap \{ H_{E_ j}>-1\} $ are pluripolar by the existing envelope discrepancy theorem. Their union $F$ has outer capacity zero by the existing pluripolar capacity theorem on bounded open sets. Choose open $G\supset F$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_\Omega (G)<\varepsilon $. For $0<\delta <1$ put $U_ j=\{ H_{E_ j}<-1+\delta \} $ and $V_ j=U_ j\cup G$. The $U_ j$ are relatively compact by the boundary barrier, increase with $j$, and $E_ j\subset V_ j$. If $H_{E_ j}=0$, then $U_ j$ is empty and the following inequality is immediate. Otherwise $H_{E_ j}<0$ everywhere, so total-mass comparison applies with a factor tending to one. Since $H_{E_ j}/(1-\delta )\leq H_{U_ j}$, the same total-mass comparison as above and the extremal formula give

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_\Omega (U_ j) \leq (1-\delta )^{-n}\operatorname {Cap}_\Omega ^*(E_ j). \]

The $V_ j$ are open, so Borel subadditivity and continuity from below yield

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_\Omega ^*(E) \leq \operatorname {Cap}_\Omega \Bigl (\bigcup _ jV_ j\Bigr ) \leq \varepsilon +(1-\delta )^{-n} \lim _ j\operatorname {Cap}_\Omega ^*(E_ j). \]

Let $\varepsilon ,\delta \downarrow 0$; the opposite inequality is monotonicity. For general increasing $E_ j$, take a compact exhaustion $L_ j$ with increasing interiors and apply the proved case to $E_ j\cap L_ j$. These are relatively compact, increase to $E$, and their capacities are bounded by those of $E_ j$. This proves the asserted continuity, including infinite limits. $\square$

Already in the unit disc this capacity can be infinite on the whole domain. The logarithmic profile above and its radial derivative give, in Pome's normalization,

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_{\mathbb D}(\overline{\mathbb D(0,r)}) =\frac{1}{2\log (1/r)}\longrightarrow \infty \qquad (r\uparrow 1). \]

Indeed its distributional Laplacian has total mass $2\pi /\log (1/r)$, and in one complex dimension $4\pi \, dd^ c u=\Delta u$ as measures. This is why the capacity and its continuity statements use values in $[0,\infty ]$.
