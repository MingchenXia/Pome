7.17 Isolated-pole Green envelopes and continuity9664AF03450B

9664AF03450B

For a domain $D\subset \mathbb C^ n$ and $a\in D$, let $g_ D(z,a)$ be the supremum of negative PSH functions $u$ satisfying $u(z)\leq \log |z-a|+C$ near $a$. Put $g_ D=-\infty $ when the family is empty. The pole coefficient is one relative to $\log |z-a|$. Monge–Ampere products below are used only away from the pole.

Proposition 7.17.1.3360BE3E2E50 If $D$ is bounded and $B(a,r)\subset D\subset B(a,R)$, then $g_ D$ is negative PSH, finite and locally bounded off $a$, and

\[ \log (|z-a|/R)\leq g_ D(z,a)\leq \log (|z-a|/r). \]

It is maximal off $a$, so $(dd^ cg_ D)^ n=0$ there. Enlarging the domain decreases the envelope; deleting a relatively closed pluripolar set not containing $a$ does not change it. Holomorphic maps satisfy

\[ g_{D'}(f(z),f(a))\leq g_ D(z,a). \]

On an arbitrary domain the envelope is either identically $-\infty $ or negative PSH. Contraction and domain comparisons retain the empty-family convention.

Proof. The lower logarithm is a competitor on a bounded domain. To bound every competitor above on $B(a,r)$, restrict to any complex line through $a$. If the restriction is identically $-\infty $, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise subtract the line's logarithmic pole, remove the bounded-above puncture and apply the SH maximum principle on circles of radii tending to $r$. This gives the upper bound; outside that ball it follows from negativity. The upper envelope is locally bounded above by zero, and its regularization retains the same pole bound. It is PSH and negative by the maximum principle, hence itself a competitor. Therefore it equals the raw supremum. The two logarithmic bounds imply local boundedness off $a$. On an arbitrary domain with a nonempty family, a single competitor gives a nontrivial lower bound; the same local upper pole bound and envelope argument give a negative PSH representative. No local boundedness away from the pole is asserted in that generality.

For $U\Subset D\setminus \{ a\} $ and a PSH test with boundary upper limits at most $g_ D$, the maximum principle first makes the test negative on $U$. Glue its maximum with $g_ D$ inside $U$ to $g_ D$ outside. The result is still a competitor, so it cannot exceed the envelope. This proves maximality; use the canonical bounded maximality theorem only off the pole. Domain monotonicity is competitor inclusion. On a domain with a relatively closed pluripolar subset removed, every negative competitor extends canonically across that subset by the removable-set theorem. Its logarithmic condition at the retained pole is unchanged; the two competitor comparisons give equality.

For a target competitor $u$, its composition with $f$ is either PSH or identically $-\infty $. The latter case contributes only a trivial inequality. In the former case the local holomorphic Lipschitz estimate $|f(z)-f(a)|\leq C|z-a|$ gives the required upper logarithmic pole. Thus $u\circ f$ is a source competitor. Taking the supremum proves contraction, including an empty target family. Applying contraction to a biholomorphism and its inverse gives invariance. $\square$

Proposition 7.17.2.7B9EA3C07531 Suppose that $h:\mathbb C^ n\to [0,\infty )$ is finite USC, $h^{-1}(0)=\{ 0\} $, and $h(\lambda z)=|\lambda |h(z)$. On the balanced domain $D=\{ h<1\} $ one has $g_ D(z,0)\leq \log h(z)$. Equality holds if $\log h$ is PSH. In particular,

\[ g_{B(0,R)}(z,w)=\log |A_{w/R}(z/R)|, \quad g_{P(0,R)}(z,w)=\max _ j\log \left| \frac{R(z_ j-w_ j)}{R^2-z_ j\overline w_ j}\right|. \]

Here $A$ is the canonical smooth centered ball automorphism, with $A_0$ the identity, and $P$ is the open polydisc. Its negative is the source's nonzero-parameter involution and gives the same norm.

Proof. Upper semicontinuity on the unit sphere and homogeneity give $h(z)\leq C|z|$. In particular $D$ is open, contains a ball and is connected by its radial segments. For any competitor $u$ and $z\ne 0$ in $D$, restrict to the line $\zeta z$, whose parameter disc has radius $1/h(z)>1$. Subtract $\log |\zeta |+\log h(z)$ and remove the puncture. Circle maximum principles as the radius tends to $1/h(z)$ give $u(z)\leq \log h(z)$. This works for each competitor and does not assume beforehand that the Green envelope is PSH on this possibly unbounded domain. If $\log h$ is PSH, it is itself a negative competitor by the bound $h\leq C|z|$, proving equality. The ball/polydisc formulas follow by applying this to the norm/max norm at zero and then using the checked ball and coordinate-disc biholomorphisms.

For $0<\epsilon <1$, the explicit nonconvex example has $h(z_1,z_2)=\max \{ |z_1|,|z_2|,\sqrt{|z_1z_2|/\epsilon }\} $. Its logarithm is the maximum of the two coordinate logarithms and their half-sum minus $\tfrac 12\log \epsilon $, hence PSH. Its sublevel is pseudoconvex, since $-\log (1-h)+|z|^2$ is a PSH exhaustion there. It is nonconvex: for a real $t$ just below one, the points $(t,t\epsilon )$ and $(t\epsilon ,t)$ belong to the domain while their midpoint has coordinate product $t^2(1+\epsilon )^2/4>\epsilon $. $\square$

Proposition 7.17.3.2CC830293978 Let $f:D\to D'$ be a proper holomorphic map of bounded domains in $\mathbb C^ n$, and let $\alpha ,\beta >0$. The following are equivalent:

\[ f^{-1}(f(a))=\{ a\} ,\qquad c|z-a|^\alpha \leq |f(z)-f(a)|\leq C|z-a|^\beta \quad \hbox{near }a \]

for some $c,C>0$, and

\[ \alpha g_ D(z,a)\leq g_{D'}(f(z),f(a))\leq \beta g_ D(z,a). \]

Proof. Proper Euclidean fibers are finite, and the map is open and surjective, by the canonical finite-map results. Its proper fiber maximum $v(y)=\alpha \max _{f(x)=y}g_ D(x,a)$ is PSH. Near $f(a)$ all inverse points approach $a$, by properness and the single-fiber assumption. The lower growth estimate and the local Green upper bound give $v(y)\leq \log |y-f(a)|+C'$. It is negative, hence a target competitor. Thus $\alpha g_ D\leq v\circ f\leq g_{D'}\circ f$, with the factor $\alpha $ retained. The upper growth estimate makes $\beta ^{-1}g_{D'}\circ f$ a source competitor, proving the other bound. Conversely the lower Green inequality excludes any other inverse point of $f(a)$, since $g_ D$ is finite off $a$. At $a$ both Green functions equal the corresponding logarithm up to bounded terms. Substituting those local bounds into the two inequalities gives exactly the two growth estimates. $\square$

Proposition 7.17.4.5A3A24549BAA For bounded hyperconvex $D$, $g_ D$ tends to zero at every boundary point, is continuous off its pole, and is jointly continuous on $\overline D\times D$ with values in $[-\infty ,0]$, extended by zero on $\partial D\times D$.

Proof. Fix $B(a,r)\Subset D\subset B(a,R)$ and $0<\delta <r$. The relative extremal $h_\delta =h_{\overline B(a,\delta ),D}$ is continuous, equals $-1$ on that ball, and tends to zero at the boundary, by the checked hyperconvex compact-obstacle theorem. Put $A=\log (r/\delta )$ and $B=\log (R/\delta )$. The Green upper bound gives $g_ D/A\leq -1$ on the obstacle, so $g_ D\leq A h_\delta $. Also $\ell =\log (|z-a|/R)$ satisfies $\ell /B\leq h_\delta $ everywhere. Glue $B h_\delta $ outside the closed $\delta $-ball to $\ell $ inside it. Their values agree on the sphere; the outside branch is at least $\ell $, so the canonical maximum pasting lemma, with base $\ell $ and outside open set, makes the glued function PSH. It is a negative logarithmic-pole competitor. Consequently

\[ B h_\delta \leq g_ D\leq A h_\delta \quad \hbox{outside }\overline B(a,\delta ). \]

For fixed $\delta $, the left bound proves the zero boundary limit. On a compact set away from $a$, $g_ D\geq -C$ by its lower logarithmic bound, so $|h_\delta |\leq C/A$. Hence

\[ 0\leq A h_\delta -g_ D\leq \log (R/r)\, C/A\longrightarrow 0. \]

Thus $g_ D$ is a locally uniform limit of continuous relative extrema off $a$. This supplies continuity without the source's defective five-piece lower-semicontinuity patch.

We verify uniformity in the pole. Given $a$, a neighborhood $U$ of it, and $\eta >0$, choose a small closed ball about $a$ in $U$, a uniform inner radius $r_0$ for its poles, and $R_0$ larger than all distances to points of $D$. Choose $t>0$ small enough that $B(a,t)\Subset U$ and

\[ (1+\eta )\log (3t/(2r_0))<\log (t/(2R_0)). \]

For $x,y\in B(a,t/2)$, keep $\log (|z-y|/R_0)$ inside $B(a,t)$ and outside take its maximum with $(1+\eta )g_ D(z,x)$. On the interface, the uniform ball upper bound gives a strict gap putting the logarithmic branch above the other branch. The resulting negative PSH function has a logarithmic pole at $y$, so it is at most $g_ D(z,y)$. On $D\setminus U$ it is at least $(1+\eta )g_ D(z,x)$. Interchanging $x,y$ gives

\[ (1+\eta )^{-1}\leq \frac{g_ D(z,y)}{g_ D(z,x)}\leq 1+\eta \qquad (z\in D\setminus U). \]

Fixed-pole continuity and this ratio estimate prove joint continuity off the diagonal and at the outer boundary. Near an interior diagonal point, a uniform inner-ball upper bound $g_ D(z,a)\leq \log (|z-a|/r_0)$ tends to $-\infty $. This is exactly continuity into the extended-real interval. $\square$

Proposition 7.17.5.518349DA23E4 If domains $D_ j$ increase to $D$, then $g_{D_ j}(z,a)\downarrow g_ D(z,a)$ where defined. On any pseudoconvex domain, $g_ D$ is jointly upper semicontinuous, with the same empty-family convention.

Proof. The decreasing limit is either identically $-\infty $ or PSH, by the canonical decreasing-limit theorem on the exhaustion. In the former case domain monotonicity already gives equality with $g_ D$. In the latter case it is nonpositive and retains a uniform logarithmic upper pole bound from any fixed inner ball. It is strictly negative by the maximum principle, so it is a competitor and equality follows in the other direction too. An empty intermediate family gives the same immediate equality. For a pseudoconvex domain use the checked PSH exhaustion and its increasing bounded hyperconvex sublevel components. They cover the connected domain: every compact path to a fixed base point eventually lies in a sublevel. Their jointly continuous Green functions decrease to $g_ D$ on each local product neighborhood, proving upper semicontinuity. $\square$

Proposition 7.17.6.B016253F39B4 For a bounded planar domain, $g_ D$ is minus its canonical Helms-normalized positive Green function, equivalently $-2\pi $ times the Foundations- normalized positive kernel. In particular it is symmetric in its two arguments, and for a conformal map $f:D\to \mathbb D$ one has $g_ D(z,a)=\log |\varphi _{f(a)}(f(z))|$.

Proof. On a bounded regular planar domain, the positive Helms kernel $G$ has zero boundary limits and singularity $-\log |z-a|$ plus a harmonic function. Thus $-G$ is a competitor. For any other competitor $u$, $u+G$ is subharmonic off $a$ and bounded above near $a$, so its puncture is removable. Its boundary upper limits are nonpositive, hence $u\leq -G$ by the maximum principle. For a general bounded domain, use the checked increasing exhaustion by bounded regular open sets, choosing the pole's component. Canonical positive Green exhaustion and the preceding negative envelope exhaustion pass this equality to the limit. The normalization factor is the existing identity $G_{\rm Helms}=2\pi G_{\rm Foundations}$ in real dimension two. Symmetry follows from canonical Green symmetry; the conformal formula is the already proved disc formula and invariance. No Riemann mapping existence theorem is a premise of that conditional formula. $\square$

Proposition 7.17.7.465EA6ED94FB On a bounded hyperconvex domain, $g_ D(\cdot ,a)$ is the unique negative PSH function $u$ that is locally bounded off $a$, is maximal there, tends to zero at the boundary, and satisfies $u=\log |z-a|+O(1)$ at $a$.

Proof. Fix $\epsilon ,\delta >0$. The function $(1+\epsilon )u-\delta $ lies below $g_ D$ near $a$, since their difference is $\epsilon \log |z-a|+O(1)$. It also lies strictly below near the outer boundary, where both unshifted functions tend to zero. On the intervening compact annulus use maximality of $g_ D$ to obtain $(1+\epsilon )u-\delta \leq g_ D$ throughout. Let $\delta $ and then $\epsilon $ decrease to zero. Interchange the two functions and use maximality of $u$ to get the reverse inequality. Neither an isolated-pole Monge–Ampere product nor an excluded-page proof is an input. $\square$

Example 7.17.8.34EFBF9EA39F Strict real convexity, even a positive real boundary Hessian with $C^2$ boundary, does not imply a smooth isolated-pole Green function. Take $p=5/2$, $h(z)=(|z|^2+\| z\| _ p^2)^{1/2}$ on $\mathbb C^2$, and $D=\{ h<1\} $. It is a complex norm, so $\log h$ is PSH by the canonical norm support-functional proof and $g_ D(z,0)=\log h(z)$. The continuous negative PSH exhaustion $h-1$ makes $D$ hyperconvex. The function $h^2$ is $C^2$ off zero and its real Hessian is at least twice the identity, since $\| z\| _ p^2$ is convex. Its gradient is nonzero on the unit level by Euler homogeneity. Thus $D$ has strongly convex $C^2$ boundary. At $(t,1/2)$ the logarithm is a real analytic function of $t^2$ and $|t|^{5/2}$ with a strictly positive coefficient of the latter; its third derivative is unbounded as $t\to 0$. Hence it is not $C^3$ at the interior point $(0,1/2)$. The uniqueness just proved excludes a different smooth maximal zero-boundary solution with the same pole. The source's unqualified smoothness survey statement therefore needs the boundary regularity hypotheses of its cited Lempert theorem.
