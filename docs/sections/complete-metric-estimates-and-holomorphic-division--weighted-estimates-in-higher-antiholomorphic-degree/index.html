6.25 Weighted estimates in higher antiholomorphic degree013675E8D6BD

013675E8D6BD

On an open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ we write $u=\sum _{|J|=r}u_ J\, \mathrm d\bar z_ J$, with increasing multi-indices, and put $|u|^2=\sum _ J|u_ J|^2$ and $\| u\| _\Phi ^2=\int _\Omega |u|^2\mathrm e^{-\Phi }\, \mathrm dV$, where $\mathrm dV$ is Lebesgue measure. The coefficient $u_{jK}$ is alternating in $jK$, and is zero when $j\in K$. Let $e_ j$ be exterior multiplication by $\mathrm d\bar z_ j$ and $i_ j=e_ j^*$ its contraction. On disconnected open sets, plurisubharmonic weights are understood componentwise, with the usual nontrivial, locally integrable convention. For a Hermitian matrix $A=(a_{j\bar k})$ define

\[ C_ r(A)=\sum _{j,k}a_{j\bar k}e_ ki_ j \quad \hbox{on }\Lambda ^{0,r},\qquad C_0(A)=0. \]

For $1\leq r\leq n$, proposition 10.33.6 gives

429FD29B9B0E 6.25.1
\begin{equation} \langle C_ r(A)u,u\rangle =\sum _{|K|=r-1}\sum _{j,k}a_{j\bar k}u_{jK}\overline{u_{kK}}, \qquad \sum _{K,j}|u_{jK}|^2=r|u|^2. \end{equation}

In a unitary coframe diagonalizing $A$, the eigenvalue on $\mathrm d\bar z_ J$ is $\sum _{j\in J}\lambda _ j$. Thus, if $0\leq \lambda _1\leq \cdots \leq \lambda _ n$ are the eigenvalues of $A$,

3536F4E27C58 6.25.2
\begin{equation} C_ r(A)\geq (\lambda _1+\cdots +\lambda _ r)\operatorname {Id} \geq r\lambda _1\operatorname {Id}\qquad (r\geq 1). \end{equation}

In particular $C_ r$ preserves the order of Hermitian matrices.

For a positive semidefinite Hermitian operator $B$ on this finite coefficient space use the dual energy

\[ |g|_{B^{-1}}^2 =\sup _{v\ne 0}\frac{|\langle g,v\rangle |^2}{\langle Bv,v\rangle }, \]

where $0/0=0$ and a positive numerator divided by zero is $+\infty $. The finite-dimensional dual-energy argument preceding Hörmander's smooth-weight $L^2$ estimate applies in this space without change: this function is measurable for measurable $g$ and continuous $B$, it decreases when $B$ increases, and $|g|^2\leq (1+\| B\| )|g|_{B^{-1}}^2$. For $\Phi \in C^\infty (\Omega ,\mathbb R)$ set

\[ \delta _\Phi =\sum _ j i_ j(-\partial _{z_ j}+\partial _{z_ j}\Phi ), \qquad B_ r(\Phi )=C_ r(H_\Phi ),\quad H_\Phi =(\partial _{z_ j}\partial _{\bar z_ k}\Phi ). \]

Here $\delta _\Phi $ is the formal adjoint of $\bar\partial $ for the single weight $\Phi $; it is zero on functions.

Lemma 6.25.3.FBCE435FE2B2 For $0\leq r\leq n$ and every smooth compactly supported $(0,r)$-form,

F84EB59431A3 6.25.4
\begin{equation} \| \delta _\Phi v\| _\Phi ^2+\| \bar\partial v\| _\Phi ^2 =\sum _{J,k}\| \partial _{\bar z_ k}v_ J\| _\Phi ^2 +\int _\Omega \langle B_ r(\Phi )v,v\rangle \mathrm e^{-\Phi }\, \mathrm dV. \end{equation}

The operators to a nonexistent degree are zero.

Proof. Apply theorem 10.33.2 to the trivial line bundle with squared norm $\mathrm e^{-\Phi }$, the flat form $\omega =\mathrm i\sum _ j\mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j$, and $\mathrm dz_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm dz_ n\wedge v$. The coordinate covectors are unit covectors for this form, and its volume is $2^ n\mathrm dV$; cancel this common constant. Holomorphic top degree makes $D'$ of this form zero. In these flat coordinates the weighted adjoint of $D'=\partial -\partial \Phi \wedge $ is $-\sum _ j\iota _ j\partial _{\bar z_ j}$, so its squared norm is $\sum _{J,k}\| \partial _{\bar z_ k}v_ J\| _\Phi ^2$. The curvature term is (6.25.1); the common sign from the fixed holomorphic top form cancels in both norms. For $r=0$ the curvature and codifferential terms vanish, consistently with the same identity. $\square$

We use maximal distributional domains for $\bar\partial $. Membership in the domain of its Hilbert adjoint also contains a boundary condition; a formula for its distributional expression alone does not characterize that domain.

Lemma 6.25.5.AD693145F35D Fix $1\leq q\leq n$, and let $\Phi ,\psi $ be smooth real functions on $\Omega $. Put $\Phi _ k=\Phi +(k-3)\psi $, $k=1,2,3$, and let

\[ T=\bar\partial :L^2_{0,q-1}(\mathrm e^{-\Phi _1}) \longrightarrow L^2_{0,q}(\mathrm e^{-\Phi _2}), \qquad S=\bar\partial :L^2_{0,q}(\mathrm e^{-\Phi _2}) \longrightarrow L^2_{0,q+1}(\mathrm e^{-\Phi _3}) \]

have their maximal domains. For $q=n$ the last space is zero and $S=0$. Suppose there are $\chi _\nu \in C_ c^\infty (\Omega )$ with $0\leq \chi _\nu \leq 1$, eventually equal to one near each compact set, and $|\bar\partial \chi _\nu |^2\leq \mathrm e^\psi $. Then $T,S$ are closed and densely defined, $ST=0$, and smooth compactly supported $(0,q)$-forms are dense in $\operatorname {Dom}T^*\cap \operatorname {Dom}S$ for its joint graph norm. Distributionally,

3800200095B9 6.25.6
\begin{equation} T^*v=\mathrm e^{\Phi _1-\Phi _2}\delta _{\Phi _2}v \qquad (v\in \operatorname {Dom}T^*). \end{equation}

Proof. The proof of three-weight graph-norm density works in every degree; we check its operator and norm assertions. Local $L^2$ convergence is implied by convergence in each weighted space, so distributional continuity proves closedness. Coefficient cutoffs and mollification make $C_ c^\infty $ dense in the three spaces. These forms belong to the maximal domains, and $\bar\partial ^2=0$ gives $ST=0$. Testing the adjoint definition against compactly supported forms gives (6.25.6); for a smooth compactly supported $v$ the adjoint identity holds against every element of $\operatorname {Dom}T$, so such $v$ belongs to $\operatorname {Dom}T^*$.

For a real cutoff $\chi $ the wedge operator $\bar\partial \chi \wedge $ and its unweighted contraction adjoint $i_{\partial \chi }=\sum _ j(\partial _{z_ j}\chi )i_ j$ have pointwise operator norm at most $|\bar\partial \chi |$ in every degree. This follows by choosing a unitary coframe in which the covector has only one nonzero coefficient. The defining Hilbert adjoint identity, applied to $\chi u$ with $u\in \operatorname {Dom}T$, therefore gives

\[ T^*(\chi v)=\chi T^*v- \mathrm e^{\Phi _1-\Phi _2}i_{\partial \chi }v, \qquad S(\chi v)=\chi Sv+\bar\partial \chi \wedge v. \]

In particular multiplication by $\chi _\nu $ preserves both domains. Since $\Phi _1-2\Phi _2=-\Phi _3$ and $\Phi _3-\Phi _2=\psi $, the squared norms of both error terms are at most

\[ \int _\Omega |v|^2|\bar\partial \chi _\nu |^2 \mathrm e^{-\Phi _3}\, \mathrm dV \leq \| v\| _{\Phi _2}^2. \]

Their integrands tend to zero pointwise and are dominated by $|v|^2\mathrm e^{-\Phi _2}$. Dominated convergence, also for $\chi _\nu v$, $\chi _\nu T^*v$ and $\chi _\nu Sv$, reduces the assertion to $v$ of compact support.

For this $v$, (6.25.6) implies $\delta _0v\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$, since $\delta _{\Phi _2}-\delta _0=\sum _ j(\partial _{z_ j}\Phi _2)i_ j$ is of order zero; also $\bar\partial v\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$. Both $\delta _0$ and $\bar\partial $ have constant coefficients and commute with coefficient convolution. Thus mollifications $v_\eta $ converge to $v$, with both these derivatives, in $L^2$ on a fixed compact neighborhood of the support. The remaining coefficients in (6.25.6) are smooth and bounded there, and the weights are bounded above and below there. Hence the convergence is in the required joint graph norm. No boundary integration or identification of a maximal adjoint from its formal expression was used. $\square$

By (6.25.4) and (6.25.6), every smooth compactly supported $(0,q)$-form satisfies, for $t>0$,

9E99D4881F2D 6.25.7
\begin{equation} \int \langle B_ q(\Phi )v,v\rangle \mathrm e^{-\Phi }\, \mathrm dV \leq (1+t^{-1})\| T^*v\| _{\Phi _1}^2+\| Sv\| _{\Phi _3}^2 +(1+t)\int |\bar\partial \psi |^2|v|^2 \mathrm e^{-\Phi }\, \mathrm dV. \end{equation}

Indeed $\delta _\Phi v=\mathrm e^\psi T^*v+i_{\partial \psi }v$; apply $|a+b|^2\leq (1+t^{-1})|a|^2+(1+t)|b|^2$ and the contraction bound just proved. This is the degree-independent error estimate.

Theorem 6.25.8.B766118DB089 Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ be pseudoconvex, let $2\leq q\leq n$, and let $\varphi \in C^\infty (\Omega )\cap \operatorname {PSH}(\Omega )$. If a measurable $(0,q)$-form $g$ is distributionally $\bar\partial $-closed and

\[ M=\int _\Omega |g|_{B_ q(\varphi )^{-1}}^2 \mathrm e^{-\varphi }\, \mathrm dV<\infty , \]

there is a $(0,q-1)$-form $u$ with $\bar\partial u=g$ and $\| u\| _\varphi ^2\leq M$. No global $L^2(\mathrm e^{-\varphi })$ assumption on $g$ is required. The degree-one statement is Hörmander's smooth-weight $L^2$ estimate.

Proof. If $\Omega $ is empty the assertion is trivial. Otherwise the dual-energy lower bound and boundedness above of $\varphi $ on compact sets imply $g\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$. Let $s\geq 0$ be the smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion supplied by Oka's boundary-distance criterion; this includes arbitrary unbounded or disconnected open sets and does not require boundary regularity.

We use the auxiliary functions constructed in the proof of Hörmander's smooth-weight $L^2$ estimate, the auxiliary convex weight inequalities. More precisely, for each integer $t\geq 1$ that construction gives smooth $\psi \geq 0$, cutoffs as in lemma 6.25.5, and a nonnegative convex nondecreasing $\gamma $, with

\[ \gamma (s)=\psi =0\quad \hbox{on }\{ s\leq t\} ,\qquad \gamma (s)\geq 2\psi ,\qquad H_{\gamma (s)}\geq (1+t)|\bar\partial \psi |^2\operatorname {Id}, \]

and

9B38BB042909 6.25.9
\begin{equation} \int _\Omega |g|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi -\gamma (s)+\psi }\, \mathrm dV<\infty . \end{equation}

To verify the scope of this reuse, the cutoffs there are translates of a fixed one-variable cutoff composed with $s$, and $\psi $ vanishes with its derivative on $\{ s\leq t+1\} $. On each compact band $\{ t+l\leq s\leq t+l+1\} $ the construction uses only a positive lower bound for $H_ s$, bounds for $\psi ,\bar\partial \psi $, and the finite number $\int |g|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi +\psi }$. A locally finite sum of nondecreasing smooth steps chooses $\gamma '$ above these bounds and makes its preceding integral at least the logarithm needed for a $2^{-l}$ integral bound. The number or degree of the coefficients of $g$ does not enter; their squared norm in this integral is all that is used. Thus the stated construction applies unchanged here.

Put $\Phi =\varphi +\gamma (s)$ and $\Phi _ k=\Phi +(k-3)\psi $. By (6.25.9), $g$ belongs to the middle Hilbert space and to $\ker S$. Since $B_ q$ is linear and (6.25.2) gives $B_ q(\gamma (s))\geq q(1+t)|\bar\partial \psi |^2\operatorname {Id}$, (6.25.7) implies

\[ \int \langle B_ q(\varphi )v,v\rangle \mathrm e^{-\Phi }\, \mathrm dV \leq (1+t^{-1})\| T^*v\| _{\Phi _1}^2+\| Sv\| _{\Phi _3}^2. \]

The dual-energy Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and $\gamma (s)\geq 2\psi $ now give

\[ \begin{split} |\langle g,v\rangle _{\Phi _2}|^2 & \leq M\int \langle B_ q(\varphi )v,v\rangle \mathrm e^{\varphi -2\Phi _2}\, \mathrm dV\\ & \leq M\int \langle B_ q(\varphi )v,v\rangle \mathrm e^{-\Phi }\, \mathrm dV\\ & \leq M(1+t^{-1}) \bigl (\| T^*v\| _{\Phi _1}^2+\| Sv\| _{\Phi _3}^2\bigr ). \end{split} \]

Initially $v$ is smooth and compactly supported. Both sides are continuous for the joint graph norm, so lemma 6.25.5 extends this inequality to the full domains. Hilbert-complex solvability from the dual energy estimate gives $\bar\partial u_ t=g$ with $\| u_ t\| _{\Phi _1}^2\leq M(1+t^{-1})$. On $\{ s<t\} $ the weight $\Phi _1$ is exactly $\varphi $.

For every integer $l\geq 1$ the $u_ t$, $t\geq l$, are bounded in $L^2(\{ s<l\} ,\mathrm e^{-\varphi })$. A diagonal weak subsequence by weak subsequences in Hilbert spaces gives compatible limits on all these sublevels, because restriction is a bounded linear map. Their union is $u$, and weak convergence there implies distributional convergence on $\Omega $, so $\bar\partial u=g$. Weak lower semicontinuity gives $\int _{\{ s<l\} }|u|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi }\leq M$ for every $l$; monotone convergence proves the required global estimate. $\square$

Theorem 6.25.10.AA6FAAEB4E71 Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ be pseudoconvex and $2\leq q\leq n$. Let $\varphi \in \operatorname {PSH}(\Omega )$ and $\tau \in C^\infty (\Omega ,\mathbb R)$ with $H_\tau \geq 0$. If $g$ is a measurable $(0,q)$-form with $\bar\partial g=0$ and

\[ M=\int _\Omega |g|_{B_ q(\tau )^{-1}}^2 \mathrm e^{-\varphi -\tau }\, \mathrm dV<\infty , \]

then $\bar\partial u=g$ has a solution with $\int |u|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi -\tau }\, \mathrm dV\leq M$. In particular the right-hand side may be replaced by

5324510E48FA 6.25.11
\begin{equation} \int _\Omega \frac{|g|^2}{\lambda _1+\cdots +\lambda _ q} \mathrm e^{-\varphi -\tau }\, \mathrm dV, \end{equation}

where $\lambda _1\leq \cdots \leq \lambda _ n$ are the nonnegative eigenvalues of $H_\tau $, with the same zero-denominator convention. The degree-one result is the singular-weight $L^2$ estimate with a smooth curvature lower bound.

Proof. The dual-energy bound again implies $g\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$. Choose increasing pseudoconvex $\Omega _ j\Subset \Omega $ exhausting $\Omega $ by pseudoconvex products and relatively compact exhaustions, and radial mollification radii $\eta _ j\downarrow 0$ small enough that $\varphi _ j=\varphi *\rho _{\eta _ j}$ is defined near $\overline\Omega _ j$. By the Levi criterion and decreasing convolution, these are smooth plurisubharmonic functions and, on each fixed compact set for all sufficiently large $j$, $\varphi _ j\downarrow \varphi $. There is no global convolution across $\partial \Omega $. Since $B_ q(\varphi _ j+\tau )\geq B_ q(\tau )$ and $\varphi _ j\geq \varphi $, the preceding theorem gives solutions $u_ j$ on $\Omega _ j$ with

\[ \bar\partial u_ j=g,\qquad \int _{\Omega _ j}|u_ j|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi _ j-\tau }\, \mathrm dV\leq M. \]

For compact $K\subset \Omega _ k$ and $j\geq k$ this also bounds $\int _ K|u_ j|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi _ k-\tau }$ by $M$, and hence gives a local unweighted $L^2$ bound. Diagonal weak compactness on a compact exhaustion supplies $u\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$ with $\bar\partial u=g$. For each fixed $k$, weighted and unweighted $L^2(K)$ norms are equivalent, so weak lower semicontinuity gives $\int _ K|u|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi _ k-\tau }\leq M$. First let $k\to \infty $, using monotone convergence of the densities; then exhaust $\Omega $ by such compact sets. This proves the estimate. Formula (6.25.11) follows from (6.25.2) and dual-energy monotonicity. $\square$

We record the regularity of a chosen solution, since the difference of two positive-degree primitives need not be holomorphic.

Lemma 6.25.12.FA7DAEDEEAF0 Let $1\leq q\leq n$ and $\Phi \in C^\infty (\Omega ,\mathbb R)$. If $g$ is smooth and the equation $\bar\partial u=g$ has a solution in $L^2_{0,q-1}(\Omega ,\mathrm e^{-\Phi })$, its solution of smallest norm is smooth in $\Omega $ and satisfies $\delta _\Phi u=0$.

Proof. The maximal operator $T=\bar\partial $ from degrees $q-1$ to $q$ is closed, and its kernel is a closed subspace of the weighted Hilbert space. Even if $g$ is not globally in the weighted $L^2$ space, all weighted $L^2$ solutions of the distributional equation form the affine subspace $u_0+\ker T$. Orthogonal projection therefore gives a unique smallest-norm solution $u\perp \ker T$. If $q\geq 2$, the maximal operator $P=\bar\partial $ from degree $q-2$ has range in $\ker T$. Hence $u\perp \operatorname {Ran}P$, which by the defining adjoint identity means $u\in \operatorname {Dom}P^*$ and $P^*u=0$. Testing on compact supports gives $\delta _\Phi u=0$ distributionally. For $q=1$ the same equation is automatic for degree reasons.

Thus $\bar\partial u=g$ and $\delta _0u=-\sum _ j(\partial _{z_ j}\Phi )i_ ju$ are locally $L^2$. Let $\chi \in C_ c^\infty (\Omega )$. Then $w=\chi u$ and both constant-coefficient derivatives $\bar\partial w,\delta _0w$ are $L^2$. Mollify $w$ inside a fixed compact neighborhood of its support. The unweighted identity (6.25.4), followed by integration by parts in each real coordinate, gives

\[ \tfrac 14\| \nabla w_\eta \| _{L^2}^2 =\sum _{J,k}\| \partial _{\bar z_ k}(w_\eta )_ J\| _{L^2}^2 =\| \bar\partial w_\eta \| _{L^2}^2+ \| \delta _0w_\eta \| _{L^2}^2. \]

The right side is bounded uniformly in $\eta $, since convolution commutes with both operators. Weak compactness of the derivatives and convergence $w_\eta \to w$ in $L^2$ identify the weak limits as the distributional derivatives of $w$. Therefore $u\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$.

On coefficient forms the weighted Laplacian is

\[ \Delta _\Phi =\bar\partial \delta _\Phi +\delta _\Phi \bar\partial =-\sum _ j\partial _{z_ j}\partial _{\bar z_ j} +\sum _ j(\partial _{z_ j}\Phi )\partial _{\bar z_ j} +\sum _{j,k}(\partial _{z_ j}\partial _{\bar z_ k}\Phi )e_ ki_ j. \]

This follows by $i_ je_ k+e_ ki_ j=\delta _{jk}$; its principal real part is $-\tfrac 14\Delta $ on each coefficient. Distributionally $\Delta _\Phi u=\delta _\Phi g$, a smooth right-hand side. On any relatively compact coordinate ball its divergence-form principal coefficients are $\tfrac 14\delta _{ij}\delta _{IJ}$, and its remaining coefficients are smooth first- and zeroth-order matrices. The hypotheses of interior regularity for elliptic systems in divergence form apply to the $H^1$ solution. Repeated local estimates on smaller balls give every Sobolev order; the local Sobolev embedding proved in Sobolev spaces, duality and local embedding gives smoothness. No boundary regularity or global Hodge decomposition is used. $\square$

All the preceding estimates extend coefficientwise to $(p,q)$-forms, $0\leq p\leq n$, with $|g|^2=\sum _{I,J}|g_{I,J}|^2$. Write $g=\sum _ I\mathrm dz_ I\wedge g_ I$; distributional closedness is $\bar\partial g_ I=0$. If $\bar\partial u_ I=g_ I$, then $u=(-1)^ p\sum _ I\mathrm dz_ I\wedge u_ I$ satisfies $\bar\partial u=g$, and squared norms and dual energies add over $I$. When the weight and data are smooth, choose the smallest-norm solution for each coefficient family and use the preceding lemma. For a smooth weight its bound is no larger than that of the constructed solution. This statement concerns smoothness in $\Omega $, not at its boundary.

Corollary 6.25.13.E01D1C3D97E4 Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$, be pseudoconvex, let $\varphi \in \operatorname {PSH}(\Omega )$, and fix $0\leq p\leq n$, $1\leq q\leq n$ and $0<\varepsilon \leq 1$. For a distributionally $\bar\partial $-closed $(p,q)$-form $g\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$ such that

\[ I=\int _\Omega (1+|z|^2)|g|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi }\, \mathrm dV<\infty , \]

there is a $(p,q-1)$-form $u\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$ with

114551AF385D 6.25.14
\begin{equation} \bar\partial u=g,\qquad \int _\Omega (1+|z|^2)^{-\varepsilon }|u|^2 \mathrm e^{-\varphi }\, \mathrm dV \leq \frac4{q\varepsilon ^2}I. \end{equation}

If $g$ and $\varphi $ are smooth, $u$ can be chosen smooth.

Proof. Put $s=1+|z|^2$ and $\tau =\log (1+s^\varepsilon )$. The radial complex Hessian has tangential eigenvalue $h'(s)$ and smallest eigenvalue $h'(s)+(s-1)h''(s)$, where $h(s)=\log (1+s^\varepsilon )$ and $h''\leq 0$. Direct differentiation gives

\[ h'+(s-1)h'' =\frac{\varepsilon s^{\varepsilon -2} (1+\varepsilon (s-1)+s^\varepsilon )}{(1+s^\varepsilon )^2} \geq \frac{\varepsilon ^2}{2s(1+s^\varepsilon )}=:\lambda (s)>0. \]

Indeed the numerator in parentheses is at least $\varepsilon s$, and $s^\varepsilon /(1+s^\varepsilon )\geq 1/2$. By (6.25.2), $B_ q(\tau )\geq q\lambda (s)\operatorname {Id}$. Apply theorem 6.25.10 for $q\geq 2$, and the existing the singular-weight $L^2$ estimate with a smooth curvature lower bound for $q=1$, to each holomorphic coefficient family. Since

\[ \frac{\mathrm e^{-\tau }}{q\lambda (s)} \leq \frac{2s}{q\varepsilon ^2}, \qquad s^{-\varepsilon }\leq \frac2{1+s^\varepsilon }, \]

the solution satisfies (6.25.14). If both data and weight are smooth, choose instead the solution of smallest norm for the smooth weight $\Phi =\varphi +\tau $; its norm still satisfies the intermediate bound, and lemma 6.25.12 makes it smooth. The exponent $\varepsilon $ is fixed throughout; the mollification radii in the singular-weight proof are the separate parameters $\eta _ j$. $\square$
