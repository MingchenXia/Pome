5.60 Extension of positive currents across complete pluripolar setsA3B5D3EF17A8

A3B5D3EF17A8

We use $d^ c=\frac{i}{4\pi }(\bar\partial -\partial )$, so that $dd^ c=\frac{i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial $, and in coordinates write $\beta =\frac{i}{2}\sum _ j dz_ j\wedge d\bar z_ j$. A current of bidimension $(p,p)$ on an $n$-dimensional manifold has bidegree $(n-p,n-p)$ and acts on forms of bidegree $(p,p)$. Its local trace measure is $T\wedge \beta ^ p$. By coefficient measures and compactness of positive currents, this measure controls all coefficient variations on compact coordinate sets.

Definition 5.60.1.319D3EF2599F A subset $E$ of a complex manifold $X$ is locally complete pluripolar if every point has a neighborhood $U$ and a locally integrable plurisubharmonic function $u$ on $U$ such that $E\cap U=\{ u=-\infty \} $. In the following extension statements $E$ is also required to be closed.

In particular, such a set contains no component of a coordinate neighborhood. A proper analytic subset with no component of $X$ is locally complete pluripolar: use local holomorphic equations and plurisubharmonicity of logarithmic holomorphic norms. Merely being contained in a pole set is not the hypothesis in this definition.

Lemma 5.60.2.899DA22762FD Let $E$ be closed and locally complete pluripolar in $X$. Every point has a coordinate neighborhood $\Omega $ on which there are

  1. a function $v\in \operatorname {PSH}(\Omega )$, smooth and finite on $\Omega \setminus E$, with $v=-\infty $ exactly on $E\cap \Omega $;

  2. smooth plurisubharmonic functions $v_ k$ on $\Omega $ with $0\leq v_ k\leq 1$, increasing to $1$ locally uniformly on $\Omega \setminus E$, each identically zero on a neighborhood of $E\cap \Omega $.

Proof. The assertion is immediate if $E$ is empty near the point: take $v=0$ and $v_ k=1$. Otherwise work in nested bounded coordinate balls $\Omega \Subset \Omega _0\Subset \Omega _1$ with $E\cap \Omega _1=\{ u=-\infty \} $, where $u$ is plurisubharmonic. Shrinking the balls and subtracting a constant gives $u\leq 0$ on $\Omega _1$. Choose a smooth convex nondecreasing function $\chi $ on $[0,1]$, zero on $[0,1/2]$, with $\chi (1)=1$. Put

\[ a_ k=\chi (e^{u/k}),\qquad e^{-\infty }=0. \]

These are plurisubharmonic, $0\leq a_ k\leq 1$, and increase to $1$ off $E$. For completeness, convex nondecreasing composition preserves plurisubharmonicity here: apply the smooth Hessian criterion to a decreasing local smooth regularization of $u$, and then the decreasing limit theorem. The function $t\mapsto \chi (e^{t/k})$ is convex and nondecreasing; on the bounded ranges needed for regularization it can be extended with these properties. The limit is finite everywhere after composition and hence is not identically $-\infty $. Moreover $a_ k=0$ on the open neighborhood $\omega _ k=\{ u<-k\log 2\} $ of $E\cap \Omega _1$.

Choose a positive convolution radius $\varepsilon _ k$ so small that $b_ k=a_ k*\rho _{\varepsilon _ k}$ is defined on a neighborhood of $\overline\Omega $ and is zero near $E\cap \overline\Omega $. This is possible because $E\cap \overline\Omega $ is compact and contained in the open set $\omega _ k\cap \Omega _0$; when this compact set is empty there is no vanishing constraint. Radial regularization gives smooth plurisubharmonic functions with $a_ k\leq b_ k\leq 1$ by the Levi criterion and decreasing convolution. Consequently

\[ w_ k=\max _{1\leq j\leq k}b_ j \]

is continuous plurisubharmonic, increases to $1$ off $E$, and is zero near $E\cap \Omega $. Dini's theorem gives uniform convergence on each compact subset of $\Omega \setminus E$.

Take an increasing compact exhaustion $K_ s$ of $\Omega \setminus E$ whose interiors cover it. Select strictly increasing indices $k_ s$ such that $0\leq 1-w_{k_ s}\leq 2^{-s}$ on $K_ s$. The series

\[ w=|z|^2+\sum _{s=1}^{\infty }(w_{k_ s}-1) \]

converges locally uniformly off $E$, and is $-\infty $ on $E$. Its partial sums decrease and are plurisubharmonic; their limit is finite off $E$, so decreasing nets of plurisubharmonic functions shows that $w$ is plurisubharmonic. Applying the same theorem after subtracting $|z|^2$ shows that its complex Hessian is bounded below by the identity. It is continuous off $E$ and bounded above by $\sup _\Omega |z|^2$. It also tends to $-\infty $ at $E$: for any $N$, the first $N$ terms $w_{k_ s}$ vanish on a common neighborhood of $E\cap \Omega $, so there $w\leq \sup _\Omega |z|^2-N$.

Apply relative Richberg smoothing on the manifold $\Omega \setminus E$, with error function $1$. It gives a smooth plurisubharmonic $v$ there with $w\leq v\leq w+1$. Extend $v$ by $-\infty $ on $E$. This extension is plurisubharmonic without an additional removability premise: for each positive integer $N$, the function equal to $\max \{ v,-N\} $ off $E$ and to $-N$ on $E$ is locally constant near $E$ and is plurisubharmonic elsewhere by maximum and boundary gluing of plurisubharmonic functions. These functions decrease to the extended $v$, which is finite off $E$; apply the decreasing limit theorem. Subtract a constant so that $v\leq 0$. Then

\[ v_ k=\chi (e^{v/k}) \]

has all the stated properties. It is smooth across $E$ because it is identically zero near $E$; it is plurisubharmonic by the same composition argument, increases pointwise to $1$ off $E$, and Dini's theorem gives the stated compact uniform convergence. In dimension zero local integrability makes the local pole set empty, so the initial empty-set case applies. $\square$

Theorem 5.60.3 (Skoda–El Mir extension).BB0A0DD22A5E Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n$, let $E\subset X$ be closed and locally complete pluripolar, and let $T$ be a closed positive current of bidimension $(p,p)$ on $X\setminus E$, $0\leq p\leq n$. Suppose its mass is locally finite near $E$. Then extending every coefficient measure by zero on $E$ defines a closed positive current $\widetilde T$ on $X$. Its coefficient measures give $E$ zero mass.

Proof. The mass hypothesis and compactness give $\int _{K\setminus E}T\wedge \beta ^ p<\infty $ on every compact coordinate set $K$. Trace domination therefore makes the coefficient extensions locally finite. Smooth changes of coordinates commute with restriction of coefficient measures to $X\setminus E$, so the local extensions glue. Positivity follows either from the coefficient description or by integrating nonnegative strongly positive test forms off $E$. It remains to prove closedness locally. If $p=0$, the current has real degree $2n$, so its exterior derivative vanishes by degree.

First let $p=1$ and denote the current by $\Theta $. On a neighborhood $\Omega $ supplied by lemma 5.60.2, write $\mu =\widetilde\Theta \wedge \beta $ and use its functions $v_ k$. For a smooth compactly supported real one-form $\eta $, put $K=\operatorname {supp}\eta $ and choose a nonnegative $\psi \in C_ c^\infty (\Omega )$ equal to $1$ near $K$; write $L=\operatorname {supp}\psi $. On $\Omega \setminus E$ the measure $dv_ k\wedge d^ cv_ k\wedge \Theta $ is positive. Since $0\leq v_ k\leq 1$ and $dd^ cv_ k\geq 0$,

45
\begin{align} 2\int \psi \, dv_ k\wedge d^ cv_ k\wedge \Theta & \leq \int \psi \, dd^ c(v_ k^2)\wedge \Theta \notag \\ & =\int v_ k^2\, dd^ c\psi \wedge \Theta \leq C_\psi \mu (L). \tag{5.60.4} \end{align}

All integrals are over $\Omega \setminus E$. The integration by parts uses only the closedness of $\Theta $ there: for each fixed $k$, $\psi v_ k^2$ and its derivatives have compact support away from $E$. One may insert a compact cutoff in $\Omega \setminus E$ equal to one near that support; its derivative terms vanish because $v_ k$ is zero near $E$. The last inequality is coefficient domination, and its constant depends on $\psi $, not on $k$.

Choose smooth $\theta :[0,1]\to [0,1]$ equal to zero on $[0,1/3]$ and to one on $[2/3,1]$. The smooth-case Cauchy–Schwarz calculation underlying the bounded-potential current chain rule gives

FE0A285FF079 5.60.5
\begin{equation} \left|\int \theta '(v_ k)\eta \wedge dv_ k\wedge \Theta \right|^2 \leq C_\eta \left(\int \psi \, dv_ k\wedge d^ cv_ k\wedge \Theta \right) \left(\int _ K|\theta '(v_ k)|^2\, d\mu \right). \end{equation}

Here the calculation is used on $\Omega \setminus E$ and does not assume that $\widetilde\Theta $ is closed. More explicitly, the coefficient matrix $H$ with $\frac{i}{2\pi }dz_ k\wedge d\bar z_ j\wedge \Theta =h_{jk}\mu $ is positive semidefinite with trace $1/\pi $. If $e$ is the vector of coefficients of the $(1,0)$ part of $\eta $, the two densities in the canonical calculation are $|H^{1/2}\partial v_ k|^2$ and $4\pi \operatorname {Im}\langle H^{1/2}e,H^{1/2}\partial v_ k\rangle $. The scalar Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and $|H^{1/2}e|^2\leq |e|^2/\pi $ prove (5.60.5). Thus no cutoff depending on $k$ enters its constant.

The first factor in (5.60.5) is uniformly bounded by (5.60.4). The second tends to zero by dominated convergence: $v_ k\to 1$ off $E$, $\theta '(1)=0$, $\mu (K)<\infty $, and $\mu (E)=0$. Now $S_ k=\theta (v_ k)\widetilde\Theta $ is zero near $E$, so

\[ dS_ k=\theta '(v_ k)\, dv_ k\wedge \Theta \]

extended by zero. The estimate shows that $dS_ k\to 0$ on real one-form tests, hence on all one-form tests by complex linearity. Dominated convergence against every coefficient variation gives $S_ k\to \widetilde\Theta $. Weak continuity of differentiation proves $d\widetilde\Theta =0$.

For $p\geq 1$, choose a real basis $\gamma _ s$ of constant $(p-1,p-1)$-forms consisting of decomposable strongly positive forms; such a basis exists by strong and weak positivity of differential forms. The currents $T\wedge \gamma _ s$ are closed positive of bidimension $(1,1)$ and have locally finite mass by coefficient domination. Zero extension commutes with multiplication by $\gamma _ s$, so the case just proved gives $d\widetilde T\wedge \gamma _ s=0$ for every $s$. Every constant form of bidegree $(p-1,p)$ is a sum of a constant $(p-1,p-1)$-form wedged with a $(0,1)$-form; the analogous statement holds for $(p,p-1)$. Expand these forms in the basis $\gamma _ s$ and allow arbitrary smooth compactly supported coefficients. All tests of $\partial \widetilde T$ and $\bar\partial \widetilde T$ therefore vanish. This includes $p=n$ and proves the theorem. $\square$

Corollary 5.60.6.53F93547F96E If $T$ is a closed positive current on $X$ and $E$ is closed and locally complete pluripolar, both coefficient restrictions $\mathbf1_ E T$ and $\mathbf1_{X\setminus E}T$ are closed positive currents.

Proof. The restriction of $T$ to $X\setminus E$ has locally finite mass near $E$. Its zero extension is $\mathbf1_{X\setminus E}T$, closed by theorem 5.60.3. The other restriction is positive by coefficient restriction and closed as its difference from $T$. $\square$
