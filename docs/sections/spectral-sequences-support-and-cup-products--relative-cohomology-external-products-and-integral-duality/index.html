10.69 Relative cohomology, external products and integral duality5CEBC1462E62

5CEBC1462E62

Cohomology of sheaves uses the functorial Godement complex $C^ qF=G^{q+1}F$, with the alternating coface differential, as in theorem 10.43.5. Supports are closed supports of germs. The subscript $c$ means compact supports, and extension from an open set always means extension by zero. Throughout, $R$ is a nonzero principal ideal domain; $\operatorname {Ext}$ and $\operatorname {Tor}$ below mean their first algebraic derived functors over $R$. All tensor products are algebraic. Topological manifolds in this section are Hausdorff, paracompact, without boundary and of a fixed finite real dimension $n$. Connectedness and orientability are stated separately. No finite rank or finite generation of a local coefficient module is understood.

10.69.1 Relative groups and the support comparison90BE245E2695

Lemma 10.69.2.DEC4F08B6222 For a subspace $i:S\hookrightarrow X$ and a sheaf $A$ on $X$, the functorial restriction

\[ r:C_ X^\bullet A(X)\longrightarrow C_ S^\bullet (i^{-1}A)(S) \]

is onto in each degree. Its kernel defines $H^ q(X,S;A)$ and gives the natural long exact sequence of a pair and of a triple. Equivalently, this kernel is quasi-isomorphic to the mapping fiber of $r$. In degree zero it is the module of global sections of $A$ whose restriction to $S$ is zero. For constant coefficients these are locally constant functions vanishing on $S$; arbitrary values on connected components are not asserted when those components are not open. For constant coefficients these groups are the relative Godement, or Alexander–Spanier, groups. Relative pullback is functorial, and constant-coefficient relative groups are invariant under homotopies of pairs.

Proof. The restriction map in degree zero of the resolution takes an assignment $(a_ x)_{x\in X}$ to its subassignment on $S$; it is onto. Inductively, a germ at $s\in S$ of an assignment of germs on a relative open neighborhood can be extended to an ambient open neighborhood: lift each of its values by the preceding inductive surjectivity and assign zero away from $S$. This proves that the stalk map $i^{-1}G_ X^ qA\to G_ S^ q i^{-1}A$ is onto, and the outer product over points proves the next degree's surjectivity on sections. These maps commute with cofaces by naturality of augmentation. For a triple, lift a cochain on $S$ that vanishes on $T$ to $X$; its restriction to $T$ is already zero. The kernels therefore form short exact complexes. The long exact sequences, with their positive connecting maps, follow from lemma 10.43.3. Their degree-zero kernel gives the stated description of sections.

For a surjective cochain map the natural map from its kernel to its mapping fiber is a quasi-isomorphism: its quotient is the mapping fiber of the identity of the target, contracted by the identity in the adjacent degree. This observation also shows that replacing both absolute complexes by naturally quasi-isomorphic complexes preserves relative groups. The restriction and inverse-image maps of the Godement construction supply functorial pullback. On a pair, projection to $X$ and to $S$ from their products with $[0,1]$ induces isomorphisms by theorem 10.63.3. The natural pair sequences and the five lemma give the relative isomorphism. Both endpoint restrictions are its inverse, which proves homotopy invariance for maps of pairs. $\square$

Theorem 10.69.3.211571452FE5 If $\overline T\subset \operatorname {int}_ X S$, restriction gives

\[ H^ q(X,S;A)\simeq H^ q(X\setminus T,S\setminus T;A). \]

If $S$ is open or strongly paracompact in $X$, there is also a natural isomorphism

\[ H^ q(X,S;A)\simeq H^ q_{X\setminus S}(X,A), \]

where the right side allows precisely closed supports contained in $X\setminus S$. If, in addition, $X$ is locally compact Hausdorff, $S$ is closed, and $U=X\setminus S$ has compact closure in $X$, this is $H_ c^ q(U,A|_ U)$.

Proof. First, relative groups have Mayer–Vietoris for a cover by two open sets. Apply the absolute Mayer–Vietoris short complexes of theorem 10.43.7 to $X$ and to its induced open cover on $S$ and take mapping fibers. The two term Čech resolutions of flabby Godement terms are exact, so the resulting total complexes compute the relative mapping fibers just proved. This gives the claimed exact sequence, with the restriction maps on all three pair terms.

Put $V=\operatorname {int}S$ and $W=X\setminus \overline T$. These are open and cover $X$. In this relative Mayer–Vietoris sequence the pair on $V$ is $(V,V)$ and has zero cohomology; the same is true on $V\cap W$. Thus restriction gives $H^ q(X,S;A)=H^ q(W,S\cap W;A)$. The same argument on $X\setminus T$ uses the open cover by $W$ and $V\setminus T$ and gives the same middle group. The two isomorphisms are the asserted restriction. This proof uses the closure in the hypothesis.

For open $S$, restriction of $C_ X^\bullet A$ is exactly the Godement complex on $S$. A section restricting to zero has closed support disjoint from $S$, giving equality of the two kernel complexes. For strongly paracompact $S$, use instead the complex $\Gamma (S,i^{-1}C_ X^\bullet A)$ of germ sections. Each term restricts surjectively from $X$: the closed-section neighborhood extension argument in theorem 10.44.4 extends a germ section to a neighborhood, and flabbiness then extends it to $X$. Its kernel again consists of closed supports disjoint from $S$. The exact proof of theorem 10.60.1 shows that $i^{-1}C_ X^ qA$ is soft and acyclic on $S$; hence this complex computes $H^ q(S,i^{-1}A)$. Its natural map to the intrinsic Godement complex is an acyclic-resolution comparison inducing the identity in degree zero. Comparing the two short exact kernel complexes proves the support assertion.

Finally assume the additional hypotheses. A closed support in $X$ contained in $U$ is compact because it lies in $\overline U$. Conversely a compact subset of $U$ is closed in $X$. Restriction and extension by zero identify sections with these supports with compact sections on the open space $U$. The open restriction of a flabby resolution computes both groups, proving the last assertion. The open-complement requirement is essential. For $X=\mathbb R$ and $S=\mathbb R\setminus \{ 0\} $ with integer coefficients, the pair sequence gives $H^0(X,S)=0$, $H^1(X,S)=\mathbb Z$, whereas $H_ c^0(\{ 0\} ,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$ and $H_ c^1(\{ 0\} ,\mathbb Z)=0$. $\square$

Corollary 10.69.4.7B9A12C78AC8 Properly homotopic maps between locally compact Hausdorff spaces induce the same pullback on compactly supported cohomology with constant coefficients.

Proof. Write $X^+=X\cup \{ \infty \} $ for the one-point compactification; if $X$ is already compact, the new point is isolated. A proper map $f:X\to Y$ extends continuously to a map $f^+:X^+\to Y^+$ fixing infinity, since inverse images of compact sets are compact. If $H:X\times [0,1]\to Y$ is proper, it extends to $X^+\times [0,1]$ by sending all $(\infty ,t)$ to infinity. Indeed, the projection to $X$ of $H^{-1}(K)$ is compact for every compact $K\subset Y$; outside that projection the extension avoids $K$ uniformly for all $t$. This proves joint continuity at the entire added interval. The preceding theorem gives $H_ c^ q(X,A)=H^ q(X^+,\{ \infty \} ;A)$, and similarly for $Y$. Apply the actual homotopy invariance of pairs. This constructs proper pullback and proves equality of the two endpoint maps. $\square$

10.69.5 A coefficient comparison at one complex90C2945539E2

Lemma 10.69.6.9BD1AF8701FE Let $B$ be a soft sheaf of $R$-algebras on a compact Hausdorff space $X$, let $F$ be a $B$-module sheaf, and let $N$ be an $R$-module. Suppose either $F$ is stalkwise flat over $R$ or $N$ is flat. Then

\[ \Gamma (X,F)\otimes _ RN\ \xrightarrow {\sim } \Gamma (X,F\otimes _ R\underline N). \]

The sheaves $F\otimes _ R\underline N$ are soft. In particular, for a sheaf $A$, the complex $C_ X^\bullet A\otimes _ R\underline N$ is an acyclic resolution of $A\otimes _ R\underline N$ if either $A$ or $N$ is flat. Its section complex is $C_ X^\bullet A(X)\otimes _ RN$. The latter conclusion also holds for arbitrary $X$ when $N$ is finitely generated over a PID.

Proof. Every $B$-module sheaf is soft by corollary 10.44.9. For a free module $P=\bigoplus _ JR$, a section of $F\otimes P=\bigoplus _ JF$ uses only finitely many summands locally. Compactness gives one finite set of summands for the entire section, and therefore $\Gamma (X,F\otimes P)=\Gamma (X,F)\otimes P$. Take a free presentation $0\to P_1\to P_0\to N\to 0$ over the PID. Under either stated flatness assumption its tensor sequence with $F$ is exact. The first term $F\otimes P_1$ is soft, so taking sections preserves this short exact sequence. The free cases and right exactness of tensor give the displayed isomorphism of cokernels.

The ring $C_ X^ q\underline R$ is flabby and hence soft. Its module $C_ X^ qA\otimes \underline N$ is therefore acyclic. If $A$ is flat, all $C_ X^ qA$ and their cycle sheaves are flat: products and filtered limits of torsion-free modules are torsion-free, and submodules remain torsion-free. Tensoring the augmented exact resolution remains exact. If $N$ is flat, exactness follows directly. Apply the preceding section comparison term by term and theorem 10.43.8.

For the last assertion use a finite presentation of $N$. Tensoring with a finitely presented module commutes with products and filtered limits: write the tensor as the cokernel of a map between finite powers, and lift coordinates separately to identify product cokernels; filtered limits are exact. Consequently the natural map $C_ X^ qA\otimes \underline N\to C_ X^ q(A\otimes \underline N)$ is an isomorphism, including on sections, by induction on the Godement construction. The latter terms are flabby on every $X$. The preceding exactness argument finishes the proof. $\square$

Theorem 10.69.7.77686EA729F2 Let $A$ be a sheaf of modules over a PID $R$ and $N$ an $R$-module, with at least one of $A,N$ flat. If $X$ is compact Hausdorff or $N$ is finitely generated, there is a natural short exact sequence

\[ 0\longrightarrow H^ q(X,A)\otimes N \longrightarrow H^ q(X,A\otimes \underline N) \longrightarrow \operatorname {Tor}(H^{q+1}(X,A),N) \longrightarrow 0. \]

It splits as a sequence of modules, without a natural choice of splitting. If $X$ is locally compact Hausdorff, the same statement holds with compact supports and without either finiteness condition.

Proof. In the ordinary case the lemma identifies the middle group with the cohomology of the single complex $C_ X^\bullet A(X)\otimes N$. When $A$ is flat its section terms are torsion-free and flat. Apply corollary 10.67.9. When $N$ is flat, tensor is exact, the Tor term is zero and the left arrow is itself an isomorphism. The maps are the actual coefficient maps of complexes, so the naturality assertion does not depend on the chosen splitting.

For compact supports let $U\Subset X$ and $K=\overline U$. The complex $(C_ X^\bullet A)_ U|_ K$ resolves $A_ U|_ K$. Each of its terms is a module over the soft ring $(C_ X^ q\underline R)|_ K$; softness of this restriction follows from the compact strong-paracompactness and neighborhood-extension proof in lemma 10.56.5. Apply the lemma on $K$. Its section complex is $\Gamma _ c(U,C_ X^\bullet A|_ U)$, as follows by extension by zero. These complexes form a directed union equal to $\Gamma _ c(X,C_ X^\bullet A)$; every compact support is contained in some relatively compact open $U$. The exact compact-neighborhood comparison in theorem 10.56.7 therefore gives the natural quasi-isomorphism

\[ \Gamma _ c(X,C_ X^\bullet A)\otimes N \longrightarrow R\Gamma _ c(X,A\otimes \underline N). \]

Here the right side denotes its Godement representative, and the arrow is the usual augmentation comparison, obtained on the compact neighborhoods and then in their directed union. Tensor and cohomology commute with that union. Apply the algebraic theorem to this one compact-section complex. No chosen splitting is passed through a limit. $\square$

Theorem 10.69.8.767B8FC48593 Let $A$ and $B$ be sheaves of modules over a PID on $X$ and $Y$, respectively, and put $A\boxtimes B=p_ X^{-1}A\otimes p_ Y^{-1}B$. Suppose $A$ is flat and $Y$ is compact Hausdorff. If $X$ is compact Hausdorff or every $H^ q(Y,B)$ is finitely generated, external cup product is the first map of a natural short exact sequence

\[ \begin{aligned} 0\longrightarrow \bigoplus _{p+q=k}H^ p(X,A)\otimes H^ q(Y,B) & \longrightarrow H^ k(X\times Y,A\boxtimes B)\\ & \longrightarrow \bigoplus _{p+q=k+1} \operatorname {Tor}(H^ p(X,A),H^ q(Y,B))\longrightarrow 0. \end{aligned} \]

The sequence splits noncanonically. For locally compact Hausdorff $X,Y$ there is the identical compact-support statement, with all three cohomology occurrences replaced by compact-support groups, without compactness or finite-generation assumptions. The flat factor may be interchanged with the other factor, with the usual Koszul sign on external products.

Proof. Write $K_ X=C_ X^\bullet A(X)$ and $K_ Y=C_ Y^\bullet B(Y)$. There is an actual sheaf-complex morphism

\[ A\otimes \underline{K_ Y}\longrightarrow p_{X*}C_{X\times Y}^\bullet (A\boxtimes B): \]

pull back a coefficient section on $X$ and a Godement cochain on $Y$, then apply the front/back product of proposition 10.58.7. On the stalk at $x$ its cohomology map is the coefficient comparison

\[ A_ x\otimes H^ q(Y,B)\longrightarrow H^ q(Y,A_ x\otimes B), \]

by proposition 10.61.4. It is an isomorphism by compact coefficient comparison and flatness of $A_ x$. Thus the sheaf-complex morphism is a quasi-isomorphism. Resolving its terms once more by $C_ X^\bullet $ and taking the first-quadrant total computes $H^\bullet (X\times Y,A\boxtimes B)$: the target terms $p_{X*}C_{X\times Y}^ q(A\boxtimes B)$ are flabby, so the other augmentation comparison has acyclic rows.

The natural Godement tensor map supplies a morphism

\[ K_ X\otimes K_ Y\longrightarrow \operatorname {Tot}\Gamma (X,C_ X^\bullet (A\otimes \underline{K_ Y})). \]

For the spectral sequences that first take $K_ Y$ cohomology, flatness of $A$ and of $C_ X^ pA(X)$, and exactness of $\Gamma (X,C_ X^ p-)$, identify its second-page maps as

\[ H^ p(K_ X\otimes H^ q(Y,B))\longrightarrow H^ p(X,A\otimes \underline{H^ q(Y,B)}). \]

These are the actual coefficient-complex comparisons proved above; they are isomorphisms under precisely the hypotheses in the statement. This does not assert a degreewise commutation of Godement products with arbitrary tensor products. First-quadrant comparison now proves that external cup product from $K_ X\otimes K_ Y$ computes the product cohomology. Apply theorem 10.67.7, since $K_ X$ is termwise flat. Its natural exact sequence and its separately constructed splitting give the assertion.

For the compact-support case use $U\Subset X$, $V\Subset Y$, $K=\overline U$, $L=\overline V$. On $K,L$ use the acyclic resolutions $(C_ X^\bullet A)_ U|_ K$ and $(C_ Y^\bullet B)_ V|_ L$. Their terms are soft ring modules as in the preceding proof and the first resolution is stalkwise flat. Resolving each term by its own Godement complex compares their section totals with $K_ K(A_ U|_ K)$ and $K_ L(B_ V|_ L)$ by augmentations. All tensor comparisons are quasi-isomorphisms: the first complexes and their cycles are flat, and the bounded-below flat tensor comparison in theorem 10.67.7 applies. The already proved compact case identifies their external product with cohomology of $(A\boxtimes B)_{U\times V}|_{K\times L}$. The product comparison commutes with these augmentations, since the Godement product does; this is also the acyclic multiplicative comparison of theorem 10.58.9.

Take the directed union over $U,V$. The source becomes $\Gamma _ c(X,C_ X^\bullet A)\otimes \Gamma _ c(Y,C_ Y^\bullet B)$. On the target, compact subsets of $X\times Y$ lie in such rectangles, by their compact projections and relatively compact neighborhoods. The compact-neighborhood theorem therefore identifies the target with compact-support cohomology on the product. All transition maps are extension maps and all product comparisons commute with them. The resulting map of the single tensor complex is a quasi-isomorphism. Its first factor is flat, so the algebraic Künneth theorem proves both the natural exact sequence and its noncanonical splitting. $\square$

Corollary 10.69.9.D1F14C5698EC In the ordinary theorem, a finite CW complex may be used for the compact factor with constant finitely generated coefficients. When both coefficient sheaves are constant and flat, it is enough that one factor have the homotopy type of a finite CW complex and that the coefficient module on that factor be finitely generated. The coefficient qualification cannot be omitted, even when that factor is a point and both coefficient modules are flat.

Proof. Here is the required finiteness argument. For a finite CW complex, its skeletal filtration is finite. A neighborhood of the preceding skeleton inside the next skeleton consists of that skeleton and outer annular collars in the finitely many newly attached disks. Radial motion in each collar, fixed on the attaching boundary, defines a deformation retraction of this neighborhood onto the preceding skeleton, continuously on the attaching quotient. Use two nested collars and relative excision to remove the preceding skeleton and the outer closed collars. This identifies the relative cohomology of successive skeleta with the direct sum, over the new $r$-cells, of the cohomology of a disk relative to its boundary. The contractibility and sphere calculation of theorem 10.63.3, proposition 10.66.1 and the pair sequence identify that group with one copy of the coefficient module in degree $r$, and zero in other degrees. For $r=0$ these are simply the finitely many point sections. The skeletal pair sequences, by induction, now show that all cohomology modules are finitely generated: kernels and quotients of finitely generated modules over a PID are finitely generated, by lemma 10.67.4. A finite CW complex is compact Hausdorff: each finite attaching quotient is compact, and the closed equivalence relation in the finite disjoint compact disks gives the Hausdorff quotient. The ordinary theorem applies with this factor.

For the homotopy-type version choose a homotopy equivalence of that factor with the finite complex. Product with the identity of the other factor is again a homotopy equivalence. Both external coefficient sheaves are constant, with tensor product the constant sheaf of the tensor module, so constant-sheaf homotopy invariance applies to the factors and the product. Naturality of the actual external cup map transports the short exact sequence. If the compact factor is the first one, interchange factors with the stated Koszul sign; flatness of the other coefficient is exactly what is needed.

For the failure without the coefficient qualification take $R=\mathbb Z$, $X=\mathbb N$ discrete, $Y$ a point, $A=\underline{\mathbb Z}$ on $X$ and $B=\mathbb Q$ on $Y$. The degree-zero external map is

\[ (\prod _{j\ge 1}\mathbb Z)\otimes \mathbb Q \longrightarrow \prod _{j\ge 1}\mathbb Q. \]

Every element of the source has a common denominator in its image, because a tensor is a finite sum. A sequence $(1/p_ j)$ for distinct primes has no common denominator and is not in that image. All Tor terms here vanish. Thus a finite-cell compact factor with arbitrary flat coefficients does not imply the asserted ordinary Künneth isomorphism. $\square$
