6.38 Analytic multiplicity and ordinary densityAE6671C2B953

AE6671C2B953

Use $dd^ c=\frac{i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial $. For a reduced pure $p$-dimensional analytic set $A$ write $[A]$ for its positive closed integration current of theorem 5.61.2. All assertions below are local at the indicated point.

Lemma 6.38.1.A7BC2FB7F1C6 Let $(A,0)\subset (\mathbb C^ n,0)$ be reduced and pure of dimension $p$. The linear coordinate systems $z=(z',z'')$, with $z'\in \mathbb C^ p$, for which

CD38640A3802 6.38.2
\begin{equation} |z''|\leq C|z'|\quad \text{on }A\text{ near }0 \end{equation}

for some finite $C$ form an open dense subset of the invertible linear coordinate systems. For any such system, increase $C$ to a positive number if needed. For all sufficiently small $r>0$, the projection

\[ \pi :A_ r=A\cap \bigl (B_ p(0,r)\times B_{n-p}(0,Cr)\bigr ) \longrightarrow B_ p(0,r) \]

is proper with finite fibers and has fiber $\{ 0\} $ over zero. For $1\leq p\leq n$ it is a finite covering with a constant positive integer number of sheets outside a proper analytic subset of the base. For $p=0$ the germ is the reduced point and the projection is the map of that point to $\mathbb C^0$.

Proof. There are finitely many irreducible branches, by irreducible analytic branches and prime ideals. Use the triangular-equation construction in the complete proof of finite local parametrization of analytic germs simultaneously on their prime ideals. The simultaneous construction is also used in purity and dimension drop for analytic germs: at each stage finitely many nonzero germs must be distinguished in the last coordinate. Their first nonzero homogeneous parts exclude only the zero set of their nonzero product, so the direction can be chosen arbitrarily close to any prescribed one. The stop dimension of every branch is $p$, by the dimension assertion of that same proposition. Thus these finite successive choices give common coordinates arbitrarily close to any given ones, and the exact monic-root estimate in the parametrization proof gives (6.38.2) on every branch. Take the maximum of their constants and shrink their common representative.

The condition is open as well. In fixed Euclidean coordinates it implies $|z|\leq K|Lz|$ on the germ, where $Lz=z'$ and $K=(1+C^2)^{1/2}$. If $\| \widetilde L-L\| <1/(2K)$, then

\[ |\widetilde Lz|\geq |Lz|-\| \widetilde L-L\| \, |z| \geq |z|/(2K) \]

on the same representative. The remaining new linear coordinates are bounded by a constant times $|z|$, proving their cone estimate. This proves openness and the stated precise meaning of genericity.

Choose the closed product of balls inside a neighborhood where $A$ is analytic and the estimate holds. For a compact base set $K_0\subset B_ p(0,r)$, choose $t<r$ with $|z'|\leq t$ on $K_0$. All points of its inverse image satisfy $|z''|\leq Ct<Cr$. The inverse image is therefore a closed subset of a compact product strictly inside the coordinate neighborhood, so it is compact. Each fiber is a compact analytic subset of the open vertical ball, hence finite by the maximum principle on analytic branches. The cone estimate gives the single fiber over zero.

At every point of $A_ r$ the pure source dimension is $p$ and its fiber is isolated. By proper finite representatives of analytic map germs, projection is locally open there. Its global image in the connected base ball is consequently open, closed by properness, and nonempty; it is the entire ball. For completeness use the exact analytic critical set from the proof of that lemma: let $C_0$ consist of the singular locus and the regular points where $d\pi $ has rank less than $p$. Locally it is the rank-deficient set of the matrix formed by the differentials of generators of the reduced ideal of $A$ and the $p$ coordinate rows of $d\pi $. On the regular locus, maximal minors test full rank on its tangent space; on the singular locus their vanishing is automatic. Thus $C_0$ is analytic. It contains no branch: a smaller maximal rank on a regular open piece would give a positive-dimensional local fiber by the holomorphic constant-rank theorem, contradicting finiteness. The dimension estimates in proper finite representatives of analytic map germs give $\dim C_0\leq p-1$. Its proper image $E=\pi (C_0)$ is analytic by Remmert's proper mapping theorem and has dimension at most $p-1$ by dimension bounds for holomorphic images.

Over the connected set $B_ p(0,r)\setminus E$, whose connectedness follows from bounded holomorphic removal and connected complements, projection is a proper local biholomorphism. To see that it is a covering, take the finitely many points of a fiber and disjoint neighborhoods mapped biholomorphically onto neighborhoods of the base point. Properness excludes additional inverse points outside these neighborhoods over all sufficiently close base points: otherwise a sequence of them has a convergent subsequence in the compact inverse image of a compact neighborhood and limits to that fiber. Intersect the finitely many inverse-chart images. This gives an evenly covered neighborhood. The positive finite sheet number is locally constant and hence constant on the connected base complement. When $p=0$, the reduced pure germ is just $\{ 0\} $ and all the claims follow directly. For $p=n$ the zero-dimensional vertical ball is a point; the same proof applies, with no vertical coordinates. $\square$

Theorem 6.38.3.673FC5FBB2C8 Let $a\in A$, where $A$ is reduced and pure of dimension $p$ near $a$ in a complex manifold. In any coordinates centered at $a$ satisfying (6.38.2), let $m$ be the finite sheet number of the preceding projection. Then

\[ \nu ([A],a)=m. \]

In particular $m$ is independent of the cone-adapted coordinate system, the small representative and its radii. Denote it by $\operatorname {mult}_ a A$, the multiplicity of $A$ at $a$. It is a positive integer. For a reduced isolated point it is one.

Proof. Work at $a=0$. If $p=0$, both sides are the mass of the single reduced point, namely one. Suppose $p\geq 1$ and put

\[ \varphi (z)=\log |z|^2,\qquad \psi (z)=\log |z'|^2. \]

On $A\setminus \{ 0\} $ the cone estimate gives the actual bound

6576C08255BC 6.38.4
\begin{equation} 0\leq \varphi -\psi \leq \log (1+C^2). \end{equation}

Both weights have the same point pole on the support of $[A]$. They are semi-exhaustive there on the product representative: small $\varphi $-sublevels lie in a compact ambient ball inside that product, and for $t<r$ the closed $\psi $-sublevel on $A$ with $|z'|\leq t$ has $|z''|\leq Ct<Cr$, hence is compact by the same argument as in the lemma. Thus corollary 6.34.11 gives

\[ \nu ([A],\varphi )=\nu ([A],\psi ). \]

The first side is the ordinary number, by its radial identification in that corollary. The second is $m$ by corollary 6.35.2, applied to the proper finite projection and its actual covering just proved. That input uses change of variables on the $m$ sheets and zero coefficient mass on the proper analytic discriminant and its inverse image. Thus it is a projection-weight calculation made applicable to the ordinary number by (6.38.4); finite sheet number alone would not give that identification.

The ordinary number is independent of the holomorphic coordinates by theorem 6.33.10, and depends only on the germ. This proves every asserted independence and positivity. The argument applies to arbitrary, possibly nonlinear, coordinate charts in which the stated cone condition holds. $\square$

Corollary 6.38.5.CF13884F31CB For an effective integral pure $p$-cycle $Z=\sum _ i c_ i[A_ i]$,

\[ \nu (Z,a)=\sum _{i:a\in A_ i}c_ i\operatorname {mult}_ a A_ i. \]

In particular a reduced pure germ has multiplicity equal to the sum of the multiplicities of its irreducible local branches. Moreover $\operatorname {mult}_ a A=1$ if and only if $a$ is a regular point of $A$.

Proof. The local sum is finite. Distinct pure-dimensional branches meet in smaller-dimensional analytic sets, which give zero coefficient mass to their integration currents; hence the reduced integration current is the sum of the branch currents. Ordinary finite-radius masses and their limits are linear on such finite positive sums. The theorem therefore proves the cycle formula and branch formula. Each branch multiplicity is at least one, since the cone projection on that branch is surjective and has positive sheet number.

At a regular point choose submanifold coordinates, so that $A=\{ z''=0\} $ and its projection is one sheet. Conversely, if the reduced multiplicity is one, the cone projection in the lemma has one sheet outside $E$. It is an isomorphism there. The inverse image of $E$ is nowhere dense: projection is locally open on the pure source, so a nonempty source open subset cannot map into the proper analytic set $E$. Thus projection is finite and bimeromorphic onto the base ball, which is normal. By finite bimeromorphic maps to normal spaces it is an isomorphism. Its source is therefore smooth at $a$. For $p=0$ an isolated reduced point is regular and the same conclusion is direct. The case $p=n$ is the local ambient open set, with one sheet and ordinary number one. $\square$
