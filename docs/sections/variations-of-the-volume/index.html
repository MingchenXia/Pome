Corollary 10.99.1 (Volume concavity and mixed nef bounds).B05D26A510EC For pseudo-effective $\alpha ,\beta $,

\[ \operatorname {Vol}(\alpha +\beta )^{1/n} \geq \operatorname {Vol}(\alpha )^{1/n} +\operatorname {Vol}(\beta )^{1/n}. \]

For nef classes $B_ i\leq B_ i'$ in pseudo-effective order on a compact Kähler model, $\int B_1\cdots B_ n\leq \int B_1'\cdots B_ n'$.

Proof. For big $\alpha ,\beta $ take simultaneous same-class approximations $A_ j,B_ j$ above. Their sum is Kähler and has effective residual in the pullback of $\alpha +\beta $. Numerical cofinality bounds $\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha +\beta )$ below by $(A_ j+B_ j)^ n$. The inequality in the nef mixed-intersection inequality and the binomial formula bound this by $( (A_ j^ n)^{1/n}+(B_ j^ n)^{1/n})^ n$ from below. Let $j\to \infty $. For pseudo-effective classes replace both by their $\varepsilon \omega $ perturbations, and use the defining decreasing limits of class volumes. Homogeneity converts the displayed superadditivity to concavity of the homogeneous degree-one root.

For the last assertion replace one factor at a time. The difference is the pairing of a closed positive current in $B_ i'-B_ i$ with the other nef factors. Approximate those factors by Kähler forms after adding an arbitrarily small Kähler class; the integrals are nonnegative, and their polynomial limits retain this sign. $\square$

F956FFB5439C

Let $X$ be a nonempty connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$. Put $V=H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ and $W=H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbb R)$, with their perfect real integration pairing, obtained from the real Kähler Hodge decomposition, analytic Serre duality. Write $\mathcal E\subset V$ for the pseudoeffective cone and $\mathcal B$ for its interior, the big cone. We use the volume of a class, extended by zero outside $\mathcal E$, and put

\[ P(\alpha )=\langle \alpha ^{n-1}\rangle \in W. \]

For $n=1$ the product is the constant empty product $1$.

By corollary 13.31.4, the current volume agrees with the maximal non-pluripolar class mass. Its simultaneous numerical approximation is given by theorem 13.32.3. The volume of a big class is unchanged by modification: the order-preserving potential bijection of potentials under a modification of a smooth target takes a minimal potential to a minimal potential, and non-pluripolar products under modifications preserves its top mass. A pseudoeffective non-big class cannot become big upstairs: its minimal current has zero top mass by bigness and positive non-pluripolar volume, so the pulled-back minimal current also has zero top mass. Non-pseudoeffectivity is likewise preserved by the potential bijection. Thus modification invariance also holds for the zero extension of volume. In particular, volume is homogeneous of degree $n$, is nondecreasing in pseudoeffective directions and is positive exactly on $\mathcal B$, by the positive mixed-mass bound, bigness and positive non-pluripolar volume. For a pseudoeffective $\alpha $ and a Kähler class $\omega $,

653F016945FF 10.99.2
\begin{equation} \operatorname {vol}(\alpha +\varepsilon \omega ) \longrightarrow \operatorname {vol}(\alpha ) \quad (\varepsilon \downarrow 0). \end{equation}

The mixed-mass bound and the cohomological identification in definition 13.32.4, proposition 13.32.6 give the following specific properties. For every Kähler $\kappa $, $F_\kappa (\alpha )=\kappa \cdot P(\alpha )$ is finite, nonnegative, homogeneous of degree $n-1$, locally bounded on $\mathcal B$, and nondecreasing when a pseudoeffective class is added. The boundary value $P(\alpha )$ is the limit of $P(\alpha +\varepsilon \omega )$ in $W$. These statements concern numerical products of classes, not an assertion of continuity for arbitrary weakly converging currents.

We use the sharp nef Morse inequality of theorem 10.98.2, on $X$ and on every compact Kähler modification $Y$:

1990C9505835 10.99.3
\begin{equation} \operatorname {vol}(a-b)\geq a^ n-na^{n-1}\cdot b \qquad (a,b\text{ nef on }Y). \end{equation}

Lemma 10.99.4 (A cone estimate).318ABE32B25C Let $B$ be an open convex cone in a finite-dimensional real normed space. Suppose $f:B\to \mathbb R_{\geq 0}$ is locally bounded, homogeneous of degree $d\geq 0$, and satisfies $f(x+z)\geq f(x)$ for $x,z\in B$. Then $f$ is locally Lipschitz. If $d=0$, it is locally constant.

Proof. Fix $x_0\in B$ and choose $r>0$ with $\overline{B(x_0,2r)}\subset B$ and with $f$ bounded on this ball. For $x\in B(x_0,r/2)$ we have $x-x_0/2\in B$, by scaling the ball. If $0<\| h\| <r/4$, put $\delta =\| h\| /r$ and $s=2\delta <1/2$. Since $x_0\pm h/\delta \in B$, the cone property gives

\[ sx\pm h=2\delta (x-x_0/2)+(\delta x_0\pm h)\in B. \]

Thus both differences in the order comparison

\[ (1-s)x\ \leq _ B\ x+h\ \leq _ B\ (1+s)x \]

belong to $B$, and all three points belong to $B$. Monotonicity and homogeneity imply

\[ (1-s)^ d f(x)\leq f(x+h)\leq (1+s)^ d f(x). \]

For $d>0$, the mean value theorem on $[1/2,3/2]$ bounds the absolute difference from $f(x)$ by $C_ d s f(x)\leq C\| h\| $, where $C_ d=\max _{u\in [1/2,3/2]}du^{d-1}<\infty $. For $d=0$ the two bounds are equal. Taking both points in a sufficiently small ball about $x_0$ proves the assertion, including the case $h=0$. $\square$

Lemma 10.99.5.D91511968417 The map $P:\mathcal B\to W$ is locally Lipschitz.

Proof. The Kähler cone is nonempty and open in $V$, hence contains a basis $\kappa _1,\ldots ,\kappa _ m$ of $V$: start with a Kähler class, complete it to a basis, and add sufficiently small multiples of the remaining basis vectors to the first class. Apply lemma 10.99.4 to $F_{\kappa _ i}$ on $\mathcal B$. Addition of a big class is an allowed pseudoeffective direction. The perfect pairing identifies the coordinates of $P(\alpha )$ with the finitely many values $F_{\kappa _ i}(\alpha )$, so their local Lipschitz bounds prove the assertion. When $n=1$ this is also immediate from $P(\alpha )=1$. $\square$

Lemma 10.99.6 (Uniform lower variation).64857FC6958D Fix a relatively compact subset $U\Subset \mathcal B$ and a norm on $V$. There is $C_ U\geq 0$ such that, for $x\in U$ and $\| h\| \leq 1$,

082AC53CCDEB 10.99.7
\begin{equation} \operatorname {vol}(x+h)\geq \operatorname {vol}(x) +nP(x)\cdot h-C_ U\| h\| ^2. \end{equation}

Proof. Choose a Kähler class $H$ so large that $H-x$ is Kähler for every $x\in U$ and $H\pm v$ is Kähler whenever $\| v\| =1$. This follows by choosing smooth representatives of a basis of $V$ and dominating the bounded coefficient sets by one multiple of a Kähler form. The class $H$ is fixed for the whole set $U$.

For $x$, choose the same-class approximation of theorem 13.32.3 with

\[ \mu _ j^*x=A_ j+\{ E_ j\} ,\qquad A_ j\text{ Kähler},\quad E_ j\geq 0, \qquad A_ j^ n\longrightarrow \operatorname {vol}(x),\quad (\mu _ j)_*A_ j^{n-1}\longrightarrow P(x). \]

The manifolds $Y_ j$ are compact Kähler. Write $B_ j=\mu _ j^*H$. Then $B_ j-A_ j=\mu _ j^*(H-x)+\{ E_ j\} $ is pseudoeffective. If $h\ne 0$, write $h=\rho v$, where $\rho =\| h\| \leq 1$ and $\| v\| =1$, and put $G_ j=\mu _ j^*(H-v)$. The classes $A_ j,B_ j,G_ j$ are nef, and $2B_ j-G_ j=\mu _ j^*(H+v)$ is nef.

For nef classes, replacing a factor by a larger class in the pseudoeffective order increases any top mixed intersection with the other nef factors. Indeed, pair a positive current in the difference class with Kähler perturbations of the other nef classes and let the perturbations decrease to zero. Consequently

\[ 0\leq A_ j^{n-1-l}B_ j^ lG_ j\leq 2B_ j^ n=2H^ n \qquad (1\leq l\leq n-1). \]

The last equality is the degree-one projection formula.

Modification invariance and pseudoeffective monotonicity of volume, followed by (10.99.3), give

\begin{align*} \operatorname {vol}(x+h) & \geq \operatorname {vol}(A_ j+\rho \mu _ j^*v)\\ & =\operatorname {vol}\bigl ((A_ j+\rho B_ j)-\rho G_ j\bigr )\\ & \geq (A_ j+\rho B_ j)^ n -n\rho (A_ j+\rho B_ j)^{n-1}G_ j\\ & \geq A_ j^ n+n\rho A_ j^{n-1}\mu _ j^*v -2n(2^{n-1}-1)H^ n\rho ^2. \end{align*}

For the last inequality, expand both powers, retain the constant and linear terms, discard the nonnegative higher terms from the first power, and use the displayed bound for each remaining negative term. The coefficient sum is $\sum _{l=1}^{n-1}\binom {n-1}{l}=2^{n-1}-1$; for $n=1$ it is zero. Letting $j\to \infty $ proves (10.99.7) with $C_ U=2n(2^{n-1}-1)H^ n$. The case $h=0$ is immediate. $\square$

Theorem 10.99.8 (Differentiability and local regularity of volume).BF3A71BBF47B The volume is of class $C^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ on $\mathcal B$, and for $\alpha \in \mathcal B$ and $\beta \in V$,

0F43ABDC6E3C 10.99.9
\begin{equation} \left.\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\right|_{t=0} \operatorname {vol}(\alpha +t\beta ) =n\langle \alpha ^{n-1}\rangle \cdot \beta . \end{equation}

Proof. Choose a small ball $U\Subset \mathcal B$ on which $P$ has a Lipschitz constant $L$, and decrease its radius so that differences of points have norm at most one. Put $y=x+h$ with $x,y\in U$. Apply lemma 10.99.6 first at $x$ with $h$, and then at $y$ with $-h$. They give

\begin{align*} -C_ U\| h\| ^2 & \leq \operatorname {vol}(y)-\operatorname {vol}(x)-nP(x)\cdot h\\ & \leq n(P(y)-P(x))\cdot h+C_ U\| h\| ^2 \leq (nL+C_ U)\| h\| ^2. \end{align*}

The remainder is therefore $O(\| h\| ^2)$, uniformly in this ball. This proves actual Fréchet differentiability, with derivative $nP(x)$. Its local Lipschitz continuity follows from lemma 10.99.5, proving the claimed $C^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ regularity and the displayed formula. $\square$

Corollary 10.99.10 (Local Lipschitz continuity on the whole class space).1C86B2A1F465 The volume, extended by zero outside the big cone, is locally Lipschitz on $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$.

Proof. First the extension is continuous at every boundary class $a\in \partial \mathcal E$. Smooth representatives of a basis give a constant $c>0$ such that $c\| z-a\| \omega -(z-a)$ is Kähler for $z\ne a$ near $a$. For big $z$, pseudoeffective monotonicity therefore gives

\[ 0\leq \operatorname {vol}(z) \leq \operatorname {vol}(a+c\| z-a\| \omega )\longrightarrow 0, \]

by the decreasing perturbation limit and zero boundary volume. For non-big $z$ the volume already vanishes. Together with its proved continuity on the big cone and its vanishing outside $\mathcal E$, this proves continuity everywhere without assuming arbitrary boundary continuity in advance.

Fix a bounded convex ball $U\subset V$ and a Kähler basis $\kappa _1,\ldots ,\kappa _ m$. Choose one Kähler class $H$ with $H-z$ Kähler for every $z\in U$. For $z\in U\cap \mathcal B$, the Kähler test monotonicity gives

\[ 0\leq \kappa _ i\cdot P(z)\leq \kappa _ i\cdot H^{n-1}. \]

The perfect pairing thus bounds $\| nP(z)\| _{V^*}$ by a constant $M_ U$ independent of $z$. On any segment from $x$ to $y$ in $U$, the big portion is an interval, by convexity. On its interior the derivative of volume has absolute value at most $M_ U\| y-x\| $. Integrating on closed subintervals and passing to the endpoints by the continuity just proved gives the same bound up to a boundary endpoint. If both endpoints are big, integrate on the whole segment. If exactly one endpoint is big, integrate from it to the first boundary point, where volume is zero. If neither endpoint is big, both volumes are zero. In every case

\[ |\operatorname {vol}(y)-\operatorname {vol}(x)| \leq M_ U\| y-x\| . \]

This proves the asserted local Lipschitz continuity. $\square$

Theorem 10.99.11 (Orthogonality).A4F489BD4673 Every pseudoeffective $\alpha \in V$ satisfies

2CF5B19E1843 10.99.12
\begin{equation} \operatorname {vol}(\alpha ) =\langle \alpha ^{n-1}\rangle \cdot \alpha . \end{equation}

Proof. For a big class, differentiate $\operatorname {vol}((1+t)\alpha )=(1+t)^ n\operatorname {vol}(\alpha )$ at zero and use theorem 10.99.8 with direction $\beta =\alpha $.

For an arbitrary pseudoeffective class, put $\alpha _\varepsilon =\alpha +\varepsilon \omega $ with $\omega $ Kähler. The boundary product is the limit of $P(\alpha _\varepsilon )$. These classes have bounded pairing with $\omega $ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$: choose $H$ with $H-\alpha $ Kähler and use pseudoeffective monotonicity and the mixed-mass bound in the larger fixed class $H+\omega $. Hence

\[ \alpha \cdot P(\alpha _\varepsilon ) =\operatorname {vol}(\alpha _\varepsilon ) -\varepsilon \omega \cdot P(\alpha _\varepsilon ) \longrightarrow \operatorname {vol}(\alpha ), \]

by (10.99.2). Taking the limit on the left gives (10.99.12). $\square$
