10.58 Cup products and differential-resolution comparisons1EFD287D87F1

1EFD287D87F1

Let $R$ be a sheaf of commutative unital rings and $A,B$ sheaves of unital $R$-modules. Their tensor sheaf is the sheafification of $U\mapsto A(U)\otimes _{R(U)}B(U)$. It represents sheafwise balanced bilinear maps and has stalk $A_ x\otimes _{R_ x}B_ x$: a tensor is a finite sum of symbols with finitely many balancing relations, so both its representatives and any relation hold on one common neighborhood after shrinking. This proves the stalk assertion and the universal property directly from sheafification. In particular a tensor sequence is exact if the original sequence splits $R_ x$-linearly at every stalk; no flatness of an arbitrary module sheaf is presumed.

10.58.1 An explicit Godement product5BE58D5EED45

Recall $GE(U)=\prod _{x\in U}E_ x$ and $C^ pE=G^{p+1}E$. Pointwise tensoring gives natural maps

\[ \mu _ m:G^ mA\otimes _ R G^ mB\longrightarrow G^ m(A\otimes _ R B). \]

For $m=0$ this is the identity. Inductively, tensor the two assignments at each point and then apply $G\mu _ m$ to obtain $\mu _{m+1}$. These maps are associative, commute with coefficient maps, and carry two augmentations to the augmentation of the tensor. All these assertions hold pointwise for $m=1$ and then follow by induction. The $R$-action uses the canonical augmentation $R\to G^ mR$.

Write $\eta _ E^{(r)}:E\to G^ rE$ for the iterated augmentation, with $\eta ^{(0)}=1$. For $f\in C^ pA$ and $g\in C^ qB$ define

B28319B90BD6 10.58.2
\begin{equation} f\smile g=\mu _{p+q+1}\bigl ( G^{p+1}\eta _ A^{(q)}(f),\, \eta _{G^{q+1}B}^{(p)}(g)\bigr ). \end{equation}

This is a bilinear sheaf map to $C^{p+q}(A\otimes _ RB)$. The first factor extends the front $p+1$ positions and the second extends the back $q+1$ positions, sharing their endpoint. Equivalently, in the nested assignment description it tensors the front value, continued by its germ to the final position, with the back value. Formula equation 10.58.2 makes that continuation precise without choosing global representatives of germs.

Theorem 10.58.3.16CBD1C5A6C8 For nonempty closed support families $\Phi ,\Psi $ as in section 10.56, this product induces natural $R(X)$-bilinear maps

\[ H_\Phi ^ p(X,A)\times H_\Psi ^ q(X,B) \longrightarrow H_{\Phi \cap \Psi }^{p+q}(X,A\otimes _ RB). \]

They are associative, unital for the coefficient ring, and graded commutative under the tensor flip: $\beta \smile \alpha =(-1)^{pq}\tau (\alpha \smile \beta )$.

Suppose $0\to B\to B'\to B''\to 0$ and its tensor sequence with $A$ are exact. Their supported connecting maps satisfy

\[ \partial (\alpha \smile \beta '')=(-1)^ p\alpha \smile \partial \beta '', \qquad \partial (\beta ''\smile \alpha )=(\partial \beta '')\smile \alpha . \]

The coefficient and support maps in these formulas are the indicated tensor maps and the intersection family.

Proof. Let $\delta _ i^ p=G^ i\eta _{G^{p+1-i}}$ be the Godement cofaces. Expansion of the front/back formula has the same cancellation as the alternating face rule: for a product of degrees $p,q$, the cofaces numbered $i\le p$ are $(\delta _ i^ pf)\smile g$, and those numbered $i\ge p+1$ are $f\smile \delta _{i-p}^ qg$. The two extra middle terms in $(d_ Gf)\smile g+(-1)^ pf\smile d_ Gg$ are $(\delta _{p+1}^ pf)\smile g$ and $f\smile \delta _0^ qg$. They are equal, since both insert the shared-endpoint augmentation, and their signs are opposite. These coface identities follow from naturality of $\eta $ and its compatibility with $\mu _ m$; alternatively each side omits the same position of a nested assignment. Hence

9E20CC3BE2A6 10.58.4
\begin{equation} d_ G(f\smile g)=d_ Gf\smile g+(-1)^ pf\smile d_ Gg. \end{equation}

Both parenthesizations of a triple product extend the three factors to the same front, middle and back blocks and apply the associative pointwise tensor map. They are thus equal already on cochains. The global unit is the augmented section $1$ and has the unit property by the same formula.

Because cup is a bilinear sheaf map, its support is contained in the intersection of the two closed supports. A closed subset of that intersection belongs to both families. The Leibniz identity shows that cocycles multiply to cocycles and that changing a closed factor by a boundary changes the product by a boundary. This constructs the supported maps, with associativity, unit and naturality just proved.

For the first connecting identity choose supported Godement cocycles $f,g''$ and a lift $g'$ of $g''$ in the $B'$ complex, with the same allowed support. Such a lift exists by exactness of supported Godement degree functors in theorem 10.56.1. Then $d_ Gg'$ represents $\partial \beta ''$, while equation 10.58.4 makes the derivative of the product lift $f\smile g'$ equal to $(-1)^ pf\smile d_ Gg'$. This is precisely the defining connecting representative in the tensor sequence. If the lifted factor is on the left, the other factor is closed and the derivative is $d_ Gg'\smile f$, giving the second identity without an additional sign.

It remains to prove graded commutativity. The map $B\to GB$ splits $R_ x$-linearly on each stalk by evaluation at $x$: evaluation retracts augmentation and respects the action of $R_ x$. Consequently tensoring $0\to B\to GB\to Q\to 0$ by $A$ remains exact. Since $GB$ is acyclic for every support family, each $\beta \in H_\Psi ^ q(X,B)$, $q\ge 1$, is $\partial \gamma $ for some $\gamma \in H_\Psi ^{q-1}(X,Q)$. Induct on $p+q$. The degree-zero assertion is the ordinary tensor flip of sections. When $q>0$, the two connecting identities and the induction hypothesis give

\[ \beta \smile \alpha =\partial (\gamma \smile \alpha ) =(-1)^{p(q-1)}\tau \partial (\alpha \smile \gamma ) =(-1)^{pq}\tau (\alpha \smile \beta ). \]

If $q=0<p$, exchange the two factors and use the already proved case with a positive second degree. This completes the induction, including arbitrary supports and torsion coefficients. $\square$

Every bilinear coefficient morphism $A\otimes _ RB\to E$ now gives the corresponding contraction of cohomology classes by composition. In particular $H^\bullet (X,R)$ is a graded-commutative ring and $H_\Phi ^\bullet (X,A)$ is its graded module; two supported factors have support in the intersection family. These statements do not define a product of two arbitrary currents.

10.58.5 Čech comparison and pullbackA7D1721B871F

Proposition 10.58.6.501BF07CE068 For any open cover, the ordered-tuple Čech cup product is

\[ (c\smile e)_{i_0\ldots i_{a+a'}} =c_{i_0\ldots i_ a}\otimes e_{i_ a\ldots i_{a+a'}}, \]

restricted to the common intersection. The canonical Čech-to- Godement comparison preserves this product. On locally compact paracompact spaces this also holds for compact-supported Čech cohomology and its mixed product with ordinary cohomology.

Proof. The alternating coboundary has the Leibniz identity by the same front/back middle-term cancellation. In the comparison total complexes $T_ A^{a,b}=C^ a(\mathcal U,C^ bA)$, put

\[ (c\odot e)_{i_0\ldots i_{a+a'}} =(-1)^{ba'}c_{i_0\ldots i_ a}\smile _ G e_{i_ a\ldots i_{a+a'}}. \]

Its total degree is $(a+b)+(a'+b')$. Expanding the two Leibniz identities shows that $D=\delta +(-1)^ a d_ G$ satisfies $D(c\odot e)=Dc\odot e+(-1)^{a+b}c\odot De$. Indeed the horizontal second term acquires $(a+b)+b(a'+1)\equiv a+ba'$, and the vertical first and second terms acquire $a+a'+ba'$ and $a+a'+ba'+b$, respectively, exactly the signs from differentiating the displayed formula. The horizontal augmentation from global Godement sections and the vertical augmentation from Čech cochains both preserve products: the former has $a=a'=0$, the latter $b=b'=0$, and $\mu $ respects augmentation. The horizontal augmentation is a cohomology isomorphism by theorem 10.43.16; therefore the comparison obtained by inverting it preserves products.

For compact supports choose locally finite relatively compact covers as in theorem 10.56.9. A product with one finite cochain is finite: all its additional indices meet the compact union of the active member closures, hence belong to a finite set. The total product thus also preserves the compact total subcomplex when either factor is compact. The two same augmentations, using the compact comparison for that factor and the ordinary comparison for the other, prove the assertion. Refinement compatibility then gives the direct-limit statement, including contraction by coefficient maps. $\square$

For $f:X\to Y$ and a support family $\Psi $ on $Y$, let $f^{-1}\Psi $ be the closed subsets of $X$ contained in $f^{-1}K$ for some $K\in \Psi $. This is again a nonempty support family.

Proposition 10.58.7.6F68DD8223F3 The Godement pullback constructed in theorem 10.55.6 induces

\[ f^*:H_\Psi ^ q(Y,A)\longrightarrow H_{f^{-1}\Psi }^ q(X,f^{-1}A). \]

It is functorial, preserves cup products and contractions, and commutes with coefficient connecting maps. The ordinary Čech pullback on inverse-image covers agrees with it under comparison. For proper maps between locally compact Hausdorff spaces it preserves compact supports.

For sheaves over a fixed commutative ring, define $A\boxtimes _ R B=\operatorname {pr}_1^{-1}A\otimes _ R \operatorname {pr}_2^{-1}B$. Its stalk is $A_ x\otimes _ RB_ y$. The external product $\alpha \times \beta = \operatorname {pr}_1^*\alpha \smile \operatorname {pr}_2^*\beta $ has support family $\Phi \times \Psi $, the closed subsets contained in some $K\times L$, $K\in \Phi ,L\in \Psi $. It obeys

\[ (\alpha \times \beta )\smile (\alpha '\times \beta ') =(-1)^{q p'}(\alpha \smile \alpha ')\times (\beta \smile \beta '), \]

for degrees $p,q,p',q'$, with the canonical regrouping of tensor factors. It also obeys $(f\times g)^*(\alpha \times \beta )=f^*\alpha \times g^*\beta $.

Proof. Recall the explicit map $\lambda _ A:f^{-1}G_ YA\to G_ Xf^{-1}A$: an inverse-image germ of an assignment is evaluated at $f(x)$. Its iterates commute with every insertion of $\eta $. Pointwise evaluation also commutes with $\mu _ m$, so equation 10.58.2 commutes with pullback. Iterated pullbacks evaluate at the same composite points, proving functoriality. The pullback of a supported cochain has support contained in the inverse image of its closed support, since it is a sheaf map following inverse image. It therefore belongs to the stated family. The exact supported Godement degree functors and exact inverse image give the coefficient LES diagrams, so the connecting maps commute.

Apply the same $\lambda $ on each intersection of an inverse-image cover. It gives a map of the two comparison total complexes commuting with both augmentations; the usual Čech section pullback is its other edge. This proves compatibility. A proper map has compact inverse images of compact sets, giving the last support assertion. In the locally compact paracompact setting the pullback of an admissible compact cover is again locally finite and relatively compact when the map is proper, so the compact Čech description agrees as well.

The external tensor stalk is the inverse-image stalk formula and the tensor stalk formula above. The intersection of the two pulled-back support families is exactly $\Phi \times \Psi $: their two containing cylinders intersect in $K\times L$. This family is closed under finite unions, since two such products are contained in $(K_1\cup K_2)\times (L_1\cup L_2)$. The mixed sign is obtained by interchanging just $\operatorname {pr}_2^*\beta $ and $\operatorname {pr}_1^*\alpha '$, using the proved graded commutativity. Pullback functoriality and its product compatibility give the final formula. No Künneth isomorphism is inferred. $\square$

10.58.8 Multiplicative acyclic resolutions and their signs5514B60CD71A

Suppose $L,M,N$ are nonnegative sheaf complexes resolving $A,B,E$, with a bilinear pairing $L^ r\otimes M^ s\to N^{r+s}$ extending $A\otimes B\to E$ and satisfying the internal Leibniz rule $d(uv)=(du)v+(-1)^ ru(dv)$. Suppose their terms are respectively acyclic for $\Phi ,\Psi ,\Phi \cap \Psi $.

Theorem 10.58.9.3E96DCA67FB2 There are augmentational comparison isomorphisms

\[ \Theta _\Phi ^ n:H_\Phi ^ n(X,A) \longrightarrow H^ n(\Gamma _\Phi (X,L^\bullet )) \]

and likewise for $B,M$ and $E,N$, preserving the indicated pairings. If $\Psi _ n$ denotes the successive positive connecting-map comparison from the resolution section cohomology to sheaf cohomology, then

499EBF22C09F 10.58.10
\begin{equation} (\Theta _\Phi ^ n)^{-1}=\epsilon _ n\Psi _ n, \qquad \epsilon _ n=(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}. \end{equation}

Thus for the positive convention itself,

\[ \Psi _{r+s}([u v])=(-1)^{rs}\Psi _ r[u]\smile \Psi _ s[v], \]

with the specified coefficient pairing. The positive convention is retained, rather than silently declared multiplicative.

Proof. Form the finite-diagonal total complexes $\Gamma _\Phi (X,C^ aL^ r)$ with $D=d_ G+(-1)^ a d_ L$, and likewise for $M,N$. The maps from $\Gamma _\Phi C^\bullet A$ by resolution augmentation and from $\Gamma _\Phi L^\bullet $ by Godement germs are cochain maps, denoted $j_ A,j_ L$. The former is a cohomology isomorphism because $\Gamma _\Phi C^ a-$ is exact; the latter is one because the $L^ r$ are $\Phi $-acyclic. Apply the exact-augmented-row/ column proof of lemma 10.43.15; every total diagonal is finite and its proof works with these supported groups without alteration. Define $\Theta =j_ L^{-1}j_ A$.

On elements of bidegrees $(a,r)$ and $(b,s)$ set

\[ u\star v=(-1)^{rb}(u\smile _ G v), \]

followed by the internal coefficient pairing. The two Leibniz rules give $D(u\star v)=Du\star v+(-1)^{a+r}u\star Dv$. For example the internal first term has sign $a+b+rb$, and the internal second sign $a+b+rb+r$; the horizontal second has sign $a+rb$. These are exactly the signs obtained by differentiating the displayed product. Supports lie in the intersection family. Both $j_ A$ and $j_ L$ preserve pairings: their other bidegree is zero and the pairings respect augmentations. Inverting their cohomology isomorphisms proves multiplicativity of $\Theta $.

The sign in equation 10.58.10 is also the finite zigzag calculation of equation 10.55.4, now using the exact supported degree functors. Explicitly, for a closed supported $z\in L^ n(X)$ choose supported lifts $a_ j\in \Gamma _\Phi (X,C^ jL^{n-1-j})$ with $d_ La_0=\eta z$ and $d_ La_ j=d_ Ga_{j-1}$. The positive comparison is represented by $d_ Ga_{n-1}$. The relations $Da_ j=d_ Ga_ j+(-1)^ jd_ La_ j$ give $[\eta z]=\epsilon _ n[d_ Ga_{n-1}]$ in the total cohomology; for $n=0$ both maps are the kernel-section identity. This proves the formula with the existing normalization. Finally $\epsilon _{r+s}\epsilon _ r\epsilon _ s=(-1)^{rs}$, giving the last displayed relation. $\square$

Corollary 10.58.11.45A6B240A010 On a paracompact smooth manifold, ordinary de Rham comparison

\[ \Theta :H^ q(X,\underline{\mathbb R})\longrightarrow H^ q_{\rm dR}(X) \]

preserves products. This remains true for compact supports and for the compact/ordinary mixed product, with wedge on representatives. It commutes with smooth pullback, including compact supports for proper maps.

On a complex manifold let $E,F$ be holomorphic bundles. Write

\[ \Theta _ p^ q:H^ q(X,\Omega _ X^ p\otimes \mathcal O(E)) \longrightarrow H_{\bar\partial }^{p,q}(X,E) \]

for the same augmentational comparison with the actual full-type $\bar\partial $ resolution. For $\alpha $ of type $(p,q)$ and $\beta $ of type $(r,s)$ on the analytic side, coefficient tensoring and holomorphic exterior product on the sheaf side satisfy

8E59D425DD0E 10.58.12
\begin{equation} \Theta _{p+r}^{q+s}\bigl ((\Theta _ p^ q)^{-1}\alpha \smile (\Theta _ r^ s)^{-1}\beta \bigr ) =(-1)^{ps}[\alpha \wedge \beta ]. \end{equation}

The formula holds for compact/mixed supports and after a holomorphic bundle contraction. The comparisons commute with holomorphic pullback.

Proof. Use the local differential resolutions and supported acyclicity of corollary 10.57.1, and their ordinary versions in corollary 10.43.11. The real exterior complex is a differential graded algebra, so the preceding theorem applies directly with wedge. Its compact and ordinary terms have the required acyclicity.

For full-type Dolbeault forms the actual formula is

\[ \bar\partial (\alpha \wedge \beta ) =\bar\partial \alpha \wedge \beta +(-1)^{p+q}\alpha \wedge \bar\partial \beta . \]

The resolution degree is $q$, not $p+q$. Its chain pairing must therefore be $\alpha \circ \beta =(-1)^{ps}\alpha \wedge \beta $: the sign of the second differentiated term is then $ps+p+q=q+p(s+1)$, as required by the degree-$q$ Leibniz rule. This pairing is associative, since $ps+(p+r)t=rt+p(s+t)$, and at resolution degree zero it is the ordinary holomorphic coefficient wedge. Apply the theorem to this precise chain pairing to get equation 10.58.12. The same proof works in holomorphic frames for bundle coefficients and their contractions. It multiplies smooth forms only; arbitrary current-current wedge products are not used.

For naturality, use the explicit Godement pullback $\lambda $ on the two resolution total complexes and the usual smooth pullback of forms. They commute with $D$, products and both augmentations, since pullback commutes with exterior differentiation and wedge. Inverting the two augmentation isomorphisms proves the assertion. For Dolbeault use a holomorphic map, so the pulled-back coefficients and differentials have the specified types and $\bar\partial $ commutes with pullback. Properness ensures compact supports as proved above. No pullback of an arbitrary current by an arbitrary smooth map is asserted. $\square$

Proposition 10.58.13.FF9CB82C72A2 For a locally finite cover of a smooth manifold with a subordinate smooth partition $(\rho _ i)$, a constant-sheaf Čech $n$-cocycle $c$ has the de Rham representative

\[ \widetilde\Theta _ n(c)= \sum _{i_0,\ldots ,i_ n}c_{i_0\ldots i_ n}\rho _{i_ n} d\rho _{i_0}\wedge \cdots \wedge d\rho _{i_{n-1}}. \]

Each term extends by zero from its common intersection; the sum is locally finite by subordination and local finiteness of the cover. For $n=0$ this is the usual partition gluing. On cohomology its normalization is

\[ \widetilde\Theta _ n=(-1)^ n\Theta _ n =(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\Psi _ n^{-1}. \]

It too preserves products and smooth pullback. This is the partition normalization, distinct from the augmentational $\Theta $ fixed above. For finite cochains on relatively compact covers it preserves compact support as well.

Proof. Let $h$ be partition contraction, inserting an index in the first position. In the differential resolution, a closed cochain $b$ with values in its kernel sheaf satisfies $\delta (hb)=b$; therefore $d(hb)$ is a cocycle in the next kernel sheaf. Its positive connecting image is $b$, because $hb$ is an actual local lift and its Čech coboundary is $b$. This uses precisely the connecting compatibility already proved in theorem 10.44.12, not cochain surjectivity on an arbitrary fixed cover. Repeat this operation $n$ times and glue the final degree-zero cocycle by the partition. Each differential hits the newly inserted partition coefficient, since the kernel-valued cochain is $d$-closed. Moving that new $d\rho $ past the existing $t-1$ form differentials contributes $(-1)^{t-1}$ at stage $t$. Their product is $(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}$ and the resulting ordered expression is the displayed formula. Thus it equals that sign times $\Psi _ n^{-1}$. Combine with equation 10.58.10 to obtain $\widetilde\Theta _ n=(-1)^ n\Theta _ n$. The factor $(-1)^ n$ is multiplicative in total degree and commutes with pullback, proving the assertions independently of any good-cover construction. If $c$ is finite, every term is supported in the compact union of its active member closures, proving the compact assertion. $\square$
