10.56 Sheaf cohomology with prescribed supports3F59A99D55BB

3F59A99D55BB

A family of supports on a space $X$ is a nonempty collection $\Phi $ of closed subsets, closed under finite unions and passage to closed subsets. In particular $\varnothing \in \Phi $. For a sheaf $F$ of abelian groups put

\[ \Gamma _\Phi (X,F)=\{ s\in F(X):\operatorname {supp}s\in \Phi \} , \qquad \operatorname {supp}s=X\setminus \{ x:s_ x=0\} . \]

The zero-germ locus is open, so this support is closed. The support of a sum is contained in the union of the two supports, proving that $\Gamma _\Phi $ is an additive left-exact functor. For any subset $S\subset X$, the closed subsets contained in $S$ form a family, denoted $\Phi _ S$. All closed sets give ordinary global sections. The closed compact subsets form a family $c$ on any space: a closed subset of a compact space is compact, and finite unions are compact. On a Hausdorff space this is simply the family of all compact subsets. Closedness cannot be omitted in general: in the Sierpiński space with opens $\varnothing ,\{ 1\} ,\{ 0,1\} $ the compact subset $\{ 1\} $ is not closed.

Use the exact Godement resolution of theorem 10.43.5, with $C^ qF=G^{q+1}F$ and differential $d_ G$. Define

\[ H^ q_\Phi (X,F)=H^ q\bigl (\Gamma _\Phi (X,C^\bullet F),d_ G\bigr ) \quad (q\ge 0),\qquad H^ q_\Phi (X,F)=0\quad (q<0). \]

Write $H^ q_ c$ for compact supports and $H^ q_ S$ for $\Phi _ S$. This latter group concerns supports in $S$; it is not the ordinary cohomology of the restricted sheaf on $S$.

Theorem 10.56.1.391CCB0E8947 One has $H^0_\Phi (X,F)=\Gamma _\Phi (X,F)$. A short exact sequence of coefficient sheaves gives a natural long exact sequence of $H^ q_\Phi $. Inclusion of support families gives natural maps commuting with these sequences. Every flabby sheaf is $\Phi $-acyclic, for every family $\Phi $ on every space.

More precisely, if $0\to A\to B\to Q\to 0$ has flabby kernel, every section of $Q$ lifts to a section of $B$ with support contained in the support of the given section.

Proof. For a sheaf $E$, $GE(X)=\prod _{x\in X}E_ x$. An assignment with support in a closed $K$ is zero at every point outside $K$. Conversely an assignment zero there is a section zero on $X\setminus K$, so its support is contained in $K$. Given a surjection of sheaves, lift each nonzero coordinate and choose zero coordinates outside $K$. This proves that $\Gamma _\Phi (X,G-)$ is exact. Since $G$ itself is exact, every $\Gamma _\Phi (X,C^ q-)$ is exact. Thus a short exact coefficient sequence gives a degreewise short exact sequence of the defining complexes. Apply lemma 10.43.3. Its representative construction proves naturality, including for inclusions of support families. The kernel in degree zero consists of actual sections of $F$; its support in $C^0F$ equals its support in $F$ because augmentation is injective on stalks. This proves $H^0$.

For the asserted lift let $s\in Q(X)$, $K=\operatorname {supp}s$, and start with its zero lift on $X\setminus K$. Order extensions of this partial lift by extension. Gluing gives upper bounds for chains. If a maximal domain misses $x$, take a local lift $b_0$ near $x$. Its difference from the previous lift on the overlap is a section of $A$, by left exactness. Flabbiness extends that difference to the whole new neighborhood; correcting $b_0$ and gluing enlarges the domain. Hence the domain is $X$. The lift is zero on $X\setminus K$, so its support is contained in $K$.

For flabby $A$, the kernels in its Godement resolution are flabby: the quotient of a flabby sheaf by a flabby subsheaf is flabby, by the ordinary lifting proof in theorem 10.43.7. Each short kernel sequence is therefore exact after $\Gamma _\Phi $ by the just-proved support-preserving lift. This makes the supported Godement section complex exact in every positive degree. $\square$

Call a support family enlargable if for every $K\in \Phi $ there is $L\in \Phi $ with $K\subset \operatorname {int}L$. All paracompact spaces below are Hausdorff, as in section 10.44.

Theorem 10.56.2.DE3B76CEB62B Let $X$ be paracompact and $\Phi $ enlargable. If $0\to A\to B\to Q\to 0$ has soft kernel, then $\Gamma _\Phi (X,B)\to \Gamma _\Phi (X,Q)$ is onto. Every soft sheaf on $X$ is $\Phi $-acyclic.

Proof. Let $s$ have support $K\in \Phi $ and choose $L\in \Phi $ with $K\subset \operatorname {int}L$. By theorem 10.44.7 it has a global lift $b$. On the closed set $W=X\setminus \operatorname {int}L$ its image is zero, and exact inverse image and left exact sections give an $A$-section equal to $b|_ W$. Softness extends that section to $a\in A(X)$. Then $b-a$ is a lift of $s$ and its germ is zero at every point of $W$. Its support lies in $\operatorname {int}L$, hence is a closed subset of $L$ and belongs to $\Phi $. This argument allows enlargement from $K$ to $L$; it does not assert the exact-support property of the flabby case.

Flabby sheaves are soft on a paracompact space, and quotients of soft sheaves by soft subsheaves are soft, by theorem 10.44.7. Starting with a soft $A$, the kernel sequences of its Godement resolution therefore have soft kernels in every degree. The lifting just proved makes them exact after $\Gamma _\Phi $. This proves positive-degree acyclicity. $\square$

Theorem 10.56.3.7C0750CA5296 Let $0\to F\to L^0\to L^1\to \cdots $ be an exact augmented sheaf complex whose terms are $\Phi $-acyclic. Then successive positive connecting maps give natural isomorphisms

\[ H^ q\bigl (\Gamma _\Phi (X,L^\bullet )\bigr )\simeq H^ q_\Phi (X,F). \]

They commute with augmented complex morphisms. In particular an identity-augmenting morphism between two such resolutions gives an isomorphism on supported section cohomology.

Proof. Put $Z^0=F$ and $Z^ j=\ker (L^ j\to L^{j+1})$ for $j\ge 1$. The exact sequences $0\to Z^{j-1}\to L^{j-1}\to Z^ j\to 0$ and the supported long exact sequence identify

\[ \Gamma _\Phi Z^ q/d\Gamma _\Phi L^{q-1} \simeq H^1_\Phi (X,Z^{q-1}) \simeq \cdots \simeq H^ q_\Phi (X,F)\quad (q\ge 1). \]

The first step is a cokernel, and every subsequent step uses positive cohomological degree and acyclicity of the relevant $L^{j-1}$. The left side is the cohomology of the supported section complex, by left exactness of $\Gamma _\Phi $. For $q=0$ its kernel is simply $\Gamma _\Phi F$. Naturality of the connecting representatives proves the assertion about morphisms. This uses the same positive convention as theorem 10.43.8. $\square$

10.56.4 Compact supports and extension by zero7680079A38BC

Lemma 10.56.5.899BD7174FB8 A locally compact Hausdorff space is regular. Every compact subset has an open neighborhood with compact closure, and such neighborhoods are cofinal among its open neighborhoods. Every compact subspace is strongly paracompact in the ambient space. Restriction of a flabby sheaf to a compact subspace is soft. In particular, a second-countable locally compact Hausdorff space is paracompact.

Proof. For regularity choose a compact neighborhood $N$ of $x$ and an open set $O$ containing $x$. The compact Hausdorff space $N$ is paracompact (finite subcovers are locally finite), hence regular by lemma 10.44.2. Inside $N$ choose a relative open neighborhood of $x$ whose relative closure lies in $O\cap \operatorname {int}_ X N$. That neighborhood is open in $X$, and its ambient closure is the same compact relative closure. This proves regularity and arbitrarily small relatively compact point neighborhoods. Finite unions give the assertion for compact subsets.

For a compact $K$ and any ambient open cover, choose at each point an ambient open neighborhood with closure in a prescribed member; finitely many cover $K$. This is exactly the strong-paracompact refinement condition, with $W=X$. If $A$ is flabby, a section on a closed subset of $K$ extends to an ambient neighborhood by theorem 10.44.4, since that subset is compact. Flabbiness extends it to $X$, then restrict to $K$. Thus $A|_ K$ is soft.

For the final assertion choose countably many relatively compact basis members covering $X$. Repeatedly enlarge finite unions of their compact closures to relatively compact open sets, obtaining $V_ j$ with $\overline{V_ j}\subset V_{j+1}$ and $\bigcup V_ j=X$. Given any open cover, cover each compact annulus $\overline{V_ j}\setminus V_{j-1}$ by finitely many open sets subordinate to it, with closures contained also in $V_{j+1}\setminus \overline{V_{j-2}}$; take $V_ j=\varnothing $ for $j\le 0$. Regularity and compactness give these finite families. Together they cover $X$ and are locally finite: a neighborhood $V_ N$ misses every family with $j\ge N+2$. This is the required locally finite refinement. $\square$

Consequently compact supports are enlargable on a locally compact Hausdorff space. On a locally compact paracompact space soft sheaves are therefore compact-support acyclic.

For an open $U\subset X$, let $F_ U$ denote the extension-by-zero subsheaf of $F$: on an open $V$ its sections are those sections of $F(V)$ whose germs vanish at every point of $V\setminus U$. Its stalk is $F_ x$ for $x\in U$ and zero otherwise. Thus $F\mapsto F_ U$ is exact, as can be checked on stalks.

Proposition 10.56.6.984908A81943 On a paracompact space, extension by zero $A_ U$ of a soft sheaf $A$ is soft. For a closed subspace $Z$ of a locally compact paracompact space $X$, with $U=X\setminus Z$, there is a natural long exact sequence

\[ \cdots \to H_ c^ q(U,F|_ U)\to H_ c^ q(X,F) \to H_ c^ q(Z,F|_ Z)\to H_ c^{q+1}(U,F|_ U)\to \cdots . \]

For any open cover $X=U\cup V$ there is the compact-support Mayer–Vietoris sequence

\[ \cdots \to H_ c^ q(U\cap V,F)\to H_ c^ q(U,F)\oplus H_ c^ q(V,F) \to H_ c^ q(X,F)\to H_ c^{q+1}(U\cap V,F)\to \cdots . \]

Maps from an open subset are extension maps; arbitrary restriction from $X$ to an open subset need not preserve compact support.

Proof. A section of $A_ U$ on a closed $S$ gives an $A$-section there whose germs vanish on $S\setminus U$. Paste it with the zero section on $X\setminus U$, using the closed-germ-pasting argument in section 10.44. Softness of $A$ extends this section on $S\cup (X\setminus U)$ to $X$. Its germs vanish outside $U$, so the extension lies in $A_ U(X)$. This proves softness of $A_ U$.

We first identify the groups needed in these sequences. A compactly supported section on $U$ extends by zero: its compact support is closed in the Hausdorff space $X$, and one glues on $U$ and the open complement of that support. Conversely a compactly supported section of $F_ U$ on $X$ has compact support contained in $U$. Hence these section groups agree. Apply this to the resolution $(C^\bullet F)_ U$ of $F_ U$. Its terms are soft on $X$ and compact-support acyclic. On $U$ the terms $C^ qF|_ U$ are flabby (the Godement construction commutes with open restriction), hence compact-support acyclic there. This does not require an arbitrary open subset of $X$ to be paracompact, or an arbitrary open restriction of a soft sheaf to be soft. The resolution comparison therefore gives

\[ H_ c^ q(X,F_ U)\simeq H_ c^ q(U,F|_ U). \]

These identifications commute with the inclusion of open sets, by the same augmented-resolution maps.

For the closed inclusion $i:Z\to X$, direct image is exact: its stalk at a point of $Z$ is the original stalk, and its stalk outside $Z$ is zero. It preserves flabby sheaves. A flabby resolution on $Z$ therefore pushes to a flabby resolution on $X$. Compact section groups agree under this pushforward, since compact subsets of $Z$ are exactly compact subsets of $X$ contained in $Z$. Thus $H_ c^ q(X,i_*F|_ Z)=H_ c^ q(Z,F|_ Z)$. The stalkwise exact sequence $0\to F_ U\to F\to i_*F|_ Z\to 0$ gives the first long exact sequence. No extension theorem for arbitrary sections across $Z$ is asserted: surjectivity of the sheaf map is a stalkwise assertion.

Finally the sequence

\[ 0\to F_{U\cap V}\xrightarrow {s\mapsto (s,-s)}F_ U\oplus F_ V \xrightarrow {(a,b)\mapsto a+b}F\to 0 \]

is exact on every stalk because $U\cup V=X$. The supported long exact sequence and the open identifications just proved give the displayed Mayer–Vietoris sequence with its stated signs. $\square$

Theorem 10.56.7.2E236442C324 Let $X$ be locally compact Hausdorff and $F$ a sheaf. Then

\[ H_ c^ q(X,F)\simeq \varinjlim _{U\Subset X} H^ q(\overline U,F_ U|_{\overline U}), \]

where $U\Subset X$ means open with compact closure. Transition maps are extension by zero, represented by the complexes in the proof. The closed subspace $\overline U$ is essential in this formula.

Proof. The exact extension-by-zero functor and exact closed restriction make $(C^\bullet F)_ U|_{\overline U}$ a resolution of $F_ U|_{\overline U}$. Each $C^ qF$ is a module over the unital ring sheaf $C^ q\mathbb Z=G^{q+1}\underline{\mathbb Z}$, by iterating the pointwise product and module actions of $G$. Thus $(C^ qF)_ U|_{\overline U}$ is a module over $(C^ q\mathbb Z)|_{\overline U}$. This ring is soft by lemma 10.56.5, since $C^ q\mathbb Z$ is flabby. The compact Hausdorff space $\overline U$ is paracompact, so corollary 10.44.9 makes these module terms acyclic for ordinary cohomology there.

A section of $(C^ qF)_ U$ on $\overline U$ has closed support in that compact subspace and zero germs on $\overline U\setminus U$. Its support is consequently a compact subset of $U$. Restriction to $U$ followed by zero extension identifies it with a global $C^ qF$-section supported in $U$. Conversely any such section restricts to the prescribed sheaf on $\overline U$. The resolution comparison therefore identifies the displayed ordinary cohomology group with the cohomology of this global supported subcomplex.

These subcomplexes increase under $U\subset V$, and their union is $\Gamma _ c(X,C^\bullet F)$, since every compact subset lies inside some relatively compact open set. Cohomology commutes with this directed union: a cycle is a cycle at one stage, and an equality to a boundary has its primitive at a later stage. This proves the isomorphism and defines its natural extension transition maps. No equality between $(C^ qF)_ U$ and $C^ q(F_ U)$ is used. $\square$

10.56.8 Compactly supported Čech cochainsB519BE6F26DA

Let $X$ be locally compact paracompact. Covers in this subsection are locally finite covers $\mathcal U=(U_ i)$ by relatively compact open sets. They are cofinal among open covers: choose relatively compact point neighborhoods subordinate to a given cover, then take a locally finite refinement, whose members still have compact closures. Common refinements of the same kind exist by the same argument.

Use all ordered index tuples, including repetitions, as in theorem 10.43.16. Let $C_ c^ a(\mathcal U,F)$ be the subgroup of cochains with only finitely many nonzero coefficient sections. It is a subcomplex. Indeed, for each nonzero tuple a nonzero coefficient of its coboundary can add only an index whose open set meets the tuple's intersection; all such indices meet the compact closure of one member of that tuple, hence are finite by local finiteness. There are only finitely many original tuples. Refinement maps preserve finite support: every refined index in a nonzero pulled-back tuple meets the compact union of the closures of the finitely many original active members, and only finitely many refined members do so. The refinement prisms have the same property. Thus the usual refinement homotopies define the groups

\[ \check H_ c^ q(\mathcal U,F)=H^ q(C_ c^\bullet (\mathcal U,F)), \qquad \check H_ c^ q(X,F)=\varinjlim _{\mathcal U}\check H_ c^ q(\mathcal U,F). \]

Theorem 10.56.9.C9EFB9BEE0D7 On a locally compact paracompact space there are natural fixed-cover comparison maps $\check H_ c^ q(\mathcal U,F)\to H_ c^ q(X,F)$, compatible with refinement. On direct limits they commute with the coefficient connecting maps and induce isomorphisms

\[ \check H_ c^ q(X,F)\simeq H_ c^ q(X,F). \]

No good-cover or separability assumption is needed.

Proof. Use the double complex $C_ c^ a(\mathcal U,C^ bF)$ with differential $D=\delta +(-1)^ a d_ G$. Its horizontal augmentation is $\Gamma _ c(X,C^ bF)$. The ring $C^ b\mathbb Z$ is flabby, hence soft on $X$, so it has a locally finite partition of $1$ subordinate to $\mathcal U$ by corollary 10.44.9. Writing this partition as $(\rho _ i)$, the usual contraction is

\[ (hc)_{i_0\ldots i_{a-1}}= \sum _ j\rho _ j c_{j i_0\ldots i_{a-1}}. \]

Products on an intersection extend by zero because $\operatorname {supp}\rho _ j\subset U_ j$. The identity $\delta h+h\delta =1$ follows by expanding the alternating sum and using $\sum \rho _ j=1$; it is the same identity as in lemma 10.43.14. For a finite cochain the output has only tuples obtained by deleting the first index of an active tuple, so remains finite. In augmentation degree, a compactly supported section meets only finitely many $U_ i$, so its restrictions, and its partition components, are finite as well. Degree-zero cocycles glue to a global section supported in the compact union of the closures of their active members. Conversely a compactly supported global section restricts to finitely many nonzero members. Thus the horizontal augmented rows are exact.

By lemma 10.43.15, the horizontal augmentation identifies the cohomology of this finite-diagonal total complex with $H_ c^ q(X,F)$. The vertical sheaf augmentation from $C_ c^\bullet (\mathcal U,F)$ gives the fixed-cover comparison by this isomorphism. The construction commutes with refinement up to the same finite-support-preserving prism as above.

We check the coefficient exact sequence rather than assuming cochain lifts on a fixed cover. For $0\to A\to B\to Q\to 0$, apply lemma 10.44.11 to a finite $Q$-cochain. Its pullback has a lift after a locally finite refinement. The refinement still consists of relatively compact sets. Choose the zero lift whenever the corresponding original coefficient is zero. Every potentially nonzero refined tuple then uses only members meeting the compact union of the original active closures, so there are only finitely many such tuples. The lift is a finite cochain. This also applies to a finite bounding cochain. Consequently the liftable-cochain argument of theorem 10.44.12 applies with finite cochains: a class is representable by a liftable cocycle after refinement, and a liftable class which bounds becomes a boundary of a liftable cochain after another refinement. Filtered limits are exact by the eventual-zero representative argument. This constructs the natural long exact sequence of $\check H_ c^ q(X,-)$.

Compatibility with the supported Godement connecting maps follows from actual short exact total complexes: every $\Gamma (U_{i_0\ldots i_ a},C^ b-)$ is exact, and taking the algebraic direct sum over tuples, with finite support for each element, preserves exactness. The liftable finite-Čech complexes map to these totals by vertical augmentation, while the short exact supported Godement section complexes map by horizontal augmentation. Both commute with coefficient maps. The elementary long-exact-sequence naturality of lemma 10.43.3 in these two diagrams proves the asserted compatibility, with the unchanged total sign.

In degree zero the comparison is the gluing isomorphism to $\Gamma _ c(X,F)$. For $C^0F$ its positive Čech groups on every cover vanish by the partition contraction above; its positive supported Godement groups vanish by flabbiness. Embed any $F$ in $C^0F$ and put $Q=C^0F/F$. In degree one, both long exact sequences give the cokernel of $\Gamma _ c C^0F\to \Gamma _ c Q$, and the comparison is the identity on that cokernel. In higher degrees the positive connecting isomorphisms shift to $Q$ and one lower degree. Induction, using the already checked connecting compatibility, proves that the canonical comparison is an isomorphism in every degree. $\square$
