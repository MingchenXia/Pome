10.33 Vector-bundle curvature and compact Dolbeault estimatesD55631DC3838

D55631DC3838

Let $(X,\omega )$ be a Kähler manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, and let $(E,h)$ be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank $r\geq 1$ with a smooth Hermitian metric. Inner products are linear in the first variable. We use $dd^ c=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial /(2\pi )$, and the unscaled Chern curvature is denoted by $\Theta (E)$. Thus for a line bundle with squared frame norm $\mathrm e^{-\phi }$, $\mathrm i\Theta (E)=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial \phi =2\pi c_1(E,h)$. The pointwise metric on forms is induced by $g(v,w)=\omega (v,Jw)$, and $\mathrm dV=\omega ^ n/n!$.

In a holomorphic frame write $h(v,w)=w^*Hv$. Its Chern connection is

\[ D'=\partial +A\wedge ,\qquad D''=\bar\partial , \qquad A=H^{-1}\partial H. \]

If the frame changes by a holomorphic invertible matrix $G$, then $H$ changes to $G^*HG$ and $A$ to $G^{-1}AG+G^{-1}\partial G$, so these operators are intrinsic. The identities $HA=\partial H$ and $A^*H=\bar\partial H$ verify metric compatibility by differentiating $w^*Hv$. Differentiating $H^{-1}H=1$ gives $\partial A+A\wedge A=0$, whence

0BB4122DCA2A 10.33.1
\begin{equation} (D')^2=(D'')^2=0,\qquad D'D''+D''D'=\Theta (E)\wedge ,\qquad \Theta (E)=\bar\partial (H^{-1}\partial H). \end{equation}

Here matrix-valued forms act by exterior multiplication on the left, with the usual graded product rule.

Let $\delta ',\delta ''$ be the formal adjoints of $D',D''$, and put $\Delta '=D'\delta '+\delta 'D'$ and $\Delta ''=D''\delta ''+\delta ''D''$. The Lefschetz operators are $L=\omega \wedge $ and $\Lambda =L^*$. For operators of total degrees $a,b$ use $[P,Q]=PQ-(-1)^{ab}QP$.

Theorem 10.33.2.836B94BFF74C On all smooth $E$-valued forms,

8552D5DC8CAA 10.33.3
\begin{equation} [\Lambda ,D']=\mathrm i\delta '',\qquad [\Lambda ,D'']=-\mathrm i\delta ',\qquad [D',\delta '']=[D'',\delta ']=0, \end{equation}

and

FC33C3E2A980 10.33.4
\begin{equation} \Delta ''=\Delta '+B,\qquad B=[\mathrm i\Theta (E),\Lambda ]. \end{equation}

Consequently, for every compactly supported smooth $E$-valued $(p,q)$-form $v$,

77EDE4CBDD16 10.33.5
\begin{equation} \| D''v\| ^2+\| \delta ''v\| ^2 =\| D'v\| ^2+\| \delta 'v\| ^2+\int _ X\langle Bv,v\rangle \, \mathrm dV. \end{equation}

Proof. The scalar Kähler calculation in the line-bundle Bochner–Kodaira identity applies componentwise in a Chern-normal frame. Here are the frame reduction and the operator signs. Choose Kähler normal coordinates at a point by Kähler normal coordinates, rescaled so that $\omega =\mathrm i\sum _ j\mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j$ at the point. A constant frame change makes $H=1$ there. The holomorphic frame change $G(z)=1-\sum _ j(\partial _ jH)(0)z_ j$, on a sufficiently small neighborhood, then makes every first derivative of $H$ vanish at the point. Thus $A=0$ there. The coefficients of the pointwise form metric and volume also have vanishing first derivatives there.

Write $\epsilon _ j,\bar\epsilon _ j$ for exterior multiplication by $\mathrm dz_ j,\mathrm d\bar z_ j$ and $\iota _ j,\bar\iota _ j$ for their pointwise adjoints. At the point,

\[ \Lambda =-\mathrm i\sum _ j\bar\iota _ j\iota _ j,\quad D'=\sum _ j\epsilon _ j\partial _ j,\quad D''=\sum _ j\bar\epsilon _ j\partial _{\bar j},\quad \delta '=-\sum _ j\iota _ j\partial _{\bar j},\quad \delta ''=-\sum _ j\bar\iota _ j\partial _ j. \]

The adjoint formulas follow by one integration by parts; the extra zeroth-order coefficients involve only first derivatives of the metric and vanish at this point. The exterior and contraction operators satisfy $\iota _ j\epsilon _ k+\epsilon _ k\iota _ j=\delta _{jk}$ and the analogous barred relation, while all mixed anticommutators vanish. These relations give the first two identities in (10.33.3). They are identities of first-order operators: their commutators involve only first derivatives of the coefficients of $\Lambda $, which also vanish at the point. Since the point is arbitrary, the identities hold globally.

For completeness the graded Jacobi identity gives

\[ [D'',[\Lambda ,D']] =[[D'',\Lambda ],D']+[\Lambda ,[D'',D']] =\mathrm i\Delta '+[\Lambda ,\Theta (E)]. \]

Multiply by $-\mathrm i$ and use $\delta ''=-\mathrm i[\Lambda ,D']$ to obtain (10.33.4). Also $[D',[\Lambda ,D']]=0$ follows by expanding and using $(D')^2=0$; the same expansion for $D''$ proves the last two commutators in (10.33.3). Integrating the operator identity and applying the defining identities of the formal adjoints proves (10.33.5). $\square$

At a point choose an $\omega $-unitary $(1,0)$-coframe $\theta _1,\ldots ,\theta _ n$ and an $h$-unitary frame of $E$, and write

\[ \mathrm i\Theta (E)=\mathrm i\sum _{j,k}R_{j\bar k} \theta _ j\wedge \bar\theta _ k. \]

The endomorphisms satisfy $R_{j\bar k}^*=R_{k\bar j}$: in the normal holomorphic frame above $R_{j\bar k}=-\partial _ j\partial _{\bar k}H$, and $H=H^*$. The curvature form on $T^{1,0}X\otimes E$ is

\[ \mathcal R(\xi ,\xi )=\sum _{j,k} \langle R_{j\bar k}\xi _ j,\xi _ k\rangle , \qquad \xi =(\xi _1,\ldots ,\xi _ n). \]

A metric is Nakano-positive when this form is positive on every nonzero tensor. It is $m$-positive when the same holds on every nonzero tensor of rank at most $m$, the rank being the rank of $T^{1,0*}X\longrightarrow E$ determined by that tensor. Thus Nakano positivity is $m$-positivity for $m\geq \min (n,r)$.

Proposition 10.33.6.DAA549FD5760 In the unitary frames, exterior and contraction operators being denoted as before, the curvature operator on $(p,q)$-forms is

794C2246496E 10.33.7
\begin{equation} B=\sum _{j,k}R_{j\bar k} \bigl (\epsilon _ j\iota _ k+\bar\epsilon _ k\bar\iota _ j -\delta _{jk}\bigr ). \end{equation}

For $1\leq q\leq n$ and an $(n,q)$-form $v=\theta _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _ n\wedge \sum _{|J|=q}v_ J\bar\theta _ J$, with increasing $J$, this gives

64227AA6EECF 10.33.8
\begin{equation} \langle Bv,v\rangle =\sum _{|S|=q-1}\sum _{j,k} \langle R_{j\bar k}v_{jS},v_{kS}\rangle . \end{equation}

The coefficient $v_{jS}$ is alternating in $jS$ and is zero for $j\in S$. If $\mathcal R(\xi ,\xi )\geq \varepsilon |\xi |^2$ on tensors of rank at most $m$, and $m\geq \min (n-q+1,r)$, then

7D37E558834B 10.33.9
\begin{equation} \langle Bv,v\rangle \geq q\varepsilon |v|^2. \end{equation}

Proof. For each $j,k,l$, the anticommutation rules give

\[ [\epsilon _ j\bar\epsilon _ k,\bar\iota _ l\iota _ l] =\delta _{kl}\epsilon _ j\iota _ l +\delta _{jl}\bar\epsilon _ k\bar\iota _ l -\delta _{jl}\delta _{kl}. \]

Indeed, moving $\bar\epsilon _ k$ past $\bar\iota _ l$ in the first product and $\iota _ l$ past $\epsilon _ j$ in the second cancels the remaining four-operator products; use $\bar\iota _ l\bar\epsilon _ k=\delta _{lk}-\bar\epsilon _ k\bar\iota _ l$ for the final two terms. Multiplication by the factors $\mathrm i$ and $-\mathrm i$ in $\mathrm i\Theta (E)$ and $\Lambda $, and summation in $l$, proves (10.33.7).

On holomorphic top degree $\epsilon _ j\iota _ k=\delta _{jk}$, leaving $B=\sum _{j,k}R_{j\bar k}\bar\epsilon _ k\bar\iota _ j$. Taking its quadratic form gives $\sum _{j,k}\langle R_{j\bar k}\bar\iota _ jv,\bar\iota _ kv\rangle $. The common sign $(-1)^ n$ from passing the barred contraction through the holomorphic top form cancels in this pairing. Expanding in the orthonormal $\bar\theta _ S$ proves (10.33.8). For fixed $S$, the tensor $(v_{jS})_ j$ has zero rows for $j\in S$, so its rank is at most $\min (n-q+1,r)$. Apply the assumed lower bound to each such tensor and use $\sum _ S\sum _ j|v_{jS}|^2=q\sum _ J|v_ J|^2$; each increasing $J$ is counted once for each of its $q$ indices. This proves (10.33.9). $\square$

We use explicit analytic Dolbeault cohomology:

\[ H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X,E)= \frac{\ker (D'':C^\infty _{p,q}(X,E)\to C^\infty _{p,q+1}(X,E))}{D''C^\infty _{p,q-1}(X,E)}, \]

with denominator zero when $q=0$. Formula (10.33.7) and $R_{j\bar k}^*=R_{k\bar j}$ show that $B$ is pointwise Hermitian. These groups use smooth forms; the following argument includes the regularity needed for smooth primitives.

Theorem 10.33.10.9B323DF3F730 Assume now that $X$ is compact. Fix $0\leq p\leq n$ and $1\leq q\leq n$. If $B\geq \lambda \operatorname {Id}$ on $\Lambda ^{p,q}T^*X\otimes E$ for a constant $\lambda >0$, then

D5878EE600F1 10.33.11
\begin{equation} \lambda \| v\| ^2\leq \| D''v\| ^2+\| \delta ''v\| ^2 \qquad (v\in C^\infty _{p,q}(X,E)). \end{equation}

Every distributionally $D''$-closed $f\in L^2_{p,q}(X,E)$ has a solution $u\in L^2_{p,q-1}(X,E)$ with

0E5CF028319E 10.33.12
\begin{equation} D''u=f,\qquad \| u\| ^2\leq \lambda ^{-1}\| f\| ^2. \end{equation}

There is a unique solution of smallest $L^2$ norm. When $f$ is smooth this solution is smooth, belongs to the image of $\delta ''$, and satisfies $\delta ''u=0$. In particular $H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X,E)=0$. If $q=0$ and $B\geq \lambda \operatorname {Id}$ in that bidegree, then $H^{p,0}_{\bar\partial }(X,E)=0$ as well.

Proof. The energy identity proves (10.33.11). Let $T=D''$ from $L^2_{p,q-1}$ to $L^2_{p,q}$ and $S=D''$ from $L^2_{p,q}$ to $L^2_{p,q+1}$ have their maximal distributional domains; if $q=n$, take the last Hilbert space to be zero and $S=0$. Local $L^2$ convergence implies convergence of distributions, so both operators are closed. Smooth sections are dense in these spaces by a finite frame cover, partition of unity and coefficient mollification, and $ST=0$ by (10.33.1).

The matrix-coefficient graph-density argument in graph-norm density by cutoffs and mollification applies here in any bidegree and rank. More explicitly, the definition of $T^*$ gives $T^*v=\delta ''v$ distributionally. Multiplication by a chart cutoff changes $\delta ''v$ and $D''v$ only by zeroth-order bounded multiples of $v$. In a holomorphic frame both operators are first-order matrix operators with smooth coefficients. The commutator estimate with local convolution proved in that lemma applies to their joint coefficient vector and converges to zero in $L^2$. Thus local convolutions of the finitely many localized sections, summed after extension by zero, approximate every $v\in \operatorname {Dom}T^*\cap \operatorname {Dom}S$ in the joint graph norm. There is no boundary cutoff loss, since $X$ is compact without boundary. Passing (10.33.11) to this limit gives

\[ |\langle f,v\rangle |^2\leq \frac{\| f\| ^2}{\lambda } (\| T^*v\| ^2+\| Sv\| ^2). \]

The hypotheses of Hilbert-complex solvability from the dual energy estimate are now verified; it gives (10.33.12) and the unique solution orthogonal to $\ker T$, equivalently of smallest norm.

We establish smoothness without invoking a Hodge decomposition. Put

\[ Q(v,w)=\langle D''v,D''w\rangle + \langle \delta ''v,\delta ''w\rangle . \]

Use the Sobolev spaces of Sobolev spaces, duality and local embedding with an additional finite frame index for $E$; the proof there allows arbitrary finite coefficient vectors and smooth changes of frame. The principal symbol of $\Delta ''$ at a real covector $\xi $ is

\[ (\xi ^{0,1}\wedge )(\xi ^{0,1}\wedge )^* + (\xi ^{0,1}\wedge )^*(\xi ^{0,1}\wedge ) =\tfrac 12|\xi |_ g^2\operatorname {Id}. \]

Here $|\xi ^{0,1}|^2=|\xi |^2/2$, and the exterior-contraction anticommutator proves the equality coefficientwise, also for the extra bundle index. Thus in real coordinates $\Delta ''=-\tfrac 12\sum g^{ij}\partial _ i\partial _ j$ on each coefficient, plus smooth first- and zeroth-order matrix terms.

The Gårding calculation in the proof of compact elliptic regularity and coercivity consequently gives

DDF03056D479 10.33.13
\begin{equation} \| v\| _{H^1}^2\leq C\bigl (Q(v,v)+\| v\| ^2\bigr ). \end{equation}

To check precisely the increment in that argument, write the coefficient metric as a positive matrix $G$ and the volume density as $\rho $ in a chart. One integration by parts has principal form $\tfrac 12\int g^{ij}G_{AB}\partial _ jv_ A \overline{\partial _ iv_ B}\rho $, uniformly positive on the full matrix of first derivatives. Its remaining terms are bounded by $C\| v\| _{L^2}\| v\| _{H^1}$. A finite partition of unity localizes this calculation; the commutators with its cutoffs are zeroth order and have $L^2$ norm at most $C\| v\| $. Absorbing the product error with $ab\leq \tau a^2+b^2/(4\tau )$ proves (10.33.13). This is the same proved argument with half the principal coefficient and the extra finite bundle index.

By coercivity, $Q(v,v)^{1/2}$ is therefore an equivalent Hilbert norm on $H^1_{p,q}(X,E)$: its upper bound by the Sobolev norm follows from the first-order local formulas, and its lower bound follows from (10.33.13) and (10.33.11). For smooth $f$, the Riesz theorem Hilbert projection and Riesz representation gives $v\in H^1$ with $Q(v,w)=\langle f,w\rangle $ for every $w\in H^1$, that is, $\Delta ''v=f$ weakly. In each chart this weak equation has the strongly elliptic divergence coefficients $a^{ij}_{AB}=\tfrac 12g^{ij}G_{AB}\rho $. The lower-order matrix terms are allowed in interior regularity for elliptic systems in divergence form, including terms with a derivative on the test section, which are integrated by parts as specified there. Apply that lemma successively on smaller charts for every Sobolev order. It gives $v\in H^ s$ for all $s$, hence $v$ smooth by Sobolev spaces, duality and local embedding.

Suppose $D''f=0$. The identity $(D'')^2=0$ gives $\Delta ''D''v=D''\Delta ''v=0$. If $w=D''v$, integration of this equation shows $\delta ''w=0$, and $\| w\| ^2=\langle D''v,w\rangle =\langle v,\delta ''w\rangle =0$. Thus $D''v=0$, including the automatic top-degree case, and $u=\delta ''v$ satisfies $D''u=f$ and $(\delta '')u=0$. For any $a\in \ker T$ the distributional adjoint identity gives $\langle u,a\rangle =\langle v,D''a\rangle =0$; this smooth solution is therefore exactly the smallest-norm solution found above. Its estimate also follows directly from $\| u\| ^2\leq Q(v,v)=\langle f,v\rangle $ and $\lambda \| v\| ^2\leq Q(v,v)$. Every smooth closed form is consequently smoothly exact in degree $q\geq 1$. For $q=0$, apply the coercive energy inequality directly to a smooth closed section: $\delta ''$ vanishes in that degree, so the section is zero. This proves the stated analytic cohomology conclusions. $\square$

Corollary 10.33.14.59012AA8CC3F Let $X$ be compact Kähler and let $(E,h)$ be Nakano-positive. Then $H^{n,q}_{\bar\partial }(X,E)=0$ for $1\leq q\leq n$. If its curvature satisfies $\mathcal R(\xi ,\xi )\geq 2\pi a|\xi |^2$ for all tensors, with $a>0$, the smallest-norm solution in degree $(n,q)$ satisfies

\[ \| u\| ^2\leq \frac{1}{2\pi aq}\| f\| ^2. \]

More generally, strict $m$-positivity on all of $X$ suffices in every degree $q\geq 1$ for which $m\geq \min (n-q+1,r)$.

Proof. The unit tensors of rank at most $m$ form a closed subset of the unit sphere bundle, hence a compact set. Strict positivity on this set has a positive minimum $\varepsilon $. Apply proposition 10.33.6, theorem 10.33.10 with $\lambda =q\varepsilon $. The displayed normalization has $\varepsilon =2\pi a$. $\square$

Corollary 10.33.15.307B7E1852C7 For a positive line bundle $L$ over a compact complex manifold of dimension $n$, $H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X,L)=0$ when $p+q>n$. For a negative line bundle the same group is zero when $p+q<n$, including $q=0$.

Proof. In the positive case choose the Kähler form $\omega =\mathrm i\Theta (L)/(2\pi )$. In (10.33.7) the curvature coefficients are $R_{j\bar k}=2\pi \delta _{jk}$, so $B=2\pi (p+q-n)\operatorname {Id}$. In the negative case choose $\omega =-\mathrm i\Theta (L)/(2\pi )$, giving $B=2\pi (n-p-q)\operatorname {Id}$. The preceding theorem applies in exactly the asserted ranges. $\square$
