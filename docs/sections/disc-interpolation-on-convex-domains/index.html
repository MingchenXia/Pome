9.18 Disc interpolation on convex domains11A89FE66D06

11A89FE66D06

Use the disc distance $q(\zeta ,\eta )=\operatorname {atanh} |\phi _\eta (\zeta )|$ and the functions $c_ D,\delta _ D,k_ D,\sigma _ D$ defined in the invariant-distance section.

Theorem 9.18.1.7F33F823B79C On a bounded convex domain $D\subset \mathbb C^ n$, the one-disc distance $\delta _ D$ is a complete metric inducing the Euclidean topology, and $\delta _ D=k_ D$. Every pair of distinct points has an extremal disc: there are $f:\Delta \to D$ holomorphic and $0<t<1$ such that $f(0)=a$, $f(t)=b$ and $\delta _ D(a,b)=q(0,t)$.

Proof. We first prove the triangle inequality. Discs used in the infimum can be chosen holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\overline\Delta $ with image in $D$, at arbitrarily small additional distance cost. Indeed, replace a given disc $f$ by $f(r\zeta )$ with $r\uparrow 1$, and replace its two interpolation nodes by their quotients by $r$. Both nodes remain in $\Delta $ for $r$ sufficiently close to one; their distance converges to its old value. Disc automorphisms then give discs $f,g$ and real nodes $0<A<B<1$ such that

\[ f(0)=a,\quad f(A)=g(A)=b,\quad g(B)=d, \]

with $q(0,A)$ and $q(A,B)$ as close as desired to $\delta _ D(a,b)$ and $\delta _ D(b,d)$. To obtain these nodes, send the first pair to $0,A$ and the first node of the second pair to $A$; a rotation in its stabilizer places its second node on the real geodesic beyond $A$. This is the explicit disc-automorphism formula for $B=(A+s)/(1+As)$ when the second distance is $q(0,s)$.

Put

\[ L(\zeta )=\frac{(\zeta -B)(\zeta -B^{-1})}{(\zeta -A)(\zeta -A^{-1})},\qquad h=g+L(f-g). \]

The apparent pole at $A$ is removable because $f(A)=g(A)$. Moreover $L(0)=1$, $L(B)=0$, and on $|\zeta |=1$

\[ L(\zeta )=\frac{B+B^{-1}-2\operatorname {Re}\zeta }{A+A^{-1}-2\operatorname {Re}\zeta }\in (0,1). \]

Thus $h(\partial \Delta )\subset D$ by convexity. Its boundary image is compact; its convex hull is a compact subset of $D$. The harmonic mean formula in each real coordinate puts $h(\Delta )$ in that convex hull. Since $h(0)=a$ and $h(B)=d$,

\[ \delta _ D(a,d)\leq q(0,B)=q(0,A)+q(A,B). \]

Let the two approximation errors tend to zero. Zero or repeated points are immediate. The coordinate-disc lower bounds for $c_ D$ give separation, since $c_ D\leq \delta _ D$, and symmetry follows by interchanging the disc nodes. The previously proved characterization of $k_ D$ as the greatest pseudodistance below $\delta _ D$ now gives $k_ D=\delta _ D$.

For the topology, fix $a$ and $B(a,r)\subset D$. The affine disc $\zeta \mapsto a+r\zeta (b-a)/|b-a|$ gives $\delta _ D(a,b)\leq q(0,|b-a|/r)$ when $|b-a|<r$. Conversely, a fixed $R$ with $|z_ j|<R$ on $D$ gives $c_ D(a,b)\geq q(a_ j/R,b_ j/R)$ for every coordinate. The explicit disc formula therefore makes convergence in $\delta _ D$ equivalent to Euclidean convergence.

An extremizing sequence can be normalized as $f_ j(0)=a$, $f_ j(t_ j)=b$, $t_ j>0$. The infimum is finite by the disc-joining theorem, and positive by coordinate separation, so $t_ j\to t\in (0,1)$ after choosing the sequence appropriately. Boundedness and Montel's theorem give a compact-uniform limit $f:\Delta \to \overline D$ with $f(0)=a$, $f(t)=b$. It actually maps into $D$: if $f(\zeta _0)\in \partial D$, a real supporting hyperplane there has equation $\operatorname {Re}\ell (z)=s$ with $\operatorname {Re}\ell <s$ on $D$. The harmonic function $\operatorname {Re}\ell \circ f\leq s$ would attain its maximum at $\zeta _0$, contradicting $f(0)=a\in D$. This proves attainment.

Finally, a $\delta _ D$-Cauchy sequence is Euclidean Cauchy by the coordinate bounds, with limit in $\overline D$. If its limit $\xi $ were on the boundary, choose a supporting complex linear functional $\ell $ there, and set $F=s-\ell $, so $F:D\to \{ \operatorname {Re}w>0\} $ and $\operatorname {Re}F(\xi )=0$. Holomorphic contraction and a half-plane-to-disc automorphism give

\[ \delta _ D(a,z)\geq q\left(0,\left|\frac{F(z)-F(a)}{F(z)+\overline{F(a)}}\right|\right). \]

The modulus tends to one as $z\to \xi $, so the distance tends to infinity, contrary to the Cauchy property. The limit lies in $D$, and the local affine-disc bound proves metric convergence. $\square$

Proposition 9.18.2.0A57323E9BD0 Let $D$ be bounded and convex, with $C^1$ boundary. Suppose a holomorphic map $f:\Delta \to D$ extends continuously to $\overline\Delta $, maps the circle into $\partial D$, and has a holomorphic continuous dual $\widetilde f:\overline\Delta \to \mathbb C^ n$ whose boundary values satisfy

\[ \widetilde f(\zeta )=\zeta \rho (\zeta ) \overline{\nu (f(\zeta ))},\qquad |\zeta |=1, \]

where $\rho >0$ is continuous and $\nu $ is the outward unit normal. Then $f$ has a holomorphic left inverse $P:D\to \Delta $. In particular, for $\lambda ,\eta \in \Delta $,

\[ c_ D(f(\lambda ),f(\eta ))=\delta _ D(f(\lambda ),f(\eta )) =k_ D(f(\lambda ),f(\eta ))=\sigma _ D(f(\lambda ),f(\eta )) =q(\lambda ,\eta ), \]

and $g_ D(f(\lambda ),f(\eta ))=\log |\phi _\eta (\lambda )|$. The assumptions here include existence of the stationary disc.

Proof. Use the complex bilinear pairing $\langle z,w\rangle =\sum z_ jw_ j$ and set $G(z,\zeta )=\langle z-f(\zeta ),\widetilde f(\zeta )\rangle $. For fixed $z\in D$ and $|\zeta |=1$, the supporting hyperplane inequality gives

\[ \operatorname {Re}\frac{G(z,\zeta )}\zeta =\rho (\zeta )\operatorname {Re} \langle z-f(\zeta ),\overline{\nu (f(\zeta ))}\rangle <0. \]

The strict inequality follows because $z$ is interior. Thus the nonvanishing boundary function $G(z,\zeta )/\zeta $ has winding number zero, and $G(z,\zeta )$ has winding number one. Uniform convergence on circles of radius tending to one and the argument principle show that $G(z,\cdot )$ has exactly one zero in $\Delta $, counted with multiplicity. In particular the zero is simple. The holomorphic implicit-function theorem defines it as $P(z)$ locally; uniqueness patches these local functions into a holomorphic map $P:D\to \Delta $. Since $G(f(\lambda ),\lambda )=0$, we have $P\circ f=\mathrm{id}_\Delta $.

Apply holomorphic contraction to $f$ and $P$, and use the proved disc normalizations. The inequalities $c_ D\leq \sigma _ D\leq k_ D$ give the remaining equality. This argument constructs the left inverse from the precise boundary lift; it does not assert that an arbitrary extremizing disc already has that lift. $\square$

The convexity condition in the triangle theorem is essential. Put

\[ D_\epsilon =\{ (z_1,z_2):|z_1|,|z_2|<2,\ |z_1z_2|<\epsilon \} . \]

This is a domain, since contraction toward zero preserves every inequality. For $o=(0,0)$, $a=(1,0)$, $b=(0,1)$, the coordinate projections and axis discs give $\delta _{D_\epsilon }(o,a)=\delta _{D_\epsilon }(o,b)=q(0,1/2)$. However $\delta _{D_\epsilon }(a,b)\to \infty $ as $\epsilon \downarrow 0$. Otherwise choose normalized joining discs with parameters bounded above by $t_*<1$. A Montel limit has product of its two components identically zero, takes value $a$ at zero, and value $b$ at a parameter in $[0,t_*]$. The first value forces its second component to vanish identically, contradicting the second value. For small $\epsilon $ the one-disc function therefore fails the triangle inequality.

Sources. The interpolation coefficient and degenerating example come from Lempert, La metrique de Kobayashi et la representation des domaines sur la boule [Lempert1981], Section2,430–431. The boundary lift is his stationary-disc definition in Section4,434; the argument-principle construction explicitly proves its left-inverse consequence. Reuse the internal disc Schwarz–Pick, Montel, supporting-hyperplane, argument-principle and holomorphic contraction statements. The deeper existence of stationary discs is a separate supporting obligation, with its exact source scope recorded in the dependency review.
