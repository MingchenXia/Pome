6.39 Parameter weights and analytic Lelong level sets5CB28C702CB8

5CB28C702CB8

Use $dd^ c=i\partial \bar\partial /(2\pi )$ and squared logarithmic weights. Let $T$ be closed positive of bidimension $(p,p)$ on a Stein manifold $X$. For an extended-continuous plurisubharmonic weight $\phi $ use the support-relative truncation masses

\[ M_ T(\phi ,t)=\int _{\{ \phi <t\} }T\wedge (dd^ c\max \{ \phi ,s\} )^ p\quad (s<t),\qquad \nu _ T(\phi )=\lim _{t\to -\infty }M_ T(\phi ,t). \]

These are the already established quantities of theorem 6.34.2. In particular they do not require an unqualified unbounded product at the poles. Their monotonicity, collar identities and local semicontinuity will always be used with compact low sublevels on $\operatorname {Supp}T$.

Definition 6.39.1.E6495CF1E603 An extended-continuous plurisubharmonic function $\phi $ on $X\times Y$ is support-proper at low levels if for every compact $L\subset Y$ there is $b_ L\in \mathbb R$ such that

\[ \{ (x,y)\in \operatorname {Supp}T\times L:\phi (x,y)\leq b_ L\} \Subset X\times Y. \]

Its exponential is locally Hölder in the parameter if, locally in parameter coordinates and uniformly on smaller product neighborhoods,

\[ |e^{\phi (x,y)}-e^{\phi (x,y')}|\leq C|y-y'|^\gamma \quad \text{for some }0<\gamma \leq 1. \]

Lemma 6.39.2.EA46D27C1C72 After restricting the parameter to a relatively compact coordinate ball, a support-proper weight can be replaced by $w$ with the same numbers $\nu _ T(w_ y)=\nu _ T(\phi _ y)$ such that its support sublevel $\{ w\leq 0\} $ is contained in $K\times Y$ for a compact $K\subset X$, and $w$ is smooth on $\{ -1\leq w\leq 0\} $. The exponential of $w$ retains local parameter Hölder continuity when that of $\phi $ has it.

Proof. Take the closure of a slightly larger parameter ball when applying support properness. Add a nonnegative smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function $q$ on $X\times Y$, obtaining $\eta =\phi +q$. Such a $q$ is supplied by a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion of $X$, given by theorem 9.26.1, and $|y|^2$. It is bounded on the compact low-support set. Thus $\eta $ has the same poles and numbers by corollary 6.34.11; it remains support-proper. Choose $R<0$ so that its support sublevel $\{ \eta \leq R\} $ is compact in the $X$ direction. Richberg approximation on $\{ \eta >-\infty \} $ by relative Richberg smoothing gives a smooth plurisubharmonic $h$ with $\eta \leq h\leq \eta +1$. This is an approximation on the nonpolar open manifold, where $\eta $ is finite, continuous and strictly plurisubharmonic. Put

\[ C=-R+10,\qquad B=R+8,\qquad v=\max \{ \eta ,2h+C\} ,\qquad w=v-B, \]

extending $v$ by $-\infty $ at the poles. For $\eta \leq R-12$ one has $v=\eta $; for $\eta >R-10$ one has $v=2h+C$. These observations prove that the extension is plurisubharmonic and extended-continuous, and that $w-\phi =q-B$ near its poles. If $v\leq B$, then $2\eta +C\leq B$, hence $\eta \leq R-1$. If $B-1\leq v\leq B$, then $\eta >R-10$, so $v$ is smooth there. This proves the compactness and corona assertions, retaining $\operatorname {Supp}T$ in the compactness condition.

On the open set $\{ \eta <R-12\} $ one has $e^ v=e^\eta $. Away from the poles, $e^ v=\max \{ e^\eta ,e^{2h+C}\} $ is a maximum of a locally Hölder function and a smooth function. Multiplication by the smooth factors $e^ q$ and $e^{-B}$, and taking a maximum, preserve a local Hölder estimate. Thus the replacement preserves the required estimate, including at the poles. No preservation claim for an arbitrary bounded Richberg perturbation is being made. $\square$

Proposition 6.39.3.0426E4966C15 For a weight $w$ as in lemma 6.39.2, choose a smooth nondecreasing function $\chi $ with $\chi (t)=t$ for $t\leq -1$ and $\chi (t)=0$ for $t\geq 0$. On the half-plane $H=\{ \operatorname {Re}z<-2\} $ put

CC32CC117F5F 6.39.4
\begin{equation} V(y,z)=-\int _{\operatorname {Re}z}^{0}M_ T(w_ y,t)\chi '(t)\, dt. \end{equation}

There is a nonnegative smooth plurisubharmonic $\rho $ on the parameter ball such that $\rho (y)+V(y,z)$ is continuous and plurisubharmonic on $Y\times H$. These assertions do not require the Hölder hypothesis.

Proof. Write $w_ z=\max \{ w,\operatorname {Re}z\} $. Fubini and the finite-level collar identity give the fiber-integral formula

46483D5C23FE 6.39.5
\begin{equation} V(y,z)=\int _ X T\wedge \chi (w_ z)(dd^ c_ xw_ z)^ p. \end{equation}

Indeed, $M_ T(w_ y,t)$ is the mass of the bounded product $T(dd^ c_ xw_ z)^ p$ on $\{ w_ y<t\} $ when $t>\operatorname {Re}z$, and integrating $-\chi '(t)$ gives $\chi (w_ z)$. All integrals are finite: on a compact parameter and $z$ set the truncations are uniformly bounded near the compact support of the integrand, and the bounded product estimate applies. Uniform continuity of the bounded potentials and the bounded Bedford–Taylor continuity theorem show that the last integral is continuous in $(y,z)$.

Pull $T$ back by the product projection and use total differentiation in the following calculation. The terms of the product containing extra $x$ differentials vanish under fiber integration, so

\[ dd^ cV=\operatorname {pr}_{Y\times H,*} \bigl (T\wedge dd^ c\chi (w_ z)\wedge (dd^ cw_ z)^ p\bigr ). \]

To justify integration by parts, multiply by a smooth compact parameter test form. Its integrand has compact support in the $x$ direction on $\operatorname {Supp}T$; choose an $x$ cutoff equal to one there. All derivatives of that cutoff meet a region where $\chi (w_ z)=0$. The bounded-product chain rule follows first for smooth local regularizations and then by uniform convergence; it is also the recursive integration-by-parts definition of these products. On $\{ w<-1\} $, $\chi (w_ z)=w_ z$, so its contribution to this Hessian is the positive current $T(dd^ cw_ z)^{p+1}$. On the corona, $w_ z=w$ is smooth and independent of $z$. Consequently the possible negative part has only parameter components and locally bounded coefficients, bounded by the mass of $T$ on a fixed compact $x$ set times bounds for finitely many derivatives of $w$ there. Thus

\[ dd^ cV\geq -C_ L\, i\sum _ jdy_ j\wedge d\bar y_ j \quad \text{on }L\times H \]

for every compact parameter subball $L$, uniformly in $z$. A nonnegative smooth convex increasing function of $|y|^2$, with radial derivative larger than these bounds on successive compact subballs, supplies $\rho $. Its Hessian dominates the negative term. The distributional criterion for plurisubharmonicity now proves the claim for the continuous function $\rho +V$. $\square$

Proposition 6.39.6.EB911040D7E4 For $a\geq 0$ set

\[ U_ a(y)=\inf _{r<-2}\{ \rho (y)+V(y,r)-ar\} . \]

It is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty $ on a connected parameter ball. If $a>\nu _ T(w_{y_0})$, then $U_ a$ is bounded below and continuous on a neighborhood of $y_0$. If $a<\nu _ T(w_{y_0})$, then $U_ a(y_0)=-\infty $. The family $U_ a$ is increasing in $a$.

Proof. The exact product-tube minimum principle the minimum principle on convex product tubes applies to the real interval $(-\infty ,-2)$ and the imaginary-translation-invariant plurisubharmonic function $\rho +V-a\operatorname {Re}z$. Since $M_ T(w_ y,t)$ is increasing in $t$ and $r<-2$,

B8EE2E79E87A 6.39.7
\begin{equation} V(y,r)\leq rM_ T(w_ y,r)\leq r\nu _ T(w_ y). \end{equation}

This proves the second alternative.

If $\nu _ T(w_{y_0})<a$, choose $b<a$ and a sufficiently negative finite level $r_0$ with $M_ T(w_{y_0},r_0)<b$ and with zero boundary mass. The local semicontinuity theorem 6.34.8, with the uniform support compactness, gives $M_ T(w_ y,r_0)<b$ near $y_0$, after increasing $b$ slightly while retaining $b<a$. For $r<r_0$,

\[ V(y,r)\geq -C+b(r-r_0). \]

Hence $\rho +V-ar\to +\infty $ as $r\to -\infty $, uniformly near $y_0$. The infimum can therefore be taken over a fixed compact interval $[-N,-2]$, where the function is continuous. A minimum of a continuous function over a fixed compact set is continuous, proving both assertions. Monotonicity in $a$ follows from $-r>0$. $\square$

Theorem 6.39.8.E7AF0D4FB0C0 On the parameter ball let $c>0$. If $E_ c=\{ y:\nu _ T(w_ y)\geq c\} $ is proper, there is an extended-continuous plurisubharmonic $W$ whose pole set is exactly $E_ c$. Thus $E_ c$ is closed complete pluripolar. This conclusion, like proposition 6.39.3, needs no Hölder assumption.

Proof. Closedness follows from the compact-local semicontinuity theorem. Choose $a_ k\uparrow c$, $0<a_ k<c$, and a compact exhaustion $L_ k$ of the ball. For $N>2$, the finite-interval minimum

\[ U_{a_ k,N}(y)=\min _{-N\leq r\leq -2} \{ \rho (y)+V(y,r)-a_ kr\} \]

is continuous by compact minimization and plurisubharmonic by the same minimum principle, applied to $(-N,-2)$ and continuity at the endpoints. It decreases to $U_{a_ k}$ as $N\to \infty $. Since $U_{a_ k}=-\infty $ on $E_ c$, compactness and decreasingness allow a choice $N_ k$ with $w_ k:=U_{a_ k,N_ k}\leq -2^ k$ on $E_ c\cap L_ k$. All these functions have the common continuous plurisubharmonic majorant $G(y)=\rho (y)+V(y,-2)+2c$. The plurisubharmonic functions

\[ W_ N=\sum _{k=1}^ N2^{-k}w_ k+2^{-N}G \]

decrease. Outside $E_ c$, proposition 6.39.6 gives a common local lower bound for all sufficiently late $w_ k$; the common upper bound $G$ gives locally uniform convergence of the series there. Its limit $W$ is finite and continuous there, so it is not identically $-\infty $. It is plurisubharmonic as a decreasing limit, and $W=-\infty $ on $E_ c$ by the chosen bounds. Upper semicontinuity at a pole says precisely that $W$ tends to $-\infty $ there, proving extended continuity on the whole ball. $\square$

Lemma 6.39.9.4C57C066E531 Suppose $e^ w$ is parameter Hölder of exponent $\gamma $ uniformly on a compact neighborhood of all relevant low-support slices near $y_0$. For $0<\varepsilon <1$ and all sufficiently close $y\ne y_0$,

0E8B0E5F066F 6.39.10
\begin{equation} U_ a(y)\leq \rho (y)+ \bigl ((1-\varepsilon )^ p\nu _ T(w_{y_0})-a\bigr ) \left(\gamma \log |y-y_0|+\log \frac{2eM}{\varepsilon }\right), \end{equation}

where $M>0$ is a Hölder constant, enlarged if necessary.

Proof. Write $w_0=w_{y_0}$ and $w_1=w_ y$, and choose a low outer level $r_0<-2$ for which all support sublevels are compact. For $r\ll r_0$ construct on $D=\{ w_0<r_0\} $ the plurisubharmonic function

\[ \psi =\begin{cases} (1-\varepsilon )w_0+\varepsilon r-\varepsilon /2,& w_0\leq r-1,\\ \max \{ w_1,(1-\varepsilon )w_0+\varepsilon r-\varepsilon /2\} , & r-1\leq w_0\leq r,\\ w_1,& r\leq w_0<r_0. \end{cases} \]

The pieces glue whenever $M|y-y_0|^\gamma e^{1-r}\leq \varepsilon /2$. At $w_0=r$ the Hölder estimate gives $w_1\geq r+\log (1-\varepsilon /(2e))>r-\varepsilon /2$; at $w_0=r-1$ it gives $w_1\leq r-1+\log (1+\varepsilon /2)<r-1+\varepsilon /2$. These are the strict gluing inequalities.

The poles of $\psi $ on the current support are precisely the poles of $w_0$ there; near them it is the displayed affine function of $w_0$. Moreover, $\psi $ and $w_1$ can differ only where both are strictly below $r$, with compact support inside $D$ on $\operatorname {Supp}T$. For the inner piece this follows from $e^{w_1}\leq e^{r-1}+M|y-y_0|^\gamma <e^ r$; for the maximum piece it follows from the affine bound $r-\varepsilon /2$. The same Hölder lower bound excludes any pole of $w_1$ where $w_0\geq r$; all its poles are in the fixed compact low-support set. Apply lemma 6.34.1 to common bounded inner truncations on the common sublevel $\{ \psi <r\} =\{ w_1<r\} $. This proves the exact finite-level identity

\[ M_ T(w_1,r)=M_ T(\psi ,r)\geq \nu _ T(\psi ) =(1-\varepsilon )^ p\nu _ T(w_0). \]

The last equality is the common-pole bounded-change theorem, not a comparison that discards additional poles. The construction may be made on a support neighborhood; all integrals and cutoffs retain that support restriction. Now combine this inequality with equation 6.39.7, and choose $r=\gamma \log |y-y_0|+\log (2eM/\varepsilon )$. For $y$ sufficiently close this is below $r_0$, proving the estimate. $\square$

Theorem 6.39.11.91B6A0BF8212 Let $X$ be Stein, $Y$ a complex manifold and $T$ closed positive of any bidimension on $X$. If an extended-continuous plurisubharmonic weight $\phi $ on $X\times Y$ is support-proper at low levels and $e^\phi $ is locally Hölder in $Y$, then for every $c>0$

\[ \{ y\in Y:\nu _ T(\phi _ y)\geq c\} \]

is analytic in $Y$.

Proof. Work on a relatively compact parameter ball, using lemma 6.39.2. On a smaller ball a single $\gamma >0$ works uniformly on the compact low-support set: a finite product-neighborhood cover gives the minimum exponent and a common constant for sufficiently close pairs. For $a,b>0$ let $Z_{a,b}$ be the nonintegrability locus of $e^{-U_ a/b}$. If $U_ a\equiv -\infty $, put $Z_{a,b}=Y$. Otherwise it is the zero set of the coherent ideal $\mathcal I(U_ a/b)$, hence analytic, by coherence and weighted generators of multiplier ideals. Indeed, local integrability is equivalent to this ideal containing a unit; a holomorphic unit is bounded away from zero on a smaller neighborhood. No Siu theorem for higher-bidegree currents has been used here.

If $\nu _ T(w_{y_0})<c$, choose $a$ strictly between these numbers. Then $U_ a$ is locally bounded below at $y_0$, so $y_0\notin Z_{a,b}$ for every $b>0$. If $\nu _ T(w_{y_0})\geq c$ and $0<a<c$, then lemma 6.39.9 gives

\[ U_ a(y)\leq \gamma \bigl ((1-\varepsilon )^ pc-a\bigr ) \log |y-y_0|+C_\varepsilon . \]

For $0<b<\gamma (c-a)/(2\dim Y)$ choose $\varepsilon $ sufficiently small. The right side forces nonintegrability of $e^{-U_ a/b}$ by the ordinary radial integral in real dimension $2\dim Y$. Consequently

\[ E_ c=\bigcap _{0<a<c,\; 0<b<\gamma (c-a)/(2\dim Y)} Z_{a,b}, \]

where positive rational $a,b$ suffice. Arbitrary intersections of analytic sets are analytic by the exact Noetherian/component argument already established in the proof of analytic Lelong level sets for potentials and $(1,1)$-currents. This proves the claim locally, and therefore globally. In parameter dimension zero every subset is analytic and the assertion is immediate. $\square$

Corollary 6.39.12.78FD31276EAA Finite maxima of weights $\log \sum _ k|F_{jk}(x,y)|^{\lambda _{jk}}$, with holomorphic $F_{jk}$ and positive real exponents, have analytic number level sets when the support-proper hypothesis holds. Their exponentials are locally Hölder with exponent $\min (1,\min _{j,k}\lambda _{jk})$. A proper finite common-zero projection alone can instead be used to define localized pole numbers near its fibers; those localized numbers have analytic level sets.

Proof. For $\lambda \leq 1$, $||s|^\lambda -|t|^\lambda |\leq |s-t|^\lambda $; for $\lambda >1$ use the bounded-set derivative estimate. Finite sums and maxima preserve the asserted estimate. Plurisubharmonicity follows by applying the convex coordinatewise increasing log-sum-exponential function to $\lambda _{jk}\log |F_{jk}|$; truncate the inputs below, regularize locally and pass to the decreasing limit. Finite maxima preserve plurisubharmonicity. The first assertion is now theorem 6.39.11.

For localization fix $y_0$. Its zero fiber is finite. Choose disjoint small coordinate balls about those points with zero-free boundaries. Properness excludes zeros outside these balls for nearby parameters: otherwise a sequence of such zeros over $y\to y_0$ has a convergent subsequence by properness, with limit in the original fiber. Continuity gives a uniform positive lower bound for the exponential on the ball boundaries. The restricted weights therefore satisfy support properness on each ball. Apply the theorem on each of these Stein balls and sum their local pole numbers. A finite sum of nonnegative functions having analytic upper levels again has analytic upper levels: for two summands,

\[ \{ f+g\geq c\} =\bigcap _{r\in \mathbb Q,\; 0<r<c} \bigl (\{ f\geq r\} \cup \{ g\geq c-r\} \bigr ). \]

Use induction and analytic intersection stability. This construction does not assert global semi-exhaustion outside the chosen balls; for example $F(x)=e^{-x}$ on $\mathbb C$ has proper finite empty zero set but unbounded low sublevels. $\square$

Corollary 6.39.13.470C5143C0D1 Fix positive $\lambda _1,\ldots ,\lambda _ n$ and an integer $k\geq 1$ with $(k+1)\min \lambda _ j\geq \max \lambda _ j$. The number associated to $\log \max _ j|u_ j(x)|^{2\lambda _ j}$, where $(u_ j)$ is a local coordinate system vanishing at $y$, depends only on its $k$-jet. Its upper level sets are analytic on the bundle of invertible coordinate $k$-jets over $X$. In particular directional numbers in linearly transformed centered coordinates have analytic upper levels in the center and the invertible linear transformation.

Proof. If $u_ j'=u_ j+O(|x-y|^{k+1})$, the local inverse estimate $|x-y|\leq C\max _ j|u_ j|$ gives

\[ |u_ j'-u_ j|^{2\lambda _ j} \leq C(\max _ l|u_ l|)^{2(k+1)\lambda _ j} \leq C\max _ l|u_ l|^{2\lambda _ l}. \]

Thus the two exponential weights are boundedly comparable; apply the same estimate in the $u'$ coordinates for the reverse bound. Their logarithms differ boundedly and have the same point pole, so their numbers coincide. Equality in the numerical jet bound is allowed; no little-oh assertion is needed.

In local jet coordinates take the Taylor polynomials in $(x-y)$ with their jet coefficients. These are holomorphic in all variables. The nonzero Jacobian condition gives, uniformly on a smaller parameter neighborhood, a single zero at $x=y$ and a positive boundary separation on a fixed small coordinate ball. The preceding corollary applies there. Holomorphic jet changes and the just proved representative independence identify these analytic sets on overlaps. The linear-coordinate family is the special weight $\log \max _ j|(g(x-y))_ j|^{2\lambda _ j}$ on $\mathrm{GL}_ n(\mathbb C)$. $\square$
