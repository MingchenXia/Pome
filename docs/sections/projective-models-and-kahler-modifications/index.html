9.65 Projective models and Kahler modificationsA61498BE1AC4

A61498BE1AC4

Manifolds are connected unless otherwise stated. A modification is proper, bimeromorphic and an isomorphism away from nowhere dense analytic subsets upstairs and downstairs. Curvature below is $\mathrm i\Theta $, without division by $2\pi $.

Lemma 9.65.1.24EDDE45E392 A closed analytic subset of projective space is algebraic.

Proof. The cone argument in the proof of theorem 10.40.10 applies to singular analytic subsets as well. Here are its precise hypotheses in that case. For a nonempty closed analytic $B\subset \mathbb P^ N$, its inverse image in $\mathbb C^{N+1}\setminus \{ 0\} $ is an analytic cone, all of whose branches have dimension at least one. The theorem the Remmert–Stein extension theorem extends its closure across zero as an analytic cone $C$. Take finite holomorphic equations $f_ j=\sum _ m P_{jm}$ near zero, where the $P_{jm}$ are homogeneous. Scaling in $C$ shows that every $P_{jm}$ vanishes on it. Conversely their simultaneous vanishing implies that of the convergent sums $f_ j$. By Noetherianity of convergent power-series modules, the germ ideal generated by all the $P_{jm}$ is generated by a finite subfamily. If that subfamily vanishes at $z$, evaluate every other germ relation at $tz$ for sufficiently small nonzero $t$; homogeneity gives $P_{jm}(z)=0$. Scaling back from the local equations puts $z$ in $C$. Thus the finite homogeneous polynomials define $C$ globally. Projectivizing proves the claim. The empty subset uses the equation $1$. $\square$

Lemma 9.65.2.5174706A0D80 Let $f:M\to \mathbb P^ n$ be a holomorphic map from a compact connected complex $n$-manifold, with rank $n$ somewhere, $n\geq 1$. There is a homogeneous polynomial $P$ of positive degree $d$, divisible by $Z_0$, such that, for $D=\{ P=0\} $,

\[ M^\circ =M\setminus f^{-1}D\longrightarrow U=\mathbb P^ n\setminus D \]

is a finite unbranched covering. It has a fixed positive degree $e$. On $M^\circ $ the function

\[ \psi =-\log |P\circ f|_{\rm FS}^2 \]

can be normalized to be nonnegative and is a proper smooth strictly PSH exhaustion. In fact $\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial \psi =d f^*\omega _{\rm FS}^{\rm un}$, where $\omega _{\rm FS}^{\rm un}=\mathrm i\Theta (\mathcal O(1))$.

Proof. The image is analytic by Remmert's proper mapping theorem and contains an open subset, so it is all of $\mathbb P^ n$. The critical locus $C=\{ \det df=0\} $ is analytic of dimension at most $n-1$. Its proper image is analytic and has dimension at most $n-1$ by dimension bounds for holomorphic images. By the preceding lemma it is algebraic. A nonzero homogeneous polynomial vanishes on it; multiply it by $Z_0$ and use $P=Z_0$ if $C$ is empty. Off $D$ there are no critical points. Properness makes every discrete fiber finite, and inverse charts plus properness give a covering: finitely many disjoint inverse charts cover a fiber, and the complement cannot acquire new points over a sufficiently small base neighborhood. The complements of proper analytic subsets of connected manifolds are connected, by the bounded-holomorphic-removal argument; hence the covering degree is constant and positive.

The asserted Hessian is the local Chern formula, since $P$ is holomorphic and nonzero off $D$. The pullback is positive because $df$ is invertible there. The function tends to $+\infty $ at $f^{-1}D$. Compactness of $M$ proves properness; multiplying $P$ by a constant makes its squared norm at most one. $\square$

Lemma 9.65.3.4F31DD82134A Under the preceding hypotheses, fix $b\in U$ and enumerate $f^{-1}(b)=\{ x_1,\ldots ,x_ e\} $. There are global meromorphic functions $g_1,\ldots ,g_ e$ on $M$, holomorphic on $M^\circ $, such that $g_ i(x_ j)=\delta _{ij}$.

Proof. Use affine coordinates $z=(Z_1/Z_0,\ldots ,Z_ n/Z_0)$ on $U$ and put $\Omega =f^*(dz_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dz_ n)$. It is a nowhere zero holomorphic top form on $M^\circ $ and a meromorphic top form on $M$. The original metric $\omega _0=f^*(\mathrm i\sum dz_ j\wedge d\bar z_ j)$ is Kähler on $M^\circ $. Choose a smooth cutoff $\chi _ i$ supported in a small coordinate ball about $x_ i$, equal to one near $x_ i$, with support disjoint from all other $x_ j$. Its top-form datum

\[ \eta _ i=\bar\partial (\chi _ i\Omega ) \]

is closed, smooth, compactly supported and zero near the whole fiber. For any fixed $A>0$ put, off that fiber,

\[ \Phi =A\psi +n\log |z\circ f-b|^2. \]

This is smooth PSH there and its complex Hessian is at least $A d f^*\omega _{\rm FS}^{\rm un}>0$. Thus the inverse-curvature energy of $\eta _ i$ for the trivial line metric $e^{-\Phi }$ and the metric $\omega _0$ is finite: every quantity is smooth on the fixed compact support, with strictly positive curvature there.

Apply theorem 6.28.2 on the increasing sublevels of $\psi $, with the finite fiber removed. Each admits a complete Kähler background by lemma 6.28.11, applied to the holomorphic tuple $z\circ f-b$. The original-metric top-degree clause applies with line rank one and barred degree one. Its inverse energy bound is independent of the sublevel once the datum is included. The fixed weights give local weak $L^2$ bounds on the complement of the fiber. A diagonal weak limit and lower semicontinuity give

\[ \bar\partial u_ i=\eta _ i,\qquad \int _{M^\circ \setminus f^{-1}(b)} |u_ i|^2e^{-A\psi }|z\circ f-b|^{-2n}<\infty . \]

Top-form norms and volume use $\omega _0$; their product is the intrinsic top-form density, independent of that choice. Near the omitted fiber the weight is bounded below, so $u_ i$ is ordinarily locally $L^2$ across it. The analytic removal lemma 6.28.12 extends the equation over it. The coefficient of $u_ i$ in the frame $\Omega $ is holomorphic near every $x_ j$, by holomorphic distributions and minimal weighted solutions. Since $f$ is a local biholomorphism there, the nonintegrable radial weight $|z-b|^{-2n}$ forces that coefficient to vanish at each $x_ j$. Consequently

\[ g_ i=\chi _ i-u_ i/\Omega \]

is holomorphic on $M^\circ $ and has the claimed fiber values. The compact cutoff term and the preceding estimate give the weaker but nonsingular-at-the-fiber global bound

1D8298271C51 9.65.4
\begin{equation} \int _{M^\circ }|g_ i\Omega |^2 e^{-A\psi } (1+|z\circ f-b|^2)^{-n}<\infty . \end{equation}

We do not claim integrability of $g_ i$ itself against the point-pole weight; its nonzero prescribed value forbids that assertion.

It remains to prove meromorphic extension at the boundary. In a relatively compact coordinate neighborhood of a point of $f^{-1}D$, write $\tau $ for a holomorphic coefficient of $f^*P$ in a line frame, and $\Omega =(a/c)\, dw_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dw_ n$, with $a,c$ holomorphic and not identically zero. Smooth frame factors give $e^{-A\psi }\geq C|\tau |^{2A}$. Because $P$ is divisible by $Z_0$ and its remaining factor has bounded Fubini–Study norm,

\[ 1+|z\circ f-b|^2\leq C'|\tau |^{-2} \]

on this neighborhood off the boundary. Since $c$ is bounded above, the top-form density is bounded below by a positive constant times $|g_ i a|^2$. Equation (9.65.4) therefore implies

\[ \int |g_ i a|^2|\tau |^{2(A+n)}\, dV<\infty . \]

Choose an integer $N\geq A+n$. The holomorphic function $g_ i a\tau ^ N$ off $f^{-1}D$ is locally $L^2$ across it and extends holomorphically by the same analytic removal theorem. Its extension divided by $a\tau ^ N$ is a meromorphic extension of $g_ i$. The denominator is not the zero germ, since $df$ has full rank on a dense set. The local extensions agree on the dense open part, and hence glue globally. This proves the assertion. $\square$

Theorem 9.65.5.B6FE85A67BE1 Let $X$ be a compact connected complex $n$-manifold. Suppose a finite linear system in a holomorphic line bundle defines a meromorphic map to projective space of maximum rank $n$. Then $X$ is bimeromorphic to a projective algebraic variety.

Proof. For $n=0$, $X$ is a point and the assertion is immediate. Assume $n\geq 1$. Principalize the coherent base ideal of the finite linear system by principalization by smooth-center blowups. Removing its common invertible factor leaves a basepoint-free system on a compact smooth modification $M\to X$. Its morphism has a compact analytic image $B\subset \mathbb P^ N$ of dimension $n$, by Remmert's proper mapping theorem and the rank hypothesis. Choose $n+1$ independent linear forms with no simultaneous zero on $B$. Such a choice follows by successive hyperplane cuts: compactness gives finitely many irreducible components at every stage, and a hyperplane containing none of them lowers their dimensions. After $n+1$ cuts the intersection is empty. The resulting map $B\to \mathbb P^ n$ is finite. Indeed each fiber lies in the affine part of a projective linear space obtained by removing its hyperplane where all those forms vanish. A compact analytic subset of that affine space is finite by the maximum principle on analytic branches. The composite $f:M\to \mathbb P^ n$ is therefore surjective and has rank $n$ somewhere: otherwise its image would have smaller dimension by dimension bounds for holomorphic images.

Take the meromorphic functions in the preceding lemma, separating the $e$ points of one noncritical fiber. The meromorphic map

\[ (f,g_1,\ldots ,g_ e):M\dashrightarrow \mathbb P^ n\times (\mathbb P^1)^ e \]

can again be resolved using coherent denominator ideals. For a meromorphic function $g$, let $\mathcal J=\{ h\in \mathcal O_ M:hg\in \mathcal O_ M\} $. Locally write $g=a/b$ with holomorphic $a,b$ and $b\not\equiv 0$. Then $\mathcal J$ is the first-coordinate image of the kernel of $(a,-b):\mathcal O^2\to \mathcal O$, hence coherent by Oka's coherence theorem, kernels and exact sequences of coherent modules. The fractional module $\mathcal I=\mathcal O+g\mathcal O$ is coherent because multiplication by $b$ identifies it locally with the ideal $(b,a)$. The product $\mathcal K=\mathcal J\mathcal I\subset \mathcal O$ is therefore a coherent ideal. First principalize $\mathcal J$ and then the pullback of $\mathcal K$, using principalization by smooth-center blowups. Their final pullbacks $\mathcal J',\mathcal K'$ are invertible, and the former stays invertible under the second modification. In the meromorphic sheaf, pullback commutes with ideal products, so $\mathcal J'\mathcal I'=\mathcal K'$, where $\mathcal I'=\mathcal O+g\mathcal O$ upstairs. Locally, if $\mathcal J'=j\mathcal O$ and $\mathcal K'=k\mathcal O$, then $\mathcal I'=(k/j)\mathcal O$. Dividing its generators $1,g$ by $k/j$ gives two holomorphic functions generating the unit ideal; their ratio defines a holomorphic map to $\mathbb P^1$. Repeat for the finitely many $g_ i$. The resulting compact manifold $M'$ has a holomorphic map $h$ to this projective product. Its image $W$ is closed analytic, hence projective algebraic by the first lemma and the Segre embedding. The latter has all products of homogeneous coordinates as its coordinates; on each affine chart the inverse is given by coordinate ratios, proving it is a holomorphic embedding, and compactness makes its image closed.

The map $h$ is bimeromorphic onto $W$. To verify degree one, take a small ball about $b$ disjoint from the discriminant divisor. Its preimage under $f$ consists of $e$ inverse charts. The vectors $(g_1,\ldots ,g_ e)$ have distinct values at their centers, and remain distinct on a smaller ball. Over that ball the map $h$ is therefore an isomorphism onto a disjoint union of graphs. The base ideals had no zeros there, so the resolution introduced no extra points over that ball. A proper generically finite map has constant sheet degree off a proper analytic subset: remove the proper image of the critical and singular loci and use inverse charts and properness, as in the covering argument above. The regular locus of the irreducible image minus that analytic subset is connected. Thus its degree is one everywhere on that locus, and the map is an isomorphism there. Its inverse-image complement is nowhere dense, since the source is irreducible and the map is dominant. This is precisely a bimeromorphic map. Composing the two bimeromorphic correspondences gives the claimed projective model of $X$. $\square$

Lemma 9.65.6.F5A67B2A1F82 A blowup of a compact Kähler manifold along a closed smooth center of positive codimension is again compact Kähler.

Proof. Let $\pi :M'\to M$ be the blowup and $E$ its exceptional divisor. The charts of smooth-center blowup charts and exceptional divisors identify $E=\mathbb P(N_{C/M})$ and $\mathcal O_{M'}(-E)|_ E=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(N_{C/M})}(1)$: if $t$ is a pivot equation of $E$, changing to pivot $j$ replaces $t$ by $tv_ j$, which is the transition of the dual tautological line bundle. Choose a smooth Hermitian metric on $N_{C/M}$. The induced Fubini–Study metric on this restriction has strictly positive curvature on the vertical tangents of $E\to C$. Extend it to a smooth metric $h$ on $\mathcal O_{M'}(-E)$. Indeed start with any smooth metric; the logarithm of the ratio on $E$ is a smooth scalar function, extend that function in coordinate neighborhoods straightening $E$, and glue with a smooth partition of unity. Multiplying the starting metric by its exponential gives the required extension.

Put $\rho =\mathrm i\Theta _ h$ and let $\omega $ be a Kähler form on $M$. The closed form $\pi ^*\omega $ is semipositive, and its kernel at a point of $E$ is exactly the vertical tangent of $E\to C$, as follows directly from the pivot charts. It has zero kernel off $E$. Thus $\rho $ is positive on every nonzero vector in that kernel. For a fixed auxiliary Hermitian metric, the unit tangent sphere bundle of compact $M'$ is compact. On the compact zero set of $\pi ^*\omega (v,\bar v)$ the value $\rho (v,\bar v)$ is strictly positive, and it remains positive on a neighborhood of that set. On the complement the first quantity has a positive minimum and the second is bounded below. Consequently $\pi ^*\omega +\varepsilon \rho $ is strictly positive for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon >0$. It is closed and smooth, hence Kähler. For a codimension-one center the blowup is the identity, and the assertion is immediate. Compactness follows from properness of the blowup. $\square$

Lemma 9.65.7.107F4EEFA84C Every irreducible closed analytic subset $W\subset \mathbb P^ N$ has a compact connected Kähler resolution $Y\to W$.

Proof. Take the finite smooth-center blowup resolution of $W$ supplied by the stated resolution-of-singularities input, and realize its successive spaces as strict transforms inside ambient blowups starting with $M_0=\mathbb P^ N$. More precisely, suppose $W_ j\subset M_ j$ has been realized. Its next center $C_ j\subset W_ j$ is also a closed smooth complex submanifold of $M_ j$. A smooth analytic subspace of a manifold is a submanifold: its closed embedding surjects on local cotangent spaces, hence injects on tangents, and the holomorphic constant-rank theorem gives the assertion. Blow up $M_ j$ along $C_ j$.

The strict transform of $W_ j$ in this ambient blowup is exactly $\operatorname {Bl}_{\mathcal I_{C_ j}\mathcal O_{W_ j}}W_ j$, even when $W_ j$ is singular. To see this, choose local generators $f_0,\ldots ,f_ r$ of the center ideal in $M_ j$. The ambient blowup is the closure of their projective graph. The restricted center ideal on $W_ j$ has those restricted generators, and its blowup is the closure of the same graph over $W_ j\setminus C_ j$. That closure is precisely the strict transform; both have the reduced structure. The identity is independent of generators and glues by the universal property of analytic blowup. Thus the final strict transform is the given smooth resolution $Y$ of $W$. Every ambient blowup is compact Kähler by the preceding lemma, so the smooth closed submanifold $Y$ inherits a Kähler form. The graph closures are irreducible when $W$ is irreducible; therefore the final smooth resolution is connected. Its map to $W$ is proper and bimeromorphic by the chosen resolution. $\square$

Theorem 9.65.8 (Elimination for a Kähler bimeromorphic source; external input).9CA80A318258 Let $X$ and $Y$ be compact connected complex manifolds, with $Y$ Kähler, and let $Y\dashrightarrow X$ be bimeromorphic. There is a finite sequence $\sigma :\widetilde Y\to Y$ of blowups with closed smooth centers such that this meromorphic map composed with $\sigma $ is a holomorphic modification $\mu :\widetilde Y\to X$.

Theorem 9.65.9.1D3242230662 Let $X$ be a compact connected complex $n$-manifold admitting a finite linear system in a holomorphic line bundle whose meromorphic map has maximum rank $n$. With the stated external elimination input, $X$ admits a compact connected Kähler modification $\mu :\widetilde Y\to X$.

Proof. For $n=0$ take the identity of the point. Otherwise theorem 9.65.5 gives an irreducible projective model $W$ of $X$. The preceding resolution lemma gives a compact connected Kähler $Y\to W$. The inverse of the model correspondence, composed with this resolution, is a bimeromorphic map $Y\dashrightarrow X$. Apply theorem 9.65.8 to obtain $\sigma :\widetilde Y\to Y$ and the holomorphic modification $\mu :\widetilde Y\to X$. Each blowup of $\sigma $ preserves compactness and Kählerness by lemma 9.65.6. Its centers have positive codimension; trivial codimension-one steps may be omitted. The complement of a proper analytic center is connected and dense in its blowup, so connectedness persists at every step. Thus $\widetilde Y$ has all the required properties. The only new unproved existence assertion in this last construction is the explicitly stated elimination input. $\square$
