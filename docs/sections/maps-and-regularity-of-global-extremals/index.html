16.32 Maps and regularity of global extremals9D8F31A4DFA0

9D8F31A4DFA0

Use the existing raw global extremal $V_ E$, raw relative extremal $h_{E,\Omega }\in [-1,0]$ and their upper regularizations. For bounded $E$ write $K=\widehat{\overline E}$, the polynomial hull of its compact closure. All dimensions and polynomial degrees below are positive.

Theorem 16.32.1.68FB8247AB5A Let $f:\mathbb C^ n\to \mathbb C^ n$ be holomorphic and let $\alpha ,\beta $ be positive integers. The following are equivalent:

  1. $f$ is polynomial of degree at most $\beta $, and $\liminf _{|z|\to \infty }|f(z)|/|z|^\alpha >0$;

  2. $f$ is proper and, for every $E\subset \mathbb C^ n$,

    \[ \alpha V_{f^{-1}E}\leq V_ E\circ f\leq \beta V_{f^{-1}E}. \]

If $\alpha =\beta =d$, these conditions are also equivalent to $f$ being polynomial of degree at most $d$ with degree-$d$ homogeneous part $f_ d$ satisfying $f_ d^{-1}(0)=\{ 0\} $. In that case every component has degree $d$, and $V_ E\circ f=dV_{f^{-1}E}$.

Proof. The growth lower bound makes $f$ proper, and the checked finite/open proper-map theorem makes it surjective. If $u\in \mathcal L$ is nonpositive on $f^{-1}E$, the fiber maximum $v(y)=\alpha \max _{f(x)=y}u(x)$ is PSH. The growth lower bound gives $v(y)\leq \log ^+|y|+C$, so $v\in \mathcal L$ and $v\leq 0$ on $E$. Thus $\alpha u\leq V_ E\circ f$. Conversely, for a competitor $u$ of $V_ E$, the degree upper bound gives $\beta ^{-1}u\circ f\in \mathcal L$ and makes it a competitor for $f^{-1}E$. Take both suprema.

For the reverse implication put $E=\overline B(0,1)$ and $A=f^{-1}E$. Properness and surjectivity make $A$ compact with nonempty interior. Thus $V_ A^*\in \mathcal L$. The upper inequality and norm-ball formula give $\log ^+|f(z)|\leq \beta (\log ^+|z|+C)$. Cauchy's coefficient estimates on growing polydiscs force every Taylor coefficient of degree greater than $\beta $ to vanish. Enclose $A$ in $\overline B(0,R)$. The other inequality gives $\alpha \log ^+(|z|/R)\leq \log ^+|f(z)|$, proving the growth lower bound.

When the two exponents are $d$, uniform leading-term asymptotics on the unit sphere give $|f_ d(\xi )|\geq c>0$. Conversely this positive minimum gives the lower growth bound, since $f-f_ d=O(|z|^{d-1})$. The leading map is then proper and surjective itself; no component can be identically zero, so every original component has degree $d$. $\square$

Proposition 16.32.2.94014DB9102B Let $f_ j$ have positive degrees $d_ j$, $1\leq j\leq n$, and suppose their highest homogeneous parts have no common zero except zero. For $A=\{ z:|f_ j(z)|\leq 1\text{ for all }j\} $,

\[ V_ A(z)=\max _ j d_ j^{-1}\log ^+|f_ j(z)|. \]

In particular $V_ A$ is continuous. Invertible affine maps also preserve raw extremals by composition.

Proof. Take a common multiple $D$ of the $d_ j$ and put $g_ j=f_ j^{D/d_ j}$. The preceding theorem applies with degree $D$ to $g=(g_1,\ldots ,g_ n)$. The closed polydisc is the unit ball of the complex maximum norm, whose extremal is $\max _ j\log ^+|z_ j|$. Pull it back and divide by $D$. The affine assertion is the degree-one case of the same theorem. $\square$

Proposition 16.32.3.D63431730901 Let $E$ be bounded and $\Omega $ a bounded open neighborhood of $K$. There is $m>0$ with

\[ m(h_{E,\Omega }+1)\leq V_ E\quad \hbox{on }\Omega . \]

If $E$ is non-pluripolar there is also a finite $M>0$ such that $V_ E\leq M(h_{E,\Omega }+1)$. Both inequalities hold after regularization. For every bounded $E$, including polar $E$,

\[ \{ V_ E=0\} =\{ h_{E,\Omega }=-1\} . \]

If $E$ is non-pluripolar and $c>\sup _ KV_ E^*$, then for $\Omega =\{ V_ E^*<c\} $,

\[ V_ E^*=c(h_{E,\Omega }^*+1)\quad \hbox{on }\Omega . \]

Proof. The empty obstacle case is immediate, so assume $E\ne \varnothing $. The raw extremal $V_{\overline E}$ is positive outside $K$, by the polynomial identity and polynomial separation. Its compact thickening extremals increase pointwise to it and are continuous. A finite open cover of $\partial \Omega $ therefore supplies a small thickening $F$ and $m>0$ with $V_ F\geq m$ there; choose the thickening inside $\Omega $. For each relative competitor $v$, glue $\max \{ m(v+1),V_ F\} $ inside $\Omega $ to $V_ F$ outside. The boundary inequality justifies PSH gluing. This function agrees with $V_ F$ near infinity, lies in $\mathcal L$, and is zero on $E$. It is a global competitor, proving the lower bound after taking the supremum. Also $V_ E\geq V_{\overline E}$ puts its zero set inside $K$. This uses the compact closure only for the barrier, and makes no false lower semicontinuity assertion for raw $V_ E$ when $E$ is not closed.

For non-pluripolar $E$, choose $M>\sup _\Omega V_ E^*$, which is finite. Every global competitor $u$ gives the relative competitor $u/M-1$; this proves the upper bound. Even for polar $E$, this argument works for each individual $u$ with its own finite positive upper bound $M_ u$. Consequently $h=-1$ implies that every $u\leq 0$ at the point, hence $V_ E=0$. The lower bound proves the converse. Taking upper regularizations gives the stated star inequalities.

For the last assertion, $\Omega $ is bounded by the enclosing-ball lower profile, is open, and contains $K$. The preceding upper argument uses $c$ itself because every global competitor is below $V_ E^*<c$ there. Conversely glue $\max \{ c(v+1),V_ E^*\} $ inside to $V_ E^*$ outside; at every boundary point the latter is at least $c$. The glued function belongs to $\mathcal L$ and is zero on $E$ except possibly on the pluripolar discrepancy subset where $V_ E^*>0$. The checked polar-obstacle invariance makes it at most $V_ E^*$. Thus $c(h+1)\leq V_ E^*$; regularization and the upper inequality give equality. This uses the normalized polar perturbation already proved internally, without leaving the growth class. $\square$

Proposition 16.32.4.59F7B1095E20 If $E$ is compact and $\Omega $ is an open neighborhood of its polynomial hull, then its PSH hull in $\Omega $ equals that polynomial hull.

Proof. Logarithms of polynomial absolute values show that the PSH hull is contained in the polynomial hull. For the reverse inclusion, if a PSH test $v$ separated a point $a$ of the polynomial hull from $E$, truncate it from below to make $\sup _ Ev$ finite while preserving the strict gap. Choose a bounded $W\Subset \Omega $ containing the hull and $M>\sup _ Wv$. The relative competitor $(v-M)/(M-\sup _ Ev)$ has value greater than $-1$ at $a$. The preceding zero-set equivalence then gives $V_ E(a)>0$, contrary to the polynomial identity. No compact approximation of an arbitrary PSH test is assumed. $\square$

Theorem 16.32.5.15FA5C21941D Let $E$ be bounded and let $f:U\to \mathbb C^ n$ be holomorphic on an open $U\subset \mathbb C^ n$. Suppose $f^{-1}K$ is compact in $U$. Put $Z_ E=\{ V_ E=0\} $ and take all inverse images within $U$. Then

\[ V_{f^{-1}E}=V_{f^{-1}Z_ E}. \]

If $a\in f^{-1}E$ and $V_ E$ is continuous at $f(a)$, then $V_{f^{-1}E}$ is continuous at $a$. For compact $E$, in particular, $f^{-1}\widehat E\subset \widehat{f^{-1}E}$ and these two inverse images have the same polynomial hull.

If instead $f$ is defined near $K$ with values in $\mathbb C^ m$, $a\in E$, $E$ is L-regular at $a$, and $f$ has full target rank generically near $a$, then $f(E)$ is L-regular at $f(a)$.

Proof. First localize the compact inverse image: choose a relatively compact open neighborhood $O$ of it in $U$, whose boundary image misses $K$. A bounded open neighborhood $W$ of $K$ disjoint from $f(\partial O)$ makes $f:O\cap f^{-1}W\to W$ proper. Its image $W_1$ is open by finiteness of Euclidean proper fibers and is closed in $W$ by properness. Put $W_2=W\setminus W_1$ and $O_1=O\cap f^{-1}W$. All original inverse images of points in $K$ lie in $O_1$.

Each $K_ i=K\cap W_ i$ is compact and polynomially convex. Indeed its polynomial hull lies in $K$; the preceding PSH-hull theorem on $W_1\cup W_2$ and a locally constant PSH test exclude the other piece. The relative extremal $h_{E,W}$ restricts on $W_1$ to $h_{E\cap W_1,W_1}$, because bounded competitors on the disjoint open pieces can be chosen independently. Proper-map raw extremal equality therefore gives

\[ h_{f^{-1}E,O_1}=h_{E,W}\circ f\quad \hbox{on }O_1. \]

For $b\in f^{-1}Z_ E$, zero-set equivalence on $W$ makes this value $-1$. Any global competitor $u$ for $A=f^{-1}E$ is bounded above on the bounded relatively compact $O_1$; using $u/M_ u-1$ as a relative competitor proves $u(b)\leq 0$. Hence $A\subset f^{-1}Z_ E\subset \{ V_ A=0\} $. Every competitor for $A$ already satisfies the constraint on the last set, proving the raw envelope identity. For compact $E$, apply the polynomial identity to both compact inverse images to obtain the hull consequence. This does not assert that a local holomorphic inverse image is itself polynomially convex.

Suppose now $E$ is regular at $f(a)$. The global/relative lower bound on $W$ gives $h_{E,W}^*(f(a))=-1$. Proper maps are open, so upper regularization commutes with composition: images of neighborhoods are neighborhoods, which gives both limsup inequalities. If $A$ were pluripolar, its regularized relative extremal would vanish, contradicting the equality at $a$. Thus $A$ is non-pluripolar and $M_ A>\sup _{O_1}V_ A^*$ is finite. The relative upper estimate for $A$ needs no hull assumption on $O_1$. Combining it with the lower estimate for $E$ gives, near $a$,

\[ 0\leq V_ A^*\leq (M_ A/m_ E)V_ E^*\circ f. \]

The right side is zero at $a$; the raw/star sandwich proves continuity.

For the image assertion choose a bounded neighborhood $O\Subset \operatorname {Dom}f$ of $K$ and a large target ball $B$ containing $f(\overline O)$ and the polynomial hull of $f(E)$. Holomorphic relative pullback and the generic rank hypothesis give $h_{f(E),B}^*\circ f\leq h_{E,O}^*$ near $a$. The latter has value $-1$ there by the lower comparison and regularity. Thus the target relative extremal is $-1$ at $f(a)$. The image cannot be pluripolar, since its regularized relative extremal would then be zero. Its finite global-family upper bound and the relative upper comparison give $V_{f(E)}^*(f(a))=0$, as required. $\square$

Example 16.32.6.446A0A9CF0BF The compact inverse-image and hull-neighborhood hypotheses are substantive. For $f(z_1,z_2)=(z_1,z_1z_2^2-z_2)$ take $E=\{ |w_1|=|w_2|=1\} \cup \{ (1/3,0)\} $. Its polynomial hull is the closed unit polydisc, by the maximum principle in each variable. The inverse torus lies in $|z_1|=1$, $|z_2|<2$: for $|z_2|\geq 2$, $|z_1z_2^2-z_2|\geq |z_2|^2-|z_2|>1$. The remaining fiber is $\{ (1/3,0),(1/3,3)\} $. Thus $f^{-1}E$ is compact, but its isolated point $b=(1/3,3)$ is not regular. Indeed deleting it leaves a compact subset of the radius-two polydisc; polar invariance and the norm profile give $V_{f^{-1}E}^*(b)\geq \log (3/2)>0$, whereas the raw value is zero. The target is regular at $f(b)$, since its extremal equals the continuous unit-polydisc profile. The inverse image of the target hull is not compact: it contains $(1/j,j)$ for every positive integer $j$.

For the missing hull neighborhood, take $E=\{ |z|=1\} \cup \{ 1/2\} $ and $f(z)=1/z$. The target set $f(E)=\{ |z|=1\} \cup \{ 2\} $ has raw extremal zero at $2$, but its regularization is $\log ^+|z|$ by polar invariance. The original $E$ has the continuous closed-disc profile. The map is undefined at zero in its hull.

Regularity of an image does not force regularity of an arbitrary set mapping onto it. Let $B=\overline B((1,0),1)\subset \mathbb C^2$, $E=B\cup ([-1,1]\times \{ 0\} )$ and $f(z_1,z_2)=(z_1^2,z_2)$. The added segment maps into $f(B)$, so $f(E)=f(B)$ is regular by the image theorem. The segment is pluripolar, being contained in $\{ z_2=0\} $. Hence $V_ E^*=V_ B$, which is strictly positive at every point of the added segment outside $B$, although $V_ E=0$ there.

Proposition 16.32.7.5886156A306E Let $h$ be holomorphic near $[0,1]$. If $V_ E$ is continuous and zero at every point $h(t)$, $0<t\leq 1$, then it is continuous and zero at $h(0)$. Consequently the closure of a bounded open set with $C^1$ boundary is L-regular.

Proof. The hypothesis at one point excludes a pluripolar $E$ and makes $V_ E^*$ proper and finite. Its pullback along $h$ is nonnegative SH. It vanishes on $(0,1]$; nonthinness of a planar interval forces its value at zero to vanish as well. A constant map is already covered by the hypothesis. Upper semicontinuity and $0\leq V_ E\leq V_ E^*$ give the conclusion. This is a zero-contact assertion even if $h(0)$ belongs only to the closure of $E$.

At a $C^1$ boundary point a normal affine segment lies inside the open set except at its endpoint, by a local defining function with nonzero normal derivative. Interior points are zero-contact points by the contained-ball profile. Apply the first assertion to that segment. The raw compact lower semicontinuity and zero-star criterion then give continuity globally for the compact closure. $\square$

Proposition 16.32.8.7C8B2A4D660E Every compact $K\subset \mathbb R^ m$ has bounded open neighborhoods $D_ j$ with smooth boundary such that $\overline D_{j+1}\subset D_ j$ and $\bigcap _ jD_ j=K$. In $\mathbb C^ n$ their closures are L-regular.

Proof. We first give the needed equal-dimensional critical-value argument. If $F:U\subset \mathbb R^ m\to \mathbb R^ m$ is $C^1$, its critical values have measure zero. On a fixed compact cube in $U$, partition into small side-$h$ cubes meeting the critical set. At a critical point in such a cube, $DF$ has rank at most $m-1$. Uniform continuity of $DF$ bounds the Taylor error throughout the cube by $\epsilon h$ for small $h$. If $\| DF\| \leq M$, its image therefore lies in a box with at most $m-1$ side lengths $C_ m(M+1)h$ and one side length $C_ m\epsilon h$. Its outer volume is at most $C_{m,M}\epsilon h^ m$. There are $O(h^{-m})$ cubes. Letting $\epsilon \downarrow 0$ proves the assertion on this compact cube; a countable interior cube cover proves it on $U$.

It suffices to construct $K\subset D\Subset \{ \operatorname {dist}(x,K)<\eta \} $ for arbitrary $\eta >0$. The empty case uses $D=\varnothing $. For nonempty $K$, convolve its distance function with a smooth kernel of radius $\delta <\eta /8$ to obtain $g\in C^\infty (\mathbb R^ m)$ with $|g-\operatorname {dist}(\cdot ,K)|\leq \delta $. For $|a|<1/2$, $g_ a=g-a\cdot x$ tends to infinity uniformly as $|x|\to \infty $, since $g_ a\geq |x|/2-\sup _ K|x|-\delta $. Choose a large fixed ball outside which all these functions exceed $\eta $, and then restrict $a$ further so $|a\cdot x|<\eta /8$ on that ball. The preceding critical-value argument applied to $F=\nabla g$ supplies such an arbitrarily small regular value $a$. Every critical point of $g_ a$ then has invertible Hessian and is isolated. There are only finitely many critical points in the compact band $\{ \eta /3\leq g_ a\leq 2\eta /3\} $, because an accumulation would be a nonisolated critical point. Choose a value $c\in (\eta /3,2\eta /3)$ avoiding their values.

The set $D=\{ g_ a<c\} $ has smooth boundary by the implicit-function theorem. On $K$, $g_ a<\eta /4$; where the distance to $K$ is at least $\eta $, $g_ a>3\eta /4$ on the fixed ball and exceeds $\eta $ outside it. Thus $D$ has the required compact containment. Choose each next $\eta $ also small enough that the corresponding closed distance neighborhood of $K$ lies in the preceding $D_ j$, and make $\eta \downarrow 0$. This gives the nested sequence and its intersection. Its L-regularity follows from the preceding $C^1$ boundary assertion. Only the proved equal-dimensional $C^1$ critical-value lemma is needed, not a general Sard theorem as an unverified reference. $\square$
