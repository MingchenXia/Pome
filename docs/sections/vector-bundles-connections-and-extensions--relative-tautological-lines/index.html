10.37 Relative tautological lines6D2772AA0B40

6D2772AA0B40

Projective spaces parametrize lines. Let $E$ be a holomorphic bundle of rank $r\geq 1$ on a complex manifold $X$. Set $\pi :\mathbb P(E^*)\to X$. All duals are complex linear, and Hermitian forms are linear in the first variable. Chern curvature $\Theta $ is unscaled, so the normalized curvature of a line is $\mathrm i\Theta /(2\pi )$.

Proposition 10.37.1.B8693F38C65B The space $\mathbb P(E^*)$ is a complex manifold, locally a product with $\mathbb P^{r-1}$. The subspaces $S_{[\xi ]}=\ker \xi \subset E_{\pi ([\xi ])}$ form a holomorphic subbundle of $\pi ^*E$, and there is an exact sequence

\[ 0\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow \pi ^*E \longrightarrow \mathcal O_ E(1)\longrightarrow 0. \]

The dual of $\mathcal O_ E(1)$ is canonically the tautological line subbundle of $\pi ^*E^*$. The restriction of $\mathcal O_ E(1)$ to each projective fiber is $\mathcal O(1)$ with the usual dual-tautological convention. For rank one, $\mathbb P(E^*)=X$ and $\mathcal O_ E(1)=E$. For rank two the kernel map canonically identifies $\mathbb P(E^*)$ with $\mathbb P(E)$.

Proof. Trivializations of $E$ induce product charts on $\mathbb P(E^*)$; their changes act by projectivized invertible holomorphic matrices, and are holomorphic in the standard affine projective charts. These charts give the complex structure. The total space is Hausdorff: distinct projected points are separated in $X$, and points in the same fiber are separated within a product chart, whose base can be shrunk. A countable trivializing cover and the finite standard projective atlas give second countability.

In a local frame $e_1,\ldots ,e_ r$, on the chart $\xi _1\ne 0$ write $\xi =e_1^*+\sum _{\lambda =2}^ r\xi _\lambda e_\lambda ^*$. The vectors $e_\lambda -\xi _\lambda e_1$, $2\leq \lambda \leq r$, are a holomorphic frame of its kernel, and the class of $e_1$ frames the quotient. The same argument in all charts proves the subbundle and exactness assertions. The evaluation pairing

\[ E_ x/\ker \xi \ \longrightarrow (\mathbb C\xi )^*,\qquad [v]\longmapsto \bigl (\eta \longmapsto \eta (v)\bigr ) \]

is well defined, nonzero and holomorphic in these charts; it is therefore the canonical quotient-line identification. On a fiber this is precisely the dual of the tautological line. For $r=1$ the kernel is zero and the projection has singleton fibers, so the product charts give the asserted identification. For $r=2$ the kernel itself is a line. In coordinates its map is $[\xi _1:\xi _2]\mapsto [-\xi _2:\xi _1]$, with holomorphic inverse given by taking the annihilator. The construction commutes with changes of frame. No general identification of line and hyperplane projectivizations in other ranks is asserted. $\square$

Write $\mathcal O_ E(k)=\mathcal O_ E(1)^{\otimes k}$, using dual powers for negative integers.

Theorem 10.37.2.CB738223B3E9 For every $k\geq 0$ evaluation induces a natural sheaf isomorphism

\[ \mathcal O(\operatorname {Sym}^ kE) \ \cong \ \pi _*\mathcal O_ E(k). \]

If $r\geq 2$, then $\pi _*\mathcal O_ E(k)=0$ for $k<0$. If $r=1$, then $\pi _*\mathcal O_ E(k)=\mathcal O(E^{\otimes k})$ for every integer $k$.

Proof. The symmetric power of the quotient map in the proposition, composed with pullback of sections, gives the displayed sheaf morphism. Its construction is intrinsic and commutes with restriction. Check it over an open trivialization $E|_ U=U\times V$. The fiber section proposition 10.51.5, in scalar degree, identifies $H^0(\mathbb P(V^*),\mathcal O(k))$ with $\operatorname {Sym}^ kV$ for $k\geq 0$; it also proves negative vanishing when $\dim V\geq 2$. Its rank-one endpoint is separate.

We check holomorphic dependence on the base, rather than infer a parameter statement from this fiberwise identification. Fix $k\geq 0$ and a basis $p_1,\ldots ,p_ M$ of the degree-$k$ homogeneous polynomials on $V^*$. There are $M$ nonzero covectors $\eta _1,\ldots ,\eta _ M$ such that $(p_ j(\eta _ l))_{l,j}$ is invertible. Indeed evaluations span the dual of the polynomial space: a polynomial annihilated by every evaluation is zero, as follows by successively viewing it as a polynomial in each coordinate. A spanning set of a finite-dimensional dual space contains a basis, proving the claim.

For a holomorphic section $s$ on $U\times \mathbb P(V^*)$, evaluate its restriction at $[\eta _ l]$ against the fixed tautological tensor $\eta _ l^{\otimes k}$. The resulting scalars $f_ l(x)$ are holomorphic on $U$, because this is evaluation along a holomorphic constant projective section with a fixed nonvanishing line frame. On each fiber write its unique polynomial as $\sum _ ja_ j(x)p_ j$. The invertible constant evaluation matrix gives

\[ (a_ j(x))_ j=(p_ j(\eta _ l))_{l,j}^{-1}(f_ l(x))_ l. \]

Thus all coefficients are holomorphic. Conversely holomorphic coefficients define a joint holomorphic section in each projective chart by polynomial evaluation. The two constructions are inverse. Their intrinsic description ensures compatibility with changes of trivialization, giving the asserted sheaf isomorphism. For $k<0$ and $r\geq 2$, every fiber restriction is zero, hence the original section vanishes at every point. For $r=1$ the preceding proposition identifies $\pi $ with the identity and $\mathcal O_ E(k)$ with $E^{\otimes k}$, including negative $k$. $\square$

Proposition 10.37.3.CFED58EB29EF Give $E$ a smooth Hermitian metric and $\mathcal O_ E(1)$ its quotient metric. At $x_0$, choose holomorphic coordinates $z$ and a holomorphic frame $e_\lambda $ which is unitary and Chern-normal there. Write

\[ \Theta (E)_{x_0}e_\lambda =\sum _{j,k,\mu }c_{jk\lambda \mu }\, dz_ j\wedge d\bar z_ k\otimes e_\mu . \]

For a unit covector $a=\sum _\lambda a_\lambda e_\lambda ^*$, choose an orthonormal basis $b_1,\ldots ,b_{r-1}$ of $a^\perp $ and projective coordinates represented by $a+\sum _\alpha \zeta _\alpha b_\alpha $. Using the splitting given by this Chern-normal frame, at $(x_0,[a])$ one has

\[ \Theta (\mathcal O_ E(1)) =\sum _{j,k,\lambda ,\mu }c_{jk\mu \lambda } a_\lambda \bar a_\mu \, dz_ j\wedge d\bar z_ k +\sum _{\alpha =1}^{r-1}d\zeta _\alpha \wedge d\bar\zeta _\alpha . \]

The horizontal term is equivalently $-\langle \Theta (E^*)a,a\rangle $. There is no mixed horizontal–vertical block at this point.

Proof. The existing Chern-normal-frame construction the Levi form of a reciprocal bundle norm makes the first derivatives of the metric zero. We may additionally remove its pure holomorphic and antiholomorphic quadratic terms. In column-matrix notation, if the normal metric is

\[ M=I+B(z)+B(z)^*+\sum _{j,k}C_{j\bar k}z_ j\bar z_ k+O(|z|^3), \]

with $B$ homogeneous holomorphic quadratic, the holomorphic frame change $g=I-B(z)$ replaces $M$ by $g^*Mg$ and removes $B+B^*$. It does not change the frame or its first jet at zero and hence does not change the displayed tangent splitting or curvature coefficients. The canonical formula $\Theta =\bar\partial (M^{-1}\partial M)$ gives $\Theta _{j\bar k}(0)=-C_{j\bar k}$. Transposing to the matrix of the linear-first products gives

\[ \langle e_\lambda ,e_\mu \rangle =\delta _{\lambda \mu }-\sum _{j,k}c_{jk\lambda \mu }z_ j\bar z_ k +O(|z|^3). \]

The dual metric is the transpose of its inverse, so

\[ \langle e_\lambda ^*,e_\mu ^*\rangle =\delta _{\lambda \mu }+\sum _{j,k}c_{jk\mu \lambda }z_ j\bar z_ k +O(|z|^3). \]

The tautological line $\mathcal O_ E(-1)$ has the nonzero local holomorphic section $\varepsilon (z,\zeta )=a(z)+\sum _\alpha \zeta _\alpha b_\alpha (z)$, where the coefficients in the dual frame are constant. Orthonormality of $a,b_\alpha $ at the center gives

\[ |\varepsilon (z,\zeta )|^2 =1+\sum _\alpha |\zeta _\alpha |^2 +\sum _{j,k,\lambda ,\mu }c_{jk\mu \lambda }a_\lambda \bar a_\mu z_ j\bar z_ k+O(|(z,\zeta )|^3). \]

All omitted base–fiber terms have total degree at least three; the remainder is a joint remainder in both kinds of variables. The dual metric of the quotient line is exactly this restricted dual metric: the minimal-norm representative of a quotient class is orthogonal to $\ker \xi $, and evaluation by $\xi $ on that line has norm $|\xi |$. Thus its duality with the tautological line is isometric. Line curvature therefore gives

\[ \Theta (\mathcal O_ E(1))=\partial \bar\partial \log |\varepsilon (z,\zeta )|^2. \]

The first derivatives at the center vanish, so its Hessian is the quadratic expression displayed in the statement. The general dual curvature identity $\Theta (E^*)=-\Theta (E)^{\mathsf T}$ follows by applying the square of the dual connection to the evaluation pairing: the two curvature actions sum to zero. It proves the invariant horizontal description, with the indicated index order. For rank one the vertical sum is empty and the formula is the ordinary line curvature. If $X$ has dimension zero only the projective-fiber Fubini–Study term remains; for rank one over a point both sums vanish. $\square$
