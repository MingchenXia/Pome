16.38 Geometric polynomial approximation and local uniquenessC1EC131C19C3

C1EC131C19C3

Write $V_ K$ for the raw logarithmic extremal and $V_ K^*$ for its upper semicontinuous regularization. For a nonempty compact set $K\subset \mathbb C^ n$, say that $K$ is locally determining if, whenever a connected open set $W$ meets $K$, every holomorphic function on $W$ vanishing on $K\cap W$ is identically zero on $W$. For $f\in C(K)$ put

\[ e_ j(f,K)=\inf _{\deg p\le j}\| f-p\| _ K. \]

Theorem 16.38.1.DE0691E14C0E Let $K$ be nonempty, compact and locally determining. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. $V_ K$ is finite and continuous on $\mathbb C^ n$;

  2. every $f\in C(K)$ satisfying $\limsup _{j\to \infty } e_ j(f,K)^{1/j}<1$ extends holomorphically to an open neighborhood of $K$.

The implication from the first condition to the second does not require local determination.

Proof. Assume the first condition and choose $0<q<1$ and polynomials $p_ j$ with $\deg p_ j\le j$ and $\| f-p_ j\| _ K\le Cq^ j$. Such a choice follows directly from the strict limsup inequality, after increasing $C$ to cover finitely many initial indices. The Bernstein–Walsh bound gives, for $0<a<-\log q$ and $U=\{ V_ K<a\} $,

\[ \| p_{j+1}-p_ j\| _ U \le 2Cq^ j e^{(j+1)a}. \]

The open set $U$ contains $K$, because the raw extremal vanishes there. Thus $p_ j$ converges uniformly on $U$, and its holomorphic limit restricts to $f$ on $K$. This proves the first implication.

For the converse fix $0<q<1$. Let $\mathcal P_ j$ be the polynomials of degree at most $j$, with the norm $\| \cdot \| _ K$. This is a norm: a polynomial vanishing on $K$ vanishes on $\mathbb C^ n$ by local determination. It is a complete finite-dimensional normed space. Explicitly, the point evaluations on $K$ span its dual, so finitely many of them control all coefficients in a polynomial basis. Consider the Banach space

\[ X_ q=\left\{ (p_ j)_{j\ge 0}:p_ j\in \mathcal P_ j,\quad \| (p_ j)\| _{X_ q}=\sum _{j\ge 0}q^{-j}\| p_ j\| _ K<\infty \right\} . \]

Completeness follows by taking coordinate limits of a Cauchy sequence, then using the sum bound for the tails. The continuous linear map $T:X_ q\to C(K)$ defined by $T(p_ j)=\sum _ jp_ j|_ K$ satisfies

\[ e_ N(T(p_ j),K)\le q^{N+1}\| (p_ j)\| _{X_ q}. \]

Every element of its image therefore extends by hypothesis.

Put $U_ m=\{ \operatorname {dist}(z,K)<1/m\} $. For positive integers $m,M$ let $A_{m,M}$ be the set of $x\in X_ q$ for which $Tx$ has a holomorphic extension to $U_ m$ bounded there by $M$. These closed sets cover $X_ q$. The covering follows by compactness of $K$: any holomorphic extension is bounded on the closure of a sufficiently small $U_ m$. To check closedness, take $x_\ell \to x$ and bounded extensions $H_\ell $. Cauchy estimates on relatively compact polydiscs and compactness of equicontinuous families give a subsequence converging uniformly on compact subsets of $U_ m$. A diagonal exhaustion handles all its components. The limit is holomorphic, bounded by $M$, and restricts to $Tx$ on the compact set $K$.

Baire's theorem gives a ball $B(x_0,r)\subset A_{m,M}$ for some $m,M,r>0$. Subtracting extensions for $x_0+x$ and $x_0$, then rescaling, shows that every $Tx$ has an extension to this fixed $U_ m$ bounded by $C_ q\| x\| _{X_ q}$, where $C_ q=4M/r$ is sufficient. For a polynomial $p$ of degree $d$, take the sequence with only its $d$th coordinate nonzero. Its extension agrees with $p$ on every component of $U_ m$: each component meets $K$, and local determination applies to the difference. Consequently

\[ \| p\| _{U_ m}\le C_ q q^{-d}\| p\| _ K. \]

Every component meets $K$ since each point of $U_ m$ can be joined to a nearest point of $K$ by a segment in $U_ m$. Since $q<1$ was arbitrary, the canonical neighborhood Bernstein–Walsh criterion now gives finiteness and continuity of the raw extremal $V_ K$. $\square$

Example 16.38.2.C6B88322442F Global uniqueness on connected neighborhoods of $K$ cannot replace local determination in this characterization. Take $K=\overline\Delta \cup \{ 2\} \subset \mathbb C$. Every holomorphic function on a connected neighborhood of $K$ vanishing on $K$ is zero, by the identity principle on $\Delta $. Nevertheless $V_ K$ is discontinuous at $2$. In fact it is zero on $K$ and equals $\log ^+|z|$ off $K$: the upper bound is the disc extremal, and for $|z|>1$, $z\ne 2$, the polynomials

\[ P_ j(z)=\tfrac 13(z-2)z^{j-1},\qquad \| P_ j\| _ K\le 1, \]

give the matching lower bound by taking $j$th roots.

Yet every geometrically approximable $f\in C(K)$ has a holomorphic extension to some neighborhood of $K$. Its restriction to the disc extends by the first implication to a slightly larger disc; choose that disc to have radius less than $3/2$. On a disjoint small disc around $2$ use the constant $f(2)$. This gives the required extension. Thus the weaker uniqueness condition does not give the raw-extremal converse. Also $V_ K^*=\log ^+|z|$ is continuous, but the neighborhood Bernstein–Walsh bounds for every $t>1$ fail, by the raw criterion.

Example 16.38.3.61CD9F3FD5EA Continuity of $V_ K^*$ does not by itself imply the extension property, even when $K$ is a uniqueness set on every connected neighborhood containing it. Let

\[ a_ j=2+2^{-j},\qquad K=\overline\Delta \cup \{ 2,a_1,a_2,\ldots \} ,\qquad b_ j=\begin{cases} e^{-j^4},& j\text{ odd},\\ 0,& j\text{ even}. \end{cases} \]

Define $f=0$ on $\overline\Delta \cup \{ 2\} $ and $f(a_ j)=b_ j$. This is continuous. It has no holomorphic extension near $2$: the even subsequence would force that extension to vanish identically there, contradicting its positive values on the odd subsequence. The added countable set is polar, so polar obstacle invariance gives $V_ K^*=V_{\overline\Delta }^*=\log ^+|z|$, a continuous function. The disc again makes $K$ a uniqueness set in the stated global sense.

We verify geometric approximation explicitly. For $1\le j\le n$ put

\[ L_{j,n}(z)=\frac{z-2}{a_ j-2} \prod _{\substack {1\le k\le n\\ k\ne j}} \frac{z-a_ k}{a_ j-a_ k},\qquad Q_ n(z)=\sum _{j=1}^ n b_ jL_{j,n}(z)(z/a_ j)^{8n^2}. \]

Then $\deg Q_ n\le N_ n=8n^2+n$, $Q_ n(2)=0$, and $Q_ n(a_ j)=b_ j$ for $j\le n$. On the unit disc each numerator in $L_{j,n}$ is at most $4$ in modulus. Also

\[ |a_ j-2|=2^{-j},\qquad |a_ j-a_ k|\ge 2^{-\max (j,k)}, \]

so $\| L_{j,n}\| _{\overline\Delta }\le 2^{n^2+2n}$. Since $a_ j>2$,

\[ \| Q_ n\| _{\overline\Delta }\le n\, 2^{-7n^2+2n}. \]

For $i>n$, separate the product factors with $k<j$ and $k>j$. The positive constant

\[ C_0=\prod _{\ell \ge 1}(1-2^{-\ell })^{-1}<\infty \]

is finite because the sum of the corresponding negative logarithms converges. Direct cancellation of the powers of $2$ gives

\[ |L_{j,n}(a_ i)| \le C_0^2\, 2^{j-i} 2^{-(n-j)(n-j+1)/2}. \]

Moreover $a_ i\le a_ j$, so the extra power in $Q_ n$ is at most $1$. Splitting the sum at $j=n/2$ therefore gives, uniformly for $i>n$,

\[ |Q_ n(a_ i)|\le C\bigl (2^{-n^2/8}+n e^{-n^4/16}\bigr ) \le C'2^{-n^2/8}. \]

The omitted values satisfy $|b_ i|\le e^{-(n+1)^4}$. Combining all parts of $K$ yields

\[ \| f-Q_ n\| _ K\le C''2^{-n^2/8}. \]

For $N_ n\le m<N_{n+1}$ use $Q_ n$ as the degree-$m$ approximant. It follows that

\[ \limsup _{m\to \infty }e_ m(f,K)^{1/m} \le 2^{-1/64}<1. \]

Thus this $f$ satisfies the geometric approximation condition but has no holomorphic extension to any neighborhood of $K$.
