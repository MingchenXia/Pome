6.34 Weights and generalized Lelong masses5A44A01C3550

5A44A01C3550

Use $dd^ c=\frac{i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial $. A weight $\varphi :X\to [-\infty ,\infty )$ will be plurisubharmonic and extended-continuous, meaning that $e^\varphi $ is continuous. Write $B_\varphi (r)=\{ \varphi <r\} $, $P_\varphi =\{ \varphi =-\infty \} $ and $\varphi _ s=\max \{ \varphi ,s\} $. Then $\varphi _ s$ is finite, continuous and locally bounded. For a closed set $S$ we say that $\varphi $ is semi-exhaustive on $S$ if $S\cap \{ \varphi \leq R\} $ is compact for some real $R$. The version with a relatively compact open sublevel implies this condition after decreasing $R$. All masses below involve the support of the current, rather than a compactness assumption on ambient sublevels disjoint from that support.

Lemma 6.34.1.ECBFCBBB5EF1 Let $T$ be closed positive of bidimension $(p,p)$, and put $S=\operatorname {supp}T$. Suppose two continuous locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions $a,b$ agree on $S\cap D$ outside a compact set $K\subset D$, where $D$ is open and all measures under consideration have finite mass there. If their products agree on $S$ near $\partial D$, then

\[ \int _ D T\wedge (dd^ ca)^ p=\int _ D T\wedge (dd^ cb)^ p. \]

It suffices that the differences and their coefficient primitives be compactly supported in $D$; no regular boundary is required.

Proof. For $p=0$ both measures are $T$. For $p\geq 1$, bounded recursion and symmetry give

\[ T\wedge (dd^ ca)^ p-T\wedge (dd^ cb)^ p =dd^ c\left((a-b)\sum _{j=0}^{p-1} T\wedge (dd^ ca)^ j\wedge (dd^ cb)^{p-1-j}\right). \]

Every current inside the sum is supported in $S$, so the primitive is compactly supported in $D$. Extend it by zero and test with a compact smooth function equal to one near that support. Its $dd^ c$ pairing is zero. The product measures agree elsewhere in $D$, which proves the equality. This is the support-relative form of lemma 6.33.7; all scalar multiplications and identities used here are the bounded recursion of locality and positivity of bounded Bedford–Taylor products, decreasing continuity of bounded Bedford–Taylor products. The same recursion shows relative locality: equality of potentials on $S$ in a relative neighborhood suffices for equality of the corresponding bounded products there. $\square$

Theorem 6.34.2.57F9C2832703 Let $T$ be closed positive of bidimension $(p,p)$ and let $\varphi $ be semi-exhaustive on $S=\operatorname {supp}T$. For $s<r<R$ the finite number

E50D452944E2 6.34.3
\begin{equation} M_ T(\varphi ,r)=\int _{B_\varphi (r)}T\wedge (dd^ c\varphi _ s)^ p \end{equation}

is independent of $s$. It is nonnegative, nondecreasing and left continuous in $r$, and

\[ \nu (T,\varphi )=\lim _{r\to -\infty }M_ T(\varphi ,r)<\infty \]

exists. For $p=0$ these are $T(B_\varphi (r))$ and $T(P_\varphi )$.

If a neighborhood of the compact low support sublevels admits a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion as in theorem 7.31.2, the classical measure $\sigma _\varphi =T\wedge (dd^ c\varphi )^ p$ is defined there, including its pole mass, and

268DE476A8A7 6.34.4
\begin{equation} M_ T(\varphi ,r)=\sigma _\varphi (B_\varphi (r)),\qquad \nu (T,\varphi )=\sigma _\varphi (P_\varphi ). \end{equation}

The truncation definition itself requires no such ambient exhaustion.

Proof. Closed low sublevels on $S$ are compact. If $s,t<r$, the two potentials agree wherever $\varphi >\max \{ s,t\} $, and their bounded-product difference and its primitive are supported in $S\cap \{ \varphi \leq \max \{ s,t\} \} \Subset B_\varphi (r)$. The preceding lemma proves independence. Fixing a common truncation below two given levels gives monotonicity. Fixing one below an increasing sequence of levels gives left continuity by continuity from below of a fixed finite measure. A fixed larger level bounds the increasing masses from above, so their limit at $-\infty $ is finite. The measure case $p=0$ follows directly by continuity from above on the nested compact low support sublevels.

Extended continuity makes $L(\varphi )=P_\varphi $. Under the stated exhaustion hypothesis, that unbounded locus on $S$ is compact and avoids a relative boundary. Thus theorem 7.31.2 defines the classical products and gives their convergence for $\varphi _ s\downarrow \varphi $. At every level carrying no $\sigma _\varphi $-mass, weak convergence and the compact support cutoff give (6.34.4). Only countably many levels can carry mass in any compact range. Approximation from below by other levels gives the open-sublevel formula at every $r$; approximation from above gives its closed-sublevel version. Finally the low sublevels decrease to $P_\varphi $ on $S$, giving the pole formula. This argument does not assert a classical unbounded product on an arbitrary non-Stein space. $\square$

Proposition 6.34.5.E1AA7F10EA92 For $p\geq 1$ and every finite convex nondecreasing $\chi :\mathbb R\to \mathbb R$,

70CF1825304A 6.34.6
\begin{equation} \int _{B_\varphi (r)}T\wedge \bigl (dd^ c(\chi \circ \varphi )\bigr )^ p =\chi '_-(r)^ p M_ T(\varphi ,r). \end{equation}

On the left use the classical product when available, or the independent inner truncations $\chi \circ \varphi _ s$, $s<r$. In particular, with $N_\varphi =T\wedge (dd^ ce^\varphi )^ p$,

2B06A37CFD8E 6.34.7
\begin{equation} M_ T(\varphi ,r)=e^{-pr}N_\varphi (B_\varphi (r)),\qquad M_ T(\varphi ,r+)=e^{-pr}N_\varphi (\{ \varphi \leq r\} ). \end{equation}

Moreover $\nu (T,\lambda \varphi +c)=\lambda ^ p\nu (T,\varphi )$ for $\lambda >0$ and $c\in \mathbb R$. For $p=0$ use the direct measure definition, without a power of a possibly zero derivative.

Proof. Convex nondecreasing composition preserves plurisubharmonicity: for smooth functions this follows from the positive chain-rule Hessian, and smoothing the convex function and the bounded continuous inner potential proves the assertion by local uniform convergence. The bounded-product uniform-continuity theorem uniform convergence of bounded Bedford–Taylor products justifies the same compositions and products in the following piecewise-linear constructions. All integrals with different inner cutoffs agree by the collar lemma.

Put $a_\epsilon =\chi '_-(r-\epsilon )$. Extend $\chi $ linearly above $r-\epsilon $ with slope $a_\epsilon $; call the result $\chi ^-_\epsilon $. It is convex, agrees with $\chi $ below that level and is affine near $r$. Its mass over $B_\varphi (r)$ is $a_\epsilon ^ p M_ T(\varphi ,r)$ by relative collar equality with the affine potential. Positivity and locality give

\[ M_{\chi \circ \varphi }(B_\varphi (r-\epsilon )) \leq a_\epsilon ^ p M_ T(\varphi ,r). \]

Here the notation on the left denotes the composed measure on the indicated original sublevel, not a sublevel of $\chi \circ \varphi $. Conversely extend $\chi $ linearly below $r-\epsilon $ with that slope, leaving it unchanged above; call this $\chi ^+_\epsilon $. It has the same total mass as $\chi \circ \varphi $ over $B_\varphi (r)$ and has mass $a_\epsilon ^ pM_ T(\varphi ,r-\epsilon )$ in the smaller sublevel. These two inequalities, left continuity of the fixed composed measure and $a_\epsilon \to \chi '_-(r)$ prove (6.34.6). Take $\chi (t)=e^ t$ and then approach $r$ from above to obtain (6.34.7), including its closed endpoint. For an affine function, changing the sublevel variable gives the last assertion directly. $\square$

Theorem 6.34.8.D75AE58A4C84 Suppose $T_ j\to T$ weakly and all their supports lie in a fixed closed set $S$. If $\varphi $ is semi-exhaustive on $S$, then

F3579EFF0991 6.34.9
\begin{equation} M_ T(\varphi ,r)\leq \liminf _ jM_{T_ j}(\varphi ,r) \leq \limsup _ jM_{T_ j}(\varphi ,r)\leq M_ T(\varphi ,r+), \end{equation}

and $\limsup _ j\nu (T_ j,\varphi )\leq \nu (T,\varphi )$.

For varying weights $\varphi _ j$ with $e^{\varphi _ j}\to e^\varphi $ locally uniformly, put $U=B_\varphi (R)$. The weights are semi-exhaustive on $S\cap U$ at any fixed lower outer level for large $j$, and

\[ \limsup _ j M_{T_ j|U}(\varphi _ j,r+) \leq M_ T(\varphi ,r+)\quad (r<R). \]

Consequently their generalized numbers computed in this fixed $U$ are upper semicontinuous. This applies to the total numbers only when their relevant pole sets are eventually confined to $U$, or when the entire discussion is made on this common local domain.

Proof. The bounded continuous potentials $e^\varphi $ and the varying-current uniform-continuity theorem give $N_ j=T_ j\wedge (dd^ ce^\varphi )^ p\to N_\varphi $ weakly. The open/closed set inequalities for positive measures, with a compact cutoff equal to one near the low support sublevel, and (6.34.7) give (6.34.9). As $r\to -\infty $ the right limits decrease to $\nu (T,\varphi )$, while each $\nu (T_ j,\varphi )\leq M_{T_ j}(\varphi ,r)$. This proves the first number inequality, including $p=0$.

For varying weights, uniform convergence on the compact set $S\cap \{ \varphi \leq R\} $ implies, for every $r+\epsilon <R$,

\[ \{ \varphi _ j\leq r\} \cap S\cap U \subset S\cap \{ \varphi <r+\epsilon /2\} \]

for all large $j$. Choosing a fixed intermediate outer level also proves the stated relative semi-exhaustion in $U$. Now uniform convergence of bounded Bedford–Taylor products gives $T_ j\wedge (dd^ ce^{\varphi _ j})^ p\to N_\varphi $ on the fixed compact neighborhood. Bound the integral over the moving closed set by a compact cutoff supported in $\{ \varphi <r+\epsilon \} $ relative to $S$. The limit superior is at most $e^{-pr}N_\varphi (\{ \varphi \leq r+\epsilon \} )$. Let $\epsilon \downarrow 0$ and use continuity from above, obtaining the claimed closed-level bound. Then let $r\to -\infty $. Every cutoff is only required to contain the compact support slice; no ambient sublevel compactness is inferred. $\square$

Theorem 6.34.10.F9C08C00DB8C Let $p\geq 1$, and let $\varphi ,\psi $ be semi-exhaustive on $S=\operatorname {supp}T$. Suppose $P_\psi \cap S\subset P_\varphi \cap S$ and

\[ \ell =\limsup _{\substack {x\in S,\ \varphi (x)>-\infty \\ \varphi (x)\to -\infty }} \frac{\psi (x)}{\varphi (x)}\in [0,\infty ). \]

Interpret the hypothesis as an eventual inequality on sufficiently low finite $\varphi $-sublevels; if that part of $S$ is empty, use the inequality itself. Then $\nu (T,\psi )\leq \ell ^ p\nu (T,\varphi )$. For a common pole set, a positive finite ratio limit gives equality. A zero ratio limit gives $\nu (T,\psi )=0$. For $p=0$ the conclusion is instead the direct pole-set measure inequality, with equality for a common pole set.

Proof. Take $\lambda >\ell $ and a number $b$ strictly between them. For sufficiently negative finite $\varphi $, the ratio bound says $\psi \geq b\varphi $. Choose a low level $r$ and then $a<r$. On the compact support strip $a\leq \varphi \leq r$, $\psi $ is bounded above. Therefore for large $c$ the weight

\[ u=\max \{ \psi -c,\lambda \varphi \} \]

equals $\lambda \varphi $ on a relative collar of that strip. On sufficiently low $\varphi $-sublevels, $\psi -c-\lambda \varphi \geq (b-\lambda )\varphi -c>0$. The same equality $u=\psi -c$ holds at $\varphi =-\infty $, with the extended values understood. Since all poles of $\psi $ on $S$ lie there, compactness of a low $\psi $-sublevel shows that all sufficiently low $\psi $-sublevels lie in this equality region. Moreover $u$ is semi-exhaustive on $S$, its pole set there is exactly $P_\psi \cap S$, and relative locality gives $\nu (T,u)=\nu (T,\psi )$.

For a sufficiently low inner truncation, the collar lemma identifies the total mass of $T\wedge (dd^ c\max \{ u,s\} )^ p$ in $B_\varphi (r)$ with $\lambda ^ pM_ T(\varphi ,r)$. A sufficiently small $u$-sublevel is contained in this set. Positivity then gives $\nu (T,\psi )\leq \lambda ^ pM_ T(\varphi ,r)$. Let $r\to -\infty $ and $\lambda \downarrow \ell $. For a positive ratio limit and common poles, interchange the two weights and use the reciprocal limit. If $\ell =0$, the preceding inequality already gives zero. For $p=0$ use $\nu (T,\varphi )=T(P_\varphi )$ directly. $\square$

Corollary 6.34.11.97D1C4B2406B Weights with a common pole set on $\operatorname {supp}T$ and a bounded difference near it have the same generalized number. In particular the ordinary logarithmic weight $\log |z-a|^2$ gives exactly the ordinary number of theorem 6.33.1, in any coordinates.

Proof. The ratio limit is one; use the preceding theorem and the direct $p=0$ pole measure. For the radial identification use lemma 6.33.8, whose open-ball mass is the present truncation mass at $r=\log \rho ^2$. $\square$

Theorem 6.34.12.09A352404A2E Let $1\leq q\leq p$. Suppose both tuples $(u_1,\ldots ,u_ q)$ and $(v_1,\ldots ,v_ q)$ satisfy the mutual intersection hypotheses of theorem 7.28.1 with respect to $T$. Let $\varphi $ be semi-exhaustive on $S=\operatorname {supp}T$, and assume $u_ j=-\infty $ on $S\cap P_\varphi $. If

\[ \ell _ j=\limsup _{\substack {z\in S,\ u_ j(z)>-\infty \\ \varphi (z)\to -\infty }} \frac{v_ j(z)}{u_ j(z)}\in [0,\infty ), \]

with the corresponding eventual inequality on low sublevels, then

\[ \nu (dd^ cv_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cv_ q\wedge T,\varphi ) \leq \Bigl (\prod _ j\ell _ j\Bigr ) \nu (dd^ cu_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cu_ q\wedge T,\varphi ). \]

The final bidimension $p-q=0$ is included.

Proof. After positive rescaling of the $v_ j$, first suppose every ratio is strictly less than one. Put $w_{j,c}=\max \{ v_ j-c,u_ j\} $. The $u_ j$ tend uniformly to $-\infty $ on shrinking support sublevels near their compact common prescribed pole set. The strict ratio inequalities therefore imply $w_{j,c}=v_ j-c$ on a relative neighborhood of $S\cap P_\varphi $ for each fixed $c$. This also holds at the infinite values themselves. Relative locality and the fact that the pole set of the weight is fixed show that the generalized number of the $w_ c$ product equals that of the $v$ product.

As $c\to \infty $ these functions decrease to $u_ j$. Their unbounded loci are contained in those of the $u_ j$, so their products exist and converge by theorem 7.28.1. All product supports lie in the same closed $S$. Apply theorem 6.34.8 to these currents with the fixed weight. This gives the assertion in the strict-ratio case. For arbitrary finite ratios divide $v_ j$ by any $a_ j>\ell _ j$, apply that case, and let each $a_ j\downarrow \ell _ j$. The numbers on the right are finite, so zero ratios cause no undefined infinite multiplication. $\square$

Corollary 6.34.13.E7C1B9085489 For a tuple satisfying the preceding product hypotheses,

\[ \nu (dd^ cu_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cu_ q\wedge T,a) \geq \nu (dd^ cu_1,a)\cdots \nu (dd^ cu_ q,a)\nu (T,a). \]

Proof. If one factor on the right is zero there is nothing to prove. Otherwise let $0<c_ j<\nu (dd^ cu_ j,a)$ and put $\varphi =\log |z-a|^2$, $v_ j=c_ j\varphi $. The logarithmic-growth theorem Lelong numbers as logarithmic growth slopes implies $\limsup v_ j/u_ j<1$ near $a$. The current comparison gives the desired inequality with $c_ j$. For the $v$ product its generalized number is $(\prod c_ j)\nu (T,\varphi )$: the recursive classical powers of $dd^ c\varphi $ followed by the remaining $p-q$ powers are precisely the full $p$-fold measure in (6.34.4). Now let $c_ j\uparrow \nu (dd^ cu_ j,a)$. $\square$
