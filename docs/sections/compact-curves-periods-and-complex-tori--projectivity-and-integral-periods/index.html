10.80 Projectivity and integral periodsA5F99454FF7E

A5F99454FF7E

Integral classes mean the image of constant-sheaf integer cohomology under the fixed real de Rham comparison. In particular no singular cohomology identification or integral Poincaré duality is implicit. Hermitian forms are linear in the first argument. A complex torus is called an abelian variety here when it is projective.

Corollary 10.80.1.167D644EB419 A compact Kähler manifold $X$ with $H^2(X,\mathcal O_ X)=0$ is projective.

Proof. Work on a connected component. The zero-dimensional case is a point. In positive dimension, the sheaf/Dolbeault comparison and the Kähler Hodge decomposition give $H^{0,2}=H^{2,0}=0$. Hence all real degree-two cohomology is of type $(1,1)$. By theorem 10.64.3, it has a finite real basis of integral-image classes. Let $\alpha _1,\ldots ,\alpha _ b$ be their real harmonic representatives for a fixed Kähler form $\omega $. They are of type $(1,1)$ by theorem 10.47.13. The form $\omega $ itself is harmonic: its Hodge star is a constant multiple of $\omega ^{n-1}$ and is closed. Thus equality of harmonic representatives gives

\[ \omega =\sum _{j=1}^ b\lambda _ j\alpha _ j,\qquad \lambda _ j\in \mathbb R. \]

If $b=0$ this contradicts positive definiteness, so that case cannot occur in positive dimension.

Let $C_ j$ bound the operator norm of $\alpha _ j$ relative to $\omega $; these constants are finite by compactness. Choose rational $\mu _ j$ with $\sum _ jC_ j|\mu _ j-\lambda _ j|<1/2$. Then

\[ \omega '=\sum _ j\mu _ j\alpha _ j\geq \tfrac 12\omega >0. \]

It is a closed real $(1,1)$-form. A common positive integer denominator $m$ makes $[m\omega ']$ integral. The already proved theorem 10.40.10 gives projectivity. A compact manifold has finitely many components, so these conclusions combine, including any point components. $\square$

Theorem 10.80.2.826013CCAFEC Let $T=V/\Gamma $ be a complex torus, with $\dim _{\mathbb C}V=n$. It is projective if and only if $V$ has a positive definite Hermitian form $h$ such that

\[ \operatorname {Im}h(\gamma ,\delta )\in \mathbb Z \qquad (\gamma ,\delta \in \Gamma ). \]

For such a form the associated Kähler form is $\omega =-\operatorname {Im}h$ on the underlying real vector space. For every ordered real lattice basis $a_1,\ldots ,a_{2n}$ one has

\[ \int _{T_{jk}}\omega =\omega (a_ j,a_ k) =-\operatorname {Im}h(a_ j,a_ k),\qquad 1\leq j<k\leq 2n, \]

where $T_{jk}$ is the oriented lattice parallelogram torus.

Proof. Let $J$ be multiplication by $\mathrm i$ on $V$. For a Hermitian $h$ linear in its first argument, $\omega =-\operatorname {Im}h$ is alternating, $J$-invariant and satisfies $\omega (v,Jv)=h(v,v)>0$ for nonzero $v$. It is constant, hence closed, and descends to a Kähler form. The displayed parallelogram formula follows by pulling back to $(s,t)\mapsto sa_ j+ta_ k$. The exact integral two-period criterion theorem 10.65.1 makes its class integral. Then theorem 10.40.10 proves projectivity.

Conversely projectivity supplies an integral Kähler form. By proposition 10.65.2, its average is a constant positive real $(1,1)$-form $\omega $ in the same integral class. Set

\[ h(v,w)=\omega (v,Jw)-\mathrm i\omega (v,w). \]

$J$-invariance and alternation make its real part symmetric and its imaginary part skew. Directly $h(Jv,w)=\mathrm i h(v,w)$ and $h(v,Jw)=-\mathrm i h(v,w)$, so it is Hermitian with the specified convention; $h(v,v)=\omega (v,Jv)>0$. The same two-period theorem gives the required integral imaginary parts, with the minus sign displayed above. All $2n$ real lattice circles are used. For $n=0$ the torus is a point and all conditions are vacuous and true. $\square$

Lemma 10.80.3.5A4C95A96C6B Let $X\subset \mathbb P^ N$ be a connected compact complex submanifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, and let $\omega $ be the restricted normalized Fubini–Study form. For real closed one-forms $\gamma ,\delta $ whose classes are integral,

\[ \int _ X\gamma \wedge \delta \wedge \omega ^{n-1}\in \mathbb Z. \]

This is an integral trace assertion for the displayed classes and chosen polarization; it does not presume an arbitrary integral Poincaré pairing.

Proof. We first construct smooth successive hyperplane sections. If a compact complex submanifold $Y\subset \mathbb P^ N$ has dimension $d\geq 1$, the ambient linear forms generate every first jet of $L=\mathcal O(1)|_ Y$. In an affine chart one section has coefficient one and the others are the restricted affine coordinates. Their differentials span $T_ y^*Y$ because the inclusion has injective differential. Thus first-jet evaluation from the parameter space $\mathbb C^{N+1}$ is onto at every $y$.

The incidence of pairs $(y,c)$ for which the corresponding section has zero first jet is a holomorphic kernel bundle over $Y$, of rank $N-d$. Indeed an invertible $(d+1)$-minor of the jet evaluation solves for that many parameters holomorphically. Its total complex dimension is $N$, smaller than the parameter dimension $N+1$. Its parameter image has measure zero: on each compact coordinate box the projection is Lipschitz, and subdividing a real $a$-dimensional box into cubes of side $r$ bounds its image volume in dimension $b>a$ by $O(r^{b-a})$. Countably many such boxes cover the incidence. Choose a parameter outside this null image. Its section is nonzero on every component and its zero set is a smooth compact complex hypersurface, possibly empty, by the local holomorphic inverse function theorem. This proves the required generic section statement without a transversality theorem as an extra input.

Repeat until a smooth compact curve $C$ is reached, allowing an empty set at any stage. The single-section Poincaré–Lelong identity the line-bundle Poincaré–Lelong formula, applied at each smooth stage, gives

\[ \int _ X\gamma \wedge \delta \wedge \omega ^{n-1} =\int _ C\gamma |_ C\wedge \delta |_ C. \]

At a stage of dimension $d$, pair $[Z]-\omega $ with the closed smooth form $\gamma \wedge \delta \wedge \omega ^{d-2}$; its integral is zero because $[Z]-\omega $ is an exact current and the stage is compact. Only a single integration current is paired with a smooth form at each step; no product of arbitrary currents is taken. For $n=1$ there is no cutting and $C=X$.

Restrictions of the two classes to each component of $C$ are integral by naturality. We retain the precise comparison sign: the positive connecting convention obeys

\[ \Psi _2([\alpha \wedge \beta ]) =-\Psi _1[\alpha ]\smile \Psi _1[\beta ] \]

in degree one, by theorem 10.58.9. Hence the class of the displayed wedge on $C$ is the positive real image of the negative of an integral cup class. The proved lemma 10.79.7 identifies $H^2(C_0,\underline{\mathbb Z})$ on each connected component $C_0$ with $\mathbb Z$, its positive point-divisor generator having integral one. The integral is therefore an integer. Sum over the finitely many components; an empty $C$ contributes zero. $\square$

Theorem 10.80.4.183B8F4BFE57 For a connected projective manifold $X$, its complex tori $J(X)=\operatorname {Pic}^0(X)$ and $A(X)=\operatorname {Alb}(X)$ are abelian varieties. In particular the Jacobian of every connected compact Riemann surface is an abelian variety.

Proof. The zero-dimensional case is a point. Otherwise choose a projective embedding and put $\omega =c_1(\mathcal O(1)|_ X)$ with the induced metric, so the preceding integral trace lemma applies. Write

\[ W=H^{0,1}(X),\quad V=H^{1,0}(X)=H^0(X,\Omega _ X^1),\quad \Lambda =\operatorname {pr}^{0,1}H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb Z}). \]

Use harmonic representatives for $\omega $. The exact period theorem 10.79.4, theorem 10.79.5 identify $J(X)=W/\Lambda $ and $A(X)=V^*/\Gamma $, with both lattices full. Here $\Gamma $ consists of loop-period functionals. If $W=0$ both tori are points, so suppose its dimension is positive.

Define the linear-first Hermitian form

\[ h(u,v)=-2\mathrm i\int _ Xu\wedge \bar v\wedge \omega ^{n-1} \qquad (u,v\in W). \]

Conjugating and interchanging the two one-forms proves Hermitian symmetry. In a coframe with $\omega =\mathrm i\sum _ j\theta _ j\wedge \bar\theta _ j$ and $u=\sum _ ju_ j\bar\theta _ j$, direct expansion gives

\[ -2\mathrm i\, u\wedge \bar u\wedge \omega ^{n-1} =\frac2n\sum _ j|u_ j|^2\omega ^ n. \]

Consequently $h$ is positive definite. The norm identity is asserted only for $v=u$. For integral real harmonic one-forms $\gamma ,\delta $, write $\gamma =\gamma ^{1,0}+\gamma ^{0,1}$ and similarly for $\delta $. Their pure $(2,0)$ and $(0,2)$ wedges vanish against $\omega ^{n-1}$. Taking real parts of $\int \gamma ^{0,1}\wedge \delta ^{1,0}\wedge \omega ^{n-1}$ gives

\[ \operatorname {Im}h(\gamma ^{0,1},\delta ^{0,1}) =-\int _ X\gamma \wedge \delta \wedge \omega ^{n-1}\in \mathbb Z. \]

The preceding lemma supplies the actual integer trace. Thus theorem 10.80.2 proves that $J(X)$ is projective.

For the Albanese torus consider the complex-linear map

\[ B:W\longrightarrow V^*,\qquad B(u)(v)=\int _ Xu\wedge v\wedge \omega ^{n-1}. \]

It is an isomorphism: $B(u)=0$ implies $h(u,u)=0$, so $u=0$, and Kähler Hodge conjugation gives equal dimensions. For an integral real class $\gamma $, the integer trace lemma makes

\[ a_\gamma :\ H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})\longrightarrow \mathbb Z, \qquad \delta \longmapsto \int _ X\gamma \wedge \delta \wedge \omega ^{n-1} \]

an integer homomorphism. By the actual degree-one evaluation theorem 10.79.2, it is evaluation by a loop class modulo its torsion. Extend this equality real-linearly and then complex-linearly to de Rham cohomology. On $v\in V$ its value is $B(\gamma ^{0,1})(v)$, because the other Hodge component has out-of-range bidegree. Hence $B\Lambda \subset \Gamma $. This uses the proved character/evaluation lattice, not an identification with all integer degree-$(2n-1)$ cohomology.

Both lattices are full and $B$ is an isomorphism, so this inclusion has finite index. Explicitly, in a basis of $\Gamma $, a basis of $B\Lambda $ is a nonsingular integer matrix; its determinant and adjugate give an integer $m\geq 1$ with $m\Gamma \subset B\Lambda $. Transfer the metric by $h_ A(a,b)=h(B^{-1}a,B^{-1}b)$. Then $m^2\operatorname {Im}h_ A$ is integral on $\Gamma \times \Gamma $, since $ma,mb\in B\Lambda $. The positive Hermitian form $m^2h_ A$ and the torus criterion prove that $A(X)$ is projective. No unimodularity assertion in higher degree is needed or made.

Every compact curve is projective by corollary 10.41.3; its Jacobian is $J(X)$, also identified with $A(X)$ by the already proved theorem 10.79.13. This proves the last assertion. For a disconnected compact projective manifold, the Picard torus is the finite product of the component Picard tori; the direct sum of their integral positive forms proves it projective as well. The Albanese assertion is applied to each connected component with its chosen base point. $\square$
