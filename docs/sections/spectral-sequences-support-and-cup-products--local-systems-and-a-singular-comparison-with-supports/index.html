10.70 Local systems and a singular comparison with supports0F3379F6C320

0F3379F6C320

Proposition 10.70.1.3CBF93FB0039 A locally constant sheaf of $R$-modules is equivalently a covering space with discrete module fibers and local trivializations linear on each fiber. On a connected, locally path connected, semilocally simply connected space it is equivalently a module $L$ with a representation of the fundamental group in $\operatorname {Aut}_ R(L)$, up to change of the identification of one fiber with $L$. These hypotheses hold on a connected manifold. Transport is invariant under endpoint-fixed homotopy of paths. For a fixed base, homology is covariant under coefficient homomorphisms; pullback of the coefficient system along a map of bases is a separate, contravariant operation.

Proof. Take the disjoint union of the stalks, with the topology whose basic open sets are the germs of a local section. A local constant trivialization identifies this space with $U\times L$, with $L$ discrete. Conversely, sections of such a covering form the required sheaf, and these constructions are inverse on germs and on maps. Subdivide a path into finitely many intervals lying in trivializing opens. Continue a fiber element as a constant local section on each interval. Uniqueness on overlaps proves independence of subdivision and gives inverse transport on the reversed path and composition under concatenation. For a homotopy of paths, subdivide the parameter square into small rectangles each mapped into a trivializing open. Transport around each rectangle is the identity; the interior edges cancel, proving homotopy invariance and the endpoint formula.

For completeness the needed universal cover has a direct construction. Fix $x_0$ and take endpoint-fixed homotopy classes $[\gamma ]$ of paths starting there. Over a path connected open $U$ whose loops are nullhomotopic in $X$, extend $\gamma $ by paths in $U$. Their endpoint classes form one sheet mapped bijectively onto $U$. These sheets define a topology: two sheets meeting at a point agree over a smaller such neighborhood, by concatenation and the nullhomotopy condition. They are evenly covered charts. Path lifting is the continuation construction above. A loop in this covering projects to a loop whose path class is trivial, and its nullhomotopy lifts rectangle by rectangle; hence the covering is simply connected.

Use path concatenation in the order for which transport is a left representation. A based loop acts on a fiber by its transport. Trivializing a pulled-back local system on the simply connected cover by transport identifies it with the product with $L$. Explicitly, the pair $(\gamma ,l)$ represents $t_\gamma l$ in the endpoint fiber. Two such pairs with the same endpoint represent the same element precisely when $l'=t_{\gamma '}^{-1}t_\gamma l$. This specifies the deck/coefficient quotient without an implicit inverse convention. Conversely, this equivalence relation on path pairs constructs the covering module sheaf from the representation; the evenly covered charts above check it directly and give the prescribed transport. A different identification of the base fiber conjugates the representation, and an isomorphism of sheaves induces exactly such an intertwiner. These statements also apply component by component. Finally a coefficient map acts on the coefficient of each chain in the same direction. A homotopy of base maps gives equality of induced maps only after identifying the pullback systems by its actual path transport; the underlying base homotopy alone does not specify a coefficient map. $\square$

Let $C_ j^{\mathrm{sing}}(U,A)=\bigoplus _{\sigma :\Delta ^ j\to U} A_{\sigma (v_0)}$. A coefficient can equivalently be its unique parallel section on $\Delta ^ j$, since all paths in a simplex between fixed endpoints are homotopic. The boundary is the alternating sum of its restrictions to faces. In initial-vertex notation the face omitting $v_0$ transports the coefficient to $v_1$; the other faces retain it. Cochains $C^ j_{\mathrm{sing}}(U,A)$ assign a coefficient in $A_{\sigma (v_0)}$ to every simplex, with arbitrary, not finite, support. The coboundary is

\[ (\delta u)(\sigma )=t_{1,0}u(\sigma [v_1,\ldots ,v_{j+1}]) +\sum _{i=1}^{j+1}(-1)^ i u(\sigma [v_0,\ldots ,\widehat v_ i, \ldots ,v_{j+1}]), \]

where $t_{1,0}$ is transport along the reversed first edge. Commuting restrictions to two faces cancel with opposite signs; the two transports agree because the corresponding paths in the simplex are homotopic. Thus $\partial ^2=\delta ^2=0$.

Lemma 10.70.2.F198D931B2F3 For any open cover $\mathcal U$, the chains whose simplices lie in one cover member form a subcomplex, and its inclusion in the full singular complex is a chain homotopy equivalence. The assertion holds with any local module coefficients, for relative complexes, and for cochains. Singular homology and cohomology consequently have the pair and triple sequences and excision for $\overline T\subset \operatorname {int}S$, and Mayer–Vietoris for open covers. Singular chains commute with directed unions of opens; compact-support singular cohomology is

\[ H^ q_{c,\mathrm{sing}}(X,A)= \varinjlim _{K\subset X\ {\rm compact}} H^ q_{\mathrm{sing}}(X,X\setminus K;A). \]

Proof. All constructions can be performed in a simplex, with its pulled-back coefficient system trivialized by its initial vertex; they therefore respect any local coefficients. On affine chains in a convex set, coning to a point $b$ satisfies $\partial b+b\partial =1$ on the augmented complex. Define subdivision on an affine simplex $s$ by $Ss=b_ s(S\partial s)$, where $b_ s$ is its barycenter, starting with the identity in dimension zero. The cone identity gives $\partial Ss=S\partial s$. Define $Ts=b_ s(s-T\partial s)$, starting with zero in augmentation degree. Then

\[ \partial Ts=s-T\partial s-b_ s(\partial s-\partial T\partial s) =s-T\partial s-Ss. \]

Pushing these formulas along each singular simplex defines natural carrier-preserving $S,T$ with $\partial T+T\partial =1-S$. A barycentric subsimplex has diameter at most $j/(j+1)$ of the original in dimension $j$: its vertices are barycenters of nested faces; writing a barycenter as the convex combination of a vertex and the opposite face barycenter gives this bound, then induction on faces handles the other pairs of vertices. Iteration and a Lebesgue number of the pulled-back cover make every simplex small.

Let $m(\sigma )$ be the least subdivision number for which every geometric subsimplex, before any algebraic cancellation, maps into one cover member. A face has no larger such number. Put $T_ m=\sum _{i=0}^{m-1}TS^ i$, so $\partial T_ m+T_ m\partial =1-S^ m$, and define $D\sigma =T_{m(\sigma )}\sigma $, extended linearly with transported coefficients. Set $\rho =1-\partial D-D\partial $. This is a chain map. Moreover

\[ \rho \sigma =S^{m(\sigma )}\sigma + T_{m(\sigma )}\partial \sigma -D\partial \sigma . \]

For a face $\sigma _ j$, the remaining difference is a sum of $TS^ i\sigma _ j$ with $i\ge m(\sigma _ j)$; it is small because $T$ preserves the carrier. Thus $\rho $ has image in small chains. On small simplices $D=0$, whence $\rho $ restricts to the identity. This gives both a retraction and the stated chain homotopy. They preserve every subspace, so pass to relative quotients and, by precomposition, to cochains and their relative kernels.

The pair and triple complexes are short exact in every degree; cochain restrictions are onto by assigning zero to all simplices not in the subspace. Their algebraic sequences are therefore exact. For an excisive cover $X=\operatorname {int}S\cup \operatorname {int}B$, the intermediate complex $C(S)+C(B)$ has a homotopy-equivalent inclusion in $C(X)$: the open-cover small complex lies inside it, and both subdivision and its homotopy preserve $S$ and $B$. Its quotient by $C(S)$ is $C(B)/C(S\cap B)$, simplex by simplex. This proves excision, including the stated barred form with $B=X\setminus T$. For two opens the short exact sequence

\[ 0\to C(U\cap V)\xrightarrow {z\mapsto (z,-z)} C(U)\oplus C(V) \xrightarrow {(a,b)\mapsto a+b} C(U)+C(V)\to 0 \]

gives homology Mayer–Vietoris; the analogous cochain kernel and restriction sequence gives cohomology. The same formulas apply to pairs. For finitely many cover members the augmented Čech row is exact: for each small simplex choose one member containing it and prepend its index as the contraction. Arbitrary products of these split contractions give the cochain assertion for any cover.

A homotopy has the prism operator $P\sigma =\sum _{i=0}^ j(-1)^ iH(\sigma \times 1) [v_0,\ldots ,v_ i,w_ i,\ldots ,w_ j]$. Its interior faces cancel, leaving $\partial P+P\partial =H_{1*}-H_{0*}$, with transport along the homotopy on coefficients. This proves homotopy invariance in the form stated above. Finally a chain and a bounding chain use finitely many compact simplices. A compact set in a directed union of opens lies in one of them, proving the chain limit assertion. The last displayed formula follows by taking the directed union of the relative cochain kernels: a cycle or a primitive has its support condition at one compact stage. The condition is vanishing on simplices contained in the complement, not finite support among all singular simplices. $\square$

Theorem 10.70.3.9E4C37619D42 On a topological manifold, singular cohomology of a local system agrees naturally with its sheaf cohomology. This comparison commutes with cup product and proper pullback. For every closed $K$ it identifies singular relative cohomology of $(X,X\setminus K)$ with sheaf cohomology supported in $K$, compatibly with maps and connecting homomorphisms of these open-subspace pairs. Its directed union gives the same comparison for compact supports and for the mixed compact/ordinary cup product.

Proof. Let $\mathcal S^ q(A)$ be the sheaf of germs of singular $q$-cochains. The augmentation $A\to \mathcal S^\bullet (A)$ is exact. Indeed a coordinate ball trivializes $A$ by path transport; shrinking a germ cycle to a smaller ball and using the prism contraction proves local exactness in positive degree. In degree zero its closed sections are precisely parallel, locally constant sections. This argument requires no acyclicity assertion about the sheaves $\mathcal S^ q$.

Form the functorial first-quadrant complex

\[ Q^\bullet (U,A)=\operatorname {Tot}_{p,q\ge 0} \Gamma (U,C_ U^ p\mathcal S^ q(A)). \]

There are natural augmentation maps

\[ C^\bullet _{\mathrm{sing}}(U,A)\longrightarrow Q^\bullet (U,A) \longleftarrow C_ U^\bullet A(U). \]

The right map is a quasi-isomorphism, since each $\Gamma (U,C_ U^ p-)$ is exact and the vertical augmented sheaf complex is exact. Each term of $Q$ is a finite sum of flabby sheaves before taking sections. On a contractible coordinate ball both sides of the left map have cohomology $A$ in degree zero and zero elsewhere: use the prism contraction for singular cohomology and theorem 10.63.3 for sheaf cohomology. The map on degree zero is the identity.

First take an open subset of $\mathbb R^ n$ and cover it by convex balls trivializing the local system. All nonempty finite intersections are convex and have the just computed comparison. The augmented Čech double complex of singular cochains computes global singular cohomology: replace the global cochains by cochains on small chains using the preceding homotopy equivalence, and contract each horizontal row by prepending an index containing the simplex. The corresponding Čech complex of $Q$ computes $Q(U,A)$, since its rows are flabby and Čech acyclic by lemma 10.43.14. The maps on all intersection complexes are quasi-isomorphisms. The finite-diagonal comparison of lemma 10.43.15 proves the global comparison on this open subset of Euclidean space.

Now use any coordinate-ball cover of the manifold. Each finite intersection is an open subset of one coordinate ball, and the preceding paragraph applies there. Repeating the same Čech comparison proves the result on the whole manifold and every open subset. All comparisons came from the displayed functorial maps; changing covers only proves that those same maps are isomorphisms. Thus they commute with restrictions and with coefficient maps. There is no assumed triangulation or unproved global good-cover interface in this argument.

For closed $K$, the singular cochain restriction to $X\setminus K$ is onto, and the same is true in every degree of $Q$ by flabbiness. The natural short exact kernel complexes, and the comparison just proved on the two open spaces, identify their kernels by the five lemma. The augmented sheaf total underlying $Q$ is an exact flabby resolution of $A$, so its sections with support in $K$ compute the stated supported sheaf group. Directed union over compact $K$ proves the compact assertion. These constructions also show proper pullback compatibility, since inverse images of compact supports under a proper map remain compact.

On singular cochains use the front/back product: transport the back coefficient from the shared vertex to the initial vertex and tensor it with the front coefficient. Its middle terms cancel in the coboundary expansion, giving the usual Leibniz sign. It sheafifies to $\mathcal S^\bullet $, and the total Godement product on $Q$ has the sign $(-1)^{qp'}$ between bidegrees $(p,q)$ and $(p',q')$. Both augmentation arrows preserve these products by the Godement front/back identities of theorem 10.58.3. Thus the comparisons preserve cup products. A compactly supported factor gives a product supported in the same compact set; the relative-kernel comparison therefore proves the mixed assertion as well, with no extra sign beyond the fixed total convention. $\square$
