7.15 Integral leaves of constant-rank null distributions930CF3D498A8

930CF3D498A8

Leaves in this section carry their intrinsic manifold topology. Their inclusion into the ambient manifold is an injective immersion; the image need not be an embedded submanifold. In a foliation chart it is the connected local plaques, rather than the entire intersection of a global leaf with the chart, that are fibers of a submersion.

Proposition 7.15.1 (Frobenius).0E3D24D5541E Let $D$ be a constant-rank $C^ l$ real distribution on an open subset of $\mathbb R^ m$, where $1\leq l\leq \infty $. If the bracket of any two local $C^1$ sections is again in $D$, then there are local $C^ l$ coordinates in which $D$ is spanned by the first coordinate vectors. Its maximal connected integral leaves form a foliation by injectively immersed manifolds.

Proof. We recall the local flow fact used in the argument. A $C^ l$ vector field $Z$ has a unique local $C^ l$ flow $F_ t(x)$, with inverse $F_{-t}$. On a compact coordinate ball, bound $|Z|$ by $M$ and $|\mathrm dZ|$ by $L$, and choose $T$ with $TM$ smaller than half the radius and $TL<1/2$. On continuous paths starting at a point in the inner ball, the operator $q\mapsto x+\int _0^ t Z(q(s))\, \mathrm ds$ maps the corresponding closed path ball to itself and contracts the supremum norm by at most $TL$. Its successive iterates converge geometrically to a solution; the same estimate gives uniqueness on this time interval, and hence the local group and inverse identities. Dependence on $x$ is $C^1$: the difference quotients satisfy the integral equation whose linear limit is $A(t)=I+\int _0^ t\mathrm dZ(F_ s(x))A(s)\, \mathrm ds$. Uniform continuity of $\mathrm dZ$ on the ball and the same integral estimate make these difference quotients converge to $A$. Repeated differentiation proves $C^ l$ dependence for larger $l$. The integral estimate may be iterated to give the bound $b(t)\leq c\sum _{j\geq 0}(L|t|)^ j/j!$ when $b(t)\leq c+L\int _0^{|t|}b(s)\, \mathrm ds$, so it also justifies these difference-quotient limits without an additional ODE theorem.

Let the rank be $k$. The cases $k=0,m$ are immediate. After a real linear coordinate change at a fixed point, projection of $D$ to the first $k$ coordinates is an isomorphism nearby. Normalize a local frame to the form

\[ Z_ i=\partial _{x_ i}+\sum _{a>k}b_{ia}(x)\partial _{x_ a}, \qquad 1\leq i\leq k. \]

The first $k$ coefficients of $[Z_ i,Z_ j]$ vanish, while the bracket belongs to $D$. The isomorphism of the projection implies $[Z_ i,Z_ j]=0$. Their local flows commute. To check this at $C^1$ regularity, put $M(t)=\mathrm dF_{i,t}|_ x$ and $Y(t)=Z_ j(F_{i,t}(x))$. The variational equation gives $M'=\mathrm dZ_ i(F_{i,t}(x))M$, while the zero bracket gives $Y'=\mathrm dZ_ i(F_{i,t}(x))Y$. Thus $M^{-1}Y$ is constant, so $F_{i,t}$ preserves $Z_ j$. Uniqueness of integral curves gives $F_{i,t}F_{j,s}=F_{j,s}F_{i,t}$ wherever both local compositions are defined.

With the fixed point translated to zero, put

\[ \Phi (t_1,\ldots ,t_ k,y) =F_{1,t_1}\circ \cdots \circ F_{k,t_ k}(0,\ldots ,0,y). \]

At zero its derivative is invertible. The inverse function theorem makes it a local $C^ l$ coordinate map, and flow commutation gives $\partial _{t_ i}\Phi =Z_ i\circ \Phi $. Its slices with fixed $y$ are the required local integral plaques. Join plaques by finite overlapping chains to obtain maximal connected leaves. The local normal form makes their plaques compatible, so these leaves are disjoint and cover the domain, with the indicated intrinsic manifold charts and immersion. They are Hausdorff because distinct points have disjoint ambient neighborhoods. They are second countable: use a countable foliation-chart cover of the ambient domain; a plaque meets another chart in at most countably many connected pieces, each lying in one plaque there. Starting with one plaque and iterating finite chains therefore reaches only countably many plaque charts covering its leaf. This completes the global leaf construction. $\square$

Proposition 7.15.2.C2EBFD69CEA4 A $C^1$ real submanifold of $\mathbb C^ n$ whose tangent spaces are complex vector spaces is locally a complex submanifold. The same conclusion holds locally for an immersed real submanifold.

Proof. At a fixed point let the complex tangent dimension be $k$ and choose a complex linear projection onto that tangent space. Its restriction to the submanifold has invertible real derivative, so the real inverse theorem writes a neighborhood as the graph of a $C^1$ map $g:W\subset \mathbb C^ k\to \mathbb C^{n-k}$. The graph of $\mathrm dg$ is a complex vector space at every point. This is equivalent to complex linearity of $\mathrm dg$, hence to $\bar\partial g=0$. The existing scalar distributional Cauchy–Riemann theorem makes $g$ holomorphic. The graph is therefore a complex submanifold. An immersion is an embedding on sufficiently small intrinsic coordinate neighborhoods, so the same argument applies there. $\square$

Theorem 7.15.3.8FE75D79B9CA Let $\alpha _1,\ldots ,\alpha _ s$ be $C^ l$ complex-valued $(1,1)$-forms on an open subset $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, with $l\geq 1$. For real tangent vectors, let

\[ D_ x=\{ v:\alpha _ j(v,w)=0\text{ for every real }w \text{ and every }j\} . \]

Assume that $D$ has constant dimension and that $\mathrm d\alpha _ j(X,Y,Z)=0$ whenever $X,Y$ lie in $D$. Then its maximal leaves form a $C^ l$ foliation by injectively immersed complex submanifolds, of complex dimension $\tfrac 12\dim _{\mathbb R}D$. In particular this holds for closed forms with constant-dimensional common nullspace.

If the forms are $\mathrm dd^ c u_ j$ with $u_ j\in C^{l+2}(\Omega ,\mathbb C)$, then the real and imaginary parts of each $u_ j$ are pluriharmonic on each leaf. In any ambient holomorphic coordinates, all functions $\partial _{z_ a}\operatorname {Re}u_ j$ and $\partial _{z_ a}\operatorname {Im}u_ j$ restrict holomorphically to the leaves.

Proof. For one form write $\alpha =(\mathrm i/2)\sum a_{jk}\, \mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ k$ and $A=(a_{jk})^{\mathsf T}$. With the Hermitian product linear in its first variable, its real nullspace is

\[ N(\alpha )=\ker A\cap \ker A^*. \]

Indeed $\alpha (v,w)=0$ is equivalent to $\langle Av,w\rangle =\langle Aw,v\rangle $ for all $w$; applying this both to $w$ and to $\mathrm iw$ makes both sides zero. Thus $D$ is a complex vector space at every point. It is the kernel of the matrix obtained by stacking all $A_ j,A_ j^*$. Constant rank makes it a $C^ l$ distribution: near a point choose an invertible maximal minor and solve for its pivot variables in terms of the free ones to obtain a $C^ l$ frame.

For local sections $X,Y$ of $D$ and any vector field $Z$, the formula for exterior differentiation gives $\mathrm d\alpha _ j(X,Y,Z)=-\alpha _ j([X,Y],Z)$, since all the other terms contain a null argument or derivatives of an identically zero pairing. The hypothesis therefore puts $[X,Y]$ in $D$. Frobenius gives real integral leaves, and their complex tangent spaces make them complex by the preceding proposition.

For a potential, pullback to the leaf commutes with $\partial ,\bar\partial $. Its vanishing pulled-back $(1,1)$-form therefore says that both real and imaginary parts have zero complex Hessian on the leaf. They are pluriharmonic by the existing Hessian criterion. To check the gradient assertion, let $f$ be one of these real potentials and write a local holomorphic leaf parametrization $\psi $. The nullspace condition and Hermitian symmetry give

\[ \partial _{\bar t_ b}(f_{z_ a}\circ \psi ) =\sum _ k f_{z_ a\bar z_ k}(\psi (t)) \overline{\partial _{t_ b}\psi _ k}=0. \]

This proves holomorphy. A complex null pairing vanishes in both real and imaginary parts, so the same calculation applies to each of the four real potentials when complex $u_ j$ are used. $\square$

Proposition 7.15.4.992E9692EF3B Suppose that $\alpha $ is a reducible $(1,1)$-form and that $\beta $ is a real semipositive $(1,1)$-form on $\Omega $. Let $p,q\geq 0$ with $p+q\leq n$ and assume everywhere that

\[ \alpha ^ p\wedge \beta ^ q\ne 0,\qquad \alpha ^{p+1}=0,\qquad \alpha ^ p\wedge \beta ^{q+1}=0. \]

Here reducibility means $\ker A=\ker A^*$ for the coefficient matrix above. Then the common real nullspace has constant complex dimension $n-p-q$. Thus the preceding theorem applies if the forms are $C^1$ and their exterior derivatives satisfy its nullspace condition.

Proof. The exterior-power expansion in the stated order is

\[ \alpha ^ p=p!\, \frac{\mathrm i^{p^2}}{2^ p} \sum _{|J|=|K|=p}\det (a_{jk})_{j\in J,k\in K} \, \mathrm dz_ J\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ K. \]

It follows by expanding the product and moving all antiholomorphic factors past the later holomorphic factors; the phase is $(\mathrm i/2)^ p(-1)^{p(p-1)/2}=\mathrm i^{p^2}/2^ p$. Thus the first two assumptions imply $\operatorname {rank}A=p$. Reducibility splits the space orthogonally as its range $E$ and kernel $E^\perp $, with $A$ invertible on $E$. In these coordinates $\alpha ^ p$ is a nonzero multiple of the top complex volume on $E$. Consequently the other two wedge conditions say exactly that the compression $C=P_{E^\perp }B|_{E^\perp }$ of the Hermitian positive matrix of $\beta $ has rank $q$. For $v\in E^\perp $, positivity gives

\[ Cv=0\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \langle Bv,v\rangle =0\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad Bv=0, \]

using a unitary eigenbasis of $B$. Hence $\ker A\cap \ker B=\ker C$ has dimension $n-p-q$. This is the common real nullspace since both forms are reducible. The argument includes $p=0$ or $q=0$ with the usual zeroth power. $\square$

In particular, a $C^3$ real plurisubharmonic function with constant complex Hessian rank $r$ has a null foliation of complex dimension $n-r$. The function is pluriharmonic along its leaves, and its ambient holomorphic partial derivatives restrict holomorphically. Rank $n-1$ gives Riemann surfaces. This follows directly from the constant-rank theorem, since $\mathrm dd^ c u$ is closed and semipositive.

Example 7.15.5.849194DD5398 The semipositivity in the wedge criterion cannot be replaced by reducibility. In $\mathbb C^3$, put

\[ \alpha =\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\mathrm dz_1\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_1, \qquad \beta =\frac{\mathrm i}{2} (\mathrm dz_1\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_2+ \mathrm dz_2\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_1+ \mathrm dz_3\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_3). \]

Both coefficient matrices are Hermitian, hence reducible, and $\alpha \wedge \beta \ne 0$, $\alpha ^2=0$ and $\alpha \wedge \beta ^2=0$. Nevertheless the matrix of $\beta $ is invertible, so the common nullspace is zero rather than a complex line. The lower-right compression of that matrix has rank one, whereas its kernel does not describe the kernel of the full indefinite matrix.

For the corresponding potentials $u=|z_1|^2$ and $v=2\operatorname {Re}(z_1\bar z_2)+|z_3|^2$, there is no positive-dimensional complex leaf on which both are pluriharmonic and all their ambient holomorphic partial derivatives are holomorphic. Pluriharmonicity of $u$ forces $z_1$ constant; then that of $v$ forces $z_3$ constant. Holomorphy of $v_{z_1}=\bar z_2$ forces $z_2$ constant as well.

An illustration of the intrinsic leaf convention is obtained on $(\mathbb C^*)^2$ by taking an irrational real $a$ and $h(z,w)=\log |w|-a\log |z|$. The smooth PSH function $h^2$ has complex Hessian $2(\partial h)\otimes \overline{\partial h}$ of constant rank one. Its leaves have parametrizations $t\mapsto (z_0\mathrm e^ t,w_0\mathrm e^{at})$. They are injective holomorphic immersions. Along $t=2\pi \mathrm i k$ the first coordinate is fixed and the second is dense on its circle; hence the leaf is not an embedded graph near a point, despite its nonzero first-coordinate tangent projection. This is why local plaques must be distinguished from entire global leaf intersections.
