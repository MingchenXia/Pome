16.40 Local regularity of analytic polyhedraA69F3CCAC47E

A69F3CCAC47E

We use the analytic principalization input in its real analytic category: for a nonzero real analytic function $F$ on a real analytic manifold $M$, there is a proper surjective analytic modification $\pi :\widetilde M\to M$ such that $F\circ \pi $ is locally a monomial times a nonvanishing analytic function. The spaces have the same dimension. This is the principal-ideal case of the permitted resolution input; its algorithmic proof is external (Bierstone–Milman, Theorem 2.4, with the real analytic category in (0.1)(2) and Theorem 1.10). The exact real category and properness are important here.

Lemma 16.40.1.DD2047D70ACD Let $A$ be an open subset of $\mathbb R^ N$, let $a\in \overline A\setminus A$, and suppose that near $a$ membership in $A$ is a finite Boolean combination of sign conditions on real analytic functions. There is a real analytic map $\gamma $ near $0$ with $\gamma (0)=a$ and $\gamma (t)\in A$ for all sufficiently small $t>0$.

Proof. Work in a connected coordinate ball about $a$. Replace identically zero functions in the formula by their constant truth values. All remaining functions $f_1,\ldots ,f_ s$ are nonzero analytic functions. If none remain, membership is locally constant, contrary to the boundary assumption. Their product $F$ is nonzero. Its zero set has empty interior, by the real analytic identity theorem, so there are $x_ j\in A\setminus \{ F=0\} $ tending to $a$. After passing to a subsequence all signs $\operatorname {sgn}f_ i(x_ j)$ are fixed.

Principalize $(F)$ on the ball. Surjectivity gives lifts $y_ j\in \pi ^{-1}(x_ j)$. Properness, applied over a compact smaller ball containing the sequence, gives a subsequence tending to $b\in \pi ^{-1}(a)$. Choose analytic coordinates $y$ centered at $b$ in which $F\circ \pi $ is a monomial times a unit. In these coordinates each factor has the form

\[ f_ i\circ \pi =c_ i(y)\prod _{\ell =1}^ k y_\ell ^{m_{i\ell }}, \qquad m_{i\ell }\geq 0,\qquad c_ i(0)\ne 0. \]

Here is the elementary factorization justification. In the ring of convergent real power series each coordinate is prime, since setting it to zero gives an integral domain. Repeatedly remove the coordinate factors from the product identity. The remaining factors have product a unit and are consequently units themselves. Shrink the chart so that all $c_ i$ keep their signs.

Because $F(x_ j)\ne 0$, the first $k$ coordinates of $y_ j$ are nonzero. Pass to a subsequence with fixed signs $s_\ell \in \{ -1,1\} $. In the chart take the analytic line

\[ y_\ell (t)=s_\ell t\quad (1\leq \ell \leq k),\qquad y_\ell (t)=0\quad (k<\ell \leq N). \]

For small positive $t$, every $f_ i(\pi (y(t)))$ has exactly its sign on the sequence $x_ j$. Thus the entire Boolean formula has the same truth value, including its equality conditions. It follows that $\gamma (t)=\pi (y(t))$ lies in $A$. This map is analytic at zero and equals $a$ there. It cannot be constant, because $a\notin A$. The case $k=0$ cannot occur: all the functions would be nonzero at $a$, making membership locally constant there. $\square$

Theorem 16.40.2.8ED77FB38EC4 Let $A\subset \mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$, be a bounded nonempty open set which is semianalytic in a neighborhood of every point of its closure. Put $K=\overline A$. For every $a\in K$ and $r>0$, $K\cap \overline B(a,r)$ is L-regular at $a$. In particular $V_ K$ is continuous on $\mathbb C^ n$.

Consequently this holds for the closure of every nonempty relatively compact open analytic polyhedron

\[ A=\{ z\in U:|f_ j(z)|<1,\ 1\leq j\leq m\} \Subset U, \qquad f_ j\in \mathcal O(U), \]

with arbitrary finite $m$; no order-$n$ or finite-fiber hypothesis is required.

Proof. Fix $a\in K$ and $r>0$, and set $K_ r=K\cap \overline B(a,r)$. If $a\in A$, the set $K_ r$ contains a ball centered at $a$. The explicit ball profile and obstacle monotonicity give $V_{K_ r}^*(a)=0$ and regularity there.

Otherwise use the preceding lemma, identifying $\mathbb C^ n$ with $\mathbb R^{2n}$. Shrink its parameter interval so that $\gamma (t)\in A\cap B(a,r)$ for $0<t\leq \varepsilon $. The convergent real power series of its complex coordinates extend holomorphically near this interval. Every $\gamma (t)$ with $t>0$ is an interior point of $K_ r$, so a contained ball gives $V_{K_ r}^*(\gamma (t))=0$.

The compact $K_ r$ contains a ball and is non-pluripolar. Its regularized extremal is therefore finite PSH. Pulling it back by $\gamma $ gives a nonnegative subharmonic function, zero on the punctured real interval. The interval is nonthin at its endpoint, so its value at zero is zero. Thus $V_{K_ r}^*(a)=0$. Since $0\leq V_{K_ r}\leq V_{K_ r}^*$, upper semicontinuity proves raw continuity at $a$. This is precisely the existing analytic accessibility argument, now with its arc constructed.

Taking $r$ large enough to contain $K$ proves zero regularized contact at every point of $K$. The existing compact zero-contact criterion then gives $V_ K=V_ K^*$ continuous on the whole space. For the polyhedron, $1-|f_ j|^2$ is real analytic, and relative compactness puts $K$ inside $U$, so its strict inequalities give the required semianalytic description near every point of $K$. $\square$
