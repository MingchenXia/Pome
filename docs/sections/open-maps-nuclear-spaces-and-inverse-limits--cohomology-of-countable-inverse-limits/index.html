2.14 Cohomology of countable inverse limits0A983B5FB454

0A983B5FB454

All spaces are Hausdorff complex Fréchet spaces, and all linear maps and differentials are continuous. A complex may be unbounded. For a complex $A^\bullet $ write

\[ Z^ q(A)=\ker (d:A^ q\to A^{q+1}),\qquad B^ q(A)=d(A^{q-1}),\qquad H^ q(A)=Z^ q(A)/B^ q(A). \]

Cohomology carries the quotient topology; its boundary subspace need not be closed. The quotient map is open, since the inverse image of the image of an open set $U$ is $U+B^ q(A)$.

An inverse sequence has maps $r_\nu :A_{\nu +1}\to A_\nu $, $\nu \geq 0$; write $r_{\mu \nu }=r_\nu \cdots r_{\mu -1}$ for $\mu >\nu $ and $r_{\nu \nu }=\operatorname {Id}$. Its inverse limit is the subspace

\[ \varprojlim A_\nu =\{ (a_\nu )\in \prod _{\nu \geq 0}A_\nu : r_\nu a_{\nu +1}=a_\nu \} . \]

It is closed in the product, as each coordinate difference $r_\nu a_{\nu +1}-a_\nu $ is continuous. A countable product is Fréchet: for complete invariant defining metrics $d_\nu $, the metric $\sum _\nu 2^{-\nu -1}\min \{ 1,d_\nu \} $ defines its product topology, and completeness follows by taking coordinate limits and then bounding the tail of the sum. Closed subspaces are complete. Thus inverse limits and the closed cycle spaces used below are Fréchet. Inverse limits of complexes are taken in each degree, with coordinatewise differential, and

6333A6C85DF6 2.14.1
\begin{equation} Z^ q(\varprojlim A_\nu ^\bullet ) =\varprojlim Z^ q(A_\nu ^\bullet ) \end{equation}

as topological vector spaces: both sides have precisely the induced product topology and the same elements.

We can choose the metrics so every transition is $1$-Lipschitz. Indeed, starting with complete invariant defining metrics $e_\nu $, replace them by

\[ d_\nu (x,y)=\max _{0\leq \mu \leq \nu } e_\mu (r_{\nu \mu }x,r_{\nu \mu }y). \]

The finite maximum defines the original topology and is complete: a $d_\nu $-Cauchy sequence is $e_\nu $-Cauchy, and continuity of the finitely many maps gives convergence also in $d_\nu $. The displayed definition gives the claimed Lipschitz property. Such initial metrics can be obtained from increasing defining seminorms $p_ j$ by $\sum _ j2^{-j}\min \{ 1,p_ j(x-y)\} $; completeness follows by the same Cauchy and finite-tail argument. For complexes we make this choice separately in each degree, and use superscripts $d_\nu ^ k$ for the resulting metrics.

Lemma 2.14.2.139720D2EFCA If all transition maps in an inverse sequence of Fréchet spaces have dense image, every projection $\varprojlim A_\nu \to A_ N$ has dense image.

Proof. Fix $a_ N\in A_ N$ and $\varepsilon >0$. Inductively choose $a_{\nu +1}\in A_{\nu +1}$, $\nu \geq N$, with

\[ d_\nu (r_\nu a_{\nu +1},a_\nu ) <\varepsilon 2^{N-\nu -2}. \]

For each fixed $\mu \geq N$, the sequence $(r_{\nu \mu }a_\nu )_{\nu \geq \mu }$ is Cauchy: the successive distances are bounded by the displayed summable sequence, because all earlier transitions are $1$-Lipschitz. Let $a^\mu $ be its limit. Continuity gives $r_\mu a^{\mu +1}=a^\mu $. Define lower coordinates by the image of $a^ N$. This is an inverse-limit element with $d_ N(a^ N,a_ N)\leq \varepsilon /2$. Arbitrary small metric balls give density. $\square$

Lemma 2.14.3.86AC6A041C11 Let $f:A^\bullet \to C^\bullet $ be a chain map. If $f^{q-1}(A^{q-1})$ is dense in $C^{q-1}$ and $H^ q(f)$ has dense image, then $f^ q(Z^ q(A))$ is dense in $Z^ q(C)$.

Proof. Openness of the quotient map $Z^ q(C)\to H^ q(C)$ shows that

\[ \overline{f^ q(Z^ q(A))+B^ q(C)}=Z^ q(C). \]

Explicitly, the image of any nonempty open subset of $Z^ q(C)$ is a nonempty open cohomology subset, so it meets the assumed dense image. Every $dy\in B^ q(C)$ is a limit of $df^{q-1}u$ by degree $q-1$ density. The latter elements equal $f^ q(du)$ and belong to $f^ q(Z^ q(A))$. Its closed linear span, which is just the closure of this linear subspace, therefore contains $B^ q(C)$, proving the assertion. This uses the actual quotient topology and is valid for non-Hausdorff cohomology. $\square$

Theorem 2.14.4.50E84369B7A2 Let $(E_\nu ^\bullet ,r_\nu )$ be an inverse sequence of Fréchet complexes whose transitions have dense image in every degree, and put $E^\bullet =\varprojlim E_\nu ^\bullet $. The natural continuous linear map

\[ \Theta ^ q:H^ q(E)\longrightarrow \varprojlim H^ q(E_\nu ) \]

is surjective. If every transition $H^{q-1}(E_{\nu +1})\to H^{q-1}(E_\nu )$ has dense image, then $\Theta ^ q$ is also injective. Here the right side has its subspace topology in the product of the quotient cohomology spaces.

Proof. Continuity follows from continuity of every coordinate cohomology map and the definition of the product topology. Let $(c_\nu )$ be a compatible family of cohomology classes. Choose $x_0\in Z^ q(E_0)$ representing $c_0$. Suppose that $x_\nu $ representing $c_\nu $ has been chosen. A representative $v\in Z^ q(E_{\nu +1})$ of $c_{\nu +1}$ satisfies

\[ r_\nu v-x_\nu =d b_\nu \qquad (b_\nu \in E_\nu ^{q-1}). \]

Density in degree $q-1$ and continuity of $d$ let us choose $t\in E_{\nu +1}^{q-1}$ so that, with $s_\nu =b_\nu -r_\nu t$,

F2EC9B6D00D9 2.14.5
\begin{equation} d_\nu ^{q-1}(s_\nu ,0)<2^{-\nu -1},\qquad d_\nu ^ q(ds_\nu ,0)<2^{-\nu -1}. \end{equation}

Set $x_{\nu +1}=v-dt$. It still represents $c_{\nu +1}$, and $r_\nu x_{\nu +1}-x_\nu =ds_\nu $.

For each fixed $\mu $, the series $u_\mu =\sum _{\nu \geq \mu }r_{\nu \mu }s_\nu $ converges in $E_\mu ^{q-1}$ by the first estimate, completeness and the Lipschitz transitions. The second estimate makes $r_{\nu \mu }x_\nu $ Cauchy in $E_\mu ^ q$. Its limit $\xi _\mu $ is a cycle. Taking limits in the finite telescoping identity gives

\[ \xi _\mu =x_\mu +d u_\mu . \]

The limits are compatible by continuity of the transitions, so $\xi =(\xi _\mu )\in Z^ q(E)$, and $[\xi _\mu ]=c_\mu $. Controlling the primitives themselves in (2.14.5) is what ensures this equality of classes even when $B^ q(E_\mu )$ is not closed.

For injectivity, let $x\in Z^ q(E)$ with every $x_\nu $ a boundary. The preceding density lemma, in degree $q-1$, shows that $Z^{q-1}(E_{\nu +1})\to Z^{q-1}(E_\nu )$ has dense image; it uses degree $q-2$ density of the complexes. Choose $y_0$ with $dy_0=x_0$. Given $y_\nu $, choose any $v\in E_{\nu +1}^{q-1}$ with $dv=x_{\nu +1}$. Then $r_\nu v-y_\nu $ is a cycle. Approximate it by $r_\nu z$, $z\in Z^{q-1}(E_{\nu +1})$, and set $y_{\nu +1}=v-z$ so that

\[ d_\nu ^{q-1}(r_\nu y_{\nu +1},y_\nu )<2^{-\nu -1}. \]

As in the first lemma, the coordinate sequences converge to a compatible $y\in E^{q-1}$. Continuity gives $dy=x$, so $[x]=0$. $\square$

Theorem 2.14.6 (Abstract Mittag–Leffler theorem).10E2183C49C3 Retain the inverse sequence and dense degreewise transitions of the preceding theorem. For a fixed integer $q$:

  1. If all $H^ q(E_{\nu +1})\to H^ q(E_\nu )$ are surjective, then $H^ q(E)\to H^ q(E_0)$ is surjective and open.

  2. If all those maps have dense image, then $Z^ q(E)\to Z^ q(E_0)$, and hence $H^ q(E)\to H^ q(E_0)$, have dense image.

  3. If all $H^{q-1}(E_{\nu +1})\to H^{q-1}(E_\nu )$ have dense image and all $H^ q(E_{\nu +1})\to H^ q(E_\nu )$ are injective, then $H^ q(E)\to H^ q(E_0)$ is injective.

  4. Let $F^\bullet $ be a Fréchet complex and $\varphi :F^\bullet \to E^\bullet $ a chain map with dense image in each degree. If every $H^ q(F)\to H^ q(E_\nu )$ has dense image, then $\varphi ^ q(Z^ q(F))$ is dense in $Z^ q(E)$, and $H^ q(F)\to H^ q(E)$ has dense image.

In particular, if both the first and third sets of hypotheses hold, $H^ q(E)\to H^ q(E_0)$ is a topological isomorphism, with the quotient topologies whether or not they are Hausdorff. All conclusions also hold with any fixed initial index $N$.

Proof. For (1), lift any class in $H^ q(E_0)$ recursively along the surjective cohomology transitions to a compatible family. Surjectivity of $\Theta ^ q$ lifts it to $H^ q(E)$. A surjective cohomology map of Fréchet complexes is open by theorem 2.12.5; the openness part of that result requires no compactness or closed boundaries.

For (2), lemma 2.14.3 makes the transitions on the closed Fréchet cycle spaces dense. Apply lemma 2.14.2 and (2.14.1). A continuous quotient sends a dense subset to a dense subset of its image closure, so the cohomology image is dense as well.

For (3), an element in the kernel maps to zero in every $H^ q(E_\nu )$: each finite composition into $H^ q(E_0)$ is injective. Injectivity of $\Theta ^ q$ now gives the assertion.

For (4), the projection $E^{q-1}\to E_\nu ^{q-1}$ has dense image by lemma 2.14.2. Density of $\varphi ^{q-1}$ and continuity of that projection imply that $F^{q-1}\to E_\nu ^{q-1}$ is dense. Apply the cycle-density lemma to $F^\bullet \to E_\nu ^\bullet $. Given $x\in Z^ q(E)$, choose $z_ N\in Z^ q(F)$ with

\[ d_ N^ q\bigl ((\varphi z_ N)_ N,x_ N\bigr )<2^{-N}. \]

The Lipschitz transitions imply convergence to $x$ in every fixed coordinate, hence in $Z^ q(E)$. This proves both stated densities. Finally, combine (1), (3) and openness for the isomorphism assertion. Dropping finitely many initial spaces does not change the inverse limit: the omitted coordinates are recovered continuously from the first retained one. $\square$

Proposition 2.14.7.F529678A1F94 Suppose there is a commuting inverse sequence of short exact sequences of Fréchet spaces

\[ 0\longrightarrow A_\nu \xrightarrow {i_\nu }B_\nu \xrightarrow {p_\nu }C_\nu \longrightarrow 0, \]

where $i_\nu $ identifies $A_\nu $ with the closed kernel of $p_\nu $. If $A_{\nu +1}\to A_\nu $ has dense image for every $\nu $, then

\[ 0\longrightarrow \varprojlim A_\nu \longrightarrow \varprojlim B_\nu \longrightarrow \varprojlim C_\nu \longrightarrow 0 \]

is strictly exact: the first arrow is a closed topological embedding and the last is open and surjective. Density of the transitions on $B_\nu $ or $C_\nu $ is not required.

Proof. Choose coherent complete metrics on the $B_\nu $. Fix $c=(c_\nu )\in \varprojlim C_\nu $ and a lift $b_0$ of $c_0$. Given a lift $b_\nu $, take any lift $v\in B_{\nu +1}$ of $c_{\nu +1}$. Commutativity makes $r_\nu v-b_\nu $ an element of $i_\nu (A_\nu )$. Density of $A_{\nu +1}\to A_\nu $ and continuity of $i_\nu $ give $a\in A_{\nu +1}$ such that $b_{\nu +1}=v-i_{\nu +1}a$ satisfies

\[ d_\nu (r_\nu b_{\nu +1},b_\nu )<2^{-\nu -1}. \]

It remains a lift of $c_{\nu +1}$. Coordinate limits as in lemma 2.14.2 give a compatible $b\in \varprojlim B_\nu $, and $p_\mu b_\mu =c_\mu $ follows by continuity from every term of the $\mu $-coordinate sequence. This proves surjectivity. The map is open by proposition 2.12.1, since both limits are Fréchet. Its kernel is precisely the inverse limit of the closed kernels $i_\nu (A_\nu )$; the coordinate topological identifications identify this closed subspace with $\varprojlim A_\nu $, including its topology. $\square$
