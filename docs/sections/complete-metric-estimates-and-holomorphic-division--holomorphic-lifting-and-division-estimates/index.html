21F1C844A564

Use the unscaled curvature, increasing barred multi-indices and metric volumes of section 6.26. All bundle metrics in the first five statements are smooth. An inverse-curvature energy always has the semidefinite dual meaning; it does not presume an invertible curvature matrix.

Lemma 6.28.1.2771F5EE97D1 For the Dolbeault Laplacian of a smooth Hermitian bundle and base metric, every distributional harmonic form with locally $L^2$ coefficients is smooth.

Proof. The principal symbol of $P=D''\delta ''+\delta ''D''$ is a positive scalar quadratic form times the coefficient identity: the symbols of wedge and contraction satisfy their usual anticommutator identity. On a relatively compact real coordinate ball, multiplication by the smooth positive square root of the coefficient metric times the volume density identifies the $L^2$ pairing with the ordinary coefficient pairing. The conjugated operator still has this scalar principal symbol. It has divergence form $-\partial _ i(a^{ij}\partial _ j)$ plus smooth matrix terms of order at most one, with $a$ uniformly positive on a smaller ball.

Choose a cutoff $\chi $ in that ball and set $v=\chi w$, extended by zero in the coordinate space. Since $Pw=0$, $Pv=[P,\chi ]w$ belongs to $H^{-1}$ and has norm bounded by a constant times the local $L^2$ norm of $w$. Extend the coefficients smoothly beyond the compact support, preserving uniform ellipticity there, and let $J_ t$ be ordinary convolution. The matrix first-order commutator calculation in graph-norm density by cutoffs and mollification gives

\[ \| [a^{ij}\partial _ j,J_ t]v\| _2\leq C\| v\| _2. \]

Consequently $[P,J_ t]v$ is bounded in $H^{-1}$: write its principal part as $-\partial _ i[a^{ij}\partial _ j,J_ t]v$; its lower-order commutators have the same or better bound. Convolution is bounded on $H^{-1}$, so $PJ_ tv$ is uniformly bounded there.

Integration by parts and uniform positivity give, for smooth forms $z$ supported in this fixed ball,

\[ \| z\| _{H^1}^2\leq C\bigl (|\langle Pz,z\rangle |+\| z\| _2^2\bigr ). \]

Indeed the principal integral dominates the squared first derivatives, and each lower-order integral is bounded by $C\| z\| _2\| z\| _{H^1}$ and absorbed by Young's inequality. Apply this to $J_ tv$, using $H^{-1}$–$H^1$ duality, to obtain a uniform $H^1$ bound. Weak compactness and $J_ tv\to v$ in $L^2$ show that $v\in H^1$. Thus $w\in H^1_{\rm loc}$. The checked vector divergence-form interior theorem interior regularity for elliptic systems in divergence form, successively applied to $Pw=0$ on nested balls, now gives every Sobolev order and smoothness. This argument supplies the $H^1$ starting hypothesis of that theorem. $\square$

Theorem 6.28.2.E15FB3F8A946 Let $(X,\omega )$ be complete Kähler, let $(E,h)$ be a smooth Hermitian holomorphic bundle, and let $1\leq q\leq n$. Fix $0\leq p\leq n$. Suppose $B=[\mathrm i\Theta (E),\Lambda _\omega ]\geq 0$ in bidegree $(p,q)$. If $F$ is locally $L^2$, is closed distributionally, and has finite inverse energy $M=\int _ X|F|_{B^{-1}}^2\, \mathrm dV_\omega $, then

48191F980D4B 6.28.3
\begin{equation} D''u=F,\qquad \| u\| _2^2\leq M \end{equation}

for an $L^2$ form $u$ of type $(p,q-1)$. Global ordinary $L^2$ integrability of $F$ is not required.

If $X$ merely possesses a complete Kähler metric and $\omega $ is any Kähler metric, the same conclusion holds in degree $(n,q)$ provided $E$ is $m$-semipositive with $m\geq \min (n-q+1,\operatorname {rank}E)$. The norm and inverse energy in this conclusion use the original $\omega $. Smooth data admit a smooth choice in either assertion.

Proof. For compactly supported smooth $v$, curvature Cauchy–Schwarz and theorem 10.33.2 give

\[ |\langle F,v\rangle |^2 \leq M\int _ X\langle Bv,v\rangle \, \mathrm dV_\omega \leq M\bigl (\| \delta ''v\| _2^2+\| D''v\| _2^2\bigr ). \]

Define $Av=(\delta ''v,D''v)$ in the direct sum of degrees $(p,q-1)$ and $(p,q+1)$. The displayed bound makes pairing with $F$ a well-defined bounded functional on $\operatorname {Ran}A$. Hilbert representation on its closure gives $(u,w)$ in that closure, with $\| u\| _2^2+\| w\| _2^2\leq M$, and

\[ F=D''u+\delta ''w \]

distributionally. In top barred degree the second space is zero. Otherwise $w$ is an $L^2$ limit of forms $D''v_ j$, hence $D''w=0$ distributionally. Applying $D''$ to the last identity, using $D''F=0$, gives $D''\delta ''w=0$. Thus $w$ is distributionally harmonic and is smooth by lemma 6.28.1.

Take the complete gradient cutoffs of lemma 6.26.1. Integrating the harmonic equation against $\chi _ R^2w$ and using $D''w=0$ gives

\[ \| \chi _ R\delta ''w\| _2^2 \leq 2\| \chi _ R\delta ''w\| _2\, \| (\bar\partial \chi _ R\wedge )^*w\| _2. \]

All integrals here have compact support; no prior global derivative bound is assumed. It follows that $\| \chi _ R\delta ''w\| _2^2\leq C R^{-2}\| w\| _2^2$. Letting $R\to \infty $ on each compact set proves $\delta ''w=0$, and establishes (6.28.3).

For the original-metric assertion put $\omega _\varepsilon =\omega +\varepsilon \widehat\omega $. These metrics are complete. The full coefficient inequalities of lemma 6.27.1 show semipositivity and bound the inverse energy by $M$ for each $\omega _\varepsilon $. The first assertion applies to the locally $L^2$ datum for each of these metrics. The fixed-comparison-density weak limit in the proof of theorem 6.27.6 now applies verbatim: first take local weak limits; test the norm with fixed $\omega _{\varepsilon _ k}$ for indices $j\geq k$; then let $\varepsilon _ k\downarrow 0$ and exhaust $X$. It gives the original norm bound. This argument uses no global ordinary norm of $F$. Finally lemma 6.27.8 gives a smooth minimum-norm primitive for smooth data. $\square$

Theorem 6.28.4.FD2B7A8ED856 Let $(X,\omega )$ possess a complete Kähler metric. Let $G:E\to Q$ be a surjective holomorphic bundle map, with the induced quotient metric on $Q$. Put $r=\operatorname {rank}E$, $a=\operatorname {rank}Q$, $s=r-a$, and fix $0\leq k\leq n$. Set $m=\min (n-k,s)$. Suppose $E$ is $m$-semipositive and a Hermitian line bundle $L$ satisfies

\[ \mathrm i\Theta (L)\geq (m+\epsilon )\mathrm i\Theta (\det Q), \qquad \epsilon >0. \]

Every closed $L^2$ form $f$ of type $(n,k)$ with values in $Q\otimes L$ has a closed lift $h$ with values in $E\otimes L$ such that

1FDBE18C9CE0 6.28.5
\begin{equation} Gh=f,\qquad \| h\| _2^2\leq (1+m/\epsilon )\| f\| _2^2. \end{equation}

Smooth data admit a smooth lift. When $m=0$, no curvature hypothesis is needed and the constant is one.

Proof. If $Q=0$ take $h=0$. If $s=0$, the inverse of $G$ is holomorphic and isometric for the quotient metric. If $k=n$, the orthogonal lift $G^*f$ is automatically closed by degree and isometric. These cases give the stated $m=0$ clause. Assume henceforth $s\geq 1$, $k<n$, so $m\geq 1$.

Let $S=\ker G$ and use the orthogonal splitting $E=S\oplus Q$. The complete connection and curvature calculation of proposition 10.34.9 writes the second fundamental form as $\beta =\sum _ j\mathrm dz_ j B_ j$ and gives

\[ R_ S=R_ E|_ S-C_\beta ,\qquad R_ Q=R_ E|_ Q+(B_ jB_ l^*)_{j\bar l},\qquad C_\beta (v)=\left|\sum _ jB_ jv_ j\right|^2. \]

Put $\alpha =\operatorname {tr}_ S C_\beta $. The rank-one tests contained in $m$-semipositivity give $\operatorname {tr}_ Q R_ E|_ Q\geq 0$, hence $R_{\det Q}\geq \alpha $. Moreover $m\alpha \otimes I_ S-C_\beta \geq _ m0$ by theorem 10.34.2, including $s=m=1$. The curvature assumption therefore gives

8CCE15D3C2B6 6.28.6
\begin{equation} R_{S\otimes L}\geq _ m(m+\epsilon )\alpha \otimes I_ S-C_\beta \geq _ m(\epsilon /m)C_\beta . \end{equation}

The last difference is $(1+\epsilon /m)(m\alpha \otimes I_ S-C_\beta )$.

In degree $(n,k+1)$ the coefficient tensors $(v_{jJ})_ j$ have rank at most $\min (n-k,s)=m$, by proposition 10.33.6. If $B=[\mathrm i\Theta (S\otimes L),\Lambda _\omega ]$, then

B33FBDD0925E 6.28.7
\begin{equation} \langle Bv,v\rangle \geq (\epsilon /m) \sum _{|J|=k}\left|\sum _ j B_ jv_{jJ}\right|^2, \qquad |\beta ^*\wedge f|_{B^{-1}}^2\leq (m/\epsilon )|f|^2. \end{equation}

Indeed the adjoint of $f\mapsto \beta ^*\wedge f$ is contraction with coefficients $\sum _ jB_ jv_{jJ}$, with the common top-form sign cancelled in its squared norm. Pairing with $f$ and applying finite-dimensional Cauchy–Schwarz proves the second inequality, also on a kernel of $B$.

Write $j:S\to E$ for inclusion. The $(0,1)$ connection block gives $D''(G^*f)=-j(\beta ^*\wedge f)$. Thus $F=\beta ^*\wedge f$ is locally $L^2$ and closed distributionally: apply $(D'')^2=0$ and use that $j$ is holomorphic and injective. It need not be globally $L^2$. Its finite inverse energy is bounded by $(m/\epsilon )\| f\| _2^2$ by (6.28.7). The original-metric part of theorem 6.28.2 supplies $D''u=F$ with that squared norm bound. Set $h=G^*f+ju$. Then $Gh=f$, $D''h=0$, and orthogonality gives pointwise $|h|^2=|f|^2+|u|^2$. This proves (6.28.5). If $f$ is smooth, choose the smooth primitive supplied by the same theorem. $\square$

Corollary 6.28.8.2F176031B9F6 Under the bundle and curvature hypotheses of theorem 6.28.4, if $X$ has a smooth PSH exhaustion, the induced map

\[ H^ k(X,K_ X\otimes \mathcal O(E\otimes L))\longrightarrow H^ k(X,K_ X\otimes \mathcal O(Q\otimes L)) \]

is surjective.

Proof. Use the smooth bundle-valued Dolbeault resolution in corollary 10.43.11 and represent a class by a smooth closed $(n,k)$-form $f$. Shift the exhaustion $\rho $ to be nonnegative. The compact-band convex weight construction (6.26.9) gives increasing convex $\chi $ for which $f$ is $L^2$ after replacing the metric of $L$ by $h_ L\mathrm e^{-\chi (\rho )}$. Its added curvature is semipositive, so all lifting inequalities persist. A complete Kähler metric exists by lemma 6.26.8. The preceding theorem gives a smooth closed lift, representing a preimage of the given class. This proves surjectivity, without any assertion that the entire next kernel-bundle cohomology group vanishes. $\square$

Theorem 6.28.9.A32EC1620046 Let $G:E\to Q$ be surjective, now with an arbitrary smooth metric on $Q$, on a Kähler manifold possessing a complete Kähler metric. Keep $r,a,s,k,m$ as above and assume $E\geq _ m0$ and $\mathrm i\Theta (L)\geq (m+\epsilon )\mathrm i\Theta (\det Q)$. Put $H=GG^*$, $D=\det H$, and $H^{\rm adj}=\operatorname {adj}H$. For closed $(n,k)$ data satisfying

\[ I=\int _ X\langle H^{\rm adj}f,f\rangle D^{-m-1-\epsilon }\, \mathrm dV_\omega <\infty \]

there is a closed lift with

E2E00DB68AAD 6.28.10
\begin{equation} Gh=f,\qquad \int _ X|h|^2D^{-m-\epsilon }\, \mathrm dV_\omega \leq (1+m/\epsilon )I. \end{equation}

Here all unmarked bundle norms use the original metrics.

Proof. The case $a=0$ is trivial, with $D=1$; assume $a\geq 1$. Since $G$ is onto, $H$ is positive definite and $A=G^*H^{-1}$ satisfies $GA=I$. The map $AG$ is the orthogonal projection onto $(\ker G)^\perp $: it is self-adjoint and idempotent, has kernel $\ker G$, and is the identity on $\operatorname {Ran}G^*$. Thus $Av$ is the least-norm lift, and the quotient metric is

\[ |v|_{Q'}^2=|Av|_ E^2=\langle H^{-1}v,v\rangle _ Q, \qquad \det h_{Q'}=D^{-1}\det h_ Q. \]

The cofactor identity $HH^{\rm adj}=DI$ gives $H^{-1}=D^{-1}H^{\rm adj}$. These expressions are frame-invariant: the endomorphism transforms by conjugacy and its determinant is a global positive scalar. The determinant metric identity gives

\[ \mathrm i\Theta (\det Q')=\mathrm i\Theta (\det Q)+\mathcal H(\log D). \]

Replace $h_ L$ by $h_{L'}=h_ LD^{-m-\epsilon }$. Its curvature is $\mathrm i\Theta (L)+(m+\epsilon )\mathcal H(\log D)$, so it dominates $(m+\epsilon )\mathrm i\Theta (\det Q')$. Apply theorem 6.28.4 to $(Q',L')$. Its datum norm is exactly $I$ and its lift norm is the left side of (6.28.10). No inverse or fractional power is asserted at a singular matrix in this step. $\square$

Lemma 6.28.11.8746D0F80187 Let $(X,\omega )$ have a smooth PSH exhaustion $\rho \geq 0$, and let $\sigma $ be a holomorphic section of a smooth Hermitian bundle whose zero set $Y$ is nowhere dense. For each $c>0$, the open manifold $\{ \rho <c\} \setminus Y$ admits a complete Kähler metric. Alternatively, without an exhaustion, if $\omega $ is complete and the bundle curvature satisfies $R_ V\leq C\omega \otimes I$, $C\geq 0$, globally in the Griffiths sense, then $X\setminus Y$ has a complete Kähler metric.

Proof. On the compact set $\{ \rho \leq c\} $ the bundle curvature is bounded. On its complement of $Y$, the Chern-normal-frame differentiation formula

\[ \mathcal H(\log |\sigma |^2)(\xi ,\bar\xi ) =\frac{|D'_\xi \sigma |^2}{|\sigma |^2} -\frac{|\langle D'_\xi \sigma ,\sigma \rangle |^2}{|\sigma |^4} -\frac{\langle R_ V(\xi ,\bar\xi )\sigma ,\sigma \rangle }{|\sigma |^2} \]

shows $\mathcal H(\log |\sigma |^2)\geq -C\omega $ there: the first two terms are nonnegative by Cauchy–Schwarz. Subtract a constant to obtain $\tau =\log |\sigma |^2-A\leq -2$ and choose $a=1/(C+1)$. Set

\[ r=-a\log (-\tau ),\qquad b=-\log (c-\rho ),\qquad \gamma =\omega +\mathcal H(r)+\mathcal H(b). \]

The chain rule gives

\[ \mathcal H(r)\geq -\tfrac 12\omega + a^{-1}\mathrm i\partial r\wedge \bar\partial r, \qquad \mathcal H(b)\geq \mathrm i\partial b\wedge \bar\partial b. \]

Thus $\gamma $ is Kähler and the gradient of $F=-r+b$ is bounded for $\gamma $. The function $F$ is bounded below. Its closed sublevels are compact in the indicated open manifold: $r$ is bounded above and $b$ below, so a bound on their sum bounds $b$ above and $r$ below. It forces $\rho $ away from $c$ and $|\sigma |$ away from zero on the compact ambient sublevel. The bounded-gradient exhaustion criterion corollary 6.26.3 proves completeness. This construction uses only curvature bounds on this compact set.

For the alternative assertion keep $\tau =\log |\sigma |^2$, now possibly unbounded above. Set $a=1/(2\pi (C+1))$ and choose $\chi '(t)=a(\arctan t+\pi /2)$, so $0<\chi '\leq 1/(2(C+1))$ and $\chi ''=a/(1+t^2)$. Then

\[ \gamma =\omega +\mathcal H(\chi (\tau )) \geq \tfrac 12\omega + \mathrm i\partial (\sqrt a\operatorname {arsinh}\tau ) \wedge \bar\partial (\sqrt a\operatorname {arsinh}\tau ). \]

A $\gamma $-Cauchy sequence converges for the complete $\omega $. The bounded-gradient function $\sqrt a\operatorname {arsinh}\tau $ has Cauchy values, so its limit cannot lie in $Y$, where it tends to $-\infty $. Local smooth metric equivalence at the limit proves $\gamma $ convergence and completeness. $\square$

Lemma 6.28.12.52E74CE23DF6 Let $Y$ be a nowhere dense analytic subset of a complex manifold. If $v$ is a locally $L^2$ bundle-valued form and $w$ is a locally $L^1$ form of the next barred degree, and $D''v=w$ distributionally off $Y$, then the equation holds everywhere. In particular a locally $L^2$ form closed off $Y$ is closed everywhere.

Proof. First work near a regular point of $Y$, with coordinates in which $Y=\{ z_1=\cdots =z_ d=0\} $ and $d\geq 1$. Its regular part is contained in the coordinate hypersurface $z_1=0$. Take a cutoff $\lambda _ t$ which vanishes for $|z_1|\leq t$, equals one for $|z_1|\geq 2t$, and has derivative at most $C/t$. On a fixed compact polydisc, $\| \mathrm d\lambda _ t\| _2\leq C'$ because the transition tube has volume $O(t^2)$. Testing the equation away from $z_1=0$ with $\lambda _ t$ times a compact test form, the error is bounded by $C'\| v\| _{L^2(\{ |z_1|\leq 2t\} )}$ and tends to zero. The other terms, including the $L^1$ pairing with $w$, converge by dominated convergence. This proves removability near the regular locus. Smooth metrics and bundle coefficients change these compact estimates by fixed constants.

Apply this argument successively to the finite singular-locus filtration of analytic singular strata and Hausdorff dimension. First it extends the equation through $Y_{\rm reg}$, leaving only $Y_{\rm sing}$; repeat with its regular part. Dimension drops strictly, so the process terminates. Analytic sets here have measure zero, by that corollary or measure-zero holomorphic zero sets; hence choices of coefficients on $Y$ do not affect the form or the equation. $\square$

Theorem 6.28.13.A729272B8D69 The conclusion of theorem 6.28.9 remains true for a generically surjective holomorphic $G:E\to Q$ if $X$ has a smooth PSH exhaustion. Its integral hypotheses and estimate are taken over $U=X\setminus Y$, where $Y$ is the rank-deficient analytic set. The datum is assumed locally $L^2$ and closed on $X$.

Proof. For $a\geq 1$ take $\sigma =\bigwedge ^ aG\in \mathcal O(\operatorname {Hom}(\bigwedge ^ aE,\det Q))$. Then $Y=\{ \sigma =0\} $ is analytic and nowhere dense on each component by hypothesis. On $U$, the kernel is a holomorphic bundle: where $G=(G_0,G_1)$ has an invertible $a$-minor, its kernel frame is $w\mapsto (-G_0^{-1}G_1w,w)$. The adjugate matrix is defined polynomially even on $Y$. It is positive semidefinite there: diagonalize $H$ unitarily, when its adjugate eigenvalues are the products of all but one nonnegative eigenvalue. For $a=1$ the empty product gives $H^{\rm adj}=1$. Only on $U$ do we use $H^{-1}$ and negative powers of $D$.

Choose $c_ j\uparrow \infty $. The preceding completeness lemma gives complete Kähler metrics on $U_ j=\{ \rho <c_ j\} \setminus Y$. The arbitrary-metric theorem, with the restricted original metric $\omega $, produces closed lifts $h_ j$ with uniform weighted norm at most $(1+m/\epsilon )I$. On every compact subset of $U$ this weight is fixed, smooth and positive, so diagonal weak limits give a closed lift $h$ on $U$. Fixed weighted weak lower semicontinuity on compact sets and exhaustion preserve the displayed integral bound.

On a compact subset of $X$, the smooth $D$ is bounded above. Since $m+\epsilon >0$, $D^{-m-\epsilon }$ is bounded below on $U$ there. Consequently $h$ extends as an $L^2_{\rm loc}$ form across the null set $Y$. The preceding removal lemma gives $D''h=0$ on $X$. The identity $Gh=f$ holds almost everywhere and hence as distributions on $X$. The same estimates hold because $Y$ is null. This proof does not assert smoothness of a positive-degree lift across the rank locus. $\square$

Corollary 6.28.14.CC5F6C2B80CC Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ possess a complete Kähler metric, let $g=(g_1,\ldots ,g_ r)$ be holomorphic, $r\geq 1$, and let $\phi $ be smooth PSH. Suppose $g$ is not identically zero on any component. Put $m=\min (n,r-1)$, $Z=\{ g=0\} $ and $\epsilon >0$. For holomorphic $f$ such that

\[ I=\int _{\Omega \setminus Z}|f|^2|g|^{-2(m+1+\epsilon )} \mathrm e^{-\phi }\, \mathrm dV<\infty , \]

there exist holomorphic $h_1,\ldots ,h_ r$ on $\Omega $ with

3CE759FE5C62 6.28.15
\begin{equation} f=\sum _ jg_ jh_ j,\qquad \int _{\Omega \setminus Z}|h|^2|g|^{-2(m+\epsilon )} \mathrm e^{-\phi }\, \mathrm dV \leq (1+m/\epsilon )I. \end{equation}

Here $\mathrm dV$ may be Lebesgue measure; the same constant occurs on both sides.

Proof. The open manifold $U=\Omega \setminus Z$ possesses a complete Kähler metric even without a PSH exhaustion of $\Omega $. Indeed $\tau =\log |g|^2$ is smooth PSH on $U$. Choose $\chi '(t)=\arctan t+\pi /2>0$ and $\chi ''(t)=(1+t^2)^{-1}$. Then $\gamma =\widehat\omega +\mathcal H(\chi (\tau ))$ is Kähler, dominates the given complete metric, and dominates $\mathrm i\partial \operatorname {arsinh}\tau \wedge \bar\partial \operatorname {arsinh}\tau $. A $\gamma $-Cauchy sequence converges for $\widehat\omega $ to a point of $\Omega $. Its values of $\operatorname {arsinh}\tau $ are Cauchy by the gradient bound; thus its limit cannot lie in $Z$, where $\tau \to -\infty $. At a point of $U$ smooth local metric equivalence gives convergence for $\gamma $. This proves completeness.

Apply theorem 6.28.9 on $U$ to the flat trivial bundles $E=\mathcal O^ r$, $Q=\mathcal O$, $G=g$, and $L$ with metric $\mathrm e^{-\phi }$, taking $k=0$ and the Euclidean original metric. Here $H=D=|g|^2$ and $H^{\rm adj}=1$. Identify functions with their multiples of the global holomorphic top form; its norm times metric volume is a common constant times Lebesgue measure, which cancels in the estimate. The lift is closed in degree $(n,0)$, so its coefficients are holomorphic on $U$ by holomorphic distributions and minimal weighted solutions. The weight in the lift integral is bounded below on each compact set, since $|g|$ and $\phi $ are locally bounded above. Thus $h$ is locally $L^2$ across $Z$. Apply lemma 6.28.12 and the same holomorphy lemma to extend it holomorphically. The identity extends as well. When $r=1$, $m=0$, the argument is simply $h=f/g$ on $U$, followed by this same $L^2$ extension; no division by $m$ occurs. $\square$

Corollary 6.28.16.C5757B16FD09 The same scalar conclusion holds for any PSH weight $\phi $ on a pseudoconvex open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, with its usual upper-semicontinuous representative, provided $\phi \not\equiv -\infty $ on each component.

Proof. Use the increasing relatively compact pseudoconvex exhaustion domains and radial regularizations from theorem 6.25.10. They give $\Omega _ j\uparrow \Omega $ and smooth PSH $\phi _ j\downarrow \phi $ on every fixed $\Omega _ k$ for $j\geq k$, with $\phi _ j\geq \phi $. Each $\Omega _ j$ admits a complete Kähler metric by its smooth PSH exhaustion and lemma 6.26.8. The preceding smooth-weight result gives holomorphic lifts $h_ j$ on $\Omega _ j$ with squared weighted norm at most $(1+m/\epsilon )I$. The input bound holds since $\mathrm e^{-\phi _ j}\leq \mathrm e^{-\phi }$.

On a fixed compact subset, $|g|$ is bounded above and the $\phi _ j$ are bounded above by one fixed smooth approximant. The weighted bounds therefore give local ordinary $L^2$ bounds even across $Z$. Take a diagonal weak limit $h$, which is holomorphic distributionally and hence holomorphic. Multiplication by $g$ preserves local weak convergence, giving $g\cdot h=f$. On a fixed $\Omega _ k\setminus Z$, use the fixed weight $|g|^{-2(m+\epsilon )}\mathrm e^{-\phi _ k}$, first on compact sets. For $j\geq k$ its integral is bounded by the corresponding $\phi _ j$ integral. Weak lower semicontinuity and exhaustion give the same bound for $h$. Now let $k\to \infty $ with the regularization indices fixed before each weak limit; monotone convergence of the weights gives (6.28.15) with $\phi $. Zero and infinite values of the weight are interpreted by this monotone limit. No smooth approximation on an arbitrary nonpseudoconvex complete Kähler open set is asserted here. $\square$
