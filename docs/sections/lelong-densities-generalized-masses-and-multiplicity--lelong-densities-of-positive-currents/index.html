6.33 Lelong densities of positive currents38F79B8D878B

38F79B8D878B

Use $d^ c=\frac{i}{4\pi }(\bar\partial -\partial )$ and $dd^ c=\frac{i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial $. In this section write

\[ \beta =dd^ c|z|^2=\frac{i}{2\pi }\sum _{j=1}^ n dz_ j\wedge d\bar z_ j. \]

Thus $\beta $ is $1/\pi $ times the Euclidean Hermitian form. For a complex $p$-plane $L$ through the origin, $\int _{L\cap B(0,r)}\beta ^ p=r^{2p}$: its restriction is $p!\pi ^{-p}$ times Euclidean $2p$-volume, and the $2p$-ball has volume $\pi ^ p r^{2p}/p!$. No additional factorial belongs in the density below.

Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ be open, $n\ge 1$, and let $T$ be a closed positive current of bidimension $(p,p)$, $0\le p\le n$. Put $\mu =T\wedge \beta ^ p$. This is a positive Radon measure, by coefficient measures and compactness of positive currents. For $a\in \Omega $ and $0<r<\operatorname {dist}(a,\mathbb C^ n\setminus \Omega )$ set

\[ D_ T(a,r)=\frac{\mu (B(a,r))}{r^{2p}}. \]

All balls in this definition are open.

Theorem 6.33.1.7ACCEC6E0526 The function $r\mapsto D_ T(a,r)$ is nonnegative, nondecreasing and left continuous. Its finite limit

\[ \nu (T,a)=\lim _{r\downarrow 0}D_ T(a,r) \]

exists. For $p\ge 1$ put $\alpha _ a=dd^ c\log |z-a|^2$ on $\Omega \setminus \{ a\} $. For every $0<r_1<r_2$ with $\overline{B(a,r_2)}\subset \Omega $,

F451CA98766C 6.33.2
\begin{equation} D_ T(a,r_2)-D_ T(a,r_1) =\int _{\{ r_1\le |z-a|<r_2\} }T\wedge \alpha _ a^ p. \end{equation}

Consequently

\[ D_ T(a,r)-\nu (T,a) =\int _{\{ 0<|z-a|<r\} }T\wedge \alpha _ a^ p, \qquad D_ T(a,r+)=\frac{\mu (\overline{B(a,r)})}{r^{2p}}. \]

For $p=0$, $T$ is a positive measure, $D_ T(a,r)=T(B(a,r))$ and $\nu (T,a)=T(\{ a\} )$; the same annular identity holds with $\alpha _ a^0=1$.

Proof. Translate $a$ to zero and put $\rho =|z|^2$. Direct differentiation gives

04F9973F92E1 6.33.3
\begin{equation} \alpha =\frac{\beta }{\rho } -\frac{d\rho \wedge d^ c\rho }{\rho ^2},\qquad d^ c\log \rho =\rho ^{-1}d^ c\rho . \end{equation}

The Hermitian matrix of $\log \rho $ is $\rho ^{-1}I-\rho ^{-2}(\bar z_ jz_ k)_{j,k}$; Cauchy–Schwarz shows it is nonnegative. Also $\rho ^{-1}\beta -\alpha =\rho ^{-2}d\rho \wedge d^ c\rho $ is nonnegative. These are strongly positive $(1,1)$-forms. Expanding a difference of powers therefore gives, as measures off zero,

601F25304514 6.33.4
\begin{equation} 0\le T\wedge \alpha ^ p\le \rho ^{-p}\mu . \end{equation}

First let $p\ge 1$ and $T$ be smooth. Stokes' formula, with spheres oriented as boundaries of balls, gives

\[ \int _{B(0,r)}T\wedge \beta ^ p =\int _{\partial B(0,r)}T\wedge d^ c\rho \wedge \beta ^{p-1}. \]

Indeed $T$ and $\beta $ are closed and $T$ has even real degree, so the exterior derivative of the integrand on the right is $T\wedge \beta ^ p$. On a sphere $\rho =r^2$ the pullback of $d\rho $ is zero. Hence equation 6.33.3 implies

\[ \int _{\partial B(0,r)}T\wedge d^ c\log \rho \wedge \alpha ^{p-1} =r^{-2p}\int _{B(0,r)}T\wedge \beta ^ p. \]

Apply Stokes on the closed annulus. Its inner sphere has the negative boundary orientation. This proves the annular identity for smooth $T$ and, by positivity of $\alpha ^ p$, monotonicity.

For general $T$, convolve its coefficient distributions with a nonnegative smooth approximate identity of integral one, $\rho _\varepsilon (x)=\varepsilon ^{-2n}\rho _1(x/\varepsilon )$. On any fixed relatively compact coordinate set this gives smooth closed positive $T_\varepsilon \to T$ weakly, as in the regularization following coefficient measures and compactness of positive currents. Thus $T_\varepsilon \wedge \beta ^ p\to \mu $ vaguely, and $T_\varepsilon \wedge \alpha ^ p\to T\wedge \alpha ^ p$ vaguely away from zero. In a fixed ball only countably many spheres have positive $\mu $-mass: for each positive integer $m$ there are only finitely many disjoint spheres of mass at least $1/m$. By equation 6.33.4, the same good radii give zero $T\wedge \alpha ^ p$-mass to the spheres. Vague convergence of positive measures gives convergence on relatively compact sets with zero-mass boundary. Passing to the smooth annular identity therefore proves it whenever both radii are good. In particular $D_ T$ is nondecreasing on this dense set of radii.

Continuity from below for the measure of open balls gives left continuity of $D_ T$. Approximating each endpoint from below by good radii extends monotonicity to every radius. For the identity itself, choose good $r_{j,k}\uparrow r_ j$, $j=1,2$, with $r_{1,k}<r_{2,k}$. The annulus indicators converge pointwise to the indicator of $\{ r_1\le |z|<r_2\} $. They are supported in one compact annulus away from zero, where equation 6.33.4 gives finite mass. Dominated convergence proves equation 6.33.2 with exactly its half-open endpoint convention. The increasing nonnegative density has a finite limit at zero, bounded by its value at any fixed larger radius. Letting the inner radius decrease to zero proves the punctured-ball formula by continuity from below. Continuity from above of the finite measure of decreasing balls gives the stated right limit. In particular the density need not be right continuous when a sphere carries mass.

For $p=0$ no Stokes formula with a negative power is used. All assertions follow directly from the corresponding continuity properties of the measure $T$, and $B(0,r_2)\setminus B(0,r_1)=\{ r_1\le |z|<r_2\} $. $\square$

Corollary 6.33.5.6FD01B620D33 Let $K\subset \Omega $ be compact and $0<r_0<\operatorname {dist}(K,\mathbb C^ n\setminus \Omega )$. Write $K_{r_0}=\{ z:\operatorname {dist}(z,K)\le r_0\} $. Then

\[ \mu (B(a,r))\le \frac{\mu (K_{r_0})}{r_0^{2p}}r^{2p} \quad (a\in K,\ 0<r\le r_0). \]

If $p>0$, $\mu $ and all coefficient measures of $T$ have no atoms.

Proof. Monotonicity bounds $D_ T(a,r)$ by $D_ T(a,r_0)\le \mu (K_{r_0})/r_0^{2p}$. This proves the estimate. Let $r\downarrow 0$ for the atom assertion, and use the coefficient domination in coefficient measures and compactness of positive currents. $\square$

6.33.6 A bounded-potential comparison at the center5705A65255D8

The products in the next two lemmas are the existing bounded products of locality and positivity of bounded Bedford–Taylor products, decreasing continuity of bounded Bedford–Taylor products; their base current may have any positive bidimension.

Lemma 6.33.7.C9AA991622D8 Suppose $p\ge 1$, $D\Subset \Omega $ is open, and $v,w$ are continuous plurisubharmonic functions on a neighborhood of $\overline D$. If $v=w$ on a neighborhood of $\partial D$, then

\[ \int _ D T\wedge (dd^ cv)^ p=\int _ D T\wedge (dd^ cw)^ p. \]

No smoothness of the boundary is required.

Proof. Bounded-product symmetry and its defining recursion give

\[ T\wedge (dd^ cv)^ p-T\wedge (dd^ cw)^ p =dd^ c\left((v-w)\sum _{j=0}^{p-1} T\wedge (dd^ cv)^ j\wedge (dd^ cw)^{p-1-j}\right). \]

For each summand the current after the weight has bidimension $(1,1)$; the recursion applies to $v$ and $w$ separately and is linear in their difference. The weighted current vanishes near $\partial D$. Restrict it to $D$ and extend it by zero; its support is a compact subset of $D$, so this extension introduces no boundary term. The same identity holds for this extension and for the difference measure restricted to $D$ and extended by zero. Pair that identity with a smooth compactly supported function equal to one near $\overline D$. The $dd^ c$ pairing is zero, because the derivatives of the test function vanish near the weighted current's support. On the left this pairing is exactly the difference of the two integrals over $D$: their measures agree near $\partial D$. This proves the claim. $\square$

Lemma 6.33.8.50567CE0F546 For $p\ge 1$, $s<\log r^2$ and $\overline{B(a,r)}\subset \Omega $, the bounded continuous plurisubharmonic function $u_ s(z)=\max \{ \log |z-a|^2,s\} $ satisfies

21D941444C0C 6.33.9
\begin{equation} \int _{B(a,r)}T\wedge (dd^ cu_ s)^ p=D_ T(a,r). \end{equation}

Here $u_ s(a)=s$. This statement does not form a product of unbounded potentials at $a$.

Proof. Again take $a=0$. Smooth $t\mapsto \max \{ t,s\} $ by convolution with an even nonnegative one-dimensional mollifier of integral one and support $[-\delta ,\delta ]$. The result $\chi _\delta $ is convex and nondecreasing, equals $s$ for $t\le s-\delta $, equals $t$ for $t\ge s+\delta $, and differs from $\max \{ t,s\} $ by at most $\delta $. Thus $\chi _\delta (\log |z|^2)$ extends smoothly and plurisubharmonically across zero, where it is constant, and converges uniformly to $u_ s$. It equals $\log |z|^2$ near the sphere of radius $r$ when $\delta <\log r^2-s$.

For smooth $T$, Stokes gives the total product mass in the ball as

\[ \int _{\partial B(0,r)}T\wedge d^ c\chi _\delta (\log |z|^2)\wedge (dd^ c\chi _\delta (\log |z|^2))^{p-1} =D_ T(0,r), \]

by the smooth boundary calculation in theorem 6.33.1. The uniform-continuity theorem uniform convergence of bounded Bedford–Taylor products passes to $u_ s$. Its product measure has zero mass on this sphere, since near it both $T$ and $u_ s$ are smooth. Hence the equality holds for smooth $T$.

Now use the smooth closed positive $T_\varepsilon $ above and the same uniform-continuity theorem with fixed continuous potential $u_ s$. The product measures converge vaguely. Outside $\{ |z|\le e^{s/2}\} $ their limit equals $T\wedge \alpha ^ p$ by locality, and its sphere masses there are dominated by those of $\mu $, by equation 6.33.4. Thus at every good $r>e^{s/2}$ the equality for $T_\varepsilon $ passes to $T$. Both sides of equation 6.33.9 are left continuous in $r$. Approaching an arbitrary such $r$ by good radii proves the formula in its full stated range. $\square$

Theorem 6.33.10.3FA889730FFE The number $\nu (T,a)$ is independent of holomorphic coordinates. It therefore defines the Lelong number of every closed positive current of bidimension $(p,p)$ on a complex manifold. In bidegree $(1,1)$ it agrees with the existing potential definition of Lelong numbers of potentials and positive $(1,1)$-currents.

Proof. For $p=0$ the number is the mass of the atom $\{ a\} $, which is invariant under a change of variables. Suppose $p\ge 1$, take $a=0$, and write a second chart as $w=F(z)$, $F(0)=0$. After shrinking, both $F$ and $F^{-1}$ have bounded first derivatives on the relevant coordinate balls. Integration of the derivative along segments in each ball gives constants $A,B>0$ with $A|z|\le |F(z)|\le B|z|$ near zero. Thus

\[ \varphi =\log |z|^2,\qquad \psi =\log |F(z)|^2 \quad \hbox{satisfy}\quad \psi -\varphi =O(1). \]

Let $T_ w=(F^{-1})^*T$ be the current in the second chart. Bounded products commute with this biholomorphic change of variables by locality and positivity of bounded Bedford–Taylor products. Thus lemma 6.33.8 in the $w$-chart also gives

C7A67D553B9E 6.33.11
\begin{equation} \int _{\{ \psi <t\} }T\wedge (dd^ c\max \{ \psi ,s\} )^ p =D_{T_ w}(0,e^{t/2})\qquad (s<t) \end{equation}

whenever this ball is contained compactly in that chart.

Fix $\lambda >1$ and a sufficiently small $R>0$ with $D=B(0,R)\Subset \Omega $ in the common chart domain. Choose $C$ so large that $u=\max \{ \psi -C,\lambda \varphi \} =\lambda \varphi $ on a neighborhood of $\partial D$. On the other hand

\[ \psi -C-\lambda \varphi =(\psi -\varphi )-C+(1-\lambda )\varphi \longrightarrow +\infty \quad (z\to 0), \]

so $u=\psi -C$ on a punctured neighborhood of zero. For sufficiently negative $t$, the set $E_ t=\{ \psi <t\} $ is contained compactly in this neighborhood together with zero and in $D$. Choose $s<\min \{ \lambda \log R^2,t-C\} $. The functions

\[ v=\max \{ u,s\} ,\qquad w=\max \{ \lambda \varphi ,s\} \]

are continuous bounded plurisubharmonic near $\overline D$ and agree near its boundary. Their product masses over $D$ are therefore equal, by lemma 6.33.7. Positivity, locality, scaling and equation 6.33.11 give

\[ \begin{aligned} \lambda ^ pD_ T(0,R) & =\int _ D T\wedge (dd^ cw)^ p =\int _ D T\wedge (dd^ cv)^ p\\ & \ge \int _{E_ t}T\wedge (dd^ c\max \{ \psi ,s+C\} )^ p =D_{T_ w}(0,e^{t/2})\ge \nu (T_ w,0). \end{aligned} \]

This holds for every sufficiently small $R$; $C,t,s$ may depend on $R$. Let $R\downarrow 0$ and then $\lambda \downarrow 1$ to get $\nu (T_ w,0)\le \nu (T,0)$. Apply the same argument to $F^{-1}$ for the reverse inequality. Comparable balls were used only to establish the bounded difference of logarithmic weights; the bounded-product comparison is what removes the comparison constants from the density.

For bidegree $(1,1)$, $p=n-1$, the density is exactly $D_ u(a,r)$ in the Euclidean Jensen–Lelong formula when $T=dd^ cu$. That theorem identifies its limit with the potential Lelong number. When $n=1$ this is also the $p=0$ atom case. In complex dimension zero the only case is a measure on a discrete set, and the atom definition applies directly. $\square$

6.33.12 Level sets and their Hausdorff measure79683058F688

For $d>0$ use diameter-normalized Hausdorff measure

\[ \mathcal H^ d(A)=\lim _{\delta \downarrow 0} \inf \left\{ \sum _ j(\operatorname {diam}A_ j)^ d: A\subset \bigcup _ jA_ j,\ \operatorname {diam}A_ j\le \delta \right\} . \]

For $d=0$ use counting measure, so that a nonempty singleton has cost one. A different fixed normalization changes only the following displayed numerical constant.

Theorem 6.33.13.2B9769222A07 The function $a\mapsto \nu (T,a)$ is upper semicontinuous. More generally, if closed positive currents $T_ j\to T$ weakly in a fixed coordinate domain, all of bidimension $(p,p)$, and $a_ j\to a$, then

\[ \limsup _{j\to \infty }\nu (T_ j,a_ j)\le \nu (T,a). \]

For every $c>0$ the level set $E_ c=\{ a:\nu (T,a)\ge c\} $ is closed and has locally finite $\mathcal H^{2p}$ measure. More precisely, for every compact $K\subset \Omega $,

F89B30DD3077 6.33.14
\begin{equation} \mathcal H^{2p}(E_ c\cap K) \le \frac{4^{2p}}{c}\mu (E_ c\cap K). \end{equation}

These local finiteness conclusions hold in holomorphic charts on a complex manifold, and hence for any smooth Hermitian metric there.

Proof. Put $\mu _ j=T_ j\wedge \beta ^ p$, so $\mu _ j\to \mu $ vaguely. Choose $0<\rho <r$ with $\overline{B(a,r)}\subset \Omega $ and $\mu (\partial B(a,r))=0$. For all sufficiently large $j$, $B(a_ j,\rho )\subset \overline{B(a,r)}$, and monotonicity and the upper bound for vague convergence on compact sets give

\[ \limsup _ j\nu (T_ j,a_ j) \le \limsup _ j\frac{\mu _ j(\overline{B(a,r)})}{\rho ^{2p}} \le \frac{\mu (\overline{B(a,r)})}{\rho ^{2p}}. \]

Let $\rho \uparrow r$ and then let $r\downarrow 0$ through the good radii. The right side tends to $\nu (T,a)$. Taking $T_ j=T$ proves upper semicontinuity, including $p=0$, and consequently closedness of $E_ c$. No upper semicontinuity of open-ball mass is assumed.

Let $A=E_ c\cap K$, a compact set. The result is immediate if $A$ is empty. Suppose $p>0$ and take $\varepsilon >0$ small enough that the closed $\varepsilon $-neighborhood $A_\varepsilon $ is a compact subset of $\Omega $. Choose a maximal finite family $a_1,\ldots ,a_ N\in A$ whose pairwise distances are at least $2\varepsilon $. Such a maximal family exists: compactness gives a finite cover by balls of radius less than $\varepsilon $, each of which contains at most one member of a separated family, so a greedy selection terminates. The open balls $B(a_ j,\varepsilon )$ are disjoint, and the balls $B(a_ j,2\varepsilon )$ cover $A$, by maximality. Monotonicity gives

\[ cN\varepsilon ^{2p} \le \sum _{j=1}^ N\mu (B(a_ j,\varepsilon )) \le \mu (A_\varepsilon ). \]

The covering balls have diameter $4\varepsilon $. Thus their total $2p$-cost is at most $4^{2p}\mu (A_\varepsilon )/c$. Let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. The definition of $\mathcal H^{2p}$ and continuity from above, $\mu (A_\varepsilon )\downarrow \mu (A)$, prove equation 6.33.14.

For $p=0$, each point of $A$ has an atom of mass at least $c$. Every finite subset therefore has cardinality at most $\mu (K)/c$. This forces $A$ itself to be finite, and summing its atoms yields $\# A\le \mu (A)/c$, exactly the displayed inequality for $2p=0$. Finally, on a relatively compact coordinate ball a smooth Hermitian metric is bounded above and below by positive multiples of the Euclidean metric. Integration along segments in a slightly larger ball gives the local upper Lipschitz bound needed for Hausdorff measure; a finite cover of a compact set by smaller balls transfers the local finiteness assertion to that metric. $\square$
