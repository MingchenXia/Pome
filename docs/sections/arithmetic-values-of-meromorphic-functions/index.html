Lemma 9.70.2.BAA8DAF93F9F If an integer matrix has $L$ rows, $M>L$ columns, and entries of absolute value at most $B\geq 1$, its kernel contains a nonzero integer vector $c$ with

\[ \log \| c\| _\infty \leq \log 3+ \frac{L}{M-L}\log (3MB). \]

Proof. Choose an integer $H>(3MB)^{L/(M-L)}$ with $H\leq 3(3MB)^{L/(M-L)}$. The $(H+1)^ M$ integer vectors in $\{ 0,\ldots ,H\} ^ M$ have at most $(2MBH+1)^ L\leq (3MBH)^ L$ images. Since $H^{M-L}>(3MB)^ L$, two vectors have the same image. Their nonzero difference is the required kernel vector. $\square$

Lemma 9.70.3.08B68F4101B6 Let $K\subset \mathbb C$ be a number field of degree $D$, let $F_1,\ldots ,F_ N$ be holomorphic near finitely many points $w_1,\ldots ,w_ h$, and suppose all values $F_ i(w_ j)$ lie in $K$. Suppose every $\partial _{z_ l}F_ i$ is a fixed polynomial in the $F_ i$ with coefficients in $K$. There are an integral primitive element $\theta $ for $K$, integers $A\geq 2$, $C_0\geq 1$, and $C_1>0$ such that for every monomial $F^\alpha $ of degree $s$ and every multi-index $\kappa $ of length $k$, the number

\[ A^{C_0(s+k+1)}\partial ^\kappa (F^\alpha )(w_ j) \]

is in $\mathbb Z[\theta ]$ and has coefficients in $1,\theta ,\ldots ,\theta ^{D-1}$ of size at most $\exp (C_1(s+k+1))\, [C_1(s+k+1)]^ k$. The same upper bound, with a changed $C_1$, holds at every field embedding before multiplication by this power of $A$.

Proof. We recall the finite field facts being used. A finite extension of $\mathbb Q$ is separable: the derivative of an irreducible polynomial in characteristic zero has no common factor with that polynomial. Successively adjoining roots therefore gives exactly $D$ embeddings in $\mathbb C$. If two generators are combined as $a+qb$, only finitely many rational $q$ identify two distinct embedding values. Choose a rational value avoiding them. Its $D$ distinct conjugates show that it generates the field. Induction gives a primitive element, and integer scaling makes its minimal polynomial monic with integer coefficients: for a root of a rational monic polynomial, clearing denominators after the substitution $x=\theta /A$ gives such a scaling. This gives an integral primitive $\theta $. All the finitely many differential-polynomial coefficients and point values belong to $\mathbb Z[\theta ,1/A]$ after one common choice of $A$.

Replace differentiation by polynomial derivations $\mathcal D_ l=\sum _ iQ_{li}(X)\partial _{X_ i}$. If the $Q_{li}$ have degrees at most $b$, $k$ applications produce polynomial degree at most $s+k\max (b-1,0)$. At the next step the coefficient sum norm is multiplied by at most $C(s+(k+1)b)$. Each multiplication uses fixed elements of $\mathbb Z[\theta ,1/A]$; multiplication in the fixed basis, with powers of $\theta $ reduced by its monic equation, costs at most a fixed factor and a fixed power of $A$. Evaluation at the fixed point values costs at most an exponential in the resulting degree and the same linear power of $A$. Induction gives exactly the stated coefficient and denominator bounds. Applying any embedding to the finitely many fixed coefficients proves the conjugate bound by the identical polynomial induction. No analytic estimate on uncontrolled derivatives is being assumed here. $\square$

Lemma 9.70.4.5A16550DA466 Let $F_1,\ldots ,F_ N$ be meromorphic on $\mathbb C^ n$ and suppose $F_1,\ldots ,F_ d$, $d>n$, are algebraically independent over $\mathbb Q$, with respective finite orders at most the nonnegative numbers $\rho _1,\ldots ,\rho _ d$. Let $K\subset \mathbb C$ be a number field of degree $D$. Suppose the ring $K[F_1,\ldots ,F_ N]$ is stable under coordinate derivations, and choose a nonempty finite set $\{ w_1,\ldots ,w_ h\} $ where all $F_ i$ are holomorphic and have values in $K$. For every $\epsilon >0$ there are $r\geq 1$, constants $C_1,C_2>0$, and an unbounded sequence of positive integers $t$, with nonzero entire functions $G_ t$, such that

36DC9A8C6743
\begin{align} \operatorname {ord}_{w_ j}G_ t& \geq t\quad (1\leq j\leq h), \tag{9.70.5}\\ M_{G_ t}(r)& \geq (C_1t)^{-Dt}, \tag{9.70.6}\\ M_{G_ t}(R(t))& \leq C_2^ t,\qquad R(t)=\left(\frac{t^{d-n}}{\log t}\right)^{1/(\sum _{i=1}^ d\rho _ i+\epsilon )}. \tag{9.70.7} \end{align}

Here finite order at most $\rho $ means a quotient of entire functions bounded, for every $a>\rho $, by $C_ a\exp (|z|^ a)$.

Proof. Put $A_0=(\sum _ i\rho _ i+\epsilon )/(d-n)$ and choose $0<\eta <\epsilon /(2d)$. Write $a_ i=\rho _ i+\eta $. Discard indices with $a_ i\geq A_0$, and call the remaining set $J$, with $e=|J|$. Since $\sum _ i a_ i<A_0(d-n)$, fewer than $d-n$ indices were discarded; thus $e>n$. Moreover

10E6C67574C9 9.70.8
\begin{equation} \gamma =e-A_0^{-1}\sum _{i\in J}a_ i-n>0. \end{equation}

For large integers $T$ put $s_ i=\lfloor T/R(T)^{a_ i}\rfloor $, $i\in J$. Every exponent $1-a_ i/A_0$ is positive, so $s_ i\to \infty $. The monomials $X^\alpha $, $0\leq \alpha _ i\leq s_ i$, number

\[ M=\prod _{i\in J}(s_ i+1)\geq cT^{n+\gamma }, \]

where the logarithmic factors only increase this lower bound. Their total degrees are at most $CT$.

Require a polynomial $P=\sum _\alpha c_\alpha X^\alpha $ to satisfy $\partial ^\kappa P(F)(w_ j)=0$ for $|\kappa |<T$. Expand in the fixed number-field basis and clear common $A^{CT}$ denominators, using the preceding jet lemma. This gives an integer matrix with $L\leq Dh\binom {T+n-1}n\leq CT^ n$ rows and entries $B\leq \exp (CT\log T)$. The small-kernel-vector lemma gives nonzero integer coefficients with

51C15E261502 9.70.9
\begin{equation} \log \max _\alpha |c_\alpha | \leq C T^{-\gamma }(T\log T+\log M)+C\leq CT. \end{equation}

The function $f=P(F_ i:i\in J)$ is not identically zero, by algebraic independence over $\mathbb Q$. Let $t\geq T$ be the least vanishing order of $f$ at the chosen points, and choose $w_ j$ and a coordinate derivative $\partial ^\kappa $, $|\kappa |=t$, with nonzero value $b=\partial ^\kappa f(w_ j)\in K$.

The jet lemma and (9.70.9) show that $A^{Ct}b\in \mathbb Z[\theta ]$ and that every conjugate of $b$ has modulus at most $e^{Ct}(Ct)^ t$. For a nonzero $x\in \mathbb Z[\theta ]$, multiplication by $x$ on the basis $1,\theta ,\ldots ,\theta ^{D-1}$ has integer matrix and nonzero integer determinant. Evaluation at the distinct roots of the minimal polynomial diagonalizes it by the Vandermonde matrix. Its determinant is therefore the product of all conjugates. It follows that

EF59C873F2EE 9.70.10
\begin{equation} |b|\geq e^{-Ct}(Ct)^{-(D-1)t}. \end{equation}

This also covers $D=1$.

For $i\in J$ write $F_ i=A_ i/H_ i$ with the prescribed finite-order entire numerator and denominator, and put $G=P(F_ i:i\in J)\prod _{i\in J}H_ i^{s_ i}$. It is nonzero and entire, and vanishes to order at least $t$ at every chosen point. We do not assume $H_ i(w_ j)\ne 0$. Choose a complex direction on which none of the finitely many nonzero germs $H_ i$ at $w_ j$ vanishes identically; their first nonzero homogeneous terms have a common nonvanishing direction. In coordinates with this last direction, choose a small last-radius circle on which all these one-variable restrictions are nonzero. Uniform continuity then gives a small product polydisc whose entire distinguished boundary has $|H_ i|\geq c>0$ and lies where all the $F_ i$ are holomorphic. Its size is fixed, independent of $P,T,t$. Choose a ball $B(0,r)$ containing these finitely many polydiscs. On their distinguished boundaries, $|f|\leq e^{C\sum s_ i}M_ G(r)$. Cauchy estimates and the fixed linear coordinate changes give the original-coordinate derivative bound

\[ |b|\leq t!\, e^{C(t+\sum s_ i)}M_ G(r) \leq t!e^{Ct}M_ G(r). \]

The use of original derivatives is essential: their values are in $K$, while arbitrary new-direction derivatives need not be. Combine this with (9.70.10) and $t!\leq t^ t$ to prove the small-radius lower bound.

For $t\geq T$ each function $u/R(u)^{a_ i}$ is increasing for large $u$, because its power $1-a_ i/A_0$ is positive. Thus $s_ iR(t)^{a_ i}\leq t$. Finite-order growth, the number of monomials and (9.70.9) give

\[ \log M_ G(R(t))\leq CT+\log M+ C\sum _{i\in J}s_ i(1+R(t)^{a_ i})\leq Ct. \]

Since $t\geq T\to \infty $, pass to a subsequence of distinct $t$ and call these functions $G_ t$. All three bounds follow with fixed constants. No assertion of algebraic differential equations beyond the stated polynomial ring stability was needed. If no points are prescribed, take $G_ t=1$ for all sufficiently large $t$, so the empty-point version holds directly. $\square$

Theorem 9.70.11.406E1C3E45A6 Under the number-field, order, algebraic-independence and derivation-stability assumptions of lemma 9.70.4, let

\[ S=\{ z:\ F_ i\text{ holomorphic at }z\text{ and }F_ i(z)\in K \text{ for all }i\} . \]

Either $S$ is empty, or it is contained in the zero set of a nonconstant polynomial of degree $\delta $ satisfying

56F7B016989C 9.70.12
\begin{equation} \frac{\delta +n(n-1)/2}{n!} \leq [K:\mathbb Q]\frac{\sum _{i=1}^ d\rho _ i}{d-n}. \end{equation}

Proof. If $S$ is empty there is nothing to prove. Henceforth assume it is nonempty, and write $D=[K:\mathbb Q]$. Let $\delta \geq 1$ be an integer such that $S$ is not contained in any zero set of degree less than $\delta $. Evaluation on $S$ is then injective on the space of polynomials of degree less than $\delta $, so finitely many distinct points of $S$ already make it injective, by choosing a basis from the spanning evaluation functionals. In particular their least vanishing degree is at least $\delta $. The finite-set Schwarz inequality therefore has coefficient at least $c_\delta =(\delta +n(n-1)/2)/n!$. Apply it to the arithmetic $G_ t$ and the fixed $r$ in that lemma:

\[ -Dt\log (C_1t)\leq t\log C_2 -tc_\delta \log \frac{R(t)}{Cr}. \]

Divide by $t\log t$ and let $t\to \infty $, obtaining $c_\delta (d-n)/(\sum _ i\rho _ i+\epsilon )\leq D$. Let $\epsilon \downarrow 0$. If $S$ were not contained in any algebraic hypersurface, this would hold for every $\delta $, a contradiction. Hence if $S$ is nonempty it has a positive least vanishing degree $\delta $, and the same argument proves exactly the claimed bound. Empty $S$ requires no degree bound and is an explicit separate alternative. $\square$
