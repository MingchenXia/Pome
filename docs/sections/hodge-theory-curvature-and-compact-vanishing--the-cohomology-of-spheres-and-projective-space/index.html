10.50 The cohomology of spheres and projective space16DB10AEA8D5

16DB10AEA8D5

All de Rham groups in this section use smooth complex forms. Their coefficients are complex numbers; no integral or singular-cohomology comparison is assumed.

Proposition 10.50.1.DD72F1B19307 If a Hausdorff smooth manifold with a countable basis is the union of two open sets $U,V$, there is a natural long exact sequence

\[ \cdots \longrightarrow H^{j-1}_{\rm dR}(U\cap V) \longrightarrow H^ j_{\rm dR}(X) \longrightarrow H^ j_{\rm dR}(U)\oplus H^ j_{\rm dR}(V) \longrightarrow H^ j_{\rm dR}(U\cap V)\longrightarrow \cdots . \]

The middle arrow is restriction, and the next arrow takes the difference of restrictions. Negative-degree groups are zero.

Proof. The smooth partition construction in proposition 10.43.10 supplies $\rho _ U+\rho _ V=1$, with closed supports inside $U,V$ respectively. For a smooth form $\eta $ on $U\cap V$, extend $\rho _ V\eta $ by zero to $U$, and extend $-\rho _ U\eta $ by zero to $V$. These extensions are smooth: near each point outside the other open set the corresponding cutoff is identically zero. Their difference on the intersection is $\eta $. The restriction/difference sequence

\[ 0\longrightarrow \mathcal A^\bullet (X) \longrightarrow \mathcal A^\bullet (U)\oplus \mathcal A^\bullet (V) \longrightarrow \mathcal A^\bullet (U\cap V)\longrightarrow 0 \]

is therefore exact in every degree. Its kernel assertion is smooth pasting, and its arrows commute with $d$. The cochain long exact sequence lemma 10.43.3 proves the claim. The cutoff lift need not commute with $d$; only the displayed complex arrows are required to do so. $\square$

Lemma 10.50.2.2E46F3014687 For the sphere $S^ m$, $m\geq 1$,

\[ H^ j_{\rm dR}(S^ m)= \begin{cases} \mathbb C,& j=0\ \hbox{or}\ j=m,\\ 0,& \hbox{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

For $S^0$ the degree-zero group is $\mathbb C^2$ and all positive ones vanish.

Proof. Cover $S^ m$ by the complements of its north and south poles. Each is diffeomorphic to $\mathbb R^ m$ by stereographic projection, and hence has just the cohomology of a point by the de Rham homotopy formula. Their intersection retracts smoothly onto the equator $S^{m-1}$: write a point as $(\sqrt{1-h^2}\, \xi ,h)$ with $|h|<1$, and replace $h$ by $(1-t)h$, keeping $\xi $ fixed. The same homotopy formula identifies its de Rham cohomology with that of the equator. For $m=1$, the equator has two points. The degree-zero difference map from the two contractible opens has diagonal image $\mathbb C$ in $\mathbb C^2$, so its cokernel is $\mathbb C$ and gives $H^1(S^1)$ by proposition 10.50.1. For $m\geq 2$ the intersection is connected. The degree-zero difference map is onto, giving $H^1(S^ m)=0$; in degrees $j\geq 2$ the same sequence identifies $H^ j(S^ m)$ with $H^{j-1}(S^{m-1})$. Induction proves all positive-degree assertions. In degree zero, closed functions are constant on each connected component, proving both the remaining assertion and the base case $S^0$. $\square$

Theorem 10.50.3.558E1268EEED Let $\omega =\omega _{\rm FS}$ be the normalized Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^ n$, $n\geq 0$. Then

\[ H^*_{\rm dR}(\mathbb P^ n,\mathbb C) =\mathbb C[t]/(t^{n+1}),\qquad |t|=2,\quad t=[\omega ]. \]

In particular each even group in degrees $0,2,\ldots ,2n$ is one-dimensional and every odd group is zero. The Dolbeault groups, and their sheaf interpretations, are

\[ H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(\mathbb P^ n) \simeq H^ q(\mathbb P^ n,\Omega ^ p)= \begin{cases} \mathbb C,& 0\leq p=q\leq n,\\ 0,& p\ne q. \end{cases} \]

For $p=q$, the class of $\omega ^ p$ is a generator. Moreover $\int _{\mathbb P^ n}\omega ^ n=1$, including the point case $n=0$.

Proof. First compute the additive de Rham groups without any Hodge-theoretic assumption. For $n=0$ they are those of a point. For $n\geq 1$ let $p_0=[1:0:\cdots :0]$, let $U=\mathbb P^ n\setminus \{ p_0\} $, and let $V$ be a small coordinate ball centered at $p_0$. The homotopy

\[ [z_0:z']\longmapsto [(1-t)z_0:z'],\qquad z'\ne 0, \]

retracts $U$ smoothly onto the hyperplane $\mathbb P^{n-1}$. The open set $V$ is contractible, and $U\cap V$ is a punctured $2n$-ball, smoothly retracting to $S^{2n-1}$ by radial projection. Apply the preceding two results and the de Rham homotopy formula. The degree-zero difference map is onto because the intersection is connected; induction gives $H^1(\mathbb P^ n)=0$. For $2\leq j\leq 2n-2$ the two adjacent intersection groups vanish, so $H^ j(\mathbb P^ n)=H^ j(\mathbb P^{n-1})$. At the top end, $H^{2n-1}(\mathbb P^ n)=0$ and the connecting map gives $H^{2n}(\mathbb P^ n)\simeq H^{2n-1}(S^{2n-1})=\mathbb C$. For $n=1$, only the already stated degree-one and top-degree steps are needed. Higher groups vanish by real dimension. This proves all the claimed additive groups.

The existing curvature computation the Fubini–Study curvature computation proves that $\omega $ is a positive closed real $(1,1)$-form. Thus its top power has positive integral. For every $0\leq k\leq n$, the class $[\omega ^ k]$ is nonzero: if $\omega ^ k=d\eta $ for $k\geq 1$, Stokes applied to $\eta \wedge \omega ^{n-k}$ would make that positive integral zero. The degree-zero class is the constant function1. Each of the one-dimensional even groups therefore has the asserted generator, and multiplication of these powers gives the displayed ring. The relation $t^{n+1}=0$ follows by form degree and there are no others, since all the lower powers are nonzero.

For the integral normalization, in the affine chart $\mathbb C^ n$ the Hessian matrix of $\log (1+|z|^2)$ has one eigenvalue $(1+|z|^2)^{-2}$ and $n-1$ eigenvalues $(1+|z|^2)^{-1}$. With $dd^ c=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial /(2\pi )$ this gives

\[ \omega ^ n=\frac{n!}{\pi ^ n}(1+|z|^2)^{-n-1}\, dV_{\mathbb R^{2n}}. \]

The omitted hyperplane has zero smooth volume, as follows in charts from its real codimension two and Fubini. Polar integration gives

\[ \int _{\mathbb R^{2n}}(1+|z|^2)^{-n-1}dV =\frac{2\pi ^ n}{(n-1)!} \int _0^\infty \frac{r^{2n-1}}{(1+r^2)^{n+1}}\, dr =\frac{\pi ^ n}{n!}. \]

For the last integral use $s=r^2/(1+r^2)$, obtaining $\tfrac 12\int _0^1s^{n-1}ds=1/(2n)$. The polar sphere-area constant follows by applying polar integration to the product Gaussian integral $\int _{\mathbb C^ n}e^{-|z|^2}dV=\pi ^ n$; integration by parts gives its radial integral $(n-1)!/2$. This proves the normalization without an integral homology assertion.

Finally $\mathbb P^ n$ is compact Kähler, so theorem 10.47.13 applies. The even one-dimensional group is already spanned by a closed pure $(k,k)$ form $\omega ^ k$, and every odd group is zero. Its direct Hodge sum therefore has no other type components. The natural pure-type Dolbeault identifications give the displayed groups, and the bundle resolution corollary 10.43.11 identifies them with $H^ q(\mathbb P^ n,\Omega ^ p)$. The point case is immediate. $\square$
