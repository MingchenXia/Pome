10.86 Finite sheaf complexes for Bott–Chern cohomology0D9A7613ACD3

0D9A7613ACD3

Let $\Omega ^ j$ be holomorphic $j$-forms and $\overline\Omega ^ j$ antiholomorphic $j$-forms. Let $\mathcal Z^{j,q}=\ker b$ on smooth $(j,q)$-forms or on currents, as specified.

Lemma 10.86.1.6C01A2D464F2 For $q=0$ the complex $(\mathcal Z^{\bullet ,0},a)$ resolves $\mathbb C$ in degree zero. For $q\ge 1$ its only cohomology sheaf is in degree zero and is naturally $b\overline\Omega ^{q-1}$. These statements hold both for smooth forms and for currents.

Proof. All assertions are local. The smooth/current Dolbeault lemma and its conjugate, including holomorphic regularity at degree zero, are local Dolbeault exactness for forms and currents, corollary 10.43.11. For $q=0$ they identify $\mathcal Z^{j,0}$ with $\Omega ^ j$; the holomorphic radial homotopy is exact except for constants. Explicitly, on a star-shaped polydisc integrate contraction with the radial vector field along $z\mapsto tz$; differentiating gives $ah+ha=I-\operatorname {ev}_0$, and holomorphic coefficients remain holomorphic under this integral. Conjugation gives the identical statement for antiholomorphic forms.

For $q\ge 1$, local Dolbeault exactness gives the short exact sequence of horizontal complexes

\[ 0\longrightarrow \mathcal Z^{\bullet ,q-1} \longrightarrow \mathcal A^{\bullet ,q-1} \xrightarrow {\, (-1)^ j b\, }\mathcal Z^{\bullet ,q} \longrightarrow 0. \]

Use $\mathcal D$ in the middle for currents. The factor $(-1)^ j$ makes the quotient arrow commute with $a$. The middle horizontal complex has only degree-zero cohomology $\overline\Omega ^{q-1}$, by the conjugate Dolbeault lemma. Induction and the cochain long exact sequence show that the right complex has no positive-degree cohomology and has degree-zero cohomology $\overline\Omega ^{q-1}/b\overline\Omega ^{q-2}$, with constants in the denominator when $q=1$. The antiholomorphic radial homotopy identifies that quotient with $b\overline\Omega ^{q-1}$. These are actual kernel sheaves; no global primitive is asserted. $\square$

Fix $p,q\ge 1$. Put $k_0=p+q$ and define a bounded complex $L_{p,q}$ by

\[ L^ k=\bigoplus _{r+s=k,\ r<p,\ s<q}\mathcal A^{r,s} \quad (k\le k_0-2),\qquad L^ k=\bigoplus _{r+s=k+1,\ r\ge p,\ s\ge q}\mathcal A^{r,s} \quad (k\ge k_0-1). \]

Its differential is the truncated $a+b$ except for the joining map $ab:\mathcal A^{p-1,q-1}\to \mathcal A^{p,q}$. The square is zero, including at the two joints, by $a^2=b^2=ab+ba=0$. Let $M_{p,q}$ be the same complex of currents. Let $S'$ be $\Omega ^ j$ in degrees $0\le j<p$, with differential $a$ truncated at the last term, and let $S''$ be $\overline\Omega ^ j$ in degrees $0\le j<q$, with differential $b$. Their sum inside $L$ is denoted $S$; in degree zero it is $\mathcal O+\overline{\mathcal O}$, with constants counted once.

Proposition 10.86.2.6ED26B437C1C For $p,q\ge 1$ the actual inclusions $S\to L_{p,q}\to M_{p,q}$ are quasi-isomorphisms. For $p,q\ge 2$ their cohomology sheaves are $\mathbb C$ in degree zero, $a\Omega ^{p-1}$ in degree $p-1$, and $b\overline\Omega ^{q-1}$ in degree $q-1$; when $p=q$ the last two sheaves are a direct sum in their common degree. For $p=1<q$ replace the degree-zero sheaf by $\mathcal O$; for $q=1<p$ it is $\overline{\mathcal O}$; for $p=q=1$ it is $\mathcal O+\overline{\mathcal O}$ and all positive-degree sheaves vanish. The remaining terminal sheaf in the one-index cases is as in the preceding display.

Proof. First assume $q\ge 2$ and filter all three complexes by the first bidegree. This is a finite filtration, so it suffices to show that the actual inclusion $S\to L$ induces an isomorphism on its second page at every stalk. For a lower column $r<p$, vertical cohomology is $\Omega ^ r$ in row zero and

\[ \mathcal A^{r,q-1}/b\mathcal A^{r,q-2} \simeq \mathcal Z^{r,q} \]

in row $q-1$, by sending a class to $b$ of a representative. All other rows vanish. For an upper column $r\ge p$, the degree shift in the definition of $L$ places its sole vertical group $\mathcal Z^{r,q}$ in that same row $q-1$. Multiplying the lower identifications by $(-1)^ r$ and the upper ones by $(-1)^{p-1}$ makes the differential along this row $a$, including the join induced by $ab$. The row-zero complex is the truncated holomorphic de Rham complex $S'$.

For $S$, vertical cohomology gives precisely that row-zero complex and $b\overline\Omega ^{q-1}$ in position $(0,q-1)$. The latter maps to the row $(\mathcal Z^{r,q},a)$ by its actual inclusion. It is its degree-zero cohomology and that row is otherwise exact by lemma 10.86.1. The second pages are therefore isomorphic under the inclusion. Finite filtered comparison proves the asserted quasi-isomorphism, without suppressing the total degree $r+q-1$ of the terminal row. Exactly the same argument applies to currents: the vertical kernel in degree zero is still holomorphic by the distributional Dolbeault lemma, and the preceding resolution lemma explicitly included currents. Consequently $S\to M$ is also a quasi-isomorphism and so is $L\to M$. If $q=1<p$, interchange the two bidegrees and conjugate this proof. A fixed minus on one side of the joining map compensates for $ba=-ab$ and does not change any comparison.

If $p=q=1$, the first page for $L$ is the one-row complex

\[ \mathcal A^{0,0}\xrightarrow {ab}\mathcal Z^{1,1} \xrightarrow a\mathcal Z^{2,1}\xrightarrow a\cdots . \]

The horizontal resolution lemma says the positive part is exact: at its first term every kernel is $a\mathcal Z^{0,1}$, and local Dolbeault exactness makes $\mathcal Z^{0,1}=b\mathcal A^{0,0}$. Its degree-zero kernel consists of $f$ with $abf=0$. Then $bf$ is an antiholomorphic closed one-form, hence is $bg$ for an antiholomorphic function $g$ locally; $f-g$ is holomorphic. Thus the kernel is $\mathcal O+\overline{\mathcal O}$. The current proof is identical, since degree-zero $a$- or $b$-closed distributions are regular antiholomorphic or holomorphic functions. This proves the two remaining quasi-isomorphisms.

Finally the short exact sequence of sheaf complexes

\[ 0\to \mathbb C[0]\xrightarrow {c\mapsto (c,-c)} S'\oplus S''\to S\to 0 \]

and the two holomorphic radial homotopies compute its local groups. If both truncation lengths exceed one, each truncated complex has constants in degree zero. Its last cohomology quotient $\Omega ^{p-1}/a\Omega ^{p-2}$ is identified by the next, untruncated differential with $a\Omega ^{p-1}$; the conjugate statement gives $b\overline\Omega ^{q-1}$. If one length is one, its only term is $\mathcal O$ or $\overline{\mathcal O}$ in degree zero; quotienting the shared constants gives exactly the listed endpoint groups. If both are one it gives their sum. This also verifies every overlap and the corrected terminal exponents. $\square$

Theorem 10.86.3.69060D8AFA59 On a compact complex manifold, Bott–Chern and Aeppli cohomology are finite dimensional in every bidegree, for smooth forms and for currents. No Kähler hypothesis is required.

Proof. For $p,q\ge 1$, the complex $L_{p,q}$ has fine smooth-form terms, hence acyclic terms by proposition 10.43.10. Its global cohomology in degree $p+q-1$ is precisely BC by its definition. The quasi-isomorphism just proved and theorem 10.55.2, corollary 10.55.3 identify this group with $\mathbb H^{p+q-1}(X,S)$. The displayed short exact sequence for $S$ reduces finite dimensionality to the truncated holomorphic and antiholomorphic complexes and the constant sheaf. The latter has finite cohomology by de Rham comparison and compact Hodge theory. Each term $\Omega ^ j$ has finite cohomology $H^{j,\ell }_{\bar\partial }$ by corollary 10.43.11, theorem 10.46.6. The second hypercohomology spectral sequence has finitely many terms on each diagonal, all finite dimensional, so its finite filtration proves finite dimensionality for each truncated complex. The long exact hypercohomology sequence then proves it for $S$.

If $q=0$, BC is the subspace of global holomorphic $p$-forms that is $a$-closed, with no nonzero $ab$ denominator. It is finite dimensional as a subspace of $H^{p,0}_{\bar\partial }$. The case $p=0$ is its conjugate. This includes $(0,0)$ and avoids the invalid inclusion of a nonzero $S^0$ into a zero lower corner of $L$. Out-of-range groups are zero.

For Aeppli, the natural maps give an exact sequence at the middle term

\[ H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X)\longrightarrow H^{p,q}_{\rm A}(X) \xrightarrow {\ b\ }H^{p,q+1}_{\rm BC}(X). \]

Indeed $ab\alpha =0$ makes $b\alpha $ closed for both maps; adding $au+bv$ changes it by $-abu$. If its BC class is zero, $b\alpha =abu$, so $b(\alpha +au)=0$ and the Aeppli class comes from a Dolbeault class. Both adjacent groups are finite dimensional, so the middle one is too. The smooth/current isomorphisms already proved give all current assertions, including the endpoints and zero-dimensional manifolds. $\square$
