2.18 Elliptic operators of arbitrary order36FFBD062A7E

36FFBD062A7E

Manifolds are smooth, Hausdorff, second countable and without boundary. Bundles are real or complex and of finite rank. A positive smooth density and bundle inner products define formal adjoints; complex inner products are linear in the first variable. Fourier transforms use $e^{-2\pi i x\cdot \xi }$, and $\langle \xi \rangle =(1+|\xi |^2)^{1/2}$.

See Proposition 2.17.2.

Proof. The product rule gives the scalar-conjugation identity. The highest power has every derivative landing on the exponential, each through its first derivative. A term of power $t^ j$ leaves at most $d-j$ derivatives on $u$. The left side is intrinsic, so its highest coefficient gives the asserted coordinate and frame invariance. Taking all highest-order derivatives on the section in a composition gives the symbol product, even if that product vanishes and the actual composite order drops.

Use smooth orthonormal local frames and write the density as $\rho (x)\, dx$. Integration by parts gives

\[ P^*v=\rho ^{-1}\sum _{|\alpha |\leq d}(-1)^{|\alpha |} \partial ^\alpha (\rho a_\alpha ^*v). \]

This is a differential operator with the claimed leading symbol. The integration identity on compactly supported test sections proves uniqueness, hence the formulas glue on overlaps. A cutoff equal to one near a compact intersection of supports gives the same identity for arbitrary sections with that compact intersection. Derivatives of the cutoff contribute zero, since one of the two sections is then zero near each such point. This also defines the action on distributional sections by transposition. The argument uses a density and does not require orientability. $\square$

Lemma 2.18.1.0D76F8A1E29B On a compact smooth $m$-manifold and a smooth bundle, Sobolev spaces $H^ s$ are defined for every real $s$, locally by

\[ \| u\| _{H^ s(\mathbb R^ m)}^2 =\int \langle \xi \rangle ^{2s}|\widehat u(\xi )|^2\, d\xi . \]

Finite charts, frames and cutoffs give equivalent norms independent of these choices. Their Hilbert duals under the density/metric pairing are $H^{-s}$. Smooth sections are dense; every distribution belongs to some $H^{-N}$. One has $H^ s\subset C^ l$ when $s>l+m/2$, and $H^{s+1}\hookrightarrow H^ s$ is compact for every real $s$. If $r<a$, then, for every $\varepsilon >0$,

\[ \| u\| _{H^{a-1}}\leq \varepsilon \| u\| _{H^ a} +C_{\varepsilon ,a,r}\| u\| _{H^ r}. \]

Proof. If $m=0$, all these spaces are finite dimensional and the assertions are immediate. Suppose $m\geq 1$. Here are the real-order details extending the coefficient Sobolev construction in Sobolev spaces, duality and local embedding. For $0<\theta <1$, Fourier inversion and the Plancherel theorem and the change of variables $h\mapsto |\xi |^{-1}h$ give, initially for rapidly decreasing smooth functions,

\[ \iint \frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^2}{|h|^{m+2\theta }}\, dx\, dh =c_{m,\theta }\int |\xi |^{2\theta }|\widehat u(\xi )|^2\, d\xi . \]

The constant is the integral with numerator $|e^{2\pi i h_1}-1|^2$: near zero it is $O(|h|^2)$, and at infinity it is bounded. It is finite and positive. Rotation invariance removes the direction of $\xi $. Fourier-space smooth approximation extends the identity by completion. Adding $\| u\| _2^2$ gives an equivalent $H^\theta $ norm. A bounded smooth multiplier with bounded first derivatives acts boundedly: split its difference into its bounded value times the difference of $u$ and its Lipschitz difference times $u$. The latter term is bounded by $C\| u\| _2^2$ using $\min \{ |h|^2,1\} $ in the integral.

On compact coordinate pieces, a smooth change of coordinates and its inverse have bounded Jacobians and are locally bi-Lipschitz. The difference integral therefore proves boundedness on $H^\theta $. For precision, subdivide into finitely many sufficiently small convex pieces: approximation of the derivative by its invertible central value gives both Lipschitz bounds there. A partition handles these pieces. In the zero extensions, pairs leaving a larger piece have distance bounded below from the cutoff support and add only an $L^2$ term. For an integer $q\geq 0$, the Fourier norm of $H^{q+\theta }$ is equivalent to the sum of the $H^\theta $ norms of derivatives of orders at most $q$. The chain and product rules now prove the same coordinate/multiplier assertions in every nonnegative real order. Negative orders follow by transposition, including the smooth Jacobian and metric multipliers. A finite partition and its reconstruction map identify the dual global norm with the corresponding local $H^{-s}$ norms. This proves all the claimed norm and duality identifications.

Fourier cutoff approximation, followed by multiplication with a fixed larger spatial cutoff, proves density locally in each order; sum the finite pieces globally. Fourier inversion and Cauchy–Schwarz give $C^ l$ bounds because $\int \langle \xi \rangle ^{2l-2s}d\xi <\infty $. A compactly supported distribution has finite order, hence is bounded on some $C^ l$ test norm. This embedding puts it in $H^{-N}$ for sufficiently large $N$. A finite partition gives the global assertion.

For compactness, a bounded sequence in $H^{s+1}$ supported in one fixed compact coordinate set has $H^ s$ Fourier tail at most $C/R$ on $|\xi |>R$. On each bounded frequency ball its Fourier transforms and their first derivatives are uniformly bounded: pair the distributions with $\chi (x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot \xi }$ and $x_ j\chi (x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot \xi }$, where $\chi =1$ near that support, and use the bounded $H^{-s-1}$ norms of these test functions for bounded $\xi $. Finite frequency grids and the derivative bound give a uniformly convergent subsequence on the ball; a diagonal choice over integer $R$, together with the tail bound, is Cauchy in $H^ s$. Sum over the finite charts. This proof also covers negative $s$. Finally the pointwise weight inequality $\langle \xi \rangle ^{a-1}\leq \varepsilon \langle \xi \rangle ^ a+ C_\varepsilon \langle \xi \rangle ^ r$ follows by separating large and bounded frequencies. Integration and finite localization prove the final estimate, with an arbitrary prescribed small coefficient. $\square$

Lemma 2.18.2.3C443B01B6F3 Suppose a matrix symbol $a(x,\xi )$ is the sum of an $x$-independent symbol and one supported in a fixed compact $x$-set, and satisfies

\[ |\partial _ x^\alpha \partial _\xi ^\beta a(x,\xi )| \leq C_{\alpha \beta }\langle \xi \rangle ^{\mu -|\beta |}. \]

Its Fourier operator $\operatorname {Op}(a)$ is bounded $H^ s\to H^{s-\mu }$ for every real $s$; its adjoint has the same order bound. On rapidly decreasing smooth functions it is defined by

\[ \operatorname {Op}(a)u(x)= \int e^{2\pi ix\cdot \xi }a(x,\xi )\widehat u(\xi )\, d\xi . \]

If $P$ is elliptic of positive order $d$ in a bundle chart, there is, near any relatively compact smaller chart, an exact identity on sections supported there,

\[ v=A Pv-R^*v, \]

where $A:H^ s\to H^{s+d}$ and $R^*:H^ s\to H^{s+1}$ are bounded for every real $s$. These operators need not be differential.

Proof. For a compact-$x$ symbol, integration by parts in its $x$-Fourier transform gives, for every $N$,

\[ |\widehat a(\eta -\xi ,\xi )| \leq C_ N\langle \eta -\xi \rangle ^{-N}\langle \xi \rangle ^\mu . \]

The weighted Fourier kernel from $H^ s$ to $H^{s-\mu }$ is thus bounded by $C_ N\langle \eta -\xi \rangle ^{-N+|s-\mu |}$. Here the elementary inequalities $\langle \eta \rangle \leq \sqrt2\langle \xi \rangle \langle \eta -\xi \rangle $ and its interchange control their ratio to any real power. Choose $N>|s-\mu |+m$. Both integrals of this kernel, in either variable, are uniformly bounded. Cauchy–Schwarz with its absolute value, followed by integration, gives its $L^2$ operator bound. The $x$-independent part is a Fourier multiplier with the asserted bound. Duality gives the adjoint bound in every order. In particular smooth bounded-coefficient differential operators, localized if necessary, have their expected Sobolev orders.

Write $P^\dagger $ for the Euclidean coefficient adjoint in this chart and set $Q=P^\dagger P$. The Fourier principal symbol of $P$ is $p_ d(x,\xi )=(2\pi i)^ d\sigma _ P(x,\xi )$. The principal symbol of $Q$ is the positive matrix $q(x,\xi )=p_ d(x,\xi )^*p_ d(x,\xi )$. On a compact subchart and $|\xi |=1$ its smallest eigenvalue has a strictly positive lower bound: continuity and compactness give the minimum, and injectivity excludes zero.

We construct a self-adjoint global extension. Let $Q_0$ be the constant homogeneous differential operator with Fourier symbol $q(x_0,\xi )$ at a fixed chart point. Choose $0\leq \chi \leq 1$ compactly supported in the chart and equal to one on a neighborhood of the smaller chart. On $\mathbb R^ m$ set

\[ \widetilde Q=Q_0+\chi (Q-Q_0)\chi . \]

The last term is extended by zero. This operator is formally self-adjoint and agrees with $Q$ near the smaller chart. Its principal symbol $\widetilde q=\chi ^2q+(1-\chi ^2)q(x_0,\xi )$ remains positive definite, uniformly on all $x$ and the unit sphere. Outside a fixed compact set it is $q(x_0,\xi )$.

Choose a smooth frequency cutoff $\rho $ equal to zero near zero and one for $|\xi |\geq 2$. The explicit matrix symbol

\[ b(x,\xi )=\rho (\xi )\widetilde q(x,\xi )^{-1} \]

has order $-2d$. Differentiating the inverse matrix identity proves all its symbol estimates; homogeneity gives one extra inverse power per $\xi $ derivative. Its difference from the corresponding frozen symbol is compactly supported in $x$. Put $B=\operatorname {Op}(b)$. There is an exact, finite computation of $\widetilde Q B$: if $\widetilde Q=\sum a_\alpha (x)\partial ^\alpha $, its symbol is

\[ \sum _{\alpha }\sum _{\beta \leq \alpha } \binom \alpha \beta a_\alpha (x)(2\pi i\xi )^{\alpha -\beta } \partial _ x^\beta b(x,\xi ). \]

This follows directly by differentiating the Fourier integral. The degree-$2d$, $\beta =0$ terms sum to $\rho I$. Every other term has order at most $-1$. Thus

\[ \widetilde Q B=I+R, \]

where $R$ has order $-1$ in the just proved class; outside the fixed compact $x$-set its symbol is $(\rho -1)I$, a smooth low-frequency multiplier. Transposing this identity, using self-adjointness of $\widetilde Q$, gives $B^*\widetilde Q=I+R^*$. These identities initially hold on rapidly decreasing smooth functions and extend to all applicable Sobolev spaces by density and duality.

For $v$ supported in the smaller chart, $\widetilde Qv=Qv$. Extend the coefficients of $P^\dagger $ smoothly with a compact cutoff equal to one there and put $A=B^*P^\dagger $ with this extension. It has order at most $-d$ by the bounds above. Hence $v=APv-R^*v$, with the required gains. The construction used one matrix inverse and a finite Leibniz formula; no asymptotic symbol sum or unproved composition theorem is being invoked. $\square$

Theorem 2.18.3.DB125476638D Let $P:E\to F$ have smooth coefficients and injective principal symbol, of positive order $d$. If $u$ is a distributional section and $Pu\in H^ s_{\rm loc}$ for a real $s$, then $u\in H^{s+d}_{\rm loc}$. In particular $Pu$ smooth implies $u$ smooth. On a compact manifold, for every real $s$ and $r<s+d$,

\[ \| u\| _{H^{s+d}}\leq C_{s,r} (\| Pu\| _{H^ s}+\| u\| _{H^ r}) \]

whenever the right side is finite. In particular, if $u\in L^2$ and $Pu\in H^ k$, $k\geq 0$, then

\[ u\in H^{k+d},\qquad \| u\| _{H^{k+d}}\leq C_ k(\| Pu\| _{H^ k}+\| u\| _2). \]

The constants depend on the fixed operator, norms and compact localizations, as well as the displayed orders.

Proof. Localize inside a chart where the preceding identity holds. Choose cutoffs $\varphi ,\psi $ supported there, with $\psi =1$ near $\operatorname {Supp}\varphi $. Write $f=Pu$. For $v=\varphi u$, the identity gives

\[ v=A\bigl (\varphi f+[P,\varphi ](\psi u)\bigr )-R^*v. \]

The commutator has order at most $d-1$. If $\psi u\in H^ t$ and $f\in H^ s$ locally, the three terms on the right belong respectively to $H^{s+d}$, $H^{t+1}$ and $H^{t+1}$. Thus $v\in H^{\min \{ s+d,t+1\} }$, with a bound by the localized $H^ s$ norm of $f$ and $H^ t$ norm of $u$. A compactly supported localization of any distribution starts in some $H^{-N}$ by the Sobolev lemma. Finitely many nested cutoffs and iteration reach $s+d$, proving the local result. If $f$ is smooth, repeat for every order and use Sobolev embedding.

For a smooth global $u$ on a compact manifold, take $t=s+d-1$ in the same identity and sum over finitely many smaller charts and cutoffs. It gives

\[ \| u\| _{H^{s+d}}\leq C_ s (\| Pu\| _{H^ s}+\| u\| _{H^{s+d-1}}). \]

The interpolation inequality in lemma 2.18.1, with a sufficiently small coefficient, absorbs the intermediate norm and gives the asserted bound with any $r<s+d$. Distributional regularity already puts a section with finite right side in $H^{s+d}$; density in that space extends the estimate to it. Taking $r=0$ gives the final assertion. $\square$

Theorem 2.18.4.912C25048C3D Let $M$ be compact of positive dimension and let $P:E\to F$ be elliptic of positive order $d$, with equal bundle ranks. Then for every real $s$ the bounded operator

\[ P:H^{s+d}(M,E)\longrightarrow H^ s(M,F) \]

is Fredholm. Its kernel $K$ and the distributional kernel $K^*$ of its formal adjoint are finite dimensional and smooth, independent of $s$, and its range is

\[ \{ f\in H^ s(M,F):\langle f,v\rangle =0\text{ for every }v\in K^*\} . \]

Here the pairing with a smooth section is the distributional extension of the $L^2$ pairing. There are continuous direct sums

\[ H^ s(M,F)=P H^{s+d}(M,E)\oplus K^*,\qquad C^\infty (M,F)=P C^\infty (M,E)\oplus K^*. \]

They are orthogonal for $s=0$ in the first identity and in the $L^2$ pairing for the smooth identity. The index is $\dim K-\dim K^*$ and does not depend on the Sobolev order.

Proof. All distributional null solutions are smooth by the preceding theorem. Fix $s$, put $a=s+d$, and use its estimate with remainder $H^{a-1}$. On the kernel the $H^ a$ norm is bounded by the $H^{a-1}$ norm. Compactness of that inclusion makes the closed unit ball of the kernel compact in $H^ a$: a lower-order convergent subsequence is upgraded by the estimate on differences. An infinite orthonormal sequence would contradict compactness, so the kernel is finite dimensional. Its elements are exactly the fixed smooth space $K$.

Let $\Pi _ K$ be the finite-rank projection defined using an $L^2$ orthonormal basis of $K$. Pairing with its smooth members makes $\Pi _ K$ continuous in every Sobolev order. There is a bound

\[ \| u\| _{H^ a}\leq C_ s\| Pu\| _{H^ s} \qquad (\Pi _ Ku=0). \]

Otherwise choose a normalized sequence violating it, with $Pu_ j\to 0$. After a subsequence it converges in $H^{a-1}$; the general estimate on differences makes it converge in $H^ a$. Its limit has norm one, is in $K$ and has projection zero, a contradiction. If $Pu_ j$ converges in $H^ s$, subtract $\Pi _ Ku_ j$ and apply this bound to differences. The resulting sections converge in $H^ a$ and their limit maps to the target limit. The range is therefore closed.

Equal ranks make the principal symbol invertible. The adjoint symbol formula makes $P^*$ elliptic as well. The same argument gives its finite-dimensional smooth distributional kernel $K^*$. The annihilator of the range in $(H^ s)^*=H^{-s}$ consists precisely of distributional solutions of $P^*v=0$: test first against smooth domain sections and then use density. Thus it is exactly $K^*$. A closed linear subspace is the intersection of the kernels of its continuous annihilators, by Hilbert projection and Riesz representation. This proves the range description and finite codimension. Its $L^2$ projection onto $K^*$ is continuous on $H^ s$, giving the first direct sum and index.

For a smooth target, the Sobolev decomposition at $s=0$ writes $f=Pu+v$ with $u\in H^ d$ and $v\in K^*$. The right side $Pu=f-v$ is smooth, so regularity makes $u$ smooth. Conversely the range annihilates $K^*$ by integration by parts. Thus the smooth range is the common kernel of finitely many continuous smooth functionals, and is closed in the smooth topology. This proves the second direct sum and its orthogonality. The index formula uses the same smooth spaces for every $s$. $\square$

If $d=0$ and $\dim M>0$, ellipticity is pointwise injectivity. The smooth left inverse $(P^*P)^{-1}P^*$ gives the corresponding order-zero regularity estimates directly. With equal ranks $P$ is a smooth bundle isomorphism, its inverse acts boundedly in every Sobolev order, and its index and both kernels are zero. No compact positive-order argument is needed. A compact zero-dimensional manifold is a finite set: all section and distribution spaces are finite dimensional and smooth, and the assertions reduce to linear algebra. In that case ellipticity on nonzero cotangent vectors is vacuous, so pointwise invertibility is not inferred from it; for an endomorphism the finite-dimensional index is zero.
