13.12 Quasi-continuous functionsAD5CC24EA726

AD5CC24EA726

Let $X$ be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta $ be a closed real smooth $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a big cohomology class.

Definition 13.12.1.C96BE5099B6A Let $A\subseteq X$ be a Borel subset. The $\theta $-capacity $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta }(A)$ of $A$ is defined as

\[ \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta }(A)\coloneqq \sup \left\{ \int _ A\theta _{\varphi }^ n : \varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ),V_{\theta }-1\leq \varphi \leq V_{\theta } \right\} . \]
Here $V_{\theta }$ is defined in (13.13.7) below. As we will recall below, the non-pluripolar product $\theta _{\varphi }^ n$ is well-defined. The capacity is not very sensitive to the choice of $\theta $:

Theorem 13.12.2.C5C10F2AB708 Let $\theta '$ be another closed real smooth $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a big cohomology class. Then there are continuous functions $f,g\colon [0,\infty )\rightarrow [0,\infty )$, with $f(0)=g(0)=0$, such that for any Borel subset $E\subseteq X$, we have

\[ \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta }(E)\leq f\left( \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta '}(E)\right),\quad \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta '}(E)\leq g\left( \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta }(E)\right). \]

Proof. Use the comparison with Kähler capacity with the reference potentials defining $\operatorname{Cap}_\theta$ and $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta'}$. It gives continuous nondecreasing functions $F,G,F',G'$, all zero at zero, with \[\operatorname{Cap}_\theta\leq F\circ\operatorname{Cap}_\omega,\quad\operatorname{Cap}_\omega\leq G\circ\operatorname{Cap}_\theta,\quad\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta'}\leq F'\circ\operatorname{Cap}_\omega,\quad\operatorname{Cap}_\omega\leq G'\circ\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta'}.\] The reference potentials have positive mass because the classes are big. Set $f=F\circ G'$ and $g=F'\circ G$. Composing the respective inequalities proves the two assertions for every Borel set. The compositions are continuous and vanish at zero, as required.

A more general result is proved in [Lu21]. Similar comparison results hold between $\theta $-capacity and the classical Bedford–Taylor capacity, see [Section 9.2, GZ17] for the proof. As a consequence, we can freely apply the results in [Section 4.2, GZ17], even though capacity has a different meaning there.

Definition 13.12.3.BA13EE505D9B Let $U$ be an open subset of $X$. A function $f\colon U\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$ is quasi-continuous if it is Borel measurable and for any $\epsilon >0$, there is an open subset $G\subseteq U$ such that

  1. E9A5229B982B

    $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta }(G)\leq \epsilon $;

  2. EC64A29E7BCB

    $f|_{U\setminus G}$ is real-valued and continuous.

Thanks to theorem 13.12.2, the notion of quasi-continuous functions is independent of the choice of $\theta $. Note that if $f,g\colon U\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$ are two Borel measurable functions equal quasi-everywhere (see definition 5.47.2), then $f$ is quasi-continuous if and only if $g$ is.

Theorem 13.12.4.9F9E57CC1422 Let $A\subseteq X$ be a Borel set. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. 9860890C10B2

    $A$ is pluripolar;

  2. 0031AD934DC8

    $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta }(A)=0$.

Proof. By capacity comparison, zero $\theta$-capacity is equivalent to zero Kähler capacity. The comparison with coordinate-ball capacities makes this equivalent to zero local Bedford--Taylor capacity in every member of a finite coordinate cover. The local zero-capacity theorem identifies these zero sets with the pluripolar sets. This proves both implications. For a Borel set, one may first apply the local statement to compact subsets and then use the capacitability and zero-capacity characterization proved with that theorem; a mere measure-zero argument would not suffice.

See [Theorem 4.40, GZ17] for the proof.

Example 13.12.5.472C675FCEA8 A quasi-plurisubharmonic function on an open subset $U\subseteq X$ is always quasi-continuous. See [Theorem 4.20, GZ17] for the proof.

More generally, if $\varphi ,\psi $ are two quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $U$, then the following function

\[ f(x)\coloneqq \begin{cases} \varphi (x)-\psi (x),& \textup{if }(\varphi \lor \psi )(x)\neq -\infty ;\\ \infty ,& \textup{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

is quasi-continuous.1

1. ↩ In the literature, people usually say carelessly that $\varphi -\psi $ is quasi-continuous.

Definition 13.12.6.F7490AE48EAC Let $U$ be an open subset of $X$. Let $(f_ i)_{i\in I}$ be a net of Borel measurable functions $f_ i\colon U\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$, and $f\colon U\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$ be a Borel measurable function. We say $(f_ i)_{i\in I}$ converges to $f$ in capacity if for any $\delta >0$, we have

\[ \lim _{i\in I} \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta }\Bigl ( \{ f_ i>f+\delta \} \Bigr )=0,\quad \lim _{i\in I} \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta }\Bigl ( \{ f_ i<f-\delta \} \Bigr )=0{}^{\href{#sgpt-a0000011920}{2}}. \]

We sometimes write $f_ i\xrightarrow {\mathrm{C}} f$.

We say the net converges locally in capacity on $U$ if, for every compact $K\Subset U$ and every $\delta>0$, both $\operatorname{Cap}_\theta(K\cap\{f_i>f+\delta\})$ and $\operatorname{Cap}_\theta(K\cap\{f_i<f-\delta\})$ tend to zero. These are separate order inequalities, so no subtraction of equal infinities is needed. On $U=X$, compactness makes local convergence equivalent to convergence in capacity on $X$.

2. ↩ It is very tempting to write $\lim _{i\in I} \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta }\left( \{ |f_ i-f|>\delta \} \right)=0$, as in [Definition 4.23, GZ17] for example. But the set where $f_ i-f$ is not defined is not a pluripolar set in general. Hence this abuse of notation is not acceptable.
Note that $f$ is not uniquely determined by the net $(f_ i)_{i\in I}$. Thanks to theorem 13.12.2, the notion of convergence in capacity is independent of the choice of $\theta $.

Proposition 13.12.7.D56D6D4A5E94 Let $U$ be an open subset of $X$. Let $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ be a net in $\mathrm{PSH}(U,\theta )$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(U,\theta )$. Assume one of the following conditions holds:

  1. 11DA1A704FF9

    $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ is decreasing and $\varphi $ is the limit of the net;

  2. C18393916362

    $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ is increasing and converges almost everywhere to $\varphi $.

Then $(\varphi_i)_{i\in I}$ converges to $\varphi$ locally in capacity on $U$. If $U=X$, the convergence holds in capacity on all of $X$.

Proof of the local statement. Fix a compact set $K\Subset U$ and $\delta>0$. Quasi-continuity and capacity-zero exceptional sets may be used on $K$: this follows from local/global capacity comparison and comparison of big-class capacities. In particular, outside an open set of arbitrarily small capacity a quasi-plurisubharmonic function is finite and continuous.

Suppose first that the net decreases pointwise to $\varphi$. Remove such an open set $G$ for $\varphi$. On the compact set $L=K\setminus G$, the functions $\varphi_i-\varphi$ are upper semicontinuous and nonnegative. The closed sets \[F_i=\{x\in L:\varphi_i(x)-\varphi(x)\geq\delta\}\] form a decreasing directed family with empty intersection. If none were empty, directedness would give the finite intersection property, contradicting compactness. Thus $F_i$ is empty eventually. The positive exceptional set on $K$ then lies in $G$, and the negative exceptional set is empty. Let $\operatorname{Cap}_\theta(G)$ tend to zero.

For the increasing case, each $\varphi_i\leq\varphi$ everywhere. Indeed, monotonicity and almost-everywhere convergence imply this order almost everywhere, and uniqueness of quasi-plurisubharmonic representatives upgrades it to pointwise order. The family is therefore locally uniformly bounded above. Apply Choquet selection, on a countable coordinate cover after adding smooth local corrections, to obtain countably many indices whose supremum has upper semicontinuous regularization $\varphi$. Choose increasing indices $i_m$ dominating the first $m$ selected indices. Then $\psi_m=\varphi_{i_m}$ increases, and $(\sup_m\psi_m)^*=\varphi$. The pluripolar discrepancy theorem gives $\psi_m\uparrow\varphi$ outside a pluripolar set.

Remove an open set $G$ containing that exceptional set and the poles, and making all of $\varphi,\psi_1,\psi_2,\ldots$ continuous on $L=K\setminus G$. Countable subadditivity and exceptional-set budgets $\varepsilon/2^{m+2}$ make $\operatorname{Cap}_\theta(G)<\varepsilon$. The continuous functions $\psi_m$ increase to the continuous finite function $\varphi$ on the compact set $L$. The finite-cover proof of Dini's theorem gives uniform convergence there. For every $i\geq i_m$, we have $\psi_m\leq\varphi_i\leq\varphi$; hence the negative exceptional set on $K$ lies in $G$ eventually, and the positive one is empty. Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. If $U=X$, take $K=X$ to obtain convergence in capacity on the entire compact manifold.

Why the open-set qualification is necessary. On $X=\mathbb P^1$, take $\theta=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm c}\log(1+|z|^2)$, $U=\{|z|<1\}$ and $u_j=|z|^{2j}$. These smooth bounded plurisubharmonic functions decrease to zero locally uniformly on $U$. Nevertheless, $\operatorname{Cap}_\theta(\{u_j>1/2\})=1$ for every $j$. To see this, put $R_j^2=1-1/(4j)$ and \[v_j=\log\max\{|z|^2,R_j^2\}-\log(1+|z|^2).\] This is a global $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function with $-1\leq v_j\leq0$; its minimum is $\log(R_j^2/(1+R_j^2))\geq\log(3/7)>-1$. Its curvature measure is normalized angular measure on $|z|=R_j$, where $u_j=(1-1/(4j))^j\geq3/4$. This gives capacity at least one. Every admissible test has total curvature mass one, so the capacity is exactly one. The exceptional annuli escape each compact subset of $U$.

Theorem 13.12.8.272876B00528 Let $U$ be an open subset of $X$. Let $(\chi _ i\colon U\rightarrow [0,\infty ))_ i$ be a sequence of quasi-continuous functions. Assume that the sequence is uniformly bounded and converges in capacity to $\chi \colon U\rightarrow [0,\infty )$. For each $j=1,\ldots ,p$, let $(\varphi _ j^ i)_ i$ be uniformly bounded sequences in $\mathrm{PSH}(U,\theta _ j)$ converging in capacity to bounded $\varphi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(U,\theta _ j)$. Then

\[ \chi _ i\, \bigwedge _{j=1}^ p \theta _{j,\varphi _ j^ i}\rightharpoonup \chi \, \bigwedge _{j=1}^ p \theta _{j,\varphi _ j} \]

weakly as currents on $U$.

Proof. Weak convergence of currents is tested on compact subsets. By local/global capacity comparison and comparison of big-class capacities, the hypotheses imply convergence in local Bedford–Taylor capacity on each relatively compact coordinate ball. Thus the argument also applies if all the stated convergences are only local in capacity.

The limit $\chi$ has a quasi-continuous representative equal to it outside a set of capacity zero. Here is the usual subsequence argument on a fixed compact neighborhood of the support of a test form. Choose indices $i_m$ such that the exceptional sets where $|\chi_{i_m}-\chi|>2^{-m}$ have summable capacities, with arbitrarily small tail sums. Enlarge these sets to open sets using outer regularity. Also remove open sets of summable capacities on which the quasi-continuous $\chi_{i_m}$ may fail to be continuous. Outside the union of the tails, $\chi_{i_m}$ converges uniformly to $\chi$, so its limit is continuous there. The total removed capacity can be made arbitrarily small. The bound for $\chi_i$ passes to $\chi$ outside a capacity-zero set; modifying $\chi$ there does not affect any bounded Bedford–Taylor product.

On a slightly larger coordinate ball choose smooth functions $q_j=A_j|z|^2$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm c}q_j\geq\theta_j$. Then $u_j^i=\varphi_j^i+q_j$ and $u_j=\varphi_j+q_j$ are plurisubharmonic, uniformly bounded on compact subsets, and converge in local capacity. Write \[\theta_j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm c}\varphi_j^i =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm c}u_j^i+(\theta_j-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm c}q_j).\] Expand the mixed product. Each term is a bounded Bedford–Taylor product multiplied by a fixed smooth form. Apply weighted continuity under convergence in capacity to each term, with weight $\chi_i$ and the quasi-continuous representative of $\chi$. This gives the asserted limit on the ball. A finite partition of unity on the compact support of a test form proves the result on $U$.
