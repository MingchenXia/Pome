9.59 Products, closed subspaces and projective charts232528C54E90

232528C54E90

Finite unions of affine charts are glued by sheaf isomorphisms on open overlaps satisfying the cocycle rule. Gluing the underlying spaces by those identifications and defining a section to be a compatible tuple gives a sheaf whose stalks are the original local rings. We use the algebraic separation condition that its diagonal is closed in its algebraic product; the product is specified next. This is the finite-type closed-point ringed-space convention.

Proposition 9.59.1.476B5A661E43 The algebraic product of $\operatorname {Spm}A$ and $\operatorname {Spm}B$ is $\operatorname {Spm}(A\otimes _ kB)$ with its localization sheaf. These affine products glue canonically for finite affine atlases and have the categorical product property. Closed subspaces are locally given by quotient rings, or by coherent ideal sheaves. Intersection has ideal $I+J$ and the least closed union has ideal $I\cap J$. The product ideal $IJ$ gives a possibly larger thickening of that union and has the same underlying set.

Proof. The tensor algebra is finitely generated. Its maps to $k$ are exactly pairs of maps $A\to k$, $B\to k$, so its closed points are the set-theoretic product. For every finite-type affine source, the tensor universal property and the preceding full faithfulness give the required universal property for maps. Maps from a general glued source to an affine target are likewise homomorphisms to its global sections: construct them on each affine chart and use the forced fraction maps to glue. This proves the product property for general sources as well. Localizing one or both tensor factors gives the same ring as localizing their tensor product at the corresponding elements. Thus the products of overlapping affine charts are open, agree on triple overlaps, and glue independently of the chosen affine refinements.

For a finite presentation matrix over $A_ s$, its kernel is a finite module, and localization is exact: if a fraction is killed by a localized map, some power of its denominator kills its image before localization, giving a localized kernel element. Consequently its kernel sheaf is locally generated by finitely many elements. This proves coherence of the localization sheaf and of kernels, images and finite quotients. A locally finitely generated ideal is therefore coherent, and its local quotient descriptions glue to closed subspaces. On $\operatorname {Spm}A$ a quotient $A/I$ has the underlying closed set $V(I)$ and local rings $(A/I)_{\mathfrak m}=A_{\mathfrak m}/I_{\mathfrak m}$. Factoring a quotient morphism is exactly containment in the reverse direction of its defining ideals. Hence $I+J$ gives the intersection and $I\cap J$ the least closed subspace containing both. Since $IJ\subset I\cap J$ and their radicals agree, the stated thickening and underlying-set assertions follow. They also hold for ideal sheaves because they can be checked on localizations. Taking $I=J=(T)\subset k[T]$ distinguishes the doubled point $k[T]/(T^2)$ from its least union with itself, $k[T]/(T)$. $\square$

The product topology generally differs from the Zariski topology just constructed. In the affine line every proper algebraic closed set is finite, so every two nonempty opens meet. In the product of two such topologies the diagonal is dense, since two cofinite sets meet, but in the algebraic plane it is the proper closed set $V(x-y)$. The local regular function $(1-x-y)^{-1}$ at $(0,0)$ also does not belong to $k[x]_{(x)}\otimes _ k k[y]_{(y)}$: an element of that tensor has a separated denominator $p(x)q(y)$, with both factors nonzero at zero. Equality would force $1-x-y$ to divide that product, impossible after substituting $y=1-x$. The line with a doubled origin, obtained by gluing two affine lines off zero, fails separation: in the product of its two different origin charts the diagonal is the punctured line $x=y\ne 0$. Its closure contains the distinct pair of origins, so the diagonal is not closed.

Theorem 9.59.2.4D90B8C4C837 Let $B=\bigoplus _{m\ge 0}B_ m$ be a graded $k$-algebra generated by its finite-dimensional spaces $B_0,B_1$. No reducedness is assumed. For a positive-degree homogeneous $s$ let $B_{(s)}=(B[1/s])_0$. The affine spaces $U_ s=\operatorname {Spm}B_{(s)}$ glue by $U_ s\cap U_ t=U_{st}$ to a finite-type separated ringed space $\operatorname {Proj}_{\rm cl}B$. A basis $s_0,\ldots ,s_ N$ of $B_1$ gives a finite cover by the $U_{s_ i}$. One has $\operatorname {Proj}_{\rm cl}k[T_0,\ldots ,T_ N]=\mathbb P^ N_ k$, and every such $B$ gives a closed subspace of some $\mathbb P^ N_ k$. A graded homomorphism $B\to C$ gives a morphism on the open set of $\operatorname {Proj}_{\rm cl}C$ where the image of $B_1$ does not vanish simultaneously. It is a global morphism when this open is the whole space; a graded surjection gives a closed embedding. Positive Veronese subalgebras give the same projective ringed space.

Proof. If $B_1=0$ all positive components vanish and the construction is empty; its finite cover is empty and it is a closed subspace of $\mathbb P^0_ k$. Assume otherwise for the following chart argument. If $s$ has degree $m$, then $B_{(s)}$ is generated over $B_0$ by the finite-dimensional space $B_ m/s$: a numerator of degree $dm$ is a sum of products of $d$ degree-$m$ monomials in $B_1$. Thus it is a finite-type algebra. For $t$ of degree $n$,

\[ B_{(st)}=B_{(s)}[1/(t^ m/s^ n)]. \]

Indeed the right side is the degree-zero part after both $s$ and $t$ are inverted; multiplying homogeneous numerators and denominators by powers makes their degrees agree, proving each inclusion. These canonical localizations commute on triple overlaps, so the preceding gluing construction applies, initially to the degree-one charts.

For a point of $U_ s$, write $s$ as a $B_0$-linear combination of degree-$m$ monomials $s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_ m}$. The identity $s/s=1$ implies that at least one fraction $s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_ m}/s$ is nonzero there. Its nonvanishing open is $U_ s\cap U_{s_{i_1}}\cap \cdots \cap U_{s_{i_ m}}$, by the same localization equality. Thus every $U_ s$ is covered by the degree-one charts. This proves the finite-cover assertion and identifies all the charts with the originally glued space.

The polynomial graded ring has $k[T_0,\ldots ,T_ N]_{(T_ i)}=k[T_ j/T_ i:j\ne i]$. Their overlaps are the usual projective coordinate changes, proving the projective-space assertion. The diagonal of projective space is closed: its equations in the product charts are $T_ iS_ j-T_ jS_ i=0$, and at a pair of points they hold exactly when their projective coordinates are proportional. To reduce general $B$ to this case, replace $B_0$ by $k1$ and keep every positive-degree component. The resulting subalgebra $B^+$ is generated by the $k$-space $B_1$: a coefficient from $B_0$ can be absorbed into the first positive-degree factor. Its degree-zero localization is unchanged, since $b_0=(b_0s)/s$. A polynomial graded algebra on a basis of $B_1$ therefore surjects onto $B^+$. In each affine projective chart it gives the quotient by the dehomogenized homogeneous relations. These quotient ideals agree on overlaps, giving a closed embedding. The diagonal of a closed subspace is closed by restriction of the ambient diagonal, proving separation even with nilpotents.

A graded homomorphism gives the localization maps $B_{(s)}\to C_{(\phi (s))}$. They glue exactly on the indicated union of nonvanishing opens. In a graded surjection the images of $B_1$ generate $C$ in positive degree, so those opens cover, and the local maps are surjective, proving the closed-embedding claim. Without a covering condition a global morphism need not exist: $k[x_0,x_1]\to k[t_0,t_1,t_2]$, $x_ i\mapsto t_ i$, gives the rational projection $[t_0:t_1:t_2]\mapsto [t_0:t_1]$. At the base point $[0:0:1]$ any extension would restrict on the two lines $t_1=0$ and $t_0=0$ to the incompatible constants $[1:0]$ and $[0:1]$. These constants are forced on the whole lines by continuity, since a point is closed and the punctured line is dense. Finally $B_{(s)}=B_{(s^\ell )}$ for every positive $\ell $: multiply a fraction by enough powers of $s$ to make its denominator exponent divisible by $\ell $. The charts with degrees divisible by $\ell $ cover, since they include every $U_{s_ i^\ell }$, and their rings are those of the Veronese algebra $\bigoplus _ mB_{\ell m}$. This proves its canonical projective identification. $\square$
