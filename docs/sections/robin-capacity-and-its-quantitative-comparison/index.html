16.37 Robin capacity and its quantitative comparisonAFD242A2AED9

AFD242A2AED9

Use $dd^ c=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial /(2\pi )$ and $m_ n=2^{-n}$. The logarithmic class $\mathcal L$ consists of entire PSH functions bounded above by $\log ^+|z|+C$; its subclass $\mathcal L^+$ has also the lower bound $\log ^+|z|-C$. All members of $\mathcal L^+$ are locally bounded. Write $\mu _ u=(dd^ cu)^ n$ and

\[ \gamma (u)=\limsup _{|z|\to \infty }(u(z)-\log |z|). \]

For a bounded non-pluripolar set $E$, put $u_ E=V_ E^*$, $\gamma (E)=\gamma (u_ E)$ and $c(E)=e^{-\gamma (E)}$; set $c(E)=0$ for pluripolar $E$.

Proposition 16.37.1.5CD6FC21D2D4 If $f$ is a real $C^\infty $ function on an open subset of $\mathbb R^ m$, the image of $\{ df=0\} $ has one-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero. In particular every nonempty real interval contains a regular value.

Proof. Put $C_ k=\{ D^\alpha f=0:1\leq |\alpha |\leq k\} $. First, $f(C_ m)$ is null. Work on a compact cube inside the domain, with a slightly larger compact neighborhood still in the domain. Divide it into cubes of side $h$. If a small cube meets $C_ m$, choose $a$ in that intersection. Uniform continuity of the derivatives of order $m$, and Taylor's formula at $a$, give

\[ |f(y)-f(a)|\leq \epsilon h^ m \]

on that cube for all sufficiently small $h$, after absorbing the fixed dimension constant in the choice of the modulus of continuity. There are $O(h^{-m})$ cubes, so these intervals cover the image of $C_ m$ with total length at most $C\epsilon $. Let $\epsilon \downarrow 0$ and exhaust the domain by countably many such cubes. This also proves the assertion for $m=1$.

Proceed by induction on $m$. At a point of $C_ k\setminus C_{k+1}$, $1\leq k<m$, there is a derivative $g=D^\beta f$, $|\beta |=k$, with $dg\ne 0$. The implicit function theorem makes $\{ g=0\} $ a smooth hypersurface near that point. The points of $C_ k$ on it are critical points of the restriction of $f$, since $df=0$ there. The induction hypothesis in a coordinate chart on this hypersurface makes their values null. A countable subcover of these neighborhoods, for each $k$, together with the already treated $C_ m$, covers $C_1$. $\square$

Proposition 16.37.2.13360BBA8D19 Let $u,v\in \mathcal L^+$ and $u\geq v$. If $b=\limsup _{|z|\to \infty }(u-v)$, then all the following integrals are finite and

\[ \int _{\mathbb C^ n}u\, d\mu _ v- \int _{\mathbb C^ n}v\, d\mu _ u\leq n b m_ n. \]

Proof. On $X=\mathbb P^ n$ use $\omega =dd^ c\Gamma $ on the affine chart, where $\Gamma =\tfrac 12\log (1+|z|^2)$. The canonical growth correspondence extends $a=u-\Gamma $ and $d=v-\Gamma $ as bounded $\omega $-PSH potentials. Put

\[ T_ u=\omega +dd^ ca,\quad T_ v=\omega +dd^ cd,\quad h=a-d\geq 0, \qquad S=\sum _{j=0}^{n-1}T_ v^ jT_ u^{n-1-j}. \]

All mixed top masses are $m_ n$ by bounded cohomological mass. Let $H=\{ Z_0=0\} $ and $t=|Z_0|^2/\| Z\| ^2$. For $\eta >0$ set

\[ g_\eta =-\tfrac 12\log (t+\eta ),\qquad B_\eta =\omega -dd^ cg_\eta . \]

The form $B_\eta $ is smooth and positive: it is half the curvature of the positive quadratic form $|Z_0|^2+\eta \| Z\| ^2$. It represents $\{ \omega \} $, tends smoothly to zero off $H$, and $\int _ X B_\eta \wedge S=nm_ n$.

The weighted Chern–Levine–Nirenberg theorem with base current $1$ and $n$ bounded potentials makes $\log |s|$ integrable against every mixed top product here, in a chart in which $s$ defines $H$. Consequently $\Gamma $ is integrable against $T_ u^ n,T_ v^ n$ and the displayed affine integrals are finite. This uses integrability against bounded products, without forming a product with the divisor current.

Bounded global integration by parts and $T_ v^ n-T_ u^ n=-dd^ ch\wedge S$ give

\begin{align*} I_\eta & :=\int _ X(a+g_\eta )T_ v^ n-\int _ X(d+g_\eta )T_ u^ n\\ & =-\sum _{l=1}^{n-1}\int _ Xh\, T_ v^ lT_ u^{n-l} +\int _ Xh B_\eta \wedge S \leq \int _ Xh B_\eta \wedge S. \end{align*}

For example, this follows by first writing $I_\eta =\int hT_ v^ n-\int h\, dd^ c(d+g_\eta )\wedge S$ and using $dd^ c(d+g_\eta )=T_ v-B_\eta $. For any $\delta >0$, $h\leq b+\delta $ on a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood of $H$. Bounded products with the smooth $B_\eta $ do not charge $H$. On its compact complement, $h$ is bounded and $B_\eta \to 0$ smoothly. Thus

\[ \limsup _{\eta \downarrow 0}I_\eta \leq n(b+\delta )m_ n. \]

On the affine chart $g_\eta \uparrow \Gamma $; the preceding integrability, or monotone convergence after adding a constant, passes both integrals to the limit. Let $\delta \downarrow 0$. $\square$

Theorem 16.37.3.27353237EC80 There is a constant $C_ n>0$ such that every compact $K\subset \overline B(0,1)$ satisfies

\[ \alpha (K)\leq c(K)\leq C_ n\alpha (K)^{1/n},\qquad \alpha (K)=\exp \bigl (-\sup _{\overline B(0,1)}V_ K^*\bigr ). \]

The same upper bound holds with any exponent $0<\delta \leq 1/n$.

Proof. The polar case has both capacities zero. Otherwise put $u=V_ K^*$ and $M=\sup _{\overline B(0,1)}u$. The norm-ball formula gives

\[ 0\leq \log ^+|z|\leq u(z)\leq M+\log ^+|z|. \]

In particular $\gamma (u)\leq M$, proving $\alpha \leq c$.

The normalized family $w=u-M$ is bounded above by $\log ^+|z|$ and satisfies $\sup _{\overline B(0,1)}w=0$. The canonical subharmonic compactness alternative gives a uniform $L^1$ bound on $B(0,2)$ for this family. Indeed any sequence without that bound has, by the common local upper bounds and compactness, either an $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ convergent subsequence or a subsequence tending locally uniformly to $-\infty $; the first contradicts the missing bound and the second contradicts the normalized supremum on the closed unit ball. The sphere average of a subharmonic function is at least its concentric ball average. Hence

\[ M\leq \mathop{\mathrm{avg}}_{|z|=2}u+C'_ n. \]

Take $v=\log ^+(|z|/2)\leq u$. Its measure is $m_ n$ times normalized surface measure on that sphere. The equilibrium support theorem gives $\operatorname {supp}\mu _ u\subset K$, where $v=0$. The preceding proposition, with $b=\gamma (u)+\log 2$, therefore gives

\[ \mathop{\mathrm{avg}}_{|z|=2}u\leq n(\gamma (u)+\log 2). \]

Thus $M\leq n\gamma (u)+C''_ n$, which is the upper estimate after exponentiation. Finally $0\leq \alpha \leq 1$ implies $\alpha ^{1/n}\leq \alpha ^\delta $ for the stated exponents. $\square$

Proposition 16.37.4.786CD8A5F7AA If $u\in \mathcal L^+$ and $\epsilon >0$, there is $q>0$ and arbitrarily large $R$ such that the fraction of the sphere $|z|=R$ occupied by

\[ F_\epsilon =\{ u-\log ^+|z|>\gamma (u)-\epsilon \} \]

is at least $q$.

Proof. Let $M(R)=\max _{|z|=R}u$. The norm-ball formula shows that $M(R)-\log R$ decreases to $\gamma (u)$ for $R>1$. Choose $0<t<1/2$ with $\log (1+t)<\epsilon /2$. For large $R$, put $s=R/(1+t)$ and choose $z_0$ on $|z|=s$ with $u(z_0)=M(s)$. On $B(z_0,ts)\subset B(0,R)$,

\[ u\leq u(z_0)+\log (1+t)<u(z_0)+\epsilon /2. \]

This ball is outside the unit ball for large $R$. Off $F_\epsilon $, its values are at most $\log R+\gamma (u)-\epsilon <u(z_0)-\epsilon /2$. The submean inequality at $z_0$ forces at least half its volume to lie in $F_\epsilon $. Therefore

\[ |F_\epsilon \cap B(0,R)|\geq p|B(0,R)|,\qquad p=\tfrac 12(t/(1+t))^{2n}>0 \]

for all sufficiently large $R$. If all sufficiently large spheres had fraction less than $p/2$, polar-coordinate integration would contradict this volume estimate. Take $q=p/2$. $\square$

Theorem 16.37.5.65A744B77195 For decreasing compact sets $K_ j$ with intersection $K$, $c(K_ j)\downarrow c(K)$. Together with monotonicity and bounded-union increasing continuity, this makes $c$ a generalized capacity on the subsets of every bounded open set.

Proof. Affine translation and dilation give $c(a+rE)=r c(E)$, by change of variables in the growth class and $\log |a+rz|-\log |z|\to \log r$ at infinity. Thus assume all relevant compacts lie strictly inside the unit ball. If the intersection is empty, compactness makes some $K_ j$ empty. Otherwise replace the sequence temporarily by closed thickenings

\[ E_ k=\{ \operatorname {dist}(z,K)\leq d/k\} \Subset B(0,1). \]

They are regular: each of their points belongs to a positive-radius closed ball contained in $E_ k$. Its continuous norm-ball extremal bounds $V_{E_ k}$ from above and is zero at that point. They satisfy $E_{k+1}\subset \operatorname {Int}E_ k$. Every original $K_ j$ is eventually contained in each $E_ k$, by compactness. Monotonicity will therefore give the original conclusion once it is proved for these thickenings.

If $K$ is pluripolar, fixed-ball capacity continuity gives $\alpha (E_ k)\downarrow 0$, and the preceding upper comparison proves $c(E_ k)\downarrow 0$. Suppose henceforth $K$ is non-pluripolar. Put $u_ k=V_{E_ k}$ and $u=V_ K^*$. These functions lie in $\mathcal L^+$, $u_ k$ is continuous, and

\[ 0\leq \log ^+|z|\leq u_ k\leq u,\qquad u_ k\uparrow u \quad \hbox{almost everywhere}. \]

The last assertion follows from the raw decreasing-compact envelope theorem and its pluripolar discrepancy set. The measures $\mu _{u_ k}$ and $\mu _ u$ are supported on $E_ k$ and $K$, respectively, and have mass $m_ n$. Moreover $u=0$ $\mu _ u$-almost everywhere: the exceptional part of $K$ is pluripolar and bounded products do not charge it. Bounded increasing continuity with the fixed locally bounded quasi-continuous weight $u$, and a cutoff equal to one near $E_1$, gives

58579BCF3224 16.37.6
\begin{equation} \int u\, d\mu _{u_ k}\longrightarrow \int u\, d\mu _ u=0. \end{equation}

For fixed $k$, radial smooth convolutions $f=u_ k*\rho _\eta $ decrease to $u_ k$ uniformly on $E_ k$. If $\sup _{E_ k}f\leq \delta $, then $f-\delta $ is an extremal competitor, so $0\leq f-u_ k\leq \delta $ on the whole affine space. Their bounded projective potentials consequently converge uniformly as well. The boundary-energy inequality for $u_ k+\delta \geq f$, and $\int f\, d\mu _{u_ k}\leq \delta m_ n$, imply

\[ 0\leq \int u_ k\, d\mu _ f\leq n\delta m_ n. \]

The function $u-u_ k$ is a bounded quasi-continuous difference of projective potentials. Bounded weighted capacity continuity on $\mathbb P^ n$ therefore gives

\[ \int (u-u_ k)\, d\mu _ f\longrightarrow \int (u-u_ k)\, d\mu _{u_ k}=\int u\, d\mu _{u_ k}. \]

In particular $\int u\, d\mu _ f\to \int u\, d\mu _{u_ k}$. Also $\mu _ f(\mathbb C^ n\setminus B(0,1))\to 0$: a cutoff supported in the unit ball and equal to one near $E_ k$ captures mass tending to $m_ n$, which is the total mass of every $\mu _ f$.

Suppose $L=\lim \gamma (u_ k)<\gamma (u)=\gamma $ and choose $L<\beta <\gamma $. Since $u_ k\geq \log ^+|z|$, $\beta >0$. Choose $\epsilon >0$ and $0<a<1$ such that

\[ \gamma -\beta >4\epsilon ,\qquad a(\beta +\epsilon )<\epsilon , \qquad (1-a)(\gamma -\beta -2\epsilon )>2\epsilon . \]

For each $k\geq 2$, choose a sufficiently large good radius $R_ k>1$ from the sphere-density proposition such that $u_ k\leq \log |z|+\beta $ on $|z|\geq R_ k$. The good-sphere fraction is bounded below by the same $q>0$.

Choose a convolution $f_ k$ of $u_{k-1}$ sufficiently close that

\[ u_{k-1}\leq f_ k\leq u_ k,\qquad \int u\, d\mu _{f_ k}\longrightarrow 0,\qquad \mu _{f_ k}(\mathbb C^ n\setminus B(0,1))\leq \eta _ k, \]

where

\[ \eta _ k\leq \min \left\{ \frac1{k(\log R_ k+\beta +\epsilon )}, \frac{a^ nqm_ n}{2^{n+1}}\right\} . \]

These choices are possible by the preceding limits and (16.37.6). For the upper ordering, make the convolution zero on $E_ k$: $u_{k-1}=0$ on a neighborhood of $E_ k$, so a sufficiently small convolution is a competitor for $V_{E_ k}$. No unbounded varying-weight convergence is being invoked.

Choose a regular value $r_ k\in (\log R_ k+\beta ,\log R_ k+\beta +\epsilon )$ of $f_ k$, and put $Q_ k=\{ f_ k<r_ k\} $. The scalar critical-values proposition applies. The open set is bounded with smooth compact boundary, contains $\overline B(0,R_ k)$ by the maximum principle, and is contained in $B(0,e^{r_ k})$, since $f_ k\geq \log ^+|z|$.

Here are the needed flux statements, including the outside mass. For a smooth PSH $f$ with a compact regular sublevel $Q=\{ f<r\} $, convex smoothing of the maximum and the chain rule give

\[ (dd^ c\max (f-r,0))^ n =\mathbf1_{\{ f>r\} }\mu _ f+\nu _{f,r},\qquad \nu _{f,r}=\left.d^ cf\wedge (dd^ cf)^{n-1}\right|_{\partial Q}\geq 0. \]

Indeed, the term $(F')^ n\mu _ f$ tends to the outside restriction; the remaining term is $n(F')^{n-1}F''df\wedge d^ cf\wedge (dd^ cf)^{n-1}$. In regular-level coordinates its scalar coefficient has integral one, so it tends to the displayed boundary measure. Stokes' formula, with $S_ f=(dd^ cf)^{n-1}$, gives

\begin{align*} J_ k& :=\int _{\partial Q_ k}(u-f_ k)\, d\nu _{f_ k,r_ k}\\ & =\int _{Q_ k}(u-r_ k)\, d\mu _{f_ k} -\int _{Q_ k}(f_ k-r_ k)dd^ cu\wedge S_{f_ k}\\ & =\int _{Q_ k}u\, d\mu _{f_ k}-\int _{Q_ k}f_ k dd^ cu\wedge S_{f_ k} +r_ k\left(\int _{Q_ k}dd^ cu\wedge S_{f_ k} -\mu _{f_ k}(Q_ k)\right). \end{align*}

For nonsmooth $u$, convolve decreasingly on a neighborhood of $\overline Q_ k$. The boundary integral converges by bounded dominated convergence; the mixed term in the second line has continuous compact weight $(f_ k-r_ k)\mathbf1_{Q_ k}$, which is zero on the boundary. Thus passage to the limit requires no assumption that a mixed measure gives that level zero mass. In the smooth identity the two first-order cross terms cancel because their $(1,1)$-parts are symmetric. The global mixed mass is $m_ n$, $f_ k\geq 0$, $u\geq f_ k$, and $\mu _{f_ k}(Q_ k)\geq m_ n-\eta _ k$. Consequently

9FC6F4FC67E2 16.37.7
\begin{equation} 0\leq J_ k\leq \int u\, d\mu _{f_ k}+r_ k\eta _ k\longrightarrow 0. \end{equation}

Set

\[ f'_ k=\max (f_ k-r_ k,0),\quad u'_ k=\max (u-r_ k,0),\quad v_ k=\log ^+(|z|/R_ k),\quad h_ k=2f'_ k+2\epsilon ,\quad g_ k=(1-a)u'_ k+a v_ k. \]

At infinity $h_ k$ has logarithmic slope two and $g_ k$ slope one. The bounded comparison principle on a sufficiently large ball gives

\[ \int _{\{ h_ k<g_ k\} }(dd^ cg_ k)^ n \leq 2^ n\int _{\{ h_ k<g_ k\} }(dd^ cf'_ k)^ n. \]

On the good part of $|z|=R_ k$, $f'_ k=v_ k=0$ and $u'_ k>\gamma -\beta -2\epsilon $, so this entire part lies in $\{ h_ k<g_ k\} $. Positivity of the mixed expansion and the sphere formula for $\mu _{v_ k}$ bound the left side below by $a^ nqm_ n$. The mass of $(dd^ cf'_ k)^ n$ outside $\overline Q_ k$ is at most $\eta _ k$, and it vanishes inside $Q_ k$. Therefore

\[ \nu _{f_ k,r_ k}(\partial Q_ k\cap \{ h_ k<g_ k\} ) \geq \frac{a^ nqm_ n}{2^{n+1}}. \]

On this boundary set, $h_ k=2\epsilon $ and $a v_ k\leq a(\beta +\epsilon )<\epsilon $, since $|z|\leq e^{r_ k}$. It follows that $u-f_ k=u-r_ k>\epsilon $ there. Hence $J_ k\geq \epsilon a^ nqm_ n/2^{n+1}$, contrary to (16.37.7). Thus $\gamma (u_ k)\uparrow \gamma (u)$. Exponentiation, followed by the thickening sandwich, proves the theorem. The two other capacity axioms are the previously proved monotonicity and continuity for increasing sets with bounded union. $\square$

Proposition 16.37.8.30AAD778A456 For $n\geq 2$ and $b>1/2$, no constant $C$ satisfies $c(K)\leq C\alpha (K)^ b$ for all compact subsets of the unit ball. Thus the exponent $1/n$ is optimal when $n=2$.

Proof. Choose an integer $d\geq 2$ with $(2-1/d)b>1$, and $r>0$ with $r\sqrt n<1$. For $A\to \infty $ put

\[ B=(Ar^ d-3)A^{(d-1)/d}>0,\quad p(x,y)=Ax^ d+Bx^{d-1}+Ay^ d,\quad q(x,y)=A^{1/d}x, \quad E_ A=\{ |p|\leq 1,\ |q|\leq 1\} . \]

On $E_ A$, $|x|\leq A^{-1/d}<r$. If $|y|\geq r$, then

\[ |p|\geq A|y|^ d-A|x|^ d-B|x|^{d-1} \geq Ar^ d-1-(Ar^ d-3)=2, \]

a contradiction. Thus $E_ A$ lies in the polydisc of radius $r$. The degree-$d$ map $(p,q^ d)$ has leading terms $(A(x^ d+y^ d),Ax^ d)$ with common zero only at the origin. Regular polynomial covariance and the polydisc formula give

\[ V_{E_ A}=\max \{ 0,d^{-1}\log |p|,\log |q|\} . \]

In particular

\[ d^{-1}\log A\leq \gamma (E_ A)\leq d^{-1}\log (2A),\qquad \alpha (E_ A)^{-d}\geq |p(1,0)|\geq B. \]

It follows that

\[ \left(\frac{c(E_ A)}{\alpha (E_ A)^ b}\right)^ d \geq \frac{B^ b}{2A} =\tfrac 12(r^ d-3/A)^ b A^{(2-1/d)b-1}\longrightarrow \infty . \]

For $n>2$ take $K_ A=E_ A\times \overline\Delta _ r^{\, n-2}$. The product-max formula makes its Robin constant the maximum of $\gamma (E_ A)$ and $-\log r$, and its closed-unit-ball extremal supremum the maximum of the corresponding two suprema. The $E_ A$ terms dominate for large $A$, so the same ratio diverges. Every $K_ A$ lies in the unit ball by the choice of $r$. $\square$
