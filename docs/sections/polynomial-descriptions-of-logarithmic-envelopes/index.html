16.30 Polynomial descriptions of logarithmic envelopes61BC5104CA3A

61BC5104CA3A

Use the existing class $\mathcal L=\{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb C^ n):u\leq \log ^+|z|+C_ u\} $ and write $V_ A=\sup \{ u\in \mathcal L:u|_ A\leq 0\} $. The obstacle is pointwise and $V_ A$ denotes the raw supremum. The existing projective correspondence and logarithmic pole theorem retain their canonical entries.

Proposition 16.30.1.F5C5050EEDEB For a complex norm $N$ on $\mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$, $r>0$, and $K=\{ N(z-a)\leq r\} $,

\[ V_ K(z)=\log ^+\frac{N(z-a)}r. \]

For every polynomial $P$ of degree $d\geq 1$,

\[ |P(z)|\leq \| P\| _ K\max \{ 1,N(z-a)/r\} ^ d. \]

Proof. The existing complex-norm support argument proves $\log N$ PSH, so the displayed profile is a competitor. For any other competitor $u$ and a point $w$ with $N(w-a)>r$, put $R=N(w-a)/r$ and consider

\[ q(\zeta )=u(a+\zeta ^{-1}(w-a))+\log |\zeta |-\log R, \qquad 0<|\zeta |<R. \]

This is SH or identically $-\infty $, is bounded above at zero by the growth condition, and has boundary upper limits at most zero. Point removability and the maximum principle give $q(1)\leq 0$. Inside $K$ the constraint and the zero competitor give $V_ K=0$. Apply the result to $d^{-1}\log (|P|/\| P\| _ K)$; the zero polynomial is immediate. $\square$

Proposition 16.30.2.F983146D5FCD For decreasing compact sets $K_ j\downarrow K$, $V_{K_ j}\uparrow V_ K$ pointwise. For compact $K$, $V_ K$ is lower semicontinuous. If $K\ne \varnothing $ and $V_ K^*=0$ on $K$, then $V_ K=V_ K^*$ is continuous. In particular, for nonempty compact $K$ and $\eta >0$, the closed thickening $K_\eta =\{ \operatorname {dist}(z,K)\leq \eta \} $ has a continuous extremal, and $V_{K_\eta }\uparrow V_ K$ as $\eta \downarrow 0$.

Proof. If $u\in \mathcal L$, $u\leq 0$ on $K$, and $\epsilon >0$, upper semicontinuity and compactness put $K_ j$ in $\{ u<\epsilon \} $ for large $j$. Thus $u-\epsilon \leq V_{K_ j}$, proving the first assertion. If $K$ is empty, some $K_ j$ is empty and all the assertions concerning this limit have the evident value $+\infty $.

Radial convolution $u_ t$ remains in $\mathcal L$ and decreases to $u$. For small $t$, $u_ t<\epsilon $ on $K$: the decreasing continuous functions have no common point of the compact sets $K\cap \{ u_ t\geq \epsilon \} $. Hence $u_ t-\epsilon $ is a continuous competitor. Letting $t,\epsilon \downarrow 0$ expresses $V_ K$ as the supremum of its continuous competitors, proving lower semicontinuity.

Suppose $K\ne \varnothing $ and $V_ K^*=0$ on $K$. Near one point of $K$, $V_ K$ is bounded above on a ball, by the definition of this regularization. Applying the norm-ball bound to every competitor, after a common constant subtraction, bounds the entire family above locally and gives its regularization growth at most one. Thus $V_ K^*$ is PSH and in $\mathcal L$. Its constraint on $K$ makes it a competitor, so $V_ K^*=V_ K$; combine upper and lower semicontinuity.

Every point of $K_\eta $ belongs to a closed radius-$\eta $ ball centered on $K$ and contained in $K_\eta $. The norm-ball formula, including continuity at that point, gives $V_{K_\eta }^*=0$ there. This proves the remaining assertions without a polynomial representation as input. $\square$

Theorem 16.30.3.19CBC18237E5 Let $h:\mathbb C^ m\to [0,\infty )$ be PSH, not identically zero, with $h(\lambda z)=|\lambda |h(z)$. Then

\[ h=\left(\limsup _{j\to \infty }|Q_ j|^{1/j}\right)^* \]

for homogeneous polynomials $Q_ j$ of degree exactly $j$ whenever nonzero. Conversely any such finite-valued, nonzero regularized limit has these properties. In particular $\log h\in \mathcal L(\mathbb C^ m)$: upper semicontinuity on the unit sphere and homogeneity give $h\leq C|z|$. If, in addition, $h$ is continuous and vanishes only at zero, then

\[ h(z)=\sup \{ |Q(z)|^{1/d}:Q\text{ homogeneous of degree }d\geq 1, \ |Q|^{1/d}\leq h\text{ on }\mathbb C^ m\} . \]

Proof. The checked homogeneous-gauge argument gives $\log h$ PSH: apply the product-tube minimum principle to $h(e^ w z)-\operatorname {Re}w$; its infimum in $\operatorname {Re}w$ is $1+\log h(z)$, including value $-\infty $ at zeros. Hence $D=\{ h<1\} $ is a balanced pseudoconvex domain containing zero, by the checked gauge/Oka criterion. It is proper because $h\not\equiv 0$. The domain-of-existence theorem gives $f\in \mathcal O(D)$ with no extension from a local component across its boundary. Its Taylor expansion at zero groups into homogeneous polynomials $f=\sum _{j\geq 0}Q_ j$. The expansion is normal on $D$: for a compact $L\subset D$, a fixed dilation greater than one of its circular hull is still compact in $D$, and Cauchy's formula in the dilation parameter bounds the terms by a geometric sequence on $L$. The coefficient formula agrees near zero with the Taylor polynomial and then everywhere by the identity theorem.

Cauchy's bounds on a ball at zero make $j^{-1}\log |Q_ j|$ locally uniformly bounded above on all of $\mathbb C^ m$. Let $q$ be their regularized limsup. The same bounds on every circular orbit inside $D$ give $q\leq \log h$, also where $h=0$. The functions $q$ and $e^ q$ are logarithmically homogeneous and homogeneous of degree one, respectively. On $\{ q<0\} $ Hartogs' lemma gives local geometric bounds on the Taylor terms, so the series extends $f$ normally there. This balanced domain is connected and contains $D$. If it were larger, it would contradict the domain-of-existence property. The case $q\equiv -\infty $ would extend $f$ to all of $\mathbb C^ m$ and gives the same contradiction. Thus $\{ q<0\} =D$; homogeneity gives $e^ q=h$ everywhere, including the zero directions.

For the converse, finite-valued regularized limsup implies pointwise boundedness of $|Q_ j|^{1/j}$. The closed sets where all these functions are at most an integer cover $\mathbb C^ m$. Baire gives a ball with one common bound. The norm-ball polynomial inequality then gives local uniform upper bounds everywhere. Upper-envelope/limsup closure gives PSH for the regularized root limit; its homogeneity follows from the exact matching degrees. It is nonzero by hypothesis.

For the pointwise supremum assume $h$ continuous with zero set $\{ 0\} $. Its sublevels $K_ t=\{ h\leq t\} $, $t<1$, are compact. Fix $a$ with $h(a)=1$. The points $s_ k a$ for distinct $s_ k\uparrow 1$ form a closed discrete subset of $D$. The checked interpolation theorem gives $f(s_ k a)=k$. If $f=\sum Q_ j$ is its homogeneous Taylor expansion, the one-variable series $f(\lambda a)$ has radius precisely one; otherwise it would be bounded near $\lambda =1$. Thus $\limsup _ j|Q_ j(a)|^{1/j}=1$. For $0<t<1$, put $M_ t=\sup _{K_ t}|f|>0$. Cauchy's parameter formula on $K_ t$ gives $|Q_ j(z)|\leq M_ t(h(z)/t)^ j$ for every $z$, by homogeneity. Consequently $t^ jQ_ j/M_ t$ is admissible and its root values at $a$ have limsup $t$. Let $t\uparrow 1$. The opposite inequality is the constraint itself; homogeneity handles every nonzero point, and both sides vanish at zero. This proof uses discrete interpolation directly and needs no additional polynomial-approximation theorem. $\square$

Theorem 16.30.4.FBAB51FD36FB A function $u:\mathbb C^ n\to [-\infty ,\infty )$, not identically $-\infty $, belongs to $\mathcal L$ if and only if

\[ u=\left(\limsup _{j\to \infty }j^{-1}\log |P_ j|\right)^* \quad \text{for polynomials }\deg P_ j\leq j. \]

Proof. For $u\in \mathcal L$, homogenize by $\log H(\zeta ,z)=\log |\zeta |+u(z/\zeta )$ off $\{ \zeta =0\} $. The growth bound makes this PSH function locally bounded above there, so canonical zero-set extension gives $\log H$ globally. Its exponential is nonnegative PSH, homogeneous of degree one and not zero. Apply the preceding theorem and set $P_ j(z)=Q_ j(1,z)$. Near $\zeta =1$ use the coordinates $(\zeta ,w=z/\zeta )$: the identity $j^{-1}\log |Q_ j(\zeta ,z)|=\log |\zeta |+j^{-1}\log |P_ j(w)|$ shows explicitly that regularized limsup restricts to the claimed regularized limsup on the slice. Conversely Baire gives a ball on which all $|P_ j|^{1/j}$ are bounded. The degree bound and the norm-ball inequality give a common logarithmic majorant of slope one everywhere. The regularized limsup is therefore PSH, and has that growth. Its assumed nondegeneracy excludes the constant $-\infty $. $\square$

Theorem 16.30.5.E171A0405847 For nonempty compact $K\subset \mathbb C^ n$, set

\[ \Phi _ K(z)=\sup \{ |P(z)|^{1/\deg P}:\deg P\geq 1,\ \| P\| _ K\leq 1\} . \]

Then $V_ K=\log \Phi _ K=V_{\widehat K}$ for the polynomial hull $\widehat K$. For nonempty compact $E\subset \mathbb C^ n$ and $F\subset \mathbb C^ m$,

\[ V_{E\times F}(z,w)=\max \{ V_ E(z),V_ F(w)\} . \]

Proof. The definition gives $\log \Phi _ K\leq V_ K$. Also $\Phi _ K\geq 1$: normalize polynomials $1+\epsilon z_1$ on $K$ and let $\epsilon \to 0$. Fix $\eta >0$ and put $u=V_{K_\eta }$, which is continuous, nonnegative and in $\mathcal L$, by the earlier norm-ball and regularity results. For $j\geq 2$ define, off $\zeta =0$,

\[ H_ j(\zeta ,z)=|\zeta |\exp ((1-j^{-1})u(z/\zeta )) +j^{-1}|(\zeta ,z)|, \]

and put $H_ j(0,z)=j^{-1}|z|$. The growth bound makes the first term tend to zero locally uniformly as $\zeta \to 0$: it is bounded by $C_ j|\zeta |^{1/j}|(\zeta ,z)|^{1-1/j}$. Thus $H_ j$ is continuous, homogeneous of degree one, positive off zero and PSH. To see the last assertion, its logarithm off the hyperplane is the convex log-sum-exp of the two PSH logarithms; bounded-above zero-set extension agrees with the displayed continuous values. Exponentiation preserves PSH.

On $K$, $H_ j(1,z)=1+j^{-1}|(1,z)|\leq 1+\epsilon $ for large $j$. For each homogeneous polynomial $Q$ of degree $d$ with $|Q|^{1/d}\leq H_ j$, the polynomial $P(z)=Q(1,z)$ has degree at most $d$ and norm at most $(1+\epsilon )^ d$ on $K$. Therefore $|P(z)|^{1/d}\leq (1+\epsilon )\Phi _ K(z)$. This also holds for constant $P$, since $\Phi _ K\geq 1$; for positive degree less than $d$, use $x^{1/d}\leq \max (1,x^{1/\deg P})$. Take the pointwise homogeneous supremum to obtain $H_ j(1,z)\leq (1+\epsilon )\Phi _ K(z)$. Let $j\to \infty $, then $\epsilon \downarrow 0$ and $\eta \downarrow 0$. This proves $e^{V_ K}\leq \Phi _ K$, without assuming regularized and raw envelopes agree on $K$. Polynomial norms agree on $K$ and $\widehat K$, proving the hull identity.

For the product identity let $G_ r$ and $H_ r$ be the unions of open radius-$r$ polydiscs centered on $E$ and $F$. Gram–Schmidt on monomials ordered by total degree gives orthonormal polynomial bases $p_\alpha $ and $q_\beta $ in their polynomial $L^2$ spaces, with degrees $|\alpha |$ and $|\beta |$. The polydisc submean inequality gives $\| p_\alpha \| _ E\leq (\pi r^2)^{-n/2}$ and $\| q_\beta \| _ F\leq (\pi r^2)^{-m/2}$. Hence, writing $A=\Phi _ E(z)$ and $B=\Phi _ F(w)$, each product at $(z,w)$ is bounded by $C_ r A^{|\alpha |}B^{|\beta |}$, including constants.

For a polynomial $P$ of total degree $k$, only products with $|\alpha |+|\beta |\leq k$ occur in its orthogonal expansion: they form a basis of that degree filtration. Every coefficient is bounded by $\| P\| _{L^2(G_ r\times H_ r)}$. If $A,B$ are finite, it follows that

\[ |P(z,w)|\leq C'_ r\binom {n+m+k}{n+m} \| P\| _{G_ r\times H_ r}\max (A,B)^ k. \]

Apply this to $P^ t$, take the $kt$-th root and let $t\to \infty $; the binomial factor has subexponential growth. Then let $r\downarrow 0$. For $\| P\| _{E\times F}\leq 1$ the limiting norm bound is at most one, so $|P(z,w)|^{1/k}\leq \max (A,B)$. If either $A$ or $B$ is infinite, the upper bound is automatic. Pulling back polynomials from either factor gives the reverse inequality for $\Phi $; take logarithms. $\square$

Proposition 16.30.6.F453BE749AEC For a nonempty compact $K$, $V_ K$ is continuous if and only if for every $t>1$ there are an open neighborhood $U$ of $K$ and $M>0$ such that

\[ \| P\| _ U\leq M t^{\deg P}\| P\| _ K \]

for every polynomial $P$. In that case one can take $M=1$.

Proof. If $V_ K$ is continuous, it vanishes on $K$, so $V_ K<\log t$ on some bounded open neighborhood $U$. The polynomial identity gives the estimate with $M=1$ for nonzero $P$ of positive $K$-norm. If a nonzero polynomial vanished on $K$, its arbitrary scalar multiples would force $\Phi _ K$ to be infinite off its zero set, contradicting finite $V_ K$. Constants and the zero polynomial satisfy the estimate directly.

Conversely apply the given estimate to $P^ k$, take the $k$-th root and let $k\to \infty $. It follows that $\| P\| _ U\leq t^{\deg P}\| P\| _ K$. Thus $\Phi _ K\leq t$ on $U$ and $V_ K^*\leq \log t$ on $K$. Let $t\downarrow 1$ and use $V_ K\geq 0$ to obtain $V_ K^*=0$ there. The compact-envelope regularity proposition proves continuity everywhere. $\square$
