9.29 Discrete holomorphic jets and the Stein criterionA7FC777E11A2

A7FC777E11A2

Theorem 9.29.1.17CC905E00B7 Suppose $X$ has a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion and $\dim X=n\geq 1$. For every closed discrete subset $D=\{ a_ j\} \subset X$, every choice of nonnegative integers $\ell _ j$, and every prescribed holomorphic jet of order $\ell _ j$ at $a_ j$, there is $f\in \mathcal O(X)$ having all these jets simultaneously. The orders and coefficients may vary without a uniform bound.

Proof. Shift the exhaustion so that $\rho \geq 0$, as justified for finite continuous exhaustions in section 9.26. Use the complete Kähler form $\omega =\mathcal H(\mathrm e^\rho )$ from lemma 6.26.8. The set $D$ is countable and meets every compact set in a finite set. Choose pairwise disjoint relatively compact coordinate balls $U_ j$ centered at $a_ j$, locally finite in $X$. One explicit choice starts with disjoint metric balls of radii less than1 and less than one quarter the distance to the other points of $D$, and places smaller coordinate balls inside. Local finiteness follows because centers of balls meeting a fixed compact set lie in its compact closed metric1-neighborhood, by lemma 9.28.1. A finite discrete set is handled by the same choice with an arbitrary positive bound when the set of other points is empty.

In each ball take coordinates $z_ j(a_ j)=0$, with $U_ j=\{ |z_ j|<r_ j\} $ and $r_ j<1$. Let $P_ j$ be the polynomial of degree at most $\ell _ j$ representing the prescribed jet, and take $\eta _ j\in C_ c^\infty (U_ j)$ with $0\leq \eta _ j\leq 1$, equal to one on $\{ |z_ j|\leq r_ j/2\} $. The locally finite smooth function $f_0=\sum _ j\eta _ jP_ j$ has these jets, and $g=\bar\partial f_0$ is smooth, closed and zero near every $a_ j$.

Set $c_ j=n+\ell _ j$ and, off $D$, define the nonpositive function

\[ P=\sum _ j c_ j\eta _ j\log |z_ j|^2, \]

with value $-\infty $ at $a_ j$. The summands extend by zero outside their coordinate balls. Choose $b_ j>0$ so large that $-1-b_ j<\log ((r_ j/2)^2)$. For $N\geq 1$ put

\[ P_ N=\sum _ j c_ j\eta _ j \max \{ \log |z_ j|^2,-N-b_ j\} . \]

Then $P_ N$ is finite and continuous, and $P_ N\downarrow P$. There is a smooth nonnegative $C$ independent of $N$ such that

FC59B92C1B6E 9.29.2
\begin{equation} \mathcal H(P_ N)\geq -C\beta \quad \hbox{as currents}, \qquad \beta =\mathcal H(\rho ). \end{equation}

Indeed $\log |z_ j|^2$ is PSH by plurisubharmonicity of logarithmic holomorphic norms, and its maximum with a constant is PSH by the line submean inequality. Where $\eta _ j=1$ this proves the lower bound. On the support of a derivative of $\eta _ j$, truncation is inactive for every $N\geq 1$, so the summand is the same smooth function there for all $N$. Its negative Hessian is bounded relative to $\beta $ on the compact support of these cutoff derivatives, with the factor $c_ j$ included. Choose a nonnegative smooth majorant equal to that bound on this derivative support and supported inside $U_ j$. The locally finite sum of these majorants is the required $C$. This local verification also proves the asserted distributional inequality across the maximum's joining surface.

Let $K_ X=\Lambda ^{n,0}T^*X$ with its metric from $\omega $. Its inverse has a smooth Hermitian curvature form $R=\mathrm i\Theta (K_ X^{-1})$. Choose continuous $a\geq 0$ with $R\geq -a\omega $, for example the pointwise operator norm of $R$. The function $F=|g|_\omega ^2\mathrm e^{-P}$, given value zero at $D$, is locally integrable: $g$ vanishes near the finitely many points of $D$ in any compact set, and elsewhere $P$ is smooth. Apply (6.26.9) with $A=2(a+1)\omega $ and this $C,F$. Thus

\[ \Phi _ N=\chi (\rho )+P_ N,\qquad \mathcal H(\Phi _ N)\geq 2(a+1)\omega , \qquad M:=\int _ X|g|_\omega ^2\mathrm e^{-\chi (\rho )-P} \, \mathrm dV_\omega <\infty . \]

These are continuous strictly PSH functions on the whole manifold, including the pole centers. Apply relative Richberg smoothing, with open set $X$ and $\lambda _ N=\min \{ 1/2,1/N\} $, to obtain smooth $v_ N$ such that

5140244168BF 9.29.3
\begin{equation} \Phi _ N\leq v_ N\leq \Phi _ N+\lambda _ N, \qquad \mathcal H(v_ N)\geq (a+1)\omega . \end{equation}

No monotonicity of $v_ N$ is asserted or needed.

Identify scalar $(0,q)$-forms with $K_ X^{-1}$-valued $(n,q)$-forms by the map $v\mapsto (-1)^{nq}\sigma \wedge v\otimes \sigma ^{-1}$, where $\sigma $ is a nonvanishing local holomorphic top form. The map is independent of this frame and commutes with $\bar\partial $: the sign from differentiating past $\sigma $ changes $(-1)^{nq}$ to $(-1)^{n(q+1)}$. Its top-form/dual-frame factor has norm one. For $K_ X^{-1}$ with metric multiplied by $\mathrm e^{-v_ N}$ the curvature is $R+\mathcal H(v_ N)\geq \omega $. Its $(n,1)$ curvature operator is therefore at least the identity by proposition 10.33.6. Both the ordinary squared norm and the inverse-curvature energy of $g$ for this metric are at most $M$, since $v_ N\geq \chi (\rho )+P$. Theorem 6.26.5 consequently gives scalar functions $u_ N$ such that

1650816EF3A8 9.29.4
\begin{equation} \bar\partial u_ N=g,\qquad \int _ X|u_ N|^2\mathrm e^{-v_ N}\, \mathrm dV_\omega \leq M. \end{equation}

Here is the weight-limit justification on all of $X$. For every fixed $k$ and $N\geq k$, $v_ N\leq \Phi _ k+\lambda _ k$. Thus (9.29.4) bounds the $u_ N$ for the fixed density $\mathrm e^{-\Phi _ k-\lambda _ k}$, in particular locally in unweighted $L^2$. Diagonal weak compactness on a compact exhaustion gives $u\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$ with $\bar\partial u=g$ distributionally. For fixed $k$, weak lower semicontinuity first gives on each compact $K$

\[ \int _ K|u|^2\mathrm e^{-\Phi _ k-\lambda _ k} \, \mathrm dV_\omega \leq M. \]

The fixed densities increase as $k\to \infty $ to $\mathrm e^{-\chi (\rho )-P}$. Monotone convergence and then compact exhaustion give

8E331ACA3599 9.29.5
\begin{equation} \int _ X|u|^2\mathrm e^{-\chi (\rho )-P} \, \mathrm dV_\omega \leq M. \end{equation}

This is the fixed-weight order of limits also used in theorem 6.25.10; the smoothing errors in (9.29.3) are globally controlled, so no varying-weight lower-semicontinuity step is hidden.

Since $\bar\partial (f_0-u)=0$, the local statement holomorphic distributions and minimal weighted solutions shows that $f=f_0-u$ is holomorphic on $X$. In particular $u$ is smooth and is holomorphic near each $a_ j$. Near that point the singular density in (9.29.5) is a smooth positive factor bounded above and below by positive constants times $|z_ j|^{-2(n+\ell _ j)}$ times Lebesgue measure. If the first nonzero homogeneous Taylor term of $u$ had degree $m\leq \ell _ j$, its squared spherical integral would be bounded below by a positive constant times $r^{2m}$ for small $r$. The radial integral would dominate $\int _0^{r_0}r^{2m-2\ell _ j-1}\, \mathrm dr=\infty $, contradicting (9.29.5). Thus $u$ vanishes to order at least $\ell _ j+1$ at $a_ j$, and $f$ has the prescribed jet. If $D$ is empty, the assertion holds with any constant function. $\square$

Corollary 9.29.6.476E1C34C3CF A second countable complex manifold with a smooth strictly PSH exhaustion is holomorphically convex. Its global holomorphic functions separate every pair of points, and at each point finitely many of them give holomorphic coordinates. In particular it is Stein, whether one uses local or global point separation in the defining axioms. Conversely, holomorphic convexity and local holomorphic separation give a smooth strictly PSH exhaustion. Such a Stein manifold therefore has the discrete jet interpolation property of the theorem.

Proof. Prescribe distinct values at two points in theorem 9.29.1 to separate them. At one point prescribe the first jets of its $n$ local coordinate functions; the resulting global functions have identity Jacobian in that chart. the holomorphic inverse and submersion theorems gives the coordinate assertion.

Let $K\subset X$ be compact. Its holomorphic hull is closed, being the intersection of the closed inequalities $|f(x)|\leq \sup _ K|f|$, $f\in \mathcal O(X)$. If this hull were noncompact, properness of the exhaustion would supply points $a_ j$ in it whose exhaustion values tend to infinity. They form a closed discrete set after taking a subsequence with strictly increasing levels. Interpolation with $f(a_ j)=j$ contradicts $|f(a_ j)|\leq \sup _ K|f|<\infty $. Hence the hull is compact. The converse is precisely theorem 9.26.1, already proved without using this direction, so the dependency is acyclic. In dimension zero the manifold is discrete, holomorphic functions are arbitrary functions, and all these assertions hold directly. $\square$
