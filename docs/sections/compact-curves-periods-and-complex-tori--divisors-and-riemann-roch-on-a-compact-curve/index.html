10.78 Divisors and Riemann–Roch on a compact curve6ED81B775A7E

6ED81B775A7E

Throughout this section $X$ is a connected compact Riemann surface, $K_ X$ is its holomorphic cotangent line bundle, and $E$ denotes a holomorphic line bundle. Write $H^ q(E)=H^ q(X,\mathcal O(E))$ and $h^ q(E)=\dim _{\mathbb C}H^ q(E)$. These are the sheaf groups identified with smooth analytic Dolbeault cohomology by corollary 10.43.11. The compact bundle Hodge theorem 10.46.6 makes $h^0,h^1$ finite; $H^ q(E)=0$ for $q\geq 2$ because the Dolbeault resolution has only degrees zero and one. A holomorphic function on $X$ is constant by the local maximum principle and compactness. Put $g=h^1(\mathcal O_ X)$.

A divisor is a finite sum $D=\sum _ x m_ x[x]$ with $m_ x\in \mathbb Z$. The sheaf $E(D)$ consists of meromorphic sections $s$ of $E$ for which $\operatorname {ord}_ x(s)+m_ x\geq 0$ at every point. In a coordinate $z$ centered at $x$ and holomorphic frame $e$ of $E$, it has the free local generator $z^{-m_ x}e$. Thus it is the section sheaf of a holomorphic line bundle and

\[ E(D)=E\otimes \mathcal O_ X(D),\qquad E(D+D')=E(D)\otimes \mathcal O_ X(D'),\qquad \mathcal O_ X(D)^*=\mathcal O_ X(-D). \]

These are the usual divisor bundles with the existing frame convention; the displayed identifications follow by multiplying their local meromorphic generators.

Lemma 10.78.1.370B2D433170 If $D=\sum _ xm_ x[x]$ is effective, there is a short exact sheaf sequence

92CFC18451B2 10.78.2
\begin{equation} 0\longrightarrow \mathcal O(E)\longrightarrow E(D) \longrightarrow Q_ D(E)\longrightarrow 0. \end{equation}

The quotient is supported at the finitely many $x$ with $m_ x>0$; its stalk there has dimension $m_ x$. It is flabby, so the cohomology sequence is

EF4841C1F3FB 10.78.3
\begin{equation} 0\to H^0(E)\to H^0(E(D))\to Q_ D(E)(X) \to H^1(E)\to H^1(E(D))\to 0. \end{equation}

Here $\dim Q_ D(E)(X)=\sum _ xm_ x$. For every prescribed $a\in X$, $E$ has a meromorphic section with an actual pole at $a$, no other pole, and pole order at most $h^1(E)+1$. Every line bundle on $X$ is isomorphic to $\mathcal O_ X(D)$ for some divisor $D$.

Proof. At a point of multiplicity $m$, a quotient germ is the polar part

\[ \sum _{k=-m}^{-1}c_ k z^ k e. \]

Its coefficients give dimension $m$; elsewhere the quotient stalk is zero. More precisely the quotient sheaf is the finite sum of skyscraper sheaves with these stalks. To verify this assertion, a quotient germ at one support point is represented on a small disk containing no other support point; away from that point its image is zero, so it glues with zero on the complement. These local identifications give a stalkwise isomorphism, hence a sheaf isomorphism. Restrictions of finite skyscraper sums just forget fibers and are onto. The flabby acyclicity and sheaf long exact sequence of theorem 10.43.7 prove (10.78.3).

Take $D=m[a]$ with $m>h^1(E)$. The map from the $m$-dimensional principal-part space to $H^1(E)$ has a nonzero kernel. Exactness lifts a nonzero principal part to a global section of $E(m[a])$. Viewed as a meromorphic section of $E$, it has a genuine pole at $a$ and no other pole. One may take $m=h^1(E)+1$.

For any nonzero meromorphic section $s$, its zeros and poles are isolated in local frames and therefore finite on $X$. Put $D=\operatorname {div}s$. Multiplication by $s$ maps $\mathcal O_ X(D)$ to $\mathcal O(E)$. Locally write $s=z^{m_ x}u e$, with $u$ a holomorphic unit. The generator $z^{-m_ x}$ maps to $u e$, a holomorphic frame, so this is a bundle isomorphism. Apply this to the section just constructed. $\square$

We will need a precise degree-one acyclicity fact for divisor and principal-part sheaves; no general soft-sheaf theorem is required.

Lemma 10.78.4.F25B73A1DFD0 For each point $x$ of a compact smooth manifold let $A_ x$ be an abelian group. Let $F(U)$ be the group of assignments $a_ x\in A_ x$, $x\in U$, with locally finite nonzero support in $U$. Then $F$ is a sheaf and $H^1(X,F)=0$.

Proof. Gluing is pointwise, and the support condition is local, proving the sheaf assertion. By lemma 10.40.1, every degree-one sheaf class is represented on some open cover by an alternating Čech cocycle $(c_{ij})$, where $c_{ij}+c_{jk}=c_{ik}$. Restrict to a finite subcover $U_1,\ldots ,U_ N$, and choose a smooth partition $\rho _1+\cdots +\rho _ N=1$ with closed supports contained in the respective opens. At each point $x$ choose one index $k(x)$ with $\rho _{k(x)}(x)>0$. For $x\in U_ i$, define an assignment by

\[ b_ i(x)=c_{k(x),i}(x)\in A_ x. \]

It is a section of $F(U_ i)$, despite possible discontinuity of the index choice. Indeed, near $x_0\in U_ i$ all indices $k$ for which $x_0\notin \operatorname {Supp}\rho _ k$ are excluded by a common neighborhood, since there are finitely many. For each remaining index, $x_0\in \operatorname {Supp}\rho _ k\subset U_ k$; the support of $c_{ki}$ is locally finite in a neighborhood of $x_0$ within $U_ k\cap U_ i$. The union of these finitely many locally finite sets is locally finite. This bounds the support of $b_ i$ there. Pointwise the cocycle identity gives $b_ j-b_ i=c_{ij}$. Thus the cocycle is a coboundary on the finite cover and represents zero in sheaf cohomology, by naturality of the comparison. No multiplication of the groups $A_ x$ by smooth real numbers has been used. $\square$

Theorem 10.78.5.5A999DD34D98 Let $\mathcal M_ X^*$ be the sheaf of invertible meromorphic functions and $\operatorname {Div}_ X$ the sheaf of locally finite integer point divisors. Then

\[ H^1(X,\mathcal M_ X^*)=0 \]

and there is an exact sequence of abelian groups

D7BBBF432133 10.78.6
\begin{equation} 1\to \mathbb C^*\to \mathcal M_ X^*(X) \xrightarrow {\operatorname {div}}\operatorname {Div}(X) \xrightarrow {D\mapsto \mathcal O_ X(D)}\operatorname {Pic}(X)\to 0. \end{equation}

Proof. Local factorization of a nonzero meromorphic germ is $z^ m u$ with $m\in \mathbb Z$ and $u$ a holomorphic unit. It proves exactness of the sheaf sequence

\[ 1\to \mathcal O_ X^*\to \mathcal M_ X^* \xrightarrow {\operatorname {div}}\operatorname {Div}_ X\to 0. \]

In particular the last arrow is onto on stalks, since $z^ m$ realizes any integer. The preceding lemma with $A_ x=\mathbb Z$ gives $H^1(X,\operatorname {Div}_ X)=0$.

We check the connecting sign. For a divisor $D$ choose local meromorphic equations $f_ i$ with divisor $D$. Their quotients $f_ j/f_ i$ are holomorphic units and, by lemma 10.40.1, represent the connecting class. The frames $e_ i=1/f_ i$ of $\mathcal O_ X(D)$ satisfy $e_ i=(f_ j/f_ i)e_ j$, exactly the convention of theorem 10.40.2. Hence the connecting map sends $D$ to $\mathcal O_ X(D)$. It is onto the Picard group by lemma 10.78.1. The long exact sequence now proves both the asserted $H^1$ vanishing and (10.78.6); its initial kernel is $\mathcal O_ X^*(X)=\mathbb C^*$ by compactness and connectedness. $\square$

For a smooth Hermitian metric $h$ on $E$, use the canonical normalized curvature $c_1(E,h)=-dd^ c\log |e|_ h^2$ in a holomorphic frame and put

\[ \deg E=\int _ Xc_1(E,h),\qquad dd^ c=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial . \]

The integral is metric independent by the metric-change formula in smooth line-bundle metrics and curvature changes and Stokes. Its de Rham class is the real image of the integer-sheaf Chern class by theorem 10.40.5; no identification of all integer cohomology groups is presumed here.

Lemma 10.78.7.378F4A64CA3C If $s$ is a nonzero meromorphic section of $E$, then

90C71A91CD02 10.78.8
\begin{equation} dd^ c\log |s|_ h^2=[\operatorname {div}s]-c_1(E,h), \qquad \deg E=\sum _ x\operatorname {ord}_ x(s)\in \mathbb Z. \end{equation}

In particular $\deg \mathcal O_ X(D)=\sum _ xm_ x$; degrees add under tensor product and change sign under dualization. A nonzero holomorphic section has an effective divisor, so $h^0(E)=0$ if $\deg E<0$, or if $\deg E=0$ and $E$ is nontrivial. In degree zero a nonzero holomorphic section trivializes $E$.

Proof. In a local frame write $s=f e$ with $f$ meromorphic. The Poincaré–Lelong theorem the normalized Poincaré–Lelong formula, applied to the numerator and denominator of $f$, gives

\[ dd^ c\log |s|_ h^2 =dd^ c\log |f|^2+dd^ c\log |e|_ h^2 =[\operatorname {div}s]-c_1(E,h). \]

The norm logarithm is a global locally integrable function, including its logarithmic zeros and poles. Stokes against the constant one gives the degree identity. The divisor bundle has the meromorphic section one with divisor $D$: in its local frame $z^{-m_ x}$ its coefficient is $z^{m_ x}$. Tensor products and duals either add the curvatures or add and negate these divisors. Finally an effective divisor has nonnegative degree; if that degree is zero it is empty, and the section is a holomorphic frame everywhere. $\square$

Theorem 10.78.9 (Riemann–Roch for a line bundle).7F8CC797059F Every holomorphic line bundle $E$ on $X$ satisfies

8BE9F0A9B79D 10.78.10
\begin{equation} h^0(E)-h^1(E)=\deg E+1-g,\qquad h^1(E)=h^0(K_ X\otimes E^*). \end{equation}

The latter equality is induced by the perfect pairing

\[ H^1(E)\times H^0(K_ X\otimes E^*)\longrightarrow \mathbb C, \qquad ([\alpha ],\beta )\longmapsto \int _ X\alpha \wedge \beta . \]

Proof. Set $\chi (E)=h^0(E)-h^1(E)$. Taking alternating dimensions in (10.78.3) gives

\[ \chi (F(D))=\chi (F)+\sum _ xm_ x\qquad (D\geq 0). \]

For any divisor write $D=D_+-D_-$ with $D_\pm \geq 0$. Apply the effective case first to $(F,D_+)$ and then to $(F(D),D_-)$. The resulting identities have the same left-hand term $\chi (F(D_+))$, so subtraction gives

\[ \chi (F(D))=\chi (F)+\sum _ xm_ x \]

for all integral divisors, including negative ones. Now take $E\cong \mathcal O_ X(D)$ as already proved. Since $h^0(\mathcal O_ X)=1$ and $h^1(\mathcal O_ X)=g$, the divisor-degree identity proves the first assertion.

The second is precisely the analytic Serre pairing of theorem 10.46.7 with $n=1,p=0,q=1$, transported by the bundle Dolbeault/sheaf comparison. A smooth $E^*$-valued $(1,0)$-form killed by $\bar\partial $ is a holomorphic section of $K_ X\otimes E^*$, by its local holomorphic coefficients. Stokes makes the displayed integral independent of the representative of $[\alpha ]$, and the cited proved harmonic-star argument makes it perfect. This is complex bilinear duality, not an unsupported identification of the two spaces by a metric-linear map. $\square$

Corollary 10.78.11.43B79368A1B8 One has $h^0(K_ X)=g$, $h^1(K_ X)=1$, and

2681385859FF 10.78.12
\begin{equation} \deg K_ X=2g-2. \end{equation}

For every $a\in X$ and every integer $m$,

492C950FA104 10.78.13
\begin{equation} h^0(K_ X(-m[a]))=h^1(\mathcal O_ X(m[a])) =h^0(\mathcal O_ X(m[a]))-m+g-1. \end{equation}

If $\deg E>2g-2$, then $h^1(E)=0$ and $h^0(E)=\deg E+1-g$. In particular $h^0(\mathcal O_ X(m[a]))=m+1-g$ for every integer $m>2g-2$.

Proof. Serre duality for $\mathcal O_ X$ gives $h^0(K_ X)=g$, and for $K_ X$ gives $h^1(K_ X)=h^0(\mathcal O_ X)=1$. Apply Riemann–Roch to $K_ X$. Apply it next to $\mathcal O_ X(m[a])$, whose dual is $\mathcal O_ X(-m[a])$ and whose degree is $m$. This proves (10.78.13) also for zero and negative $m$. If $\deg E>2g-2$, then $\deg (K_ X\otimes E^*)<0$, so its holomorphic sections vanish by lemma 10.78.7. Serre and Riemann–Roch give the last assertions. $\square$

Corollary 10.78.14.5A3D6AC0AA3E For a fixed $a\in X$, the dimension increase

\[ h^0(\mathcal O_ X(m[a]))-h^0(\mathcal O_ X((m-1)[a])) \]

is zero or one for $m\geq 1$. It is one exactly when there is a meromorphic function whose only pole is $a$ and whose pole order is exactly $m$. Exactly $g$ positive integers fail this property. For $g\geq 1$ all these gaps are at most $2g-1$; for $g=0$ there are no gaps. There is a nonconstant function with only a pole at $a$ of order at most $g+1$.

Proof. Apply (10.78.2) to the inclusion $\mathcal O_ X((m-1)[a])\subset \mathcal O_ X(m[a])$. Its quotient has dimension one, and the first three terms of the section sequence give the increase bound and the exact-pole interpretation. For a large integer $M>2g-2$, telescoping the increases from $h^0(\mathcal O_ X)=1$ gives $M-g$ nongaps among $1,\ldots ,M$. If $g\geq 1$, consecutive dimensions are given by the polynomial formula for $m\geq 2g$, so all these increases are one; the same is true for every $m\geq 1$ when $g=0$. There are therefore precisely $g$ gaps in the stated range. Finally Riemann–Roch gives $h^0(\mathcal O_ X((g+1)[a]))\geq 2$, so a section independent of the constants is the required function. $\square$

Corollary 10.78.15.F8C1FB7AE853 The additive sheaf $\mathcal M(E)$ of meromorphic sections satisfies

\[ H^1(X,\mathcal M(E))=0. \]

Proof. Let $P(E)=\mathcal M(E)/\mathcal O(E)$. Local polar expansions identify it with the sheaf of locally finite assignments of principal parts in the abelian groups $A_ x=\mathcal M(E)_ x/\mathcal O(E)_ x$. To check surjectivity of this identification, realize any given principal part by its finite polar polynomial in a small frame disk; on the punctured disk its quotient class is zero and glues with zero. Locally finite families glue these representatives as quotient sections. Thus lemma 10.78.4 gives $H^1(X,P(E))=0$.

Fix a point $a$ and choose a positive integer $m>2g-2-\deg E$. Then $H^1(E(m[a]))=0$ by corollary 10.78.11. The boundary from $Q_{m[a]}(E)(X)$ onto $H^1(E)$ is therefore surjective. It factors through the boundary $P(E)(X)\to H^1(E)$ by naturality of the inclusions into $\mathcal M(E)$. The latter boundary is onto as well. The long exact sequence of $0\to \mathcal O(E)\to \mathcal M(E)\to P(E)\to 0$ now gives $H^1(X,\mathcal M(E))=0$. No commutation of sheaf cohomology with an increasing union of pole bounds has been assumed. $\square$

Corollary 10.78.16 (Riemann–Hurwitz).0DB2FC824E28 Let $f:X\to Y$ be a nonconstant holomorphic map between connected compact Riemann surfaces. For $x\in X$ let $e_ x$ be the order of $f-f(x)$ in local coordinates. There is a positive integer $d$ such that $\sum _{f(x)=y}e_ x=d$ for every $y\in Y$, and

\[ K_ X\cong f^*K_ Y\otimes \mathcal O_ X\! \left( \sum _ x(e_ x-1)[x]\right),\qquad 2g_ X-2=d(2g_ Y-2)+\sum _ x(e_ x-1). \]

Only finitely many summands in the ramification divisor are nonzero.

Proof. The map is open by its local nonconstant holomorphic power expansion and closed by compactness, so it is onto. Its fibers are finite, since their points are isolated. The usual local degree argument is short: around each point in a fiber choose a small coordinate disk whose boundary avoids that target value. All preimages of nearby values remain in their union, since otherwise compactness would give an additional point of the original fiber. In each disk the integral of the logarithmic derivative of the target coordinate expression minus the nearby value is a continuous integer and hence constant. Factoring its zeros identifies it with the sum of their orders. Thus the total fiber order is locally constant on connected $Y$, and is a fixed positive $d$.

The derivative is a nonzero holomorphic section of $K_ X\otimes (f^*K_ Y)^*$. In local coordinates its coefficient is the derivative of $z^{e_ x}u(z)$, with $u(0)\ne 0$, so its zero order is exactly $e_ x-1$. These zeros are finite on compact $X$. The meromorphic-section bundle identification already proved gives the asserted isomorphism. For any line bundle $L$ on $Y$, take a meromorphic section with divisor $\sum m_ y[y]$. Its pullback has divisor $\sum _ y m_ y\sum _{f(x)=y}e_ x[x]$. The degree-divisor identity therefore gives $\deg f^*L=d\deg L$, without a separate topological covering-degree theorem. Taking degrees in the bundle isomorphism and using (10.78.12) finishes. $\square$

Theorem 10.78.17.BE4F0C047320 Suppose $g\geq 1$ and let $u_1,\ldots ,u_ g$ be a basis of $H^0(X,K_ X)$. In a local coordinate $z$, write $u_ j=U_ j(z)\, dz$. With $N=g(g+1)/2$, the expression

CB0D8959E769 10.78.18
\begin{equation} W(u_1,\ldots ,u_ g) =\det \bigl (U_ j^{(k)}(z)\bigr )_{0\leq k\leq g-1,\, 1\leq j\leq g} (dz)^ N \end{equation}

defines a nonzero holomorphic section of $K_ X^ N$. Its zero divisor does not depend on the basis. At each $a\in X$ there are exactly $g$ integers $1\leq s_1<\cdots <s_ g\leq 2g-1$ such that

A7C22590729C 10.78.19
\begin{equation} h^0(\mathcal O_ X(m[a]))=m+1-\# \{ j:s_ j\leq m\} \qquad (m\geq 0). \end{equation}

These are precisely the pole gaps. The multiplicity of $W$ at $a$ is

\[ \mu _ a=\sum _{j=1}^ g s_ j-\frac{g(g+1)}2\geq 0, \qquad \sum _{a\in X}\mu _ a=g(g+1)(g-1). \]

The points with $\mu _ a>0$ are the Weierstrass points. There are at most $g(g+1)(g-1)$ such points. At every other point the gap sequence is $1,\ldots ,g$ and

\[ h^0(\mathcal O_ X(m[a]))= \begin{cases} 1,& 0\leq m\leq g,\\ m+1-g,& m>g.\end{cases} \]

Proof. If $z=z(w)$, the coefficients in the $w$ coordinate are $V_ j(w)=U_ j(z(w))z'(w)$. Repeated differentiation gives

\[ V_ j^{(k)}=(z')^{k+1}U_ j^{(k)}\circ z +\sum _{r<k}a_{kr}U_ j^{(r)}\circ z. \]

This follows by induction: differentiating the first term produces the next leading term and a lower derivative, and differentiating each remaining term keeps its derivative order at most $k$. The transformation matrix of the rows is triangular, with determinant $\prod _{k=0}^{g-1}(z')^{k+1}=(z')^ N$. This proves that (10.78.18) is a section of $K_ X^ N$. A constant basis change multiplies it by its nonzero determinant.

At a fixed $a$, filter $H^0(K_ X)$ by order of vanishing at $a$. Each successive quotient has dimension at most one, by the first nonzero Taylor coefficient. Every nonzero section has zero divisor of degree $2g-2$, so its vanishing order is at most $2g-2$. Consequently the filtration terminates, and choosing one section at each strict decrease gives a basis with distinct leading orders:

\[ \widetilde u_ j=(z^{s_ j-1}+O(z^{s_ j}))\, dz, \qquad 1\leq s_1<\cdots <s_ g\leq 2g-1. \]

Set $r_ j=s_ j-1$. Factoring $z^{r_ j}$ out of column $j$ and $z^{-k}$ out of row $k$ in its Wronskian gives, for $z\ne 0$,

\[ W(\widetilde u_1,\ldots ,\widetilde u_ g) =z^{\sum r_ j-\sum _{k=0}^{g-1}k} \left(\det \bigl ((r_ j)_ k\bigr )_{k,j}+O(z)\right)(dz)^ N. \]

Here $(r)_0=1$ and $(r)_ k=r(r-1)\cdots (r-k+1)$; zero falling factorials are allowed. These are monic polynomials of degrees $k$, so elementary triangular row operations turn their determinant into the Vandermonde determinant $\prod _{i<j}(r_ j-r_ i)\ne 0$. The exponent is $\mu _ a=\sum s_ j-N$, which is nonnegative because $s_ j\geq j$. Thus the expression extends holomorphically at zero, is not identically zero, and has exactly that multiplicity.

Distinct leading orders mean that a linear combination's lowest nonzero leading term cannot cancel. Hence $h^0(K_ X(-m[a]))=\# \{ j:s_ j>m\} $. Substitute this in (10.78.13) to obtain (10.78.19). Its consecutive differences give the exact gap interpretation. Also $\mu _ a=0$ exactly when every $s_ j=j$. This proves the stated dimensions at ordinary points. Finally $W$ is a nonzero section of $K_ X^ N$, so its finitely many zeros have total multiplicity $N\deg K_ X=g(g+1)(g-1)$ by the divisor-degree identity. Every positive multiplicity is an integer at least one, proving the bound on their number, including zero Weierstrass points for $g=1$. $\square$
