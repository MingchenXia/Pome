10.79 Integral periods, Picard tori and Albanese maps6209C395C4D3

6209C395C4D3

Constant sheaves have the discrete groups indicated by their coefficients. We use Godement sheaf cohomology on Hausdorff second-countable manifolds. All de Rham and Dolbeault representatives in this section use the inverse $\Psi _ q^{-1}$ of the successive positive resolution connecting maps. In particular curvature degree means integration after $\Psi _2^{-1}$. The multiplicative augmentation comparison is distinguished below. No singular-cohomology comparison, finite good-cover theorem or integral Poincaré duality is assumed.

Lemma 10.79.1.1B257374E593 The fundamental group of a connected compact smooth manifold is finitely generated. Its abelianization has the form $\mathbb Z^ b\oplus T$ with $T$ finite.

Proof. A smooth positive metric exists by partitions of unity. Its path distance induces the topology: on a relatively compact coordinate ball, metric lengths and Euclidean lengths are bounded above and below by positive constant multiples; a path leaving a smaller concentric ball has a positive lower length bound. The triangle inequality and these local bounds prove that the path distance is a metric with the original topology. Connectedness implies path connectedness since coordinate balls are path connected.

Take a finite cover by coordinate balls whose coordinate images are convex. By compactness choose $\epsilon >0$ such that every metric ball of radius $10\epsilon $ lies in one of them. Take a finite $\epsilon $-net of points, including a base point $a$. Join two net points by one fixed path of length less than $4\epsilon $ whenever their distance is less than $3\epsilon $. These finitely many edges form a graph mapped to the manifold. A based loop can be subdivided into segments of image diameter less than $\epsilon $; replace each division point by a net point at distance less than $\epsilon $, keeping $a$ at both ends. Consecutive net points are joined by an edge. Join each original division point to its net point by a path of length less than $2\epsilon $. Each resulting quadrilateral, including the original short segment and the fixed edge, lies in a ball of radius $6\epsilon $ about its first original point. It contracts in a convex coordinate ball. The loop is therefore homotopic, with base point fixed, to the graph's edge loop. The same construction on paths shows the graph component containing $a$ contains all net vertices. Choose a finite spanning tree. The loops consisting of a tree path, a non-tree edge and a return tree path generate every edge loop by cancellation of tree paths. Their images generate the fundamental group. In dimension zero the manifold is a point and the assertion is immediate.

Here are the needed integral algebra details. A subgroup of $\mathbb Z^ r$ is free and finitely generated: project onto the last coordinate, whose image is $d\mathbb Z$; if $d>0$ choose an element mapping to $d$, and split off its cyclic group. The kernel is a subgroup of $\mathbb Z^{r-1}$, so induction applies. Write the abelianization as $G=\mathbb Z^ r/L$ and put $L_ s=(\operatorname {span}_{\mathbb Q}L)\cap \mathbb Z^ r$. If $l_1,\ldots ,l_ s$ is a basis of $L$, a nonzero $s$-row minor of their coordinate matrix has some integer determinant $d\ne 0$. Cramer's rule shows $dL_ s\subset L$. Thus $L_ s/L$ is finite, since $L_ s/dL_ s$ is finite by the subgroup result. It is exactly the torsion subgroup $T$ of $G$. The quotient $G/T$ injects into the rational vector space $\mathbb Q^ r/\operatorname {span}_{\mathbb Q}L$. Choose a rational basis there and clear the finitely many denominators of the images of the original generators. This embeds $G/T$ in a finite-rank free integer group. The subgroup result makes it free; lifting its basis splits $G\cong \mathbb Z^ b\oplus T$. The case $L=0$ has $T=0$ and needs no minor. $\square$

Theorem 10.79.2.F90F5D542AFB Let $M$ be a connected compact smooth manifold and $a\in M$. For every abelian group $A$ there is a natural isomorphism

6D4EC61A4B96 10.79.3
\begin{equation} H^1(M,\underline A)\cong \operatorname {Hom}(\pi _1(M,a),A). \end{equation}

For $A=\mathbb R$, the de Rham comparison identifies it with $[\alpha ]\mapsto (\gamma \mapsto \int _\gamma \alpha )$. Consequently $H^1(M,\underline{\mathbb Z})\cong \mathbb Z^ b$, its map to $H^1_{\rm dR}(M,\mathbb R)$ is injective with full lattice image, and the image consists exactly of the classes with integer periods. The image of $H^1(M,\underline{\mathbb Q})$ is the rational span of that lattice. If $G=\pi _1(M,a)_{\rm ab}$, the evaluation map induces

\[ G/\operatorname {Tor}G \cong \operatorname {Hom}(H^1(M,\underline{\mathbb Z}),\mathbb Z). \]

Proof. By lemma 10.40.1, degree-one sheaf classes are represented by locally constant Čech cocycles $c_{ij}$ on open covers. They satisfy $c_{ij}+c_{jk}=c_{ik}$. Glue $U_ i\times A$ by the coordinate changes $t_ j=t_ i-c_{ij}$. Since these changes are locally constant, the result is a covering space locally of the form $U_ i\times A$, with a free transitive $A$-action on each fiber. It has path lifting: subdivide a path into finitely many pieces in such trivializations and keep its fiber coordinate constant on each piece. Uniqueness on overlapping intervals gives uniqueness of the lift. Subdividing a homotopy square into small trivialized rectangles shows that continuation along homotopic paths with fixed endpoints is equal. Thus continuation around a based loop is translation by an element of $A$, and gives a homomorphism $\chi :\pi _1(M,a)\to A$. A coboundary changes fiber origins and not this translation. If $\chi =0$, continuation of one chosen origin along paths gives a global section of this covering: path independence follows by closing two paths to a loop, and local trivializations give continuity. Its local coordinates show that $c_{ij}$ is a coboundary.

Conversely choose a coordinate-ball cover, and for each ball choose a path from $a$ to its center, followed by the straight coordinate path to a varying point $x$ of the ball. Call the resulting path $\lambda _ i(x)$. Given $\chi $, set

\[ c_{ij}(x)=\chi ([\lambda _ j(x)*\lambda _ i(x)^{-1}]). \]

Here $*$ means concatenation in the written order. On a small neighborhood of any overlap point the displayed loop class is constant, by contracting inside the two coordinate balls. The cocycle identity holds after applying the abelian homomorphism $\chi $. Its glued covering has monodromy $\chi $: along each coordinate segment the paths $\lambda _ i$ vary by that segment, and the changes $t_ j=t_ i-c_{ij}$ cancel the chosen access paths. This also proves compatibility with refinements and coefficient homomorphisms. The constructions are additive and inverse, proving (10.79.3).

For a closed real one-form choose local primitives $f_ i$. The de Rham resolution sends it to the cocycle $f_ j-f_ i$, by the positive connecting convention already checked in lemma 10.40.1. With the gluing rule above, a telescoping sum along a subdivided loop gives monodromy $\int _\gamma \alpha $: it is minus the sum of the successive $f_ j-f_ i$ at switching points. This proves the asserted de Rham compatibility, including its sign.

Use lemma 10.79.1 to write $G=\mathbb Z^ b\oplus T$. Homomorphisms from $T$ to $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R$ vanish, so the three groups in question identify naturally with $\mathbb Z^ b,\mathbb Q^ b, \mathbb R^ b$, and coefficient inclusions give the usual inclusions. The integer subgroup is discrete and spans the real space; its fundamental parallelotope is compact, proving that it is a full lattice. Evaluation of $G$ on its integer characters is the ordinary evaluation of $\mathbb Z^ b$ on its dual, with kernel exactly $T$. $\square$

Theorem 10.79.4.C1FB3F157E12 Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold and $q=h^{0,1}(X)$. The coefficient map

\[ H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})\longrightarrow H^1(X,\mathcal O_ X) \]

is injective with full lattice image $\Lambda _ X$ of rank $2q$. The complex torus

\[ J(X)=H^1(X,\mathcal O_ X)/\Lambda _ X \]

is naturally the subgroup $\operatorname {Pic}^0(X)$ of holomorphic line bundles whose integer-sheaf Chern class is zero. Its elements are exactly the topologically trivial smooth line bundles equipped with these holomorphic structures. There is an exact sequence

\[ 0\to J(X)\to \operatorname {Pic}(X) \xrightarrow {c_1^{\mathbb Z}}\mathrm{NS}(X)\to 0, \qquad \mathrm{NS}(X)=\ker \bigl (H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb Z}) \to H^2(X,\mathcal O_ X)\bigr ). \]

This assertion does not require discarding integer classes with zero real image or presuppose any finiteness theorem for $\mathrm{NS}(X)$.

Proof. The compact Kähler Hodge decomposition theorem 10.47.13 gives

\[ H^1_{\rm dR}(X,\mathbb C)=H^{1,0}(X)\oplus H^{0,1}(X), \qquad \overline{H^{1,0}(X)}=H^{0,1}(X). \]

Projection from the real part to $H^{0,1}$ is a real-linear isomorphism: a real class is determined by this component, and an arbitrary $\eta \in H^{0,1}$ comes from $\eta +\bar\eta $. By proposition 10.40.7, this is exactly the coefficient map to $H^1(\mathcal O_ X)$ under the canonical comparisons. The preceding full-lattice theorem proves the first assertion. If $q=0$ the quotient is a point. Otherwise translations by a full real lattice in a complex vector space act discretely; balls smaller than half the shortest nonzero lattice distance give holomorphic quotient charts. A fundamental parallelotope proves compactness of the quotient.

The remaining assertions follow from the exact exponential sequence and smooth-line classification in theorem 10.40.2. In particular its kernel at the Picard group is exactly this quotient; zero integer class is equivalent to a trivial underlying smooth line bundle. No replacement of the integer class by its real image is made. $\square$

Theorem 10.79.5.99C9CC069FA0 For a connected compact Kähler manifold put $V=H^0(X,\Omega _ X^1)$ and

\[ \Gamma _ X=\left\{ u\longmapsto \int _\gamma u: [\gamma ]\in \pi _1(X,a)\right\} \subset V^*. \]

This is a full lattice of rank $2\dim _{\mathbb C}V$ in $V^*$. The period torus $A(X)=V^*/\Gamma _ X$ is therefore a compact complex torus. The map

43EBBCC4E992 10.79.6
\begin{equation} \alpha _ a:X\longrightarrow A(X),\qquad x\longmapsto \left(u\longmapsto \int _ a^ x u\right)\bmod \Gamma _ X \end{equation}

is well defined and holomorphic, with differential $d\alpha _ a(v)(u)=u(v)$. Every holomorphic map to a complex torus which takes $a$ to zero factors uniquely through $\alpha _ a$ by a homomorphism induced by a complex-linear map of the covering vector spaces.

Proof. Holomorphic one-forms are harmonic on a compact Kähler manifold: they are $\bar\partial $-closed and their $\bar\partial $ adjoint has out-of-range antiholomorphic degree. The proved identity $\Delta _ d=2\square $ makes them $d$-closed. Hodge decomposition shows that $u\mapsto [\operatorname {Re}u]$ is a real-linear isomorphism from $V$ to $H^1_{\rm dR}(X,\mathbb R)$. Under this isomorphism a complex-linear functional $\ell \in V^*$ corresponds to the real functional $[\operatorname {Re}u]\mapsto \operatorname {Re}\ell (u)$. This identifies the underlying real vector space of $V^*$ with the real dual of $H^1_{\rm dR}(X,\mathbb R)$: a real functional $b$ on $V$ determines the complex-linear functional $u\mapsto b(u)-\mathrm i b(\mathrm i u)$. Under it the period of a loop is precisely evaluation of real cohomology on that loop. The final evaluation assertion of theorem 10.79.2 identifies these evaluations with the full dual lattice of $H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})$. This proves the lattice assertion, including the possible finite torsion kernel of the loop period map.

Two paths from $a$ to $x$ differ by a loop period. Local closed holomorphic one-forms have holomorphic primitives: the radial Poincaré primitive in a coordinate ball has differential $u$, so its $\bar\partial $ is zero. These primitives give local holomorphic lifts of (10.79.6) and its stated differential.

Let $f:X\to W/L$ be a holomorphic map to a complex torus with $f(a)=0$, where $L$ is a full lattice in the complex vector space $W$. Constant covectors on $W$ descend to holomorphic one-forms. Their pullbacks define a complex-linear map $W^*\to V$; let $B:V^*\to W$ be its dual. Integrating along a loop, local lifts of $f$ change by an element of $L$, so $B\Gamma _ X\subset L$. It induces a homomorphism $\bar B:A(X)\to W/L$. The maps $f$ and $\bar B\alpha _ a$ have identical differentials and agree at $a$; their difference has locally constant lifts, so connectedness gives equality. For uniqueness, the complex span of all $d\alpha _ a(T_ xX)$ is $V^*$: an element of its annihilator would be a holomorphic one-form vanishing at every point. Thus two covering linear maps inducing the same factorization have equal differentials and are equal. A holomorphic group homomorphism between complex tori is of this linear type: its differential is translation invariant by the group law. On the covering vector space subtract the map induced by its differential at zero; the difference has zero differential, hence is constant and zero. Evaluating at lattice points gives the required compatibility of the covering linear map with both lattices. $\square$

For completeness there is a typed current description of this map. For $n=\dim _{\mathbb C}X\geq 1$, put

\[ \mathcal S_ n:H^{n-1,n}_{\bar\partial }(X)\longrightarrow V^*, \qquad \mathcal S_ n([\eta ])(u)=\int _ Xu\wedge \eta . \]

It is an isomorphism by theorem 10.46.7, with this explicitly chosen order of factors. A path integration current $I_\xi $ has degree $2n-1$ and $dI_\xi =\delta _ x-\delta _ a$; it is not a closed de Rham current for an open path. Its component of type $(n-1,n)$ is $\bar\partial $-closed by degree, so it does have a Dolbeault class, using the smooth/current comparison. With the existing current convention $T_\eta (u)=\int \eta \wedge u$, odd total degree gives

\[ \mathcal S_ n([I_\xi ^{n-1,n}])(u)=-\int _\xi u, \qquad \alpha _ a(x)=-\mathcal S_ n([I_\xi ^{n-1,n}])\bmod \Gamma _ X. \]

The current pairing agrees with that of a smooth representative: their difference is a $\bar\partial $ boundary, which pairs to zero with a holomorphic one-form by the distributional Stokes formula. Changing the path adds a loop-period lattice element. This description uses the loop-current lattice $\mathcal S_ n^{-1}(\Gamma _ X)$; it does not identify it with the image of all of $H^{2n-1}(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})$ without a further integral duality proof. In dimension zero all these spaces and the torus are zero.

Every compact curve is Kähler: any Hermitian metric has a closed fundamental two-form by real dimension. In the following curve statements put $g=h^1(X,\mathcal O_ X)$.

Lemma 10.79.7.1CADB2D45709 On a connected compact curve, every degree-zero line bundle lies in $\operatorname {Pic}^0(X)$. For a fixed $a\in X$, the map

\[ \Phi _ a:X\longrightarrow J(X),\qquad x\longmapsto \mathcal O_ X([x]-[a]) \]

is well defined and holomorphic. Moreover

58128D058B3D 10.79.8
\begin{equation} H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})\cong \mathbb Z, \qquad c_1^{\mathbb Z}(\mathcal O_ X([a]))\longmapsto 1, \end{equation}

The real coefficient map is injective, and the $\Psi _2^{-1}$ image of this generator has integral one.

Proof. First take $x$ near $a$ in a coordinate disk, writing $z(a)=0$ and $z(x)=t$. Choose nested disks $D_ r\Subset D_ R$ about zero, with $|t|<r/2$, and the cover $U_1=D_ R$, $U_2=X\setminus \overline D_ r$. Local meromorphic equations for $[x]-[a]$ are $f_1=(z-t)/z$ and $f_2=1$. The canonical frames $e_ i=1/f_ i$ have transition $g_{12}=f_2/f_1=z/(z-t)$; this is the frame, rather than fiber-coordinate, convention. On their annular overlap it has the holomorphic logarithm

E6A14AA9C835 10.79.9
\begin{equation} c_ t(z)=\frac1{2\pi \mathrm i}\log \frac z{z-t} =-\frac1{2\pi \mathrm i}\log (1-t/z),\qquad c_0=0. \end{equation}

Thus the line bundle is in the exponential image of $H^1(\mathcal O_ X)$.

We check holomorphic parameter dependence without an assumed theorem on analytic families of line bundles. Let $\chi $ be a smooth cutoff equal to one near $\overline D_ r$ and supported in $D_ R$. The smooth cochain $b_1=-(1-\chi )c_ t$, $b_2=\chi c_ t$, extended by zero where necessary, satisfies $b_2-b_1=c_ t$. Its Dolbeault representative is the global smooth form

F6740EF6A319 10.79.10
\begin{equation} \eta _ t=\bar\partial \chi \, c_ t, \end{equation}

supported on a fixed compact subannulus. This is the positive connecting convention, since $\bar\partial b_ i=\eta _ t$. The power series in $t/z$ converges with all $z$ derivatives on that support uniformly for $t$ in smaller parameter disks. Projection onto the finite harmonic space is continuous linear by theorem 10.46.6. Therefore $t\mapsto [\eta _ t]\in H^1(\mathcal O_ X)$ is holomorphic, as is its quotient in $J(X)$.

The same local construction about every point shows that the coset of $\mathcal O_ X([x]-[a])$ modulo $\operatorname {Pic}^0(X)$ is locally constant: near $x_0$ its difference from the value at $x_0$ is $\mathcal O_ X([x]-[x_0])$, just treated. Connectedness makes it the zero coset everywhere. The same local logarithmic lifts prove global holomorphicity of $\Phi _ a$.

Every line bundle is $\mathcal O_ X(D)$ by lemma 10.78.1. If $\deg D=0$, write it as the tensor product of $\mathcal O_ X([x]-[a])^{m_ x}$, because $\sum m_ x=0$. It lies in $\operatorname {Pic}^0$. Conversely zero integer Chern class has zero real curvature class, so has degree zero. Since $\deg \mathcal O_ X([a])=1$, degree gives an isomorphism $\operatorname {Pic}(X)/\operatorname {Pic}^0(X)\cong \mathbb Z$. Finally $H^2(X,\mathcal O_ X)=0$ by the one-dimensional Dolbeault resolution, so the exponential sequence identifies this quotient with $H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})$. The Chern/curvature comparison and divisor degree give the stated positive real image, which is injective since its generator has nonzero integral. $\square$

Lemma 10.79.11.6D7C5C5A2C24 Let $X$ be a connected compact curve, let $V=H^0(X,K_ X)$, and use

\[ \mathcal S:H^1(X,\mathcal O_ X)\longrightarrow V^*, \qquad \mathcal S([\eta ])(u)=\int _ Xu\wedge \eta . \]

This Serre isomorphism maps $\Lambda _ X$ onto $\Gamma _ X$. Moreover $\pi _1(X)_{\rm ab}$ is free of rank $2g$, and integration currents give an isomorphism

\[ \pi _1(X)_{\rm ab}\longrightarrow H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb Z}) \]

whose real image is the de Rham class of the loop current, with the complex orientation of $X$.

Proof. We first justify the regular-value assertion used below. A smooth real function on a surface has critical values of measure zero. At a critical point where some second derivative is nonzero, the zero set of a suitable first derivative is a smooth curve by the inverse function theorem. Its critical subset maps into the critical values of the restricted one-variable function. Those values have measure zero: on the open set $|f'|<\delta $, disjoint intervals cover the compact critical set, and their image lengths total at most $\delta $ times the total domain length; then let $\delta \to 0$. Use countably many such curve neighborhoods. On the remaining critical set both first and second derivatives vanish. In a compact coordinate rectangle with bounded third derivatives, a square of side $r$ meeting that set has image of that set in an interval of length at most $Cr^3$, by Taylor's formula at a point of the set in the square. At most $C'r^{-2}$ squares are needed. Letting $r\to 0$ gives measure zero there too. Countable compact subrectangles and target coordinate intervals prove the assertion also for a smooth map to $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$.

Every class of $H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})$ is the connecting class of a smooth circle-valued function $f$. Indeed the exact sequence $0\to \underline{\mathbb Z}\to \mathcal A_{\mathbb R} \xrightarrow {\exp (2\pi \mathrm i\, \cdot )}\mathcal T\to 1$ has acyclic additive middle sheaf. Locally write $f=\exp (2\pi \mathrm i t_ i)$. Its real de Rham image is $\beta =dt_ i=f^*(dt)$, by the same connecting/sign calculation as in theorem 10.79.2. Choose a regular value of $f$. Its fiber $C$ is a compact oriented one-manifold, hence a finite union of circles. Orient it by requiring $dt$ followed by its orientation to give the orientation of $X$. On a small band of regular values, the vector field $\nabla t/|dt|^2$ gives a product $C\times (-\delta ,\delta )$: its flow changes $t$ at unit rate, and compactness of a slightly larger closed band ensures existence for these short times. Choose a one-form $\chi (t)dt$ of integral one supported in that band. On the circle it differs from $dt$ by an exact form, since the integral of the difference is zero and its primitive is periodic. Thus $\beta $ is cohomologous to $f^*(\chi (t)dt)$. For any closed one-form $u$, Stokes on the intervening cylinders makes $\int _{C_ t}u$ independent of $t$. Fubini in the product band therefore gives

853E7016EF11 10.79.12
\begin{equation} \int _ X\beta \wedge u=\int _ Cu. \end{equation}

An empty regular fiber gives zero and causes no exception. For a holomorphic $u$, the $(1,0)$ part of $\beta $ wedges with $u$ to zero. Hence $\mathcal S$ of its $(0,1)$ class is minus the period along $C$, which lies in $\Gamma _ X$. Joining the components to a base point changes no closed-form integral. This proves $\mathcal S\Lambda _ X\subset \Gamma _ X$.

Conversely any piecewise smooth loop on an oriented surface splits, after small homotopies in coordinate disks, into finitely many embedded oriented circles: choose finitely many coordinate arcs in transverse position, split at their finitely many double crossings and round the corners. The general coordinate-arc construction in the proof of lemma 10.77.6 justifies these operations; it precedes and does not use that lemma's cohomology hypothesis. Local primitives preserve all closed-form periods. An embedded circle has a two-sided collar, with normal coordinate $t$. The closed form $\beta =\chi (t)dt$, of normal integral one, extends smoothly by zero off the collar. Its periods on all loops are integers: put a loop in transverse position to the collar center and use on the complement of the center the primitives obtained by integrating from the two outer ends. A crossing contributes its sign, and all other increments cancel. The integral-period characterization in theorem 10.79.2 puts its class in $H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})$. Orient the normal coordinate so that $dt$ followed by the given circle orientation is positive. As in (10.79.12), $\int _ X\beta \wedge u=\int _ Cu$; using $-\beta $ under $\mathcal S$ realizes the positive period of $C$. Summing over the split loop proves $\Gamma _ X\subset \mathcal S\Lambda _ X$.

It remains to check the integral torsion assertion, rather than assume it from an integral duality theorem. The constant coefficient sequence $0\to \mathbb Z\to \mathbb R\to \mathbb R/\mathbb Z\to 0$ gives

\[ H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb R})\to H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z})\to H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})\to H^2(X,\underline{\mathbb R}). \]

The last arrow is injective by lemma 10.79.7, so the first arrow is onto. By the coefficient/monodromy theorem this means that every character of $G=\pi _1(X)_{\rm ab}$ into $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ lifts to a real character. If the finite torsion subgroup $T$ of $G=\mathbb Z^{2g}\oplus T$ were nonzero, it would have a nonzero character into $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$: quotient by a maximal proper subgroup gives a cyclic group of prime order, and embed that group as the corresponding roots of unity. Extend the character by zero on the free summand. No real character can lift it, since real characters kill torsion. Thus $T=0$.

Finally a loop's integration current is closed and has real de Rham class represented by the sum of its collar forms $\beta $ above. Indeed their integrals against every closed one-form coincide; corollary 10.43.11 and the perfect de Rham Hodge pairing of corollary 10.46.10 identify these classes. Its restriction to real forms is perfect: the real Laplacian commutes with conjugation and a real harmonic form pairs with its real Hodge star to give its positive squared norm. This gives a well-defined integral class: integer $H^1$ injects into real $H^1$, so it is independent of the chosen splitting or collars. Under $\mathcal S$ it maps to minus the loop's period. The period map is an isomorphism from $G$ onto $\Gamma _ X$, by the evaluation theorem and $T=0$. The equality of lattices just proved makes the loop-current map an isomorphism onto $H^1(X,\underline{\mathbb Z})$. It is natural under orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms, by the integration-current characterization. Through isothermal coordinates the conclusion applies to every connected compact oriented smooth real surface by theorem 10.77.1, not just one with a previously chosen complex atlas. $\square$

Theorem 10.79.13.06A0A5883E2F For a connected compact curve the map $\mathcal S$ induces an isomorphism $J(X)\cong A(X)$ of complex tori, and under it

\[ \Phi _ a(x)=\mathcal O_ X([x]-[a])=\alpha _ a(x). \]

If $g\geq 1$, this is an embedding and the global holomorphic one-forms do not all vanish at any point. If $g=1$, it is a biholomorphism onto $J(X)$, so $X$ is a complex torus of dimension one. If $g=0$, $X$ is biholomorphic to $\mathbb P^1$.

Proof. The equality of the two full lattices in the preceding lemma gives the torus isomorphism. We compute the map with all signs fixed. In (10.79.9), $\partial _ t c_ t=1/(2\pi \mathrm i(z-t))$. For $u=U(z)\, dz$, (10.79.10) gives

\[ \partial _ t\mathcal S([\eta _ t])(u) =-\frac1{2\pi \mathrm i} \int \frac{\bar\partial \chi }{z-t}\wedge U(z)\, dz =U(t). \]

The last equality is ordinary Stokes on the punctured disk: the outer boundary is zero because $\chi $ has compact support, and the inner boundary has negative orientation, so $\int \bar\partial \chi \wedge U(z)dz/(z-t)=-2\pi \mathrm iU(t)$ by Cauchy's formula. Thus there is no identification of a scalar distribution with a wrongly graded current. At $t=0$ the class is zero, so $\mathcal S([\eta _ t])(u)=\int _0^ t u$. The same calculation near every point, translated by its fixed base-point value, gives identical differentials for $\Phi _ a$ and $\alpha _ a$. Their difference is locally constant and vanishes at $a$, proving the global equality.

We use the simple-pole criterion in an explicit form. A meromorphic function with just one simple pole defines a biholomorphism to $\mathbb P^1$: for every finite $c$, Poincaré–Lelong and Stokes give total zero order one for $f-c$, so every fiber is one simple point, including the pole over infinity. The local holomorphic inverse theorem finishes. On $\mathbb P^1$, a holomorphic one-form is $F(z)dz$ with $F$ entire and $F(z)=O(|z|^{-2})$ at infinity; Cauchy's estimate makes $F=0$. Hence its genus is zero by Serre. Conversely if $g=0$, the prescribed-point principal-part lemma for $E=\mathcal O_ X$, $m=1$ supplies such a simple pole. These arguments also give the complete equivalence between genus zero, one simple pole and $X\cong \mathbb P^1$.

Suppose now $g\geq 1$. If $\Phi _ a(x)=\Phi _ a(y)$ with $x\ne y$, the line $\mathcal O_ X([x]-[y])$ is trivial. The exact sequence theorem 10.78.5 supplies a meromorphic function with divisor $[x]-[y]$. The simple-pole criterion would make the genus zero, a contradiction. This proves injectivity. There is likewise no nonconstant function with only a simple pole at any $x$, so $h^0(\mathcal O_ X([x]))=1$. The formula in corollary 10.78.11 gives $h^0(K_ X(-[x]))=g-1<h^0(K_ X)=g$. Some holomorphic one-form is nonzero at $x$. The differential formula for $\alpha _ a$ therefore makes its one-dimensional complex differential injective. Locally a target coordinate with nonzero derivative gives a graph by the inverse function theorem, so this is an immersion. Compactness and injectivity make it a homeomorphism onto its image, hence an embedding. If $g=1$, target and source have dimension one, so its image is open as well as compact and closed in the connected torus. It is the whole torus, with holomorphic inverse locally and globally. $\square$

Corollary 10.79.14.7DB1B17F312B For $n\geq 1$, every holomorphic line bundle on $\mathbb P^ n$ is uniquely $\mathcal O(k)$ for an integer $k$. Moreover $H^2(\mathbb P^ n,\underline{\mathbb Z})\cong \mathbb Z$ with generator $c_1^{\mathbb Z}(\mathcal O(1))$, positive under the curvature comparison $\Psi _2^{-1}$. For $n=0$ both the Picard group and this cohomology group are zero.

Proof. We first verify simple connectivity without an integral cell calculation. The map $S^{2n+1}\to \mathbb P^ n$ taking a unit vector to its complex line has local sections in each affine chart, obtained by normalizing the vector whose chosen coordinate is one. Thus paths lift by subdividing into those charts. A lifted loop has endpoints differing by a phase $e^{\mathrm i\theta }$; multiplying its lift by $e^{-\mathrm i t\theta }$ closes it without changing its projection. Loops can be replaced by finitely many smooth coordinate arcs, via fixed-endpoint homotopies in convex charts. Every piecewise smooth loop in $S^ m$, $m\geq 2$, misses a point: subdividing its parameter into intervals of length $1/N$ covers its image by $O(N)$ coordinate balls of radius $C/N$, whose total $m$-dimensional volume tends to zero. Stereographic projection then contracts it in $\mathbb R^ m$. Applying this to the closed lift and projecting its contraction proves $\pi _1(\mathbb P^ n)=0$.

Let $L$ be a line bundle, and let $k$ be the integer degree of its restriction to a projective line, supplied by the proved curve degree formula. The exact real projective cohomology computation theorem 10.50.3 and $\int _{\mathbb P^1}\omega _{\rm FS}=1$ give $\Psi _2^{-1}(c_1(L)_{\mathbb R})=k[\omega _{\rm FS}]$. Thus $F=L\otimes \mathcal O(-k)$ has zero real Chern class. By corollary 10.40.9 it has a metric of zero curvature. A flat unitary line connection has local parallel unit frames: its imaginary connection form is closed, so is locally $\mathrm i\, d\varphi $ by the real Poincaré lemma; multiplication by $e^{-\mathrm i\varphi }$ makes the connection zero. Transition functions between these frames are constant. Continuing a frame along paths is homotopy independent by subdividing a homotopy square into these flat charts. Simple connectivity gives a global parallel frame. For the Chern connection its $(0,1)$ part is the holomorphic $\bar\partial $ operator, so that frame is holomorphic. Consequently $F$ is trivial and $L\cong \mathcal O(k)$; uniqueness follows by restriction and degree. Finally the projective Hodge computation and sheaf comparison give $H^1(\mathcal O)=H^2(\mathcal O)=0$. The exponential sequence makes $c_1^{\mathbb Z}$ an isomorphism from this Picard group onto $H^2(\underline{\mathbb Z})$, with the stated generator and real sign. A point has only a trivial line and acyclic constant sheaves, giving the last assertion. $\square$
