9.17 Schwarz estimates and invariant distancesE21DD43D14FB

E21DD43D14FB

Write $\Delta =\{ \zeta \in \mathbb C:|\zeta |<1\} $ and $\phi _ b(\zeta )=(\zeta -b)/(1-\overline b\zeta )$. We use $\log 0=-\infty $ and $\operatorname {atanh}0=0$.

The negative Green envelope also makes sense on an arbitrary domain: if its family is empty, put $g_ D(\cdot ,a)=-\infty $. Otherwise the canonical PSH envelope theorem and comparison with an inner ball give a PSH representative with the required upper logarithmic pole. Indeed every candidate restricted to $B(a,r)\subset D$ is at most $\log (|z-a|/r)$, so regularization preserves that pole and belongs to the original family. The raw and regularized envelopes coincide. It is strictly negative by the maximum principle. The usual holomorphic contraction proof applies on these domains too: the target Green function composed with the map is either identically minus infinity, giving a trivial inequality, or a negative PSH pole competitor. Its pole estimate follows from $|f(z)-f(a)|\leq C|z-a|$ locally. Thus

\[ g_{D'}(f(z),f(a))\leq g_ D(z,a) \]

with all degenerate cases included.

Proposition 9.17.1.10AFB6AB7732 Let $D$ be bounded and $a\in D$. Suppose $v=e^ h$, where $h$ is negative PSH, $h(a)=-\infty $, and $v$ is $C^2$ near $a$. Put $E(z)=\exp (2g_ D(z,a))$. Then $v\leq E$. If $E$ is $C^2$ near $a$, then

\[ \mathcal L_ v(a;\xi )\leq \mathcal L_ E(a;\xi ) \qquad (\xi \in \mathbb C^ n). \]

Suppose additionally that every $z\ne a$ lies with $a$ on a connected one-dimensional complex submanifold on whose punctured part $g_ D(\cdot ,a)$ is harmonic. Equality of these Levi forms in all directions then implies $v=E$. In dimension one, $E$ is automatically smooth near $a$, and equality $v(z)=E(z)$ at any $z\ne a$ already implies $v=E$.

Proof. Nonnegativity, $v(a)=0$ and Taylor's formula give $dv(a)=0$ and $v(z)=O(|z-a|^2)$. Hence $\tfrac 12\log v$ is a negative PSH pole competitor, proving the pointwise bound.

We recall the complete quadratic-jet consequence proved with the canonical planar removal and nonthin-segment theorems. If $v=e^ h$ is $C^2$ near a zero, its pure quadratic terms vanish and

\[ v(a+\eta )=\mathcal L_ v(a;\eta )+o(|\eta |^2). \]

For clarity, on a complex line the bounded function $v(\zeta )/|\zeta |^2$ is SH off zero, since its logarithm is SH there. Its SH removal has a finite radial limit in every direction by Taylor's formula. Every radial segment is nonthin at zero, so all these limits equal its value at zero. The quadratic Taylor polynomial is therefore constant on the unit circle; its pure terms vanish. Apply this on every complex line and polarize. For any holomorphic curve $F$ with $F(0)=a$, $F'(0)=\xi $, it follows that

\[ \lim _{\zeta \to 0}\frac{v(F(\zeta ))}{|\zeta |^2} =\mathcal L_ v(a;\xi ). \]

Apply this to $v$ and $E$, using their pointwise order, for the Levi bound.

On any submanifold in the hypothesis, $v/E$ is SH off $a$: its logarithm is the restriction of $h-2g_ D$, a subharmonic function there, and exponentiation preserves subharmonicity. In a local parametrization $F$ at $a$, $F'(0)\ne 0$. The outer-ball Green lower bound gives $E(F(\zeta ))\geq c|\zeta |^2$ near zero. Equality of the two Levi forms and the curve limits thus give $v/E\to 1$ at $a$. The bounded SH removal of this quotient has value one there and is at most one everywhere. The maximum principle makes it identically one on that connected submanifold. The leaf hypothesis covers all points.

In dimension one $g_ D(z,a)-\log |z-a|$ is bounded and harmonic on a punctured neighborhood of $a$, so extends harmonically. Consequently $E=|z-a|^2\exp (2H(z))$ is smooth there. The same quotient is SH on $D\setminus \{ a\} $, which is connected. If it attains one there, the maximum principle gives the assertion, including $a$ by continuity. $\square$

The equality at one nonpole point does not suffice in higher dimension: on the unit ball in $\mathbb C^2$, $v=|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2/2$ has $\log v$ PSH, whereas $E=|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2$. They agree along the first coordinate axis and differ elsewhere.

Proposition 9.17.2.6BCDD3D7DD27 The function

\[ q(\zeta ,\eta )=\operatorname {atanh}|\phi _\eta (\zeta )| \]

is a distance on $\Delta $, invariant under its automorphisms, and contracts under every holomorphic self-map $f$ of $\Delta $. Moreover

\[ \frac{|f'(b)|}{1-|f(b)|^2}\leq \frac1{1-|b|^2}. \]

Equality in this derivative estimate, or in the distance estimate at a pair of distinct points, implies that $f$ is an automorphism.

Proof. The disc Green formula and contraction give $|\phi _{f(\eta )}(f(\zeta ))|\leq |\phi _\eta (\zeta )|$. For the triangle inequality use a disc automorphism to send the middle point to zero. If the other moduli are $r,s$, direct expansion gives

\[ |\phi _ w(z)|^2 =\frac{r^2+s^2-2rs\cos \theta }{1+r^2s^2-2rs\cos \theta } \leq \frac{(r+s)^2}{(1+rs)^2}. \]

Apply $\operatorname {atanh}$ and its addition formula. Symmetry, separation and automorphism invariance follow directly from $\phi $.

Normalize $b$ and $f(b)$ to zero with these automorphisms. The normalized map $F$ fixes zero and satisfies $|F(\zeta )|\leq |\zeta |$. The holomorphic extension of $F(\zeta )/\zeta $ is bounded by one; evaluation at zero proves the derivative bound after the chain rule. If its modulus is one at zero, or at a nonzero point, the maximum principle makes it a constant of modulus one. Undoing the normalization gives the asserted automorphism and both equality cases. $\square$

Proposition 9.17.3.75C57846C83D Any two points of a domain $D\subset \mathbb C^ n$ lie on the image of one holomorphic map $\Delta \to D$.

Proof. Choose a polygonal continuous path $\gamma :[0,1]\to D$ joining the points. Its vector Bernstein polynomials

\[ P_ m(t)=\sum _{j=0}^ m\gamma (j/m){m\choose j}t^ j(1-t)^{m-j} \]

have the exact endpoints and converge uniformly to $\gamma $ on $[0,1]$. Indeed, split the sum into $|j/m-t|<\delta $ and its complement. Uniform continuity bounds the first error by the modulus of continuity; the second is at most $2\sup |\gamma |/(4m\delta ^2)$ by the binomial variance bound $t(1-t)/m\leq 1/(4m)$ and Chebyshev's inequality. The image of $\gamma $ has a positive distance from the complement of $D$. Thus for large $m$, $P=P_ m$ maps $[0,1]$ into $D$. By compactness and continuity there is $\epsilon >0$ such that $P$ maps the rectangle $-\epsilon <\operatorname {Re}t<1+\epsilon $, $|\operatorname {Im}t|<\epsilon $ into $D$.

For $r<1$ close to one set

\[ H_ r(\zeta )=\tfrac 12+c_ r\operatorname {atanh}(r\zeta ),\qquad c_ r=\frac{1/2+\epsilon /2}{\operatorname {atanh}r}. \]

On $\Delta $, $|\operatorname {Re}\operatorname {atanh}(r\zeta )| <\operatorname {atanh}r$ and $|\operatorname {Im}\operatorname {atanh}(r\zeta )|<\pi /4$. For $r$ sufficiently close to one, $c_ r\pi /4<\epsilon $, so $H_ r$ maps into that rectangle. Its values at two interior real points are zero and one, since $c_ r\operatorname {atanh}r>1/2$. The map $P\circ H_ r$ has the required image points. $\square$

For an arbitrary domain $D$, define

\[ c_ D(z,w)=\sup _{f\in \mathcal O(D,\Delta )}q(f(z),f(w)),\qquad \delta _ D(z,w)=\inf _{F(\zeta )=z,\ F(\eta )=w}q(\zeta ,\eta ), \]

where $F\in \mathcal O(\Delta ,D)$. Define $k_ D$ as the infimum of $\sum _ j\delta _ D(a_ j,a_{j+1})$ over finite chains from $z$ to $w$. A Schwarz–Pick system means pseudodistances on domains contracting holomorphic maps and equal to $q$ on $\Delta $.

Proposition 9.17.4.F0CC92394671 These quantities are finite. The families $c_ D,k_ D$ are Schwarz–Pick systems, and every such system $s_ D$ satisfies

\[ c_ D\leq s_ D\leq k_ D\leq \delta _ D. \]

Furthermore

\[ \log \tanh c_ D(z,w)\leq g_ D(z,w) \leq \log \tanh \delta _ D(z,w). \]

If $\log \tanh \delta _ D(\cdot ,a)$ is PSH, or identically minus infinity, it equals $g_ D(\cdot ,a)$.

Proof. The preceding proposition gives finiteness of $\delta _ D$. Its symmetry, zero diagonal and contraction follow by reversing endpoints and composing discs. The supremum defining $c_ D$ is symmetric, has zero diagonal and satisfies the triangle inequality term by term. Composition gives its contraction. Schwarz–Pick on the disc gives $c_ D\leq \delta _ D$. Concatenating chains proves the triangle inequality for $k_ D$; reversing chains and composing their discs prove symmetry and contraction. On the disc, Schwarz–Pick and the identity disc give $c_\Delta = \delta _\Delta =q$, and its triangle inequality gives $k_\Delta =q$.

For any system $s$, contraction by maps $D\to \Delta $ gives $c_ D\leq s_ D$. Contraction by each disc gives $s_ D\leq \delta _ D$; apply the triangle inequality along every chain to obtain $s_ D\leq k_ D$. This also proves all finiteness assertions. Green contraction by maps $D\to \Delta $ and $\Delta \to D$, followed respectively by a supremum and an infimum, gives the two Green bounds, with $\log 0=-\infty $.

For the final statement, fix $B(a,r)\subset D$. A centered affine line disc gives, for $|z-a|<r$,

\[ \delta _ D(z,a)\leq \operatorname {atanh}(|z-a|/r). \]

Thus the asserted PSH function has an upper logarithmic pole. It is negative, since $\delta _ D$ is finite, and hence is a Green competitor. Combine with the opposite bound. If it is identically minus infinity, the same upper bound on $g_ D$ proves the identity immediately. $\square$

There is a useful conditional form of the convex-domain conclusion. If for a pair $z,w$ there are holomorphic maps $F:\Delta \to D$ and $p:D\to \Delta $ with $p\circ F$ the identity and $z=F(\zeta )$, $w=F(\eta )$, then the two contractions give

\[ c_ D(z,w)=k_ D(z,w)=\delta _ D(z,w)=q(\zeta ,\eta ),\qquad g_ D(z,w)=\log \tanh q(\zeta ,\eta ). \]

Indeed $p$ gives the lower bound and $F$ gives the upper bound for each quantity. This elementary implication does not establish existence of such complex geodesics on every convex domain.

Proposition 9.17.5.32C365095F79 Put

\[ L_ D(z,w)=\operatorname {atanh} \exp \bigl (\max \{ g_ D(z,w),g_ D(w,z)\} \bigr ) \]

and let $\sigma _ D$ be the infimum of $\sum _ jL_ D(a_ j,a_{j+1})$ over finite chains from $z$ to $w$. This is a Schwarz–Pick system and

\[ c_ D\leq \sigma _ D\leq k_ D. \]

On every bounded domain $\sigma _ D$ separates points.

Proof. The Green bounds and symmetry of $c_ D,\delta _ D$ give $c_ D\leq L_ D\leq \delta _ D$. Therefore these links are finite, symmetric and zero on the diagonal. Green contraction gives their contraction. Chain concatenation, reversal and composition prove the pseudodistance axioms and contraction of $\sigma _ D$. The triangle inequality for $c_ D$ gives $c_ D\leq \sigma _ D$, while link comparison gives $\sigma _ D\leq k_ D$. On the disc both bounds equal $q$, proving the required normalization. On bounded $D$, a suitably scaled coordinate function maps to $\Delta $ and separates any prescribed distinct pair. Its positive Poincare distance gives $c_ D(z,w)>0$, hence separation for $\sigma _ D$. $\square$
