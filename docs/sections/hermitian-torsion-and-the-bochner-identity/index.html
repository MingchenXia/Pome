11.48 Hermitian torsion and the Bochner identityC9B4C5E646B1

11.48.1 Hermitian torsion in the Bochner identity527E14D109BB

Let $(X,\omega )$ be a smooth Hermitian manifold of complex dimension $n\geq 1$, and $(E,h)$ a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle of rank $r\geq 1$. Use the Chern operators $D',D''$ and their formal adjoints $\delta ',\delta ''$, with $\mathrm dV=\omega ^ n/n!$ and inner products linear in the first entry. Set $L=\omega \wedge $, $\Lambda =L^*$, and

\[ \Delta '=[D',\delta '],\quad \Delta ''=[D'',\delta ''],\quad D=D'+D'',\quad \delta =\delta '+\delta ''. \]

For operators of total degrees $a,b$ the bracket means $[P,Q]=PQ-(-1)^{ab}QP$. In particular, brackets between odd operators are anticommutators. A scalar form in a bracket denotes left exterior multiplication, tensored with $I_ E$. Define

494F31975749 11.48.2
\begin{equation} a=\partial \omega \wedge ,\quad \bar a=\bar\partial \omega \wedge , \qquad \tau =[\Lambda ,a],\quad \bar\tau =[\Lambda ,\bar a]. \end{equation}

These are order-zero operators; $\tau $ and $\bar\tau $ have types $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ respectively. A bar on $\tau $ designates this scalar conjugate operator on the exterior factor, and does not conjugate the bundle $E$. Superscript $*$ denotes the pointwise adjoint for order-zero operators and the formal adjoint for differential operators. We use the unscaled Chern curvature $\Theta $; for a line frame of squared norm $e^{-\phi }$, $\mathrm i\Theta =\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial \phi =2\pi dd^ c\phi $, where $dd^ c=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial /(2\pi )$.

Theorem 11.48.3.9C30D850C771 In every bidegree and every bundle rank,

8C926C4A6B4A
\begin{align} [\delta '',L]& =\mathrm i(D'+\tau ),& [\delta ',L]& =-\mathrm i(D''+\bar\tau ), \tag{11.48.4}\\ [\Lambda ,D'']& =-\mathrm i(\delta '+\tau ^*),& [\Lambda ,D']& =\mathrm i(\delta ”+\bar\tau ^*). \tag{11.48.5} \end{align}

Proof. We first prove the scalar identities, retaining the first jet of the metric. At a given point choose holomorphic coordinates in which $\omega (0)=\omega _0=\mathrm i\sum _ jdz_ j\wedge d\bar z_ j$ and write $\gamma =\omega -\omega _0$, retaining its linear term. Put $L_0=\omega _0\wedge $ and $\Lambda _0=L_0^*$. On forms of each degree let $M$ be the smooth positive matrix defined by

\[ \langle u,v\rangle _\omega \, \mathrm dV_\omega =\langle Mu,v\rangle _0\, \mathrm dV_0, \qquad \mathrm dV_0=\omega _0^ n/n!. \]

Its first variation is

34BA424819E3 11.48.6
\begin{equation} M=I-[\gamma ,\Lambda _0]+O(|z|^2). \end{equation}

Indeed, diagonalize $\gamma $ at a point with respect to $\omega _0$, with eigenvalues $\gamma _ j$, so the eigenvalues of $\omega $ are $1+\gamma _ j$. On the coefficient $dz_ I\wedge d\bar z_ J$ the metric-volume multiplier is

\[ \frac{\prod _ j(1+\gamma _ j)}{\prod _{i\in I}(1+\gamma _ i)\prod _{j\in J}(1+\gamma _ j)} =1+\sum _ j\gamma _ j-\sum _{i\in I}\gamma _ i-\sum _{j\in J}\gamma _ j +O(|\gamma |^2). \]

The creation/contraction formula in the curvature operator on $(n,1)$-forms identifies its linear term with $-[\gamma ,\Lambda _0]$. This is an identity for the first derivative of the smooth matrix-valued function of the metric; it requires no smooth choice of diagonalizing frames. Its remainder and first derivatives vanish at zero.

Write $\bar\delta _0$ for the flat scalar adjoint of $\bar\partial $. On the source and target form bundles, the exact adjoint formula is $\bar\partial ^*=M_{q-1}^{-1}\bar\delta _0M_ q$ for fixed $p$. Thus, as an operator on first jets at zero,

FA92E1C99B83 11.48.7
\begin{equation} \bar\partial ^* =\bar\delta _0-[\bar\delta _0,[\gamma ,\Lambda _0]] =\bar\delta _0+[\Lambda _0,C],\qquad C=[\bar\delta _0,\gamma ]. \end{equation}

For the last equality use $[\bar\delta _0,\Lambda _0]=0$ and expand the three products. The flat scalar Kähler identities in the line-bundle Bochner–Kodaira identity give $[L_0,\bar\delta _0]=-\mathrm i\partial $. The operator $C$ has degree one. Since $[L_0,\gamma ]=0$, the graded Jacobi identity gives

\[ [L_0,C]=[-\mathrm i\partial ,\gamma ] =-\mathrm i\partial \gamma \wedge , \qquad [C,L_0]=\mathrm i\partial \gamma \wedge . \]

Also $[L_0,\Lambda _0]$ acts on degree $k$ by $k-n$, so $[[L_0,\Lambda _0],C]=C$. Therefore

\[ [L_0,[\Lambda _0,C]] =C-\mathrm i[\Lambda _0,\partial \gamma \wedge ]. \]

Combining this with (11.48.7) and $L=L_0+\gamma $ gives, at zero,

\[ [L,\bar\partial ^*] =-\mathrm i\partial +C-\mathrm i[\Lambda _0,\partial \gamma \wedge ]-C =-\mathrm i(\partial +\tau ). \]

Terms containing two factors of $\gamma $, with at most one derivative, vanish at zero. This accounts also for $[\gamma ,[\Lambda _0,C]]$ in this expansion. The point is arbitrary, proving the first scalar identity. Complex conjugation, for which $L,\Lambda $ and the metric-volume pairing are real, gives the second. Taking formal adjoints gives the last two.

To pass to $E$, choose a holomorphic Chern-normal frame at the point. Its metric matrix is $H=I+O(|z|^2)$ and its connection matrix $H^{-1}\partial H$ vanishes there. This frame is obtained by the constant and holomorphic linear changes proved in theorem 10.33.2; that frame construction uses no derivative of the base metric. The bundle operators and their adjoints at the point are the componentwise scalar operators above, plus zeroth-order terms vanishing at that point. Their commutators with $L$ and $\Lambda $ consequently have exactly the same values. This proves all four identities in arbitrary rank. $\square$

For an explicit coefficient convention, in an $\omega $-unitary coframe write $a$ for the three-form $\partial \omega $ and use $\iota _ j,\bar\iota _ j$ for the contractions adjoint to multiplication by $\theta _ j,\bar\theta _ j$. Since $\Lambda =-\mathrm i\sum _ j\bar\iota _ j\iota _ j$, the operator in (11.48.2) is

863809BFB0AF 11.48.8
\begin{equation} \tau =-\mathrm i\sum _ j\left( (\bar\iota _ j\iota _ ja)\wedge +(\iota _ ja)\wedge \bar\iota _ j -(\bar\iota _ ja)\wedge \iota _ j\right). \end{equation}

To verify the signs, use $\iota _ j(a\wedge u)=(\iota _ ja)\wedge u-a\wedge \iota _ ju$ and apply $\bar\iota _ j$ once more. The remaining term $a\wedge \bar\iota _ j\iota _ ju$ cancels in the commutator. This also displays the order-zero and type assertions directly.

Lemma 11.48.9.A9F492A7F5C3 With the preceding conventions,

CE861F2B34DA
\begin{align} [L,\tau ]& =3a,& [\Lambda ,\tau ]& =-2\mathrm i\bar\tau ^*, \tag{11.48.10}\\ [D',\bar\tau ^*]& =-[D',\delta '']=[\tau ,\delta ''], \tag{11.48.11}\\ -[D'',\bar\tau ^*]& =[\tau ,\delta '+\tau ^*]+T_\omega , \tag{11.48.12} \end{align}

where the type-$(0,0)$ order-zero operator is

DBCAF8FF0340 11.48.13
\begin{equation} T_\omega = \left[\Lambda ,\left[\Lambda , \frac{\mathrm i}{2}\partial \bar\partial \omega \wedge \right]\right]-[a,a^*]. \end{equation}

In particular $[a,a^*]=aa^*+a^*a$.

Proof. The bracket $[L,a]$ is zero, and $a$ has degree three. Hence

\[ [L,\tau ]=[[L,\Lambda ],a]=3a. \]

By (11.48.4), $\tau =-\mathrm i[\delta '',L]-D'$. Since $[D'',L]=\bar a$ and $[\Lambda ,\delta '']=[D'',L]^*=\bar a^*$, Jacobi gives

\[ [\Lambda ,[\delta '',L]] =[[\Lambda ,\delta ''],L]+[\delta '',[\Lambda ,L]] =[\bar a^*,L]-\delta ''=\bar\tau ^*-\delta ''. \]

Here $[\delta '',[\Lambda ,L]]=-\delta ''$ follows by applying $[\Lambda ,L]=n-k$ in the input and output degrees. Substitute the contraction identity for $[\Lambda ,D']$ to obtain $[\Lambda ,\tau ]=-2\mathrm i\bar\tau ^*$.

The Chern identities $(D')^2=(D'')^2=0$ were proved from $A=H^{-1}\partial H$ in equation 10.33.1; they do not use the Kähler condition. Expanding products therefore gives $[D',[\Lambda ,D']]=0$. The contraction identity yields $[D',\delta ''+\bar\tau ^*]=0$. Likewise $(\delta '')^2=0$ implies $[\delta '',[\delta '',L]]=0$, and the wedge identity yields $[D'+\tau ,\delta '']=0$. These two equalities prove (11.48.11).

For the last identity put $c=\partial \bar\partial \omega \wedge $, of degree four, and $A=[D'',\Lambda ]=\mathrm i(\delta '+\tau ^*)$, of degree minus one. Since $[D',L]=a$ and $[\tau ,L]=-3a$, we have

93EA79ED3332 11.48.14
\begin{equation} [\Lambda ,A]=\mathrm i[D'+\tau ,L]^*=-2\mathrm i a^*. \end{equation}

The scalar evaluation Leibniz rule gives $[D'',a]=-c$. All required Jacobi steps can now be displayed with their signs:

\begin{align*} [D'',\tau ]& =-[\Lambda ,c]+[a,A],\\ [D'',[\Lambda ,\tau ]] & =[\tau ,A]-[\Lambda ,[\Lambda ,c]]+[\Lambda ,[a,A]],\\ [\Lambda ,[a,A]] & =[\tau ,A]+[a,[\Lambda ,A]] =[\tau ,A]-2\mathrm i[a,a^*]. \end{align*}

It follows that

\[ [D'',[\Lambda ,\tau ]] =-[\Lambda ,[\Lambda ,c]]+2[\tau ,A]-2\mathrm i[a,a^*] =2\mathrm i\bigl (T_\omega +[\tau ,\delta '+\tau ^*]\bigr ). \]

Finally $\bar\tau ^*=(\mathrm i/2)[\Lambda ,\tau ]$ by (11.48.10). Multiplying the last display by $\mathrm i/2$ proves (11.48.12). Each step is valid also at endpoint bidegrees, with the corresponding out-of-range operators zero. $\square$

Theorem 11.48.15.E96E372480D4 Define

\[ \Delta '_\tau =[D'+\tau ,\delta '+\tau ^*],\qquad \Delta _\tau =[D+\tau ,\delta +\tau ^*]. \]

These are nonnegative formally self-adjoint second-order operators with the same principal parts as $\Delta '$ and $[D,\delta ]$ respectively. On every smooth bundle-valued bidegree,

2F5B471E480A
\begin{align} \Delta ''& =\Delta ' +[\mathrm i\Theta ,\Lambda ]+[D',\tau ^*]-[D'',\bar\tau ^*], \tag{11.48.16}\\ \Delta ''& =\Delta '_\tau +[\mathrm i\Theta ,\Lambda ]+T_\omega , \tag{11.48.17}\\ \Delta _\tau & =\Delta '_\tau +\Delta ”. \tag{11.48.18} \end{align}

The operator $T_\omega $ is pointwise Hermitian and depends only on the first and second derivatives of the base metric, through $\partial \omega $ and $\partial \bar\partial \omega $ and their metric contractions. For every compactly supported smooth form $u$,

9CAE0D2A2536 11.48.19
\begin{equation} \| D''u\| ^2+\| \delta ''u\| ^2 =\| (D'+\tau )u\| ^2+\| (\delta '+\tau ^*)u\| ^2 +\int _ X\langle ([\mathrm i\Theta ,\Lambda ]+T_\omega )u,u\rangle \, \mathrm dV. \end{equation}

Proof. By the contraction identity, $\delta ''=-\mathrm i[\Lambda ,D']-\bar\tau ^*$. The Chern curvature relation $[D',D'']=\Theta \wedge $ and Jacobi give

\[ [D'',[\Lambda ,D']] =[\Lambda ,\Theta ]+\mathrm i[D',\delta '+\tau ^*]. \]

Multiply by $-\mathrm i$ and subtract $[D'',\bar\tau ^*]$ to obtain (11.48.16). Substitution of (11.48.12) combines its remaining three terms into

\[ \Delta '+[D',\tau ^*]+[\tau ,\delta ']+[\tau ,\tau ^*]+T_\omega =\Delta '_\tau +T_\omega , \]

proving (11.48.17). The mixed identity gives $[D'+\tau ,\delta '']=0$, and its formal adjoint is $[D'',\delta '+\tau ^*]=0$. Expanding $\Delta _\tau $ proves (11.48.18).

For either odd operator $Q=D'+\tau $ or $Q=D+\tau $, the graded bracket $[Q,Q^*]=QQ^*+Q^*Q$ has quadratic form $\| Qu\| ^2+\| Q^*u\| ^2$ and is formally self-adjoint. Adding the order-zero $\tau $ does not change the second-order principal part. Integrating (11.48.17) now gives the energy identity.

The pointwise curvature computation in proposition 10.33.6 uses only a unitary exterior coframe and the Chern curvature symmetry, so applies to the present Hermitian metric as well. In that coframe write $\mathrm i\Theta =\mathrm i\sum R_{j\bar k}\theta _ j\wedge \bar\theta _ k$. Write $\epsilon _ j,\bar\epsilon _ j$ for multiplication by $\theta _ j,\bar\theta _ j$. In a Chern-normal frame $R_{j\bar k}=-\partial _ j\partial _{\bar k}H$, so $R_{j\bar k}^*=R_{k\bar j}$, and that computation gives

\[ [\mathrm i\Theta ,\Lambda ] =\sum _{j,k}R_{j\bar k} (\epsilon _ j\iota _ k+\bar\epsilon _ k\bar\iota _ j-\delta _{jk}). \]

Taking its adjoint and exchanging $j,k$ proves it is Hermitian in every bidegree. The difference of the three formally self-adjoint operators $\Delta ''$, $\Delta '_\tau $ and this curvature operator is $T_\omega $. Since $T_\omega $ has order zero, formal self-adjointness implies pointwise Hermitian symmetry: testing its skew-adjoint part against arbitrary compactly supported coefficient vectors forces that smooth matrix to vanish at each point. The explicit formula (11.48.13) proves the claimed dependence on the base metric. $\square$

When $d\omega =0$, both torsion operators and $T_\omega $ vanish. The identities then give the Kähler formulas of theorem 10.33.2.

Corollary 11.48.20.222BB53B613A Suppose $X$ is compact and, in a fixed bidegree $(p,q)$, $[\mathrm i\Theta ,\Lambda ]+T_\omega $ is positive definite at every point. Then the smooth analytic Dolbeault group $H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X,E)$ vanishes. For $q\geq 1$ there is a constant $\lambda >0$ such that each smooth $D''$-closed $f$ in that bidegree has a unique smooth primitive $u$ of smallest $L^2$ norm, with

\[ D''u=f,\qquad \delta ''u=0,\qquad \| u\| ^2\leq \lambda ^{-1}\| f\| ^2. \]

Here $\lambda $ can be any uniform positive lower bound for the displayed curvature-plus-torsion operator. For $q=0$, every $D''$-closed form in that bidegree is zero.

Proof. Compactness of the unit sphere bundle gives such a $\lambda $. The energy identity gives $\lambda \| v\| ^2\leq \| D''v\| ^2+\| \delta ''v\| ^2$. Harmonic forms are zero, so theorem 10.46.6 gives the cohomology conclusion. Its smooth Green operator $G$ satisfies $\Delta ''G=I$ in the selected degree. For closed $f$, the differential commutation in theorem 10.46.4 gives $D''Gf=0$. Hence $u=\delta ''Gf$ is a smooth primitive with $\delta ''u=0$. Writing $w=Gf$, the energy identity and Cauchy–Schwarz give

\[ \lambda \| w\| ^2\leq \langle f,w\rangle \leq \| f\| \| w\| ,\qquad \| u\| ^2=\langle f,Gf\rangle \leq \lambda ^{-1}\| f\| ^2. \]

It is orthogonal to every $D''$-closed form in its input degree, by integration by parts. All other primitives differ by such a form, so it is the unique smallest-norm primitive. If $q=0$, the adjoint term has zero target and the coercive estimate directly proves the last assertion. $\square$

Corollary 11.48.21.5029210C26BC Suppose that $X$ is compact, $\partial \bar\partial \omega =0$, and put $N=n\binom n2$. If in bidegree $(p,q)$

\[ [\mathrm i\Theta ,\Lambda ]\geq bI, \qquad b>2N\sup _ X|\partial \omega |_\omega ^2, \]

then the preceding vanishing and smooth solvability conclusions hold. The metric $\omega $ is allowed to have $\partial \omega \ne 0$.

Proof. Here $T_\omega =-(aa^*+a^*a)$. A unitary exterior expansion of $\partial \omega $ has $N$ possible coefficients. Each exterior monomial has multiplication operator norm at most one on the full exterior algebra. The triangle and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities give $\| a\| _{\mathrm{op}}\leq \sqrt N|\partial \omega |_\omega $; the adjoint has the same full-algebra operator norm. Thus

\[ \langle T_\omega v,v\rangle =-\| av\| ^2-\| a^*v\| ^2 \geq -2N|\partial \omega |_\omega ^2|v|^2. \]

The assumed strict bound gives the positive uniform constant $\lambda =b-2N\sup _ X|\partial \omega |_\omega ^2$. Apply corollary 11.48.20. For $n=1$ the three-form is zero and $N=0$, with the same conclusion. $\square$
