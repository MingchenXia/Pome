9.50 Bundle cohomology on strongly convex exhaustionsA981C1EBB07E

A981C1EBB07E

Manifolds are Hausdorff, second countable and without boundary. Curvature is unscaled, $\mathcal H(f)=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial f$ and $\mathrm i\Theta (L)=2\pi c_1(L)$. Adjoints and volume are those of the indicated smooth Hermitian metrics. An exhaustion has compact closed sublevels; a finite continuous exhaustion is bounded below (restrict to any one nonempty compact sublevel), so may be shifted to be nonnegative. Strong $q$-convexity means at least $n-q+1$ positive Levi eigenvalues, for an integer $q\geq 1$.

Lemma 9.50.1.8646B8C271A2 Let $\psi $ be smooth strongly $q$-convex on an $n$-dimensional complex manifold, with $1\leq q\leq n$. For every $\varepsilon >0$ there is a smooth Hermitian metric $\omega $ such that the increasingly ordered eigenvalues of $\mathcal H(\psi )$ satisfy

\[ \gamma _1\geq -\varepsilon ,\qquad \gamma _ q=\cdots =\gamma _ n=1. \]

If $\psi $ is an exhaustion, a further smooth conformal change gives a complete Hermitian metric $\widehat\omega =f\omega $, with $f=1+|d\psi |_\omega ^2\geq 1$. Relative to it the sum of any $k$ Levi eigenvalues is at least

6CB1E8CBE35D 9.50.2
\begin{equation} f^{-1}\bigl (k-q+1-(q-1)\varepsilon \bigr ) \qquad (q\leq k\leq n). \end{equation}

Here the differential norm uses the associated real Riemannian metric.

Proof. Choose any smooth Hermitian metric $\omega _0$ and let $A_0$ be its self-adjoint endomorphism defining $\mathcal H(\psi )$. The ordered eigenvalues $\gamma _ j^0$ are continuous by lemma 10.49.2, and $\gamma _ q^0>0$. Choose a locally finite partition with supports where $\gamma _ q^0$ has a positive lower bound $a_ j$. The smooth positive function $\eta =\sum _ j\theta _ ja_ j/2$ satisfies $0<\eta \leq \gamma _ q^0$. No global positive minimum is required. Take a smooth nonincreasing cutoff $0\leq \beta \leq 1$ equal to one for $t\leq 1/3$ and zero for $t\geq 2/3$, and put

\[ a(t)=(1+\varepsilon ^{-1})\sqrt{1+t^2},\qquad F(t)=\beta (t)a(t)+(1-\beta (t))t. \]

It is smooth positive, satisfies $F(t)\geq |t|/\varepsilon $ for $t\leq 0$ and $F(t)\geq t$ for $t\geq 0$, and equals $t$ for $t\geq 1$. The exact smooth functional calculus of lemma 10.49.2 makes $A=\eta F[A_0/\eta ]$ a smooth positive endomorphism. Give the base the metric $\omega $ obtained by inserting $A$ into $\omega _0$. At a point its new Levi eigenvalues are

\[ \frac{\gamma _ j^0}{\eta F(\gamma _ j^0/\eta )}. \]

Each lies in $[-\varepsilon ,1]$ and each labelled $j\geq q$ equals one. Hence the increasingly ordered last $n-q+1$ eigenvalues are one, irrespective of any ordering of the other fractions.

When $\psi $ is exhaustive, the conformal metric in the statement satisfies $|d\psi |_{\widehat\omega }^2\leq 1$. It is complete: a Cauchy sequence has bounded $\psi $ by this Lipschitz estimate, lies in a compact sublevel, and has a convergent subsequence; smooth positive local metric comparison identifies its topology with the manifold topology, so Cauchyness gives convergence. This works on each component. Conformal change multiplies every eigenvalue by $f^{-1}$, and any $k$ indices include at least $k-q+1$ of the ones, proving (9.50.2). $\square$

Lemma 9.50.3.E85E2E5CB1FF Let $(M,\omega )$ be complete Hermitian and $(G,h)$ a smooth Hermitian holomorphic bundle. In a fixed bidegree $(p,k)$ with $0\leq p\leq n$ and $1\leq k\leq n$, suppose

\[ A=[\mathrm i\Theta (G,h),\Lambda _\omega ]+T_\omega \geq I, \]

where $T_\omega $ is the exterior-factor zero-order remainder in theorem 11.48.15. Every distributionally closed $g\in L^2_{p,k}(M,G)$ has a solution $D''u=g$ with $\| u\| ^2\leq \| g\| ^2$. Its unique minimum-norm solution satisfies $\delta ''u=0$ and is smooth when $g$ is smooth. Moreover a smooth form satisfying $D''u=\delta ''u=0$ on a fixed open set obeys, for compact $K\Subset W$ in that set,

09CCAA35AAF1 9.50.4
\begin{equation} \| u\| _{C^ r(K)}\leq C_{r,K,W}\| u\| _{L^2(W)} \qquad (r\geq 0), \end{equation}

using finitely many fixed local frames. No Kähler assumption is needed.

Proof. The exact complete-Hermitian domain lemma 6.26.4 supplies the closed maximal $D''$ operators, their maximal formal-adjoint domains and joint compactly supported smooth graph density in every degree/rank. Its assumptions apply to this smooth $h$, including when it contains an arbitrary smooth scalar weight: the cutoff errors are wedge and contraction by $\bar\partial \chi _ R$, whose norms depend only on the complete base metric, not derivatives of that weight. The local smooth-matrix commutator uses only bounds on each compact chart. Thus no global bound on weight derivatives is an extra premise.

For compactly supported smooth $v$, the complete Hermitian energy identity theorem 11.48.15 gives

\[ \| v\| ^2\leq \int \langle Av,v\rangle \leq \| D''v\| ^2+\| \delta ''v\| ^2. \]

Pass the first and last terms to the joint graph limit. With $T=D''$ from $(p,k-1)$ and $S=D''$ from $(p,k)$, this proves

\[ |\langle g,v\rangle |^2\leq \| g\| ^2 (\| T^*v\| ^2+\| Sv\| ^2) \]

on the full joint domains. For $k=n$, $S=0$ into the zero space. The equality $(D'')^2=0$ gives $ST=0$ on the maximal domains. The checked closed Hilbert-complex theorem Hilbert-complex solvability from the dual energy estimate gives the solution and bound. The affine solution space has the closed direction $\ker T$, so has a unique minimum orthogonal to that kernel. The preceding $D''$ range lies in this kernel; testing its adjoint shows $\delta ''u=0$. In antiholomorphic degree zero this is automatic.

We verify regularity and the quantitative local assertion. At a real covector $\xi $, the principal symbols of $D''$ and $\delta ''$ are exterior multiplication by $\xi ^{0,1}$ and its contraction. Their anticommutator is $|\xi |_\omega ^2I/2$, so the principal symbol of $\Delta ''$ is real scalar and positive, for any smooth Hermitian base and bundle metric. In coordinates its divergence principal form is $\tfrac 12\int g^{ij}H_{AB}\partial _ jv_ A \overline{\partial _ iv_ B}\, dV$. Integration by parts and absorption of the lower-order terms give, on compactly supported smooth vectors,

\[ \| v\| _{H^1}^2\leq C(\| D''v\| ^2+\| \delta ''v\| ^2+\| v\| ^2). \]

Localize a weak solution by a chart cutoff. Smooth-matrix commutator approximation and this bound give a bounded $H^1$ approximation; weak derivative convergence puts it in $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$. The equation $\Delta ''u=\delta ''g$ has smooth right-hand side. The complete finite-vector divergence regularity theorem interior regularity for elliptic systems in divergence form, followed by Sobolev spaces, duality and local embedding, yields smoothness. For the last assertion, on a slightly larger compact neighborhood the cutoff product rules and $D''u=\delta ''u=0$ bound the initial $H^1$ norm by $C\| u\| _{L^2(W)}$. The quantitative interior estimates for that same divergence system then bound every higher Sobolev norm on successively smaller neighborhoods by the same $L^2$ norm; its right-hand side is zero. The proved local Sobolev embedding gives (9.50.4). All metrics and coefficients in this last bound are fixed. $\square$

Theorem 9.50.5.8AB8E8F1EBBE Let $M$ be strongly $q$-complete and $E$ a holomorphic vector bundle, where $q\geq 1$. Then

\[ H^ k(M,\mathcal O(E))=0\qquad (k\geq q). \]

Explicitly, every smooth closed $E$-valued $(0,k)$-form in this range has a smooth primitive. The statement allows non-Kähler manifolds and does not impose curvature positivity on $E$.

Proof. The empty manifold, zero bundle, dimension zero and $q>n$ give no nonzero Dolbeault terms in the specified range. Otherwise take a smooth strongly $q$-convex exhaustion $\psi \geq 0$ and use lemma 9.50.1 with $\varepsilon =1/q$. Fix the resulting complete metric $\widehat\omega =f\omega $ once and for all. The pointwise line-curvature coefficient formula in proposition 10.33.6 and (9.50.2) give, on $(n,k)$ for $k\geq q$,

894DFD0DF9E2 9.50.6
\begin{equation} [\mathcal H(\psi ),\Lambda _{\widehat\omega }] \geq (qf)^{-1}I. \end{equation}

Give $E$ a fixed smooth metric, $K_ M$ its induced metric, and put $G=E\otimes K_ M^{-1}$. The fixed smooth Hermitian operators

\[ A_0^{(k)}=[\mathrm i\Theta (G),\Lambda _{\widehat\omega }] +T_{\widehat\omega }\otimes I_ G \quad (q\leq k\leq n) \]

have a continuous common norm bound $C(x)\geq 0$, the maximum of their finitely many pointwise norms. This bound is finite on every compact sublevel. All derivatives of the complete conformal metric that occur in $T_{\widehat\omega }$ are already fixed; no uniform bound at infinity or limiting conformal parameter is assumed. Multiplying the bundle norm by a scalar weight cancels in every endomorphism operator norm, so this same $C(x)$ is independent of it.

For a convex increasing smooth $\chi $ multiply the metric of $G$ by $e^{-\chi (\psi )}$. Tensor curvature and the chain rule give

\[ \mathrm i\Theta (G_\chi )=\mathrm i\Theta (G) +\chi '(\psi )\mathcal H(\psi )I_ G +\chi ''(\psi )\mathrm i\partial \psi \wedge \bar\partial \psi I_ G. \]

The last term is a semipositive scalar curvature, whose operator on $(n,k)$ is nonnegative. Thus its torsion-curvature operator satisfies

1D0C411FD4AC 9.50.7
\begin{equation} A_\chi ^{(k)}\geq \bigl (\chi '(\psi )/(qf)-C(x)\bigr )I. \end{equation}

The torsion term has not changed: it acts on the exterior factor and depends only on the fixed base metric, by theorem 11.48.15.

We give a single weight which makes this at least $I$ in all these degrees and makes any specified smooth datum $g$ square integrable. For that datum, after the canonical-form identification below, put

\[ a_ j=\int _{\{ j\leq \psi \leq j+1\} }|g|^2\, dV_{\widehat\omega }<\infty . \]

Choose nondecreasing $M_ j\geq 1$ larger than $q\sup _{\{ \psi \leq j+2\} } f(C+1)$ and than $\log (2^{j+2}(1+a_{j+1}))$. A supremum over the empty set imposes no condition. Let $\zeta $ be smooth nondecreasing, zero up to0 and one from1, and set on the real line

\[ b(t)=M_0+\sum _{j\geq 0}(M_{j+1}-M_ j)\zeta (t-j),\qquad \chi (t)=\int _0^ t b(s)\, ds. \]

These sums are locally finite. On $[j,j+1]$ one has $\chi '\geq M_ j$, so (9.50.7) is at least $I$. For $j\geq 1$, $\chi (j)\geq M_{j-1}\geq \log (2^{j+1}(1+a_ j))$, giving

\[ \int _ M |g|^2e^{-\chi (\psi )}\, dV_{\widehat\omega } \leq a_0+\sum _{j\geq 1}2^{-j-1}<\infty . \]

The integer bands may overlap on level sets, which only enlarges this upper bound. The construction works for compactly supported data with a weight chosen only from the geometry as well.

The canonical identification from $E$-valued $(0,j)$-forms to $G$-valued $(n,j)$-forms is

\[ I_ j(a\otimes v)=(a\wedge \tau )\otimes (v\otimes \tau ^{-1}), \]

where $\tau $ is a local nonzero holomorphic top form. Transition factors cancel, and appending it on the right makes $I$ commute with $D''$ without a degree sign. The induced canonical/dual metrics make it isometric. Apply lemma 9.50.3 to $I_ kg$ and the chosen weight; pull its smooth primitive back by $I_{k-1}$. This proves smooth analytic vanishing. The exact E-valued Dolbeault resolution comparison corollary 10.43.11 gives the sheaf statement. $\square$

Theorem 9.50.8.7B8A55FF470A Let $U$ be a $q$-Runge complete open subset of a strongly $q$-complete manifold $M$: for every compact $L\subset U$ some smooth strongly $q$-convex exhaustion $\psi $ of $M$ has $L\subset \{ \psi <b\} \Subset U$. For every holomorphic vector bundle $E$ and smooth closed $E$-valued $(0,q-1)$-form $h$ on $U$, there is a sequence of global smooth closed forms on $M$ converging to $h$ with all derivatives on every compact subset of $U$.

Proof. If $q>n+1$ the form space is zero. If $q=n+1$, all $(0,n)$-forms are closed; multiply $h$ by compact cutoffs on an exhaustion of $U$ and extend by zero to obtain the assertion. Dimension zero and empty $U$ are included. Thus assume $1\leq q\leq n$ and fix a nonempty compact $L\subset U$. Choose $\psi $ as in the hypothesis, shifted to be nonnegative. Compactness gives $b_0<b$ with $L\subset \{ \psi <b_0\} $. Choose a compactly supported smooth $\eta $ in $U$ equal to one on a neighborhood of $\overline{\{ \psi <b\} }$. Under the canonical complex map above, regard $h$ as a $G$-valued $(n,q-1)$-form and set $g=D''(\eta h)$. This extends by zero to a smooth closed global form; its support is compact and contained in $\{ \psi \geq b\} $.

Construct the fixed complete base metric and a geometric weight $\chi _0$ as in (9.50.7), so its operator is at least $I$ in degree $(n,q)$. Choose smooth convex nondecreasing $\theta $ with $\theta =0$ on $(-\infty ,b_0]$ and $\theta \geq 1$ on $[b,\infty )$. One can integrate a smooth nonnegative nondecreasing function flat up to $b_0$ and then scale its integral to reach one at $b$. For $\chi _\nu =\chi _0+\nu \theta $ the curvature-torsion operator remains at least $I$: both added terms are nonnegative on $(n,q)$ by (9.50.6). Let $u_\nu $ be the smooth minimum-norm solution of $D''u_\nu =g$ in this weighted metric. The exact complete-Hermitian estimate gives

\[ \int _ M|u_\nu |^2e^{-\chi _\nu (\psi )}\, dV \leq \int _ M|g|^2e^{-\chi _\nu (\psi )}\, dV \leq e^{-\nu }\int _ M|g|^2e^{-\chi _0(\psi )}\, dV. \]

The last integral is finite since $g$ has compact support. On $\{ \psi <b_0\} $ the weights and all their derivatives equal the fixed weight $\chi _0(\psi )$. There $D''u_\nu =0$ and the minimum-norm coclosedness gives $\delta ''_{\chi _0}u_\nu =0$. On each compact neighborhood $W$ of $L$ in that sublevel, the preceding estimate bounds its fixed weighted $L^2$ norm by $Ce^{-\nu /2}$. Apply (9.50.4) with this fixed base and bundle metric. For every $r$, the $C^ r(L)$ norm of $u_\nu $ tends to zero. Hence $\eta h-u_\nu $ is global closed and converges to $h$ with all derivatives on $L$.

Finally choose nested compact sets $L_ j$ with interiors exhausting $U$. For each $j$ repeat this construction with $L_ j$ and choose the parameter large enough that its error in derivatives of order at most $j$ on $L_ j$ is less than $2^{-j}$, using finitely many frames there. This sequence converges in the full smooth topology on $U$. Pull back by the canonical complex map to the stated types. $\square$
