5.56 Normal currents supported on a differentiable submanifoldB747E431041D

B747E431041D

All manifolds are Hausdorff and have a countable basis. For a current of dimension $p$ use the dimensional boundary

\[ (\partial T)(\eta )=T(\mathrm d\eta ),\qquad \partial T=0\quad (p=0). \]

On an oriented ambient manifold of dimension $m$, this is $\partial T=(-1)^{m-p+1}\mathrm dT$, with the derivative convention of section 5.53. Normality means that $T$ and $\partial T$ both have measure coefficients. Order-zero currents act on continuous compactly supported forms by their coefficient measures, as in the conventions for currents and positivity. In Euclidean coordinates we may equivalently write this action as

\[ T(\alpha )=\sum _{|I|=p}\int \alpha _ I\, \mathrm d\mu _ I, \qquad \alpha =\sum _{|I|=p}\alpha _ I\, \mathrm dx_ I. \]

This representation uses dimension rather than complementary form degree; the signs converting the two coefficient descriptions are the existing complementary-index signs.

Lemma 5.56.1.A6C399681838 If $T$ is normal on a smooth manifold and $h$ is a $C^1$ function, then $hT$ is normal and

05D16BBEFEE0 5.56.2
\begin{equation} \partial (hT)(\eta ) =(\partial T)(h\eta )-T(\mathrm dh\wedge \eta ). \end{equation}

If $F$ is a $C^1$ map between smooth manifolds and is proper on $\operatorname {Supp}T$, the formula

\[ (F_*T)(\alpha )=T(F^*\alpha ) \]

defines a normal current, preserves dimension, and satisfies $\partial (F_*T)=F_*(\partial T)$. Pullback in this formula is a continuous form, evaluated by the order-zero extension. The usual support inclusion and composition rule hold for these pushforwards.

Proof. Everything is local on compact sets. A continuous form supported in a fixed compact coordinate set has its pairing bounded by the sum of the total variations of the coefficient measures there, times its coefficient sup norm. Smooth forms are dense among continuous forms with compact support, after slightly enlarging the support, by the existing mollifier construction.

Approximate $h$ in $C^1$ on the compact set in question by smooth functions $h_ j$. The smooth product identity follows by expanding $\mathrm d(h_ j\eta )$. Its three pairings converge to those in (5.56.2), since $T$ and $\partial T$ have order zero and $h_ j$, $\mathrm dh_ j$ converge uniformly. The right side is order zero as a functional of $\eta $; thus $hT$ is normal. A partition of unity gives the statement in all charts.

For the map assertion, first suppose the source is a Euclidean open set, $T$ has compact support $L$, and $F$ takes a neighborhood of $L$ into one target chart. Mollifying the coordinate functions gives smooth $F_ j\to F$ in $C^1$ on a fixed compact neighborhood of $L$, with values in that chart. The approximants need not be injective. For every smooth target test form $\alpha $, both $F_ j^*\alpha \to F^*\alpha $ and $F_ j^*(\mathrm d\alpha )\to F^*(\mathrm d\alpha )$ uniformly on $L$: pullback uses only first derivatives of the maps. Insert one fixed source cutoff equal to one near $L$ in all pairings. The already proved smooth pushforward calculus gives

\[ (F_{j*}T)(\mathrm d\eta ) =(F_{j*}\partial T)(\eta ). \]

Order-zero continuity on both sides passes this equality to the limit. This proves boundary commutation without differentiating the generally nonsmooth form $F^*\eta $.

The resulting functionals have measure coefficients: in the displayed dimensional coefficient representation, their measures are finite sums of the pushforwards of $\mu _ I$ multiplied by the continuous minors of $DF$. Compactness of $L$ gives locally finite total variation. The same argument applies to $\partial T$. Thus $F_*T$ is normal, not merely a distributional limit.

For a general $T$ and $F$, fix the compact support $K$ of the target test. Properness on $\operatorname {Supp}T$ makes $\operatorname {Supp}T\cap F^{-1}(K)$ compact. Use a source cutoff equal to one near this compact set and compactly supported in a neighborhood covered by finitely many source charts mapping to target charts. The preceding compact argument applies to the finitely many localized currents. In their boundary formula the cutoff derivatives either sum to zero near that set or are supported away from the pullback test. They therefore contribute zero. This proves both independence of the cutoffs and the boundary identity globally. Here $\operatorname {Supp}\partial T\subset \operatorname {Supp}T$, directly from the distributional definition, so the same properness suffices for the boundary. Smooth chart partitions or additional target localization provide the stated finite reductions.

Tests supported outside $F(\operatorname {Supp}T)$ vanish; properness on the closed support makes its image closed. Finally the first-derivative chain rule gives $(G\circ F)^*\alpha =F^*G^*\alpha $ for continuous forms. The order-zero extensions and cutoffs then give composition whenever the relevant pushforwards are defined. $\square$

We specify what a normal current on a merely $C^1$ manifold means. A continuous differential form is intrinsic there: its chart transition rule uses only the continuous first derivatives. An order-zero dimensional current is a linear functional on continuous compactly supported forms, locally represented by finite families of complex Radon measures in these coordinates. Call it normal if the coordinate current in each chart has order-zero distributional boundary, computed against smooth Euclidean tests. It is enough to check one $C^1$ atlas. Indeed, on an overlap the coordinate change is a $C^1$ diffeomorphism, so lemma 5.56.1 transports both the current and its boundary by the same rule. The local boundaries agree as order-zero functionals on overlaps and hence glue. This defines an intrinsic boundary $\partial T$ and proves independence of the chosen atlas. No higher derivatives of a chart are used.

The lemma consequently holds for $C^1$ manifolds and maps as well. To see the global step explicitly, work in coordinate patches and localize by a finite $C^1$ partition near each relevant compact set. Such partitions are obtained by pulling back ordinary smooth coordinate bumps, summing them, and dividing by their positive sum. Formula (5.56.2) is coordinate invariant by the first-derivative chain rule; its extra terms cancel when the partition sums to one. The Euclidean proof then gives the asserted boundary commutation in every target chart. Gluing order-zero functionals uses a finite partition on the support of each test, just as in gluing of currents. If a manifold has a smooth structure this definition agrees exactly with its ordinary normal currents.

In particular, if the oriented source and target dimensions are $a$ and $b$, respectively, the dimensional boundary identity reads

\[ \mathrm d(F_*T)=(-1)^{a-b}F_*(\mathrm dT). \]

This retains the real pushforward parity rather than imposing complex even-dimensional conventions on the submanifold. A dimensional normal current on $Y$ itself requires no orientation of $Y$.

Theorem 5.56.3.D47CED9844B5 Let $M$ be a smooth real manifold, and let $Y\subset M$ be an embedded $C^1$ submanifold without boundary, of dimension $d$. Let $T$ be a normal current of dimension $p$ on $M$ with $\operatorname {Supp}T\subset Y$. There is a unique normal current $\Theta $ of dimension $p$ on $Y$ such that

\[ T=j_*\Theta ,\qquad j:Y\hookrightarrow M. \]

Its support is $j^{-1}(\operatorname {Supp}T)$, and $j$ is proper on this support even when $Y$ is not closed in $M$. If $p>d$, then $T=0$.

Proof. First note why the defining-function argument is legitimate at class $C^1$. If $h$ is $C^1$ and zero on $Y$, then $hT=0$ and $h\partial T=0$, since both sets of coefficient measures are supported in $Y$. The product formula gives

CF698A54DF16 5.56.4
\begin{equation} T(\mathrm dh\wedge \eta )=0. \end{equation}

It holds also for continuous compactly supported $\eta $, by order-zero continuity and smooth approximation. Thus these conormal equations do not assert that a $C^1$ defining function is a smooth test coefficient.

Work locally in smooth ambient coordinates $(s,t)$ in which $Y$ is the graph $t=f(s)$ of a $C^1$ map, with $s\in A\subset \mathbb R^ d$ and $t\in \mathbb R^ q$, $q=m-d$. After shrinking, the map

\[ F:A\times B\longrightarrow W,\qquad F(s,u)=(s,f(s)+u), \]

is a $C^1$ diffeomorphism onto an open neighborhood $W\subset M$ of this graph piece, and $Y\cap W=F(A\times \{ 0\} )$. The previous calculus makes $S=(F^{-1})_*(T|_ W)$ normal, with support in $A\times \{ 0\} $. Applying the conormal identity to the smooth coordinates $u_1,\ldots ,u_ q$ gives $S(\mathrm du_ j\wedge \eta )=0$ for every $j$.

Write the dimensional coefficient measures of $S$ in the coordinates $(s,u)$. If an increasing $p$-index contains any normal coordinate, its coefficient is zero: test its coefficient form, up to a sign, as $\mathrm du_ j\wedge \eta $. Hence only indices consisting of $s$-coordinates survive. All their measures are supported on $A\times \{ 0\} $. If $p>d$ there are no such indices, so $S=0$. For $p\leq d$, transport these measures to $A$ under the projection $P(s,u)=s$ and let $Q$ be the resulting current on $A$. In fact $Q=P_*S$, because pullback by $P$ selects precisely these tangential coefficients. This pushforward is defined: for compact $C\subset A$, its intersection with $\operatorname {Supp}S$ lies in the compact set $C\times \{ 0\} $ and is closed there. Thus $P$ is proper on $\operatorname {Supp}S$, and $Q$ is normal by the lemma.

For the inclusion $i:A\to A\times B$, $i(s)=(s,0)$, coefficient evaluation gives $S=i_*Q$. It also proves uniqueness, or one can apply $P_*$ and use $P\circ i=\mathrm{Id}$. In complementary form-degree notation the same calculation says that every coefficient term contains the full normal conormal wedge $\mathrm du_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm du_ q$, as in the hint. Transport $Q$ to $Y\cap W$ by the $C^1$ graph chart. The intrinsic definition makes it a normal current there, and composition gives its inclusion pushforward into $W$ equal to $T|_ W$.

The local currents on $Y$ agree on overlaps. For uniqueness there, flatten either graph chart and apply the injectivity just proved; equivalently, continuous tangent forms in that chart extend to continuous ambient forms by the projection and a normal cutoff. Equality on ambient smooth tests gives equality on continuous tests by order-zero density. The local currents and their boundaries therefore glue to a normal current $\Theta $ on $Y$. This gluing is valid without orienting $Y$.

The same local injection shows $\operatorname {Supp}\Theta =j^{-1}(\operatorname {Supp}T)$. The support of $T$ is closed in $M$. For any compact $K\subset M$, the set $K\cap \operatorname {Supp}T$ is compact and lies in $Y$; its inverse image under the subspace homeomorphism is compact in $Y$. This proves properness on the support, so $j_*\Theta $ is globally defined. The local identities give the global identity and uniqueness. If $p>d$ all the local currents vanish, proving the dimension assertion. $\square$

The normality assumption cannot be dropped, even on smooth submanifolds. The following examples also distinguish its two order-zero requirements. Work on $\mathbb R^2$ with coordinates $(x,y)$, and choose a fixed smooth compactly supported $\zeta $ equal to one near the origin.

First, the dimension-zero current $A(f)=\partial _ y f(0)$ is supported at the origin and has boundary zero. It is not order zero, since the uniformly bounded tests $f_ k=\zeta \sin (ky)$ give $A(f_ k)=k$. For the submanifold $Y=\{ y=0\} $ it cannot be an inclusion pushforward of any current on $Y$, even one of higher order: the test $y\zeta $ restricts to zero on $Y$, while $A(y\zeta )=1$.

Second, the dimension-one current $B(a\, \mathrm dx+b\, \mathrm dy)=b(0)$ has order zero and support at the origin, but $\partial B(f)=\partial _ y f(0)$ has order one. It also cannot come from $\{ y=0\} $, since that inclusion kills $\zeta \, \mathrm dy$ whereas $B$ evaluates it to one. Thus order zero of $T$ alone does not imply the support theorem.

Finally let the dimension-two point current be $R(c\, \mathrm dx\wedge \mathrm dy)=c(0)$ and put $C=\partial R$. Then

\[ C(a\, \mathrm dx+b\, \mathrm dy) =\partial _ xb(0)-\partial _ ya(0),\qquad \partial C=0. \]

This current is nonzero, supported on the zero-dimensional submanifold $\{ 0\} $, and has dimension one. It is not order zero: the uniformly bounded forms $\zeta \sin (kx)\, \mathrm dy$ have $C$-values $k$. Thus the dimension conclusion also fails without normality, even for a closed current. These evaluations use the same measure-versus-point-derivative distinction as the Dirac derivative as a distribution of positive order.
