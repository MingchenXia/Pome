5.57 Averaging complex planes0301E293E240

0301E293E240

Use $dd^ c=\frac{i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial $ and put $\beta =dd^ c|z|^2$ on $\mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$. Let $\sigma $ be invariant probability on the unit sphere, and $\nu _ q$ invariant probability on the complex Grassmannian $G(q,n)$, including the singleton spaces at $q=0,n$. These probabilities are those of invariant probability on unitary homogeneous spaces. For a unit vector $\xi $ write $\ell _\xi (z)=\langle z,\xi \rangle =\sum _ jz_ j\overline{\xi _ j}$ and $H_\xi =\ker \ell _\xi $.

Lemma 5.57.1.F08E0D60DA23 There is a finite constant $c_ n\leq 0$ such that, for $z\ne 0$,

6A107C250306 5.57.2
\begin{equation} \int \log |\ell _\xi (z)|^2\, d\sigma (\xi ) =\log |z|^2+c_ n. \end{equation}

Writing $C_ n=-c_ n$, one also has

F0FBE5A138FA 5.57.3
\begin{equation} \int \bigl |\log |\ell _\xi (z)|^2\bigr |\, d\sigma (\xi ) \leq |\log |z|^2|+C_ n. \end{equation}

For any nonzero complex subspace $S$, the set of $\xi $ for which $\ell _\xi |_ S$ is identically zero is $\sigma $-null.

Proof. Invariant sphere probability is normalized Euclidean surface measure: the latter is invariant under unitary transformations, so uniqueness in invariant probability on unitary homogeneous spaces applies. For $n=1$, $|\xi _1|=1$ and $c_1=0$. For $n\geq 2$, the only singularity of $\log |\xi _1|^2$ on the sphere lies on $\{ \xi _1=0\} $. Near this compact set use smooth coordinates

\[ (w,\eta )\longmapsto \bigl (w,\sqrt{1-|w|^2}\, \eta \bigr ), \qquad |w|<\tfrac 12,\quad \eta \in S^{2n-3}. \]

In finitely many compactly buffered coordinate charts for $\eta $, the surface Jacobians are smooth and bounded. Thus the integral of $|\log |\xi _1|^2|$ there is bounded by a constant times

\[ \int _{|w|<1/2}|\log |w|^2|\, dA(w)<\infty . \]

Off that neighborhood the logarithm is bounded. The same coordinates show that $\{ \xi _1=0\} $ has zero surface measure. Consequently $c_ n=\int \log |\xi _1|^2\, d\sigma $ is finite and nonpositive. Unitary invariance and scaling give (5.57.2) for every nonzero $z$. The decomposition

\[ \log |\ell _\xi (z)|^2 =\log |z|^2+\log |\ell _\xi (z/|z|)|^2 \]

has nonpositive second summand, proving (5.57.3). Finally choose any unit vector $v\in S$. The exceptional set in the last assertion is contained in $\{ \ell _\xi (v)=0\} $, which is null by the same unitary-coordinate computation. $\square$

Proposition 5.57.4.522ECB915F74 For every $0\leq q\leq n$ the weak integral

\[ A_ q=\int _{G(q,n)}[S]\, d\nu _ q(S) \]

is a positive closed current of bidimension $(q,q)$. For $r>0$,

9DCAA92A7418 5.57.5
\begin{equation} \int _{B(0,r)}A_ q\wedge \beta ^ q=r^{2q}. \end{equation}

For $q\geq 1$ the function $u(z)=\log |z|^2$ is integrable for all coefficient variations of $A_ q$ on compact sets, and

7346BFC89793 5.57.6
\begin{equation} uA_ q=\int _{G(q,n)}u[S]\, d\nu _ q(S). \end{equation}

Proof. Fix a standard $q$-plane $S_0$. For a compactly supported smooth $(q,q)$-form $\theta $, the function

\[ U\longmapsto \int _{S_0}U^*\theta \]

is continuous on $\mathrm U(n)$: all supports lie in one fixed ball, and the coefficients and derivatives of the unitary maps are uniformly bounded. Integration over that fixed compact piece of $S_0$ permits dominated convergence. This function depends only on $US_0$. The continuous surjection $\mathrm U(n)\to G(q,n)$ is a quotient map, since its source is compact and its target Hausdorff. It therefore defines a continuous function $S\mapsto [S](\theta )$. Averaging defines a current: its action on test forms supported in a fixed ball is bounded by a constant times their coefficient supremum. Positivity and closedness follow by averaging those properties of each $[S]$. For $q=0$ this is directly the origin mass; the same reasoning otherwise uses ordinary integration on the smooth planes.

On every $q$-plane, $\int _{S\cap B(0,r)}\beta ^ q=r^{2q}$, by the plane normalization of theorem 6.33.1. Averaging the trace measures gives (5.57.5); one may first test with continuous radial cutoffs and then use monotone convergence. For $q=0$ the right side is one. For $q\geq 1$,

\[ \int _{S\cap B(0,R)}|\log |z|^2|\, \beta ^ q =2q\int _0^ R t^{2q-1}|\log t^2|\, dt<\infty , \]

uniformly in $S$. Averaging positive trace tests, first with bounded continuous truncations of the absolute logarithm and then by monotone convergence, gives the same finite bound for $A_ q$. There is no atom at zero when $q\geq 1$, by (5.57.5). The coefficient domination of coefficient measures and compactness of positive currents now gives coefficient integrability and (5.57.6), by Fubini or the same bounded truncations. Thus multiplication by $u$ is an actual coefficient-$L^1$ operation here. $\square$

Lemma 5.57.7.74F34FAD7C93 For $1\leq q\leq n$ one has the global current identity

4EAEBCA3446D 5.57.8
\begin{equation} A_{q-1}=dd^ c(uA_ q),\qquad u=\log |z|^2. \end{equation}

Proof. For fixed $S\in G(q,n)$ and almost every $\xi $, the linear function $\ell _\xi |_ S$ is nonzero. Its zero divisor is the smooth $(q-1)$-plane $S\cap H_\xi $, with multiplicity one. Normalized Poincare–Lelong on $S$, followed by the proper inclusion $S\hookrightarrow \mathbb C^ n$, gives

08FF10DEE8F9 5.57.9
\begin{equation} dd^ c\bigl (\log |\ell _\xi |^2[S]\bigr )=[S\cap H_\xi ], \end{equation}

using the normalized Poincaré–Lelong formula, pushforward of currents proper on their support. For $q=1$ this is exactly the unit origin mass. Parameters where $\ell _\xi |_ S$ vanishes identically are excluded as a null set; no infinite scalar multiple of $[S]$ is used there.

All averaging of logarithms and derivatives is justified by the following joint bound. For $R>0$,

277
\begin{align} & \int \int _{S\cap B(0,R)} \bigl |\log |\ell _\xi (z)|^2\bigr |\, \beta ^ q\, d\sigma (\xi ) \notag \\ & \hspace{1cm}\leq \int _{S\cap B(0,R)}\bigl (|\log |z|^2|+C_ n\bigr )\beta ^ q <\infty , \tag{5.57.10} \end{align}

uniformly in $S$, by the preceding two results and Tonelli. Measurability can be seen first with the continuous truncations $\max \{ \log |\ell _\xi |^2,-N\} $: the unitary parametrization in the preceding proof makes all their test-form pairings continuous in $(S,\xi )$. Take their limits outside the exceptional Borel null set. Coefficient domination and (5.57.10) then permit Fubini for every test form, including its $dd^ c$. In particular,

\[ \int \log |\ell _\xi |^2[S]\, d\sigma (\xi )=(u+c_ n)[S] \]

by (5.57.2) for almost every point of $S$. Since $[S]$ is closed, averaging (5.57.9) gives

\[ \int [S\cap H_\xi ]\, d\sigma (\xi )=dd^ c(u[S]). \]

It remains to identify the distribution of the intersection plane when $S$ is also averaged. In the orthogonal-projection model of the Grassmannian, on $\{ P_ S\xi \ne 0\} $ its projection matrix is

\[ P_{S\cap H_\xi } =P_ S-\frac{(P_ S\xi )(P_ S\xi )^*}{|P_ S\xi |^2}. \]

This is continuous there. The excluded set is Borel and each $\xi $-section at fixed $S$ has measure zero, by lemma 5.57.1; hence it has product measure zero. Define the map to be one fixed $(q-1)$-plane on that set, obtaining a Borel map everywhere. Its pushforward of $\nu _ q\otimes \sigma $ is a probability on $G(q-1,n)$ and is unitary invariant, by the equivariance of the formula outside the null set. Uniqueness in invariant probability on unitary homogeneous spaces identifies it as $\nu _{q-1}$. This includes $q=1$, where the target is a singleton. Averaging the preceding current identity over $S$ and using (5.57.6) therefore gives (5.57.8). $\square$

Theorem 5.57.11.AB7EED2588E5 For every $0\leq q\leq n$,

1642738DA5E8 5.57.12
\begin{equation} \int _{G(q,n)}[S]\, d\nu _ q(S) =(dd^ c\log |z|^2)^{n-q} \end{equation}

on all of $\mathbb C^ n$, where the right side is the classical recursive product. At $q=n$ it is $1$, and at $q=0$ it is $\delta _0$. In particular the top product retains its unit origin mass. Its normalized trace masses are (5.57.5).

Proof. At $q=n$, the only plane is $\mathbb C^ n$ and $A_ n=1$. For every $q\geq 1$, the preceding results establish coefficient integrability of $u$ for $A_ q$ and the actual defining recursion $dd^ c(uA_ q)=A_{q-1}$. Induction identifies $A_ q$ with the $(n-q)$th recursive power of $dd^ cu$. These are also the classical products of theorem 7.31.2, whose compact pole condition holds for $u=\log |z|^2$ on Euclidean balls with boundary avoiding zero; the coefficient recursion is the same. At the last step it multiplies $u$ by $A_1$, for which the proved logarithmic integrability still holds, and produces $A_0=[\{ 0\} ]=\delta _0$. It never multiplies $u$ by $\delta _0$. The trace normalization and all endpoint assertions follow from the plane-average proposition. $\square$
