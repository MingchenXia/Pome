10.34 Tensor curvature and determinant twistsD41C64EC594F

D41C64EC594F

Hermitian products are linear in the first variable. For Hermitian spaces $T,E$, a Hermitian form $C$ on $T\otimes E$ is Griffiths semipositive if it is nonnegative on decomposable tensors. It is $m$-semipositive if it is nonnegative on tensors of rank at most $m$, and Nakano semipositive if it is nonnegative on all tensors. Strict versions test nonzero tensors. Tensor rank means the rank of $T^*\to E$. Write $g,h$ for the metrics and

\[ C(u)=\sum _{j,k,\lambda ,\mu }c_{jk\lambda \mu } u_{j\lambda }\overline{u_{k\mu }},\qquad (\operatorname {tr}_ E C)_{j\bar k}=\sum _\lambda c_{jk\lambda \lambda } \]

in unitary bases. Unless explicitly zero, the spaces have positive dimension. All curvature forms below are unscaled: the normalized first Chern form of a line is $\mathrm i\Theta /(2\pi )$.

Lemma 10.34.1.80CB7E919269 Let $q\geq 3$ be an integer and let $\sigma $ be uniformly distributed over the $r$-tuples of $q$th roots of unity. For $x'_\sigma =\sum _\lambda x_\lambda \overline{\sigma _\lambda }$ and $y'_\sigma =\sum _\lambda y_\lambda \overline{\sigma _\lambda }$,

\[ \mathbb E\bigl (x'_\sigma \overline{y'_\sigma } \sigma _\alpha \overline{\sigma _\beta }\bigr ) =\begin{cases} x_\alpha \overline{y_\beta },& \alpha \ne \beta ,\\ \sum _\lambda x_\lambda \overline{y_\lambda },& \alpha =\beta . \end{cases} \]

Proof. Expand the expression. Independence and $q^{-1}\sum _{\zeta ^ q=1}\zeta ^ a=0$ for $0<|a|\leq 2$ show that a term survives exactly when the multiset of the two positive phase indices equals that of the two negative ones. For $\alpha \ne \beta $ this forces the indicated single term; for $\alpha =\beta $ it forces equal indices from $x$ and $y$. The root sum follows by summing a finite geometric progression. The bound $q\geq 3$ is what excludes exponents $\pm 2$. $\square$

Theorem 10.34.2.69881B3187A9 If $C$ is Griffiths semipositive, then

\[ C+(\operatorname {tr}_ E C)\otimes h\quad \hbox{is Nakano semipositive}, \qquad m(\operatorname {tr}_ E C)\otimes h-C \quad \hbox{is $m$-semipositive} \]

for every integer $m\geq 1$. If on decomposable tensors $C(\xi \otimes v)\geq \varepsilon |\xi |^2|v|^2$, then the two lower bounds are respectively

E601DE45CB52 10.34.3
\begin{equation} (r+1)\varepsilon |u|^2\quad \hbox{for all }u,\qquad (mr-1)\varepsilon |u|^2\quad \hbox{for }\operatorname {rank}u\leq m. \end{equation}

Thus strict Griffiths positivity gives strict Nakano positivity in the first assertion, and strict $m$-positivity in the second unless $r=m=1$, when the second form is identically zero.

Proof. Write $u=\sum _\lambda \xi _\lambda \otimes e_\lambda $, put $\widehat\xi _\sigma =\sum _\lambda \overline{\sigma _\lambda }\xi _\lambda $ and $\widehat e_\sigma =\sum _\lambda \sigma _\lambda e_\lambda $. The preceding moment identity gives exactly

92B7B02AA2C7 10.34.4
\begin{equation} \bigl (C+(\operatorname {tr}_ E C)\otimes h\bigr )(u) =\mathbb E C(\widehat\xi _\sigma \otimes \widehat e_\sigma ) +\sum _\lambda C(\xi _\lambda \otimes e_\lambda ). \end{equation}

Both terms are nonnegative. To verify the diagonal count, the expectation contains all off-diagonal coefficients of $C(u)$ and the whole trace term; it counts the coefficient with all fiber indices equal only once. The final sum supplies that missing copy.

For the second assertion, let $u\ne 0$ have rank $k\leq m$, and let $F\subset E$ be the $k$-dimensional image of $T^*\to E$ defined by $u$. Restrict $C$ to $T\otimes F$. The form

\[ D=(\operatorname {tr}_ F C_ F)\otimes h_ F-C_ F \]

is Griffiths semipositive: in a unitary basis beginning with any given unit fiber vector, it is the sum of the other diagonal tests. This remains true for $k=1$, where $D=0$. Apply the first assertion to $D$ on $T\otimes F$ to get $D+(\operatorname {tr}_ F D)\otimes h_ F =k(\operatorname {tr}_ F C_ F)\otimes h_ F-C_ F\geq 0$ on all tensors. Finally

\[ m\operatorname {tr}_ E C-k\operatorname {tr}_ F C_ F =(m-k)\operatorname {tr}_ F C_ F +m\operatorname {tr}_{F^\perp }C_{F^\perp }\geq 0 \]

as a form on $T$. Evaluate on $u\in T\otimes F$.

For the quantitative statements apply these semipositive conclusions to $C-\varepsilon g\otimes h$. The trace of $g\otimes h$ is $rg$, so the two added scalar forms have coefficients $r+1$ and $mr-1$. If $C$ is strictly Griffiths positive, compactness of the two unit spheres supplies $\varepsilon >0$. This proves all strict claims and the exceptional rank-one case. $\square$

Example 10.34.5.3F9B151E377C The coefficient one in the trace-added inequality is optimal as a universal coefficient, even for strictly Griffiths-positive forms on $\mathbb C^2\otimes \mathbb C^2$.

Proof. For $T=E=\mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 2$, set

\[ C_0(u)=\sum _{j,\lambda }u_{j\lambda }\overline{u_{\lambda j}}. \]

Then $C_0(\xi \otimes v)=|\sum _ j\xi _ j\overline{v_ j}|^2\geq 0$, $\operatorname {tr}_ E C_0=g$, and $C_0(u)=-|u|^2$ for every nonzero skew-symmetric coefficient matrix. Put $C_\varepsilon =C_0+\varepsilon g\otimes h$, with $\varepsilon >0$. It is strictly Griffiths positive and its trace is $(1+n\varepsilon )g$. For every real $c<1$, the form $C_\varepsilon +c(\operatorname {tr}_ E C_\varepsilon )\otimes h$ has value

\[ \bigl (c-1+(1+cn)\varepsilon \bigr )|u|^2 \]

on such a matrix. Choosing $0<\varepsilon <(1-c)/(1+n|c|)$ makes it negative. Taking $n=2$ proves the assertion. In contrast $C_0+(\operatorname {tr}_ E C_0)\otimes h =\tfrac 12\sum _{j,\lambda }|u_{j\lambda }+u_{\lambda j}|^2$. The strict perturbation is essential to test a strict hypothesis. $\square$

Proposition 10.34.6.E048AA8C1785 For induced Hermitian metrics, Chern curvature obeys

\[ R_{E\otimes F}=R_ E\otimes I+I\otimes R_ F,\qquad R_{E^*}=-R_ E^{\mathsf T},\qquad R_{\det E}=\operatorname {tr}_ E R_ E. \]

Tensor products preserve $m$-semipositivity and strict $m$-positivity when both factors have the corresponding sign. On $E^{\otimes d}$, $d\geq 1$, a lower bound $\varepsilon $ for $E$ on rank-at-most-$m$ tensors gives the lower bound $d\varepsilon $. These assertions pass to any parallel holomorphic summand. In particular they apply to symmetric powers, exterior powers and the image of a fixed Young symmetrizer in a tensor power, whenever that image is nonzero. For Griffiths positivity take $m=1$; for Nakano positivity test all tensor ranks.

Proof. The tensor connection is the sum of its two connection actions. Their squares give the first formula, since the mixed degree-one terms cancel. Differentiating the evaluation pairing gives the negative transpose formula. On the determinant the connection form is $\operatorname {tr}A$; its curvature is $d\operatorname {tr}A =\operatorname {tr}(dA+A\wedge A)$, since $\operatorname {tr}(A\wedge A)=0$ by exchanging the matrix indices. All these metric connections have the canonical $(0,1)$-parts. In a holomorphic frame, metric compatibility then forces the connection form to be $H^{-1}\partial H$, proving uniqueness and identifying them as Chern connections. This local calculation requires no Kähler metric on the base.

For the tensor positivity claim write a tensor in $T\otimes E\otimes F$ in a unitary basis of $F$. The first curvature term is the sum of the curvature tests on its $T\otimes E$ slices. A contraction on the fiber factor cannot increase the rank of $T^*$ into that fiber, so each slice has rank at most the original tensor rank. The same argument, slicing by $E$, treats the second term. The sum of the squared slice norms is the original squared norm. This proves the quantitative bounds and, by induction, the tensor-power assertion.

Permutations of equal tensor factors are parallel and commute with every transition $G^{\otimes d}$. A fixed linear combination $Y$ of them is therefore a parallel holomorphic map of constant rank: in each tensor frame it is the same fixed matrix. Its image $W$ is a holomorphic subbundle preserved by the connection. Metric compatibility also preserves $W^\perp $, so its induced connection has zero second fundamental form and its curvature is the restriction. This proves the summand assertion. The averaging and alternating maps give symmetric and exterior powers. More generally, for a Young diagram use the product of the sum over row permutations and the signed sum over column permutations; its image is all that is meant here by the corresponding Schur tensor bundle. No irreducibility or representation-classification assertion is needed. $\square$

Theorem 10.34.7.FB187755A875 Let $E$ be a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle of rank $r\geq 1$ on a complex manifold. Griffiths semipositivity implies Nakano semipositivity of $E\otimes \det E$, and $m$-semipositivity of $E^*\otimes (\det E)^ m$ for every integer $m\geq 1$. Strict Griffiths positivity gives the strict conclusions, except that the latter bundle is flat when $r=m=1$. More strongly, for every integer $k\geq 0$,

\[ \operatorname {Sym}^ kE\otimes \det E \quad \hbox{is Nakano semipositive}. \]

It is Nakano positive when $E$ is Griffiths positive. A pointwise Griffiths lower bound $\varepsilon $ for $E$ gives the Nakano lower bound $(r+k)\varepsilon $ for this last bundle.

Proof. The first assertion is theorem 10.34.2 applied to its curvature, using proposition 10.34.6. The form $C=-R_{E^*}$ is Griffiths semipositive whenever $R_ E$ is: the negative-transpose formula, with the conjugate fiber coordinates, gives precisely the same decomposable tests. Its trace is $\operatorname {tr}R_ E$. Thus the curvature of $E^*\otimes (\det E)^ m$ is $m(\operatorname {tr}C)\otimes h-C$. The trace inequality gives the assertion and the lower bound $(mr-1)\varepsilon $. For $r=m=1$ the bundle with its induced metric is the canonically trivial $E^*\otimes E$ and has zero curvature.

We prove the stronger symmetric assertion directly. At a point use unitary bases and identify a symmetric tensor $u\in \operatorname {Sym}^ kE$ with the homogeneous polynomial

\[ p_ u(z)=\sum _{i_1,\ldots ,i_ k}u_{i_1\cdots i_ k}z_{i_1}\cdots z_{i_ k}. \]

Use the probability measure $d\gamma (z)=\pi ^{-r}e^{-|z|^2}dV(z)$ on $\mathbb C^ r$. Polar integration in each variable gives

\[ \int z^\alpha \overline{z^\beta }\, d\gamma =\begin{cases} \alpha !,& \alpha =\beta ,\\ 0,& \alpha \ne \beta ,\end{cases} \qquad \langle u,v\rangle =\frac1{k!}\int p_ u\overline{p_ v}\, d\gamma . \]

For the second identity, a monomial with multi-index $\alpha $ has $k!/\alpha !$ equal tensor coefficients; the displayed moment computes its norm exactly. The degree-zero conventions are $0!=1$ and $p_ u=u$.

Write $R_{j\bar k}e_\lambda =\sum _\mu c_{jk\lambda \mu }e_\mu $. Its symmetric action on polynomials is $\sum _{\lambda ,\mu }c_{jk\lambda \mu }z_\mu \partial _{z_\lambda }$. Gaussian integration by parts gives

\[ \int \overline z_\lambda z_\mu p_{u_ j}\overline{p_{u_ k}}\, d\gamma =\int \bigl (\delta _{\lambda \mu }p_{u_ j} +z_\mu \partial _{z_\lambda }p_{u_ j}\bigr ) \overline{p_{u_ k}}\, d\gamma . \]

Indeed $\partial _{z_\lambda }e^{-|z|^2} =-\overline z_\lambda e^{-|z|^2}$; integrate over balls and let their radii tend to infinity. Polynomial times Gaussian boundary terms tend to zero, so every integral converges absolutely. For $u=\sum _ j t_ j\otimes u_ j$ the resulting identity is

36F0B3AD01F0 10.34.8
\begin{equation} R_{\operatorname {Sym}^ kE\otimes \det E}(u) =\frac1{k!}\int R_ E\left( \Bigl (\sum _ j p_{u_ j}(z)t_ j\Bigr )\otimes \overline z\right) \, d\gamma (z). \end{equation}

Each integrand is a decomposable curvature test and hence nonnegative. If $R_ E\geq \varepsilon $ on such tests, the right side is at least $\varepsilon \sum _ j\int |z|^2|p_{u_ j}|^2d\gamma /k!$. The monomial moment formula gives $\int |z|^2|p|^2d\gamma =(r+k)\int |p|^2d\gamma $ for homogeneous degree $k$. This proves the stated bound and strictness. All expressions on the left are intrinsic; the chosen coordinates only evaluate them. $\square$

Proposition 10.34.9.A86F8BFD9ADD Let $0\to S\to E\to Q\to 0$ be a holomorphic exact sequence, with the induced subbundle and quotient metrics. If $E$ is $m$-semipositive, then $S\otimes (\det Q)^ m$ is $m$-semipositive. The implication is also strict. More precisely, writing $a=\operatorname {rank}Q$, a pointwise lower bound $\varepsilon \geq 0$ for $E$ on tensors of rank at most $m$ gives the bound $(1+ma)\varepsilon $ for this twist. Ranks one and zero of $S,Q$ are allowed with their usual conventions.

Proof. If $S=0$, the assertion is vacuous, so assume its rank is positive. Use the smooth orthogonal splitting $E=S\oplus Q$. Its Chern connection has the block form

\[ D_ E=\begin{pmatrix} D_ S & -\beta ^* \\ \beta & D_ Q \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \beta =\sum _ j dz_ j B_ j\in \mathcal A^{1,0}(\operatorname {Hom}(S,Q)). \]

Holomorphicity of $S$ makes the lower-left $(0,1)$-block zero; metric compatibility determines the opposite block. The diagonal connections have their canonical $(0,1)$-parts and preserve their induced metrics, so are the indicated Chern connections. Squaring gives, including the imaginary units,

\begin{align*} \mathrm i\Theta (S)& =\mathrm i\Theta (E)|_ S -\mathrm i\sum _{j,k}dz_ j\wedge d\overline z_ k B_ k^*B_ j,\\ \mathrm i\Theta (Q)& =\mathrm i\Theta (E)|_ Q +\mathrm i\sum _{j,k}dz_ j\wedge d\overline z_ k B_ jB_ k^*. \end{align*}

For the first sign use $\beta ^*\wedge \beta =-\sum dz_ j\wedge d\overline z_ k B_ k^*B_ j$. Let $C_ B$ be the form on $T\otimes S$ with coefficients $B_ k^*B_ j$. It is Nakano semipositive, since

\[ C_ B(u)=\left|\sum _ j B_ ju_ j\right|^2,\qquad \operatorname {tr}_ Q(B_ jB_ k^*)=\operatorname {tr}_ S(B_ k^*B_ j). \]

Consequently the twist has curvature form

\[ R_ E|_ S+m(\operatorname {tr}_ Q R_ E|_ Q)\otimes h_ S +m(\operatorname {tr}_ S C_ B)\otimes h_ S-C_ B. \]

The last two terms together are $m$-semipositive by theorem 10.34.2; when $\operatorname {rank}S=1$ this uses its non-strict rank-one clause, not a false strict assertion. The first term has lower bound $\varepsilon $ on the tested tensors. Griffiths tests in an orthonormal basis of $Q$ give $\operatorname {tr}_ Q R_ E|_ Q\geq a\varepsilon g$. This proves the displayed quantitative claim and strictness. If $Q=0$, its determinant is the flat trivial line and this is just $S=E$; if $S=0$ there are no nonzero tensors to test. $\square$

Example 10.34.10.722E8364B69C On $\mathbb P^ n$, $n\geq 1$, let $H$ be the quotient of the trivial $\mathbb C^{n+1}$ bundle by $\mathcal O(-1)$, with quotient metric. At $[1:0:\cdots :0]$ its curvature form is $C_0$ of example 10.34.5. Its dual has Nakano form $-|\operatorname {tr}u|^2$. Moreover, under the induced metric,

\[ T\mathbb P^ n\simeq H\otimes \mathcal O(1)\simeq H\otimes \det H, \qquad R_{T\mathbb P^ n}(u)=\tfrac 12\sum _{j,k}|u_{jk}+u_{kj}|^2. \]

Thus this tangent metric is Griffiths positive and Nakano semipositive, and is not Nakano positive for $n\geq 2$. For $n=1$ both $H$ and $T\mathbb P^1$ are Nakano positive.

Proof. In the affine chart $[1:z]$, the tautological line is spanned by $(1,z)$. At $z=0$, its second fundamental maps satisfy $B_ j(1)=e_ j$. The quotient curvature formula in proposition 10.34.9, applied to the flat ambient bundle, gives $R_{H,j\bar k}=B_ jB_ k^*$, hence $c_{jk\lambda \mu }=\delta _{k\lambda }\delta _{j\mu }$, the flip form $C_0$. The negative transpose gives the asserted dual trace square. Taking determinants of the tautological sequence identifies $\det H$ with $\mathcal O(1)$ after choosing a unit volume element in $\mathbb C^{n+1}$; its induced metrics agree. A tangent variation of a line $\ell $ defines the map $\ell \to \mathbb C^{n+1}/\ell $ given by differentiating a local generator modulo $\ell $. Rescaling the generator leaves this linear map unchanged. In graph coordinates it is the differential of $z$, so this is the holomorphic identification $T\mathbb P^ n=\operatorname {Hom}(\mathcal O(-1),H)$. Curvature addition now gives $C_0+g\otimes h$ and the displayed sum. On a decomposable tensor it is at least $|\xi |^2|v|^2$, whereas a nonzero skew-symmetric matrix gives zero for $n\geq 2$. Unitary transformations of the ambient vector space preserve all these metrics and are transitive on projective space, so the tests hold at every point. For $n=1$ the flip form is the scalar form $1$ and the tangent form is $2$, proving the endpoint assertion. $\square$

Corollary 10.34.11.670C664810C5 Let $X$ be compact of dimension $n\geq 1$, with positive-rank Hermitian holomorphic bundle $E$. All positivity assumptions in this corollary are strict at every point. Then:

  1. If $E$ is Griffiths positive, then $H^{n,q}_{\bar\partial }(X,\operatorname {Sym}^ kE\otimes \det E)=0$ for $k\geq 0$ and $1\leq q\leq n$. This includes $E\otimes \det E$. For any nonzero degree-$d$ Schur tensor image $W\subset E^{\otimes d}$, $d\geq 1$, one also has $H^{n,q}_{\bar\partial }(X,W\otimes \det W)=0$.

  2. If $E$ is Griffiths positive of rank $r\geq 2$, then $H^{n,q}_{\bar\partial }(X,E^*\otimes (\det E)^ m)=0$ whenever $q\geq 1$ and $m\geq \min (n-q+1,r)$. The same holds for $r=1,m>1$; no such assertion is made for $r=m=1$.

  3. In a holomorphic exact sequence $0\to S\to E\to Q\to 0$, put $s=\operatorname {rank}S$. If $E$ is $m$-positive, $m\geq \min (n-q+1,s)$, and a line bundle $L$ has a metric such that $L\otimes (\det Q)^{-m}$ is semipositive, then $H^{n,q}_{\bar\partial }(X,S\otimes L)=0$ for $1\leq q\leq n$. For $s=0$ the group is zero without conditions.

These analytic groups also have their sheaf-cohomology interpretation by corollary 10.43.11.

Proof. Each assumed positivity implies Griffiths positivity of $E$. Its determinant curvature $\mathrm i\operatorname {tr}\Theta (E)$ is a positive closed real $(1,1)$-form, so it itself supplies a Kähler metric on $X$; closedness follows locally from the determinant connection. The first two claims follow from theorem 10.34.7, proposition 10.34.6 and the exact strict rank-$m$ vanishing range of corollary 10.33.14. For the third, proposition 10.34.9 makes $S\otimes (\det Q)^ m$ strictly $m$-positive. Tensoring by the specified semipositive line adds a nonnegative scalar curvature form, so preserves that strict inequality. Apply the same rank-$m$ vanishing range to the rank-$s$ bundle $S\otimes L$. Compactness makes the pointwise strict bounds uniform on the relevant unit tensor bundles, exactly as in that canonical vanishing argument. No continuation theorem is used. $\square$

Corollary 10.34.12.10CC96C8D2BA Let $0\to S\to E\to Q\to 0$ be a holomorphic exact sequence on a compact $n$-dimensional complex manifold, and suppose $E$ is strictly $m$-positive everywhere, $m\geq 1$. Put $s=\operatorname {rank}S$. For $0\leq q<n$ and integers $l,m\geq \min (n-q,s)$, the map

\[ H^{n,q}_{\bar\partial }(X,E\otimes (\det Q)^ l) \longrightarrow H^{n,q}_{\bar\partial }(X,Q\otimes (\det Q)^ l) \]

is onto. For $1\leq q\leq n$ it is an isomorphism if $l,m\geq \min (n-q+1,s)$. In degree $q=n$ it is always onto. If $S=0$ the map is an isomorphism in every degree and twist.

Proof. The quotient curvature formula shows that $Q$ is Griffiths positive when its rank is nonzero: on a decomposable tensor the extra term is $|B(\xi )^*v|^2\geq 0$. Thus $\det Q$ is semipositive, also for $Q=0$. Let $a=\min (n-q,s)$ for surjectivity, with $s>0$ and $q<n$. Since $m\geq a$, the bundle $E$ is strictly $a$-positive. The preceding corollary with $L=(\det Q)^ l$ and exponent $a$ gives $H^{n,q+1}_{\bar\partial }(X,S\otimes (\det Q)^ l)=0$, because $l\geq a$ and $(\det Q)^{l-a}$ is semipositive. For the isomorphism condition use $a=\min (n-q+1,s)$; the same argument gives vanishing in both degrees $q$ and $q+1$, the latter automatic when $q=n$.

The bundle sequence tensored with $K_ X\otimes (\det Q)^ l$ gives a short exact sequence of global smooth Dolbeault complexes: the surjectivity in each degree follows from a smooth bundle splitting. Its cohomology long exact sequence from lemma 10.43.3 proves the assertions. For $q=n$, its next degree is zero, so surjectivity is automatic. $\square$

Remark 10.34.13.7DF710595FC8 All pointwise semipositive assertions hold on arbitrary complex bases. The compact vanishing conclusions assume strict positivity everywhere; semipositivity with strictness at only one point requires an additional continuation argument and is not asserted. Nor does the projective tangent example rule out a different metric with stronger positivity. No preservation of Nakano positivity by arbitrary quotient bundles is used: the tensor summands above are parallel. On a zero-dimensional compact base every positive-degree Dolbeault group is zero; finite components are treated componentwise. A zero Schur/exterior image has zero cohomology, while a degree-zero tensor power is the flat trivial line. The symmetric determinant theorem includes degree zero explicitly; it then says that $\det E$ is positive. No unrestricted ample-vector-bundle positivity or promised later Griffiths/Le Potier theorem follows from these metric statements.
