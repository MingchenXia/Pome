1020452BCA35

Lemma 10.74.1.53C4601E9CF2 For any path connected space with basepoint $a$, loops as singular cycles induce an isomorphism $\pi _1(X,a)^{\rm ab}\simeq H_1^{\mathrm{sing}}(X,\mathbb Z)$. On a smooth manifold every such class has a piecewise smooth loop representative. For a closed smooth one-form $\alpha $, its singular cohomology evaluation on that class is $\int _\gamma \alpha $ under the proved sheaf/de Rham comparisons.

Proof. A constant path is the boundary of its constant two-simplex. A homotopy of paths, subdivided into two triangles per prism, shows that homotopic paths have the same homology class; constant end edges contribute boundaries. The singular two-simplex obtained by collapsing a triangle onto the concatenated path has boundary $g-(f*g)+f$. Consequently concatenation is addition in homology, and path reversal is negation. This gives the homomorphism from the abelianized fundamental group.

Choose a path $\eta _ x$ from $a$ to each point $x$, with $\eta _ a$ constant. Send a one-cycle $z=\sum n_\sigma \sigma $ to

\[ \sum n_\sigma [\eta _{\sigma (v_0)}*\sigma * \eta _{\sigma (v_1)}^{-1}] \quad \hbox{in }\pi _1(X,a)^{\rm ab}. \]

The boundary of a two-simplex maps to zero: its three based edge loops combine to the boundary loop of that simplex, conjugated by the path to its initial vertex, and that loop contracts in the simplex. Thus this map descends to $H_1$. On a based loop it is the identity. In the other direction the added paths cancel in a cycle, because the coefficient of every endpoint in $\partial z$ is zero. The concatenation identity then gives back $[z]$. This proves both inverse identities without invoking a higher Hurewicz theorem.

A continuous path on a smooth manifold can be subdivided into finitely many arcs lying in coordinate balls. Replace each arc by the coordinate line segment between its endpoints. Coordinate straight-line homotopy stays inside the ball, and the pieces agree at the fixed endpoints. This proves the piecewise smooth assertion. For the last assertion, choose local primitives of $\alpha $ on coordinate balls, using the local de Rham homotopy. For any continuous path define its period as the sum of the changes of these primitives on a subordinate subdivision. On the connected overlap used by an arc, changing a primitive adds a constant and does not change that sum; refinement also leaves it unchanged. A subdivided parameter square proves homotopy invariance by cancellation of interior edges, so this defines a singular one-cocycle. On each ball it is the coboundary of the same primitive whose differential is $\alpha $. The natural Čech comparison of these primitives therefore identifies this cocycle with the de Rham class, with the same positive differential. For a piecewise smooth path the fundamental theorem of calculus identifies the sum with its usual integral. $\square$

Lemma 10.74.2.67B6FD6C1574 An oriented smooth embedded circle in an oriented smooth manifold has a trivial oriented normal bundle and an open tubular neighborhood diffeomorphic to $S^1\times \mathbb R^{m-1}$. One may choose either the tangent-first or the normal-first orientation convention, but must keep that choice when specifying its transverse generator.

Proof. First, an oriented rank-$r$ bundle on a circle is trivial. Cut the circle inside a trivializing arc. On the resulting interval extend a frame successively through finitely many trivializing intervals: on an overlap express it in the next frame and extend that transition matrix by freezing its parameter before the overlap ends. This gives a frame on the interval, with a transition in $\operatorname {GL}^+(r, \mathbb R)$ at the cut. This group is path connected by lemma 10.71.2. A path, made constant near its endpoints by a smooth reparameterization, corrects the transition. Using the given frame on the short arc at the cut ensures agreement on neighborhoods of both endpoints, not merely at the endpoints. The corrected frame consequently descends smoothly to the circle. For rank zero the assertion is immediate.

Here is an explicit neighborhood construction. Choose an inner product on $TX$ along the circle by a finite partition of unity, and identify the normal bundle with its orthogonal complement to the tangent. Choose finitely many coordinate charts $\phi _ i$ near the circle and a smooth partition $(\psi _ i)$ on the circle with compact support in those charts. For a small normal vector $v$ at $p$, start at $x_0=p$ and successively set

\[ x_ i=\phi _ i^{-1}\bigl (\phi _ i(x_{i-1})+ \psi _ i(p)(d\phi _ i)_ p v\bigr ) \]

where $\psi _ i(p)$ is supported, and use $x_ i=x_{i-1}$ elsewhere. The supports have compact buffers inside their charts, so for uniformly small $v$ all these expressions are defined. They extend smoothly across the zero parts of $\psi _ i$. The resulting map $F(p,v)=x_ N$ satisfies $F(p,0)=p$ and has vertical derivative $\sum _ i\psi _ i(p)v=v$. Its differential on the zero section is therefore the isomorphism $T_ pS^1\oplus N_ p\to T_ pX$. Here the required inverse-function assertion follows directly in coordinates. Normalize a smooth map $f$ by $f(0)=0$, $df_0=1$, and choose a closed ball of radius $a$ with $\| df-1\| \le 1/2$. For $|y|<a/2$, the map $z\mapsto z-f(z)+y$ maps this ball into itself and contracts distances by at most $1/2$. Its successive iterates are Cauchy, with a geometric bound on the tails, and have a unique fixed point $z(y)$. The same estimate gives $|z(y)-z(y')|\le 2|y-y'|$. Differentiating the difference identity $f(z(y'))-f(z(y))=y'-y$ by the first-order remainder estimate gives $dz_ y=(df_{z(y)})^{-1}$. These matrices are invertible by the convergent geometric series for $1-df$; matrix inversion is smooth, so induction on derivatives makes $z$ smooth. The estimate also makes $f$ injective on the ball. This proves the local smooth inverse needed here, and makes $F$ a local diffeomorphism near the zero section.

Compactness supplies a uniform disk radius on which that differential is invertible. It also supplies global injectivity after shrinking: otherwise two distinct points of disk bundles of radii tending to zero would have the same image. Subsequence limits of their basepoints would coincide on the circle, so both points would eventually lie in one inverse-function neighborhood of the same zero vector, a contradiction. Thus $F$ is a diffeomorphism of a small open disk bundle onto an open neighborhood. Use the frame already constructed and the orientation-preserving radial diffeomorphism between an open disk and $\mathbb R^ r$ to obtain the stated tube. The direct sum with the oriented tangent or its reversed order specifies either normal orientation convention. $\square$

Theorem 10.74.3.87DFB6289F81 Let $X$ be a compact oriented smooth manifold of real dimension $m$. There is a canonical isomorphism, retaining all torsion,

\[ \operatorname {PD}_ X:H_1^{\mathrm{sing}}(X,\mathbb Z) \xrightarrow {\sim }H^{m-1}(X,\mathbb Z), \qquad \operatorname {PD}_ X=D_{X,\mathbb Z}^{-1}. \]

On each connected component its domain is the abelianization of the based fundamental group. For an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism $f:X\to Y$ the naturality formula is $\operatorname {PD}_ Y f_*=(f^{-1})^*\operatorname {PD}_ X$. It is not a covariant-to-contravariant naturality assertion for arbitrary continuous maps.

With the current convention $T_\eta (\alpha )=\int _ X\eta \wedge \alpha $, the real image of $\operatorname {PD}_ X[\gamma ]$ is the de Rham class of the integration current $I_\gamma (\alpha )=\int _\gamma \alpha $. If $\gamma $ is embedded, choose its tube $\phi :S^1\times \mathbb R^{m-1}\xrightarrow {\sim }V$ and let $j:V\hookrightarrow X$. If this chart uses tangent followed by normal orientation, its integral class is

\[ (-1)^{m-1}\, j_!(\phi ^{-1})^*(1\times \nu ), \qquad \nu \in H_ c^{m-1}(\mathbb R^{m-1},\mathbb Z), \quad \operatorname {tr}(\nu )=1. \]

If the normal bundle is oriented before the tangent, the same formula has no extra sign. The integral isomorphism is established by cap product; real periods or currents alone do not determine its torsion part.

Proof. Cap duality on the compact manifold gives the isomorphism in all degrees; take degree $m-1$. The preceding degree-one lemma identifies its domain with abelianized loops on each component. There are only finitely many components by compactness. Orientation-preserving change of variables carries the compact fundamental class to the one on the target, and the chain cap naturality formula gives the stated diffeomorphism identity. This proves the integral assertion before any passage to real coefficients.

A piecewise smooth loop defines a closed degree-$(m-1)$ current: testing its differential on a zero-form gives the sum of the endpoint differences on its arcs, which is zero. The current comparison in corollary 10.43.11, corollary 10.57.1 provides a smooth de Rham representative $\eta $ of its class. If $\alpha $ is any closed smooth one-form, the exact-current difference pairs to zero with $\alpha $ by the current differential and Stokes. Hence $\int \eta \wedge \alpha =I_\gamma (\alpha )$. On the other hand, cap evaluation and the just proved degree-one period comparison give

\[ \int _ X\operatorname {PD}_ X[\gamma ]_{\mathbb R}\wedge \alpha =\langle [\gamma ],[\alpha ]\rangle =\int _\gamma \alpha . \]

The de Rham duality theorem proved above separates the compact $(m-1)$-classes: every nonzero vector has a nonzero algebraic dual functional, and all those functionals are represented by $H^1$. Thus the two real cohomology classes agree. This argument applies to every homology class using piecewise smooth representatives and requires no embedded-loop generation theorem.

For an embedded loop use its framed tube and the preceding external Künneth theorem. The class $1\times \nu $ generates $H_ c^{m-1}(S^1\times \mathbb R^{m-1},\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$. Its cap on the tangent-first oriented product equals $(-1)^{m-1}[S^1]$. To check the sign integrally, pair with the one-form $dt$ of period one on $S^1$: a transverse normalized top form wedges with $dt$ in that order with sign $(-1)^{m-1}$. The trace calculation proves that this is the value of cap evaluation. The tube retracts onto the circle, whose $H_1=\mathbb Z$ was computed by the sphere sequence. Thus that value determines its integral class. Open naturality of cap and the tube diffeomorphism therefore give exactly the displayed integral Thom formula, even when its image in global homology is torsion. Reversing the order used to orient the normal changes the transverse generator by $(-1)^{m-1}$ and removes the extra sign.

The transverse real class can equally be represented by the Dirac current at zero. Indeed compact top-degree current cohomology agrees with the compact de Rham group. Evaluation of a compact top current on the constant function one, defined with a cutoff equal to one near its support, vanishes on boundaries and agrees with integration on forms. The Dirac current has this value one, so is the image of $\nu $. With the indicated orientation and sign its product along the circle acts on a one-form by evaluation at the zero normal coordinate followed by integration on the circle. Its image is precisely $I_\gamma $. The cap proof, not this real description, establishes well-definedness of the integral class under all choices. For $m=0$ both groups in the first display are zero; for $m=1$ the normal rank is zero and the same formulas have sign one. $\square$
