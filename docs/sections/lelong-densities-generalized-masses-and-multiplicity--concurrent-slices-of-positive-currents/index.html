CF85D1D144CA

Use $d^ c=\frac{i}{4\pi }(\bar\partial -\partial )$ and $\beta =dd^ c|z|^2$ on $\mathbb C^ n$. Write $\alpha =dd^ c\log |z|^2$ away from zero. A current of bidegree $(c,c)$ has bidimension $(p,p)$, where $p=n-c$. All almost-everywhere assertions on a complex manifold refer to its smooth volume class. In particular they are independent of the choice of a smooth positive volume form.

Lemma 6.41.1.CACB97220D52 A current $A$ whose coefficients and exterior derivative have locally finite measure coefficients has, locally, a representation $A=U+dV$ with $U,V$ having locally integrable coefficients. For weakly convergent closed positive currents $A_ j\to A$, these representations can be chosen with $U_ j\to U$ and $V_ j\to V$ in $L^1$ on compact subsets of a smaller coordinate domain.

Proof. Work in real coordinates of dimension $m$. Let $\delta =-\sum _ l\iota _{\partial _ l}\partial _ l$ on coefficient forms. Expanding the two operators gives $d\delta +\delta d=-\Delta $ coefficientwise. With the fundamental solution $E_ m$ of fundamental solutions of the real Laplacian, put $G=-E_ m*$ on compactly supported distributions. Choose a smooth compact cutoff $\chi =1$ on a neighborhood of the domain in question. The exact distributional identity

\[ \chi A=d\delta G(\chi A)+\delta G\bigl (d(\chi A)\bigr ) \]

follows by convolution and the operator identity. Thus take $V=\delta G(\chi A)$ and $U=\delta G(d(\chi A))$. Both inputs are finite measures: $d(\chi A)=d\chi \wedge A+\chi dA$. The kernels of $\delta G$ have size $O(|x|^{1-m})$ for $m\geq 2$ and are bounded for $m=1$. They are locally integrable, by polar coordinates, so Tonelli proves the asserted $L^1$ property.

Here is also the needed compactness statement, including the mixed coefficients. If measures $\mu _ j\to \mu $ weakly have common compact support and uniformly bounded variation, and $K$ is any of these kernels, then $K*\mu _ j\to K*\mu $ in $L^1$ on compact sets. On the compact set of relevant differences approximate $K$ in $L^1$ by a smooth function. Tonelli bounds the convolution error by that $L^1$ error times the common variation bound. For a fixed smooth kernel weak convergence is uniform in the evaluation point on a compact set: a finite uniform net in the compact family of translated smooth tests reduces this to finitely many weak tests. Its convolution therefore converges in $L^1$ as well. This proves the assertion by first choosing the approximation and then $j$.

For closed positive $A_ j$, coefficient variations on a fixed compact set are uniformly bounded by their trace masses and coefficient measures and compactness of positive currents; a slightly larger positive smooth trace test bounds these masses under weak convergence. The measures $\chi A_ j$ and $d\chi \wedge A_ j$ converge weakly with these uniform bounds. Apply the preceding kernel argument to both, using the same cutoff and operators. It proves both $L^1$ convergences, with no assumption of convergence of each individual fiber restriction of the original measures. $\square$

Theorem 6.41.2.53506FB921EB Let $f:X\to B$ be a holomorphic submersion. For a closed positive current $A$ on $X$, the restrictions of a local representation $A=U+dV$ to the fibers define, for almost every $b\in B$,

\[ A_ b=U|_{X_ b}+d_{X_ b}(V|_{X_ b}). \]

They are well-defined closed positive currents of the same bidegree on the fibers, and agree with ordinary restrictions for smooth $A$. Their pairings are measurable. If $\omega _ B$ is a smooth volume form and $\theta $ is a test form of the fiber's complementary bidegree, then

9B9A398FAED5 6.41.3
\begin{equation} \int _ B A_ b(\theta |_{X_ b})\, \omega _ B(b) =A(\theta \wedge f^*\omega _ B). \end{equation}

This identity also holds for nonnegative Borel trace tests, with possibly infinite integrals. If $A_ j\to A$ are closed positive, a subsequence satisfies $(A_ j)_ b\to A_ b$ weakly for almost every $b$, simultaneously on a countable exhaustion of all fiber charts.

Proof. Use countably many submersion product charts $(z,b)$. Retain only the components of $U,V$ containing only fiber differentials; their coefficients are $L^1$ and have $L^1$ restrictions for almost all $b$. Fubini makes the resulting pairings measurable. The pure fiber component of $dV$ is exactly $d_ z(V_{\rm vert})$: derivatives in $b$ all contain a base differential. Consequently, if two $L^1$ representations differ by zero as a distribution, the corresponding fiber distributions differ by zero for almost every $b$. Indeed, test with a product of a fiber test and a base test, apply Fubini, and then use a countable dense family of fiber tests in each fixed compact support and derivative norm. This also proves independence of representatives and agreement on overlaps. Changes between product charts preserve $L^1$ on buffered compact subsets and commute with the fiber differential; the same argument applies to them. Countability leaves only one null set for gluing.

Since $dA=0$, the pure fiber component of $dU$ vanishes. The same product-test argument gives $d_ z U_{\rm vert}=0$ for almost all $b$, so $d_{X_ b}A_ b=0$. For positivity, mollify $A$ in a smaller product chart. The resulting smooth currents are closed positive and tend weakly to $A$. The preceding lemma supplies $L^1$ convergence of their two representatives. From any such convergence choose a subsequence with summable $L^1$ errors on a compact exhaustion. Tonelli gives convergence of both representatives in fiber $L^1$ for almost every $b$. Thus the smooth positive fiber currents tend weakly to $A_ b$, proving positivity. A diagonal choice over the countable chart exhaustion proves the claimed subsequence statement for general $A_ j\to A$ in exactly the same way. No full-sequence almost-everywhere assertion is needed or made.

Integration of the formula $U_{\rm vert}+d_ zV_{\rm vert}$ against $\theta $ and a compactly supported base density, followed by fiber integration by parts, proves (6.41.3). There is no base derivative term, since the wedged base volume has top degree. Partitions of unity give the global formula. Positivity then extends it from smooth scalar trace tests to Borel tests: first use increasing continuous approximations on open sets, regularity of the resulting positive measures, and then the monotone class argument. In particular the measure defined by integrating fiber trace measures is precisely the trace obtained by wedging $A$ with the smooth fiber trace form and $f^*\omega _ B$. $\square$

Lemma 6.41.4.0FAC162C7B65 For $0\leq j<n$, a smooth closed $(j,j)$-form on $\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} $ invariant under unitary transformations and positive real dilations is a constant multiple of $\alpha ^ j$. If it is positive, that constant is nonnegative.

Proof. For $j=0$ closedness makes the function constant. For $1\leq j<n$, put $t=\log |z|^2$. At $(0,\ldots ,0,a)$, isotropy by $U(n-1)$ forces the form to be a combination of $\alpha ^ j$ and $dt\wedge d^ ct\wedge \alpha ^{j-1}$. To check this explicitly, diagonal unitary phases first annihilate coefficients with unequal holomorphic and antiholomorphic index sets. Permutations of the first $n-1$ coordinates then make the coefficients with the same normal-index choice equal. These are exactly the two displayed forms. Unitary invariance makes their coefficients radial and dilation invariance makes them constant. The derivative of the second form is $-dt\wedge \alpha ^ j$, which is nonzero because $j<n$. Its coefficient must vanish. Evaluating on a tangential complex $j$-plane gives the positivity assertion. No top-degree classification, or singular-current smoothing by radial dilation, is asserted here. $\square$

Proposition 6.41.5.F5E0CCA7AC78 Let $T$ be closed positive of bidegree $(c,c)$ on $B(0,R)$, $0\leq c\leq n$, and let $c\leq q\leq n$. Put

\[ Y=\{ (z,S):S\in G(q,n),\ z\in S\} ,\qquad \pi (z,S)=z,\quad \sigma (z,S)=S, \]

and $E=\pi ^{-1}(0)$. The submersion pullback $\pi _0^*T$ on $Y_ R\setminus E$ has locally finite mass across $E$ and its zero extension $\widetilde{\pi _0^*T}$ is closed positive. Define

6A499DD7511C 6.41.6
\begin{equation} \widehat T= \begin{cases} \widetilde{\pi _0^*T},& q>c,\\ \widetilde{\pi _0^*T}+\nu (T,0)[E],& q=c. \end{cases} \end{equation}

For almost every $S$, its parallel slice under $\sigma $ is a closed positive current $T_ S$ on $S\cap B(0,R)$ of bidimension $(d,d)$, $d=q-c$. Away from zero it is the ordinary parallel restriction; if $T$ is smooth, it is the ordinary restriction everywhere. If $q>c$, the closed order-zero extension of $\pi _0^*T$ across $E$, when it exists, is unique.

Proof. The endpoint $q=n$ is the identity construction. If $c=0$, closedness makes $T$ a nonnegative constant on the ball (all its first distributional derivatives vanish, as follows also by mollification); the assertion is immediate, including $q=0$. If $c=n$, only $q=n$ is allowed. We can therefore assume $1\leq c\leq q<n$ and $1\leq p=n-c<n$.

The incidence set is the tautological holomorphic rank-$q$ bundle; its graph charts are those of proposition 10.35.1. For $z\ne 0$, its fibers under $\pi $ are the Grassmannians of $(q-1)$-planes in $\mathbb C^ n/\mathbb Cz$, of complex dimension $N=(q-1)(n-q)$; the same graph charts show that $\pi _0$ is a submersion. Its pullback is the closed positive current of pullback of currents by holomorphic submersions. Choose an invariant Kahler form $\omega _ G$ on the Grassmannian, for example the normalized form pulled back by its Plucker embedding. Then $\omega _ Y=\sigma ^*\omega _ G+\pi ^*\beta $ is Kahler: a vector killed by the first summand is vertical for $\sigma $, and $\pi $ embeds each such vector into its plane. The complex dimension of $Y$ is $n+N$, and the lift's bidimension is $N+p$.

Proper fiber integration over $\pi _0$ gives

\[ (\pi _0)_*\omega _ Y^{N+p} =\sum _{k=0}^ p {N+p\choose k}\, \beta ^ k\wedge (\pi _0)_*(\sigma ^*\omega _ G^{N+p-k}). \]

Terms outside the possible degrees vanish. Each remaining last factor is smooth closed positive, unitary and dilation invariant, of bidegree $(p-k,p-k)$; smoothness follows by local smooth fiber integration over the compact Grassmann fibers. The preceding lemma makes it a nonnegative constant multiple of $\alpha ^{p-k}$. The projection formula follows in submersion coordinates from the coefficient product-measure construction and Fubini, so the lift's mass over $\pi ^{-1}(B(0,r)\setminus \{ 0\} )$ is a finite sum of nonnegative constants times

\[ \int _{0<|z|<r}T\wedge \beta ^ k\wedge \alpha ^{p-k}. \]

For $k=0$ this is $D_ T(0,r)-\nu (T,0)$ by theorem 6.33.1. For $k\geq 1$, $0\leq \alpha \leq |z|^{-2}\beta $ and the ball bound $\mu (B(0,s))\leq C s^{2p}$, $\mu =T\wedge \beta ^ p$, give finiteness. Explicitly the dyadic shell of outer radius $2^{-l}r$ contributes at most $C' (2^{-l}r)^{2k}$; their sum converges. No equality replacing the punctured $k=0$ integral by a full normalized ball mass is used.

The zero section is a proper analytic subset. Apply theorem 5.60.3 with the just-proved local finite mass. This gives the zero extension and hence (6.41.6). Its slices exist by theorem 6.41.2. A smooth $T$ has $\nu (T,0)=0$ if $c>0$, and its smooth pullback has no coefficient mass on $E$, so it agrees with the zero extension.

Finally an order-zero closed difference between two extensions is normal and supported on $E$. Its real dimension is $2(N+p)$, whereas $\dim _{\mathbb R}E=2q(n-q)$; their difference is $2d$. If $q>c$, this is positive, so theorem 5.56.3 makes the difference zero. The theorem applies to complex coefficients by linearity; no positivity of the difference is assumed. $\square$

Lemma 6.41.7.2A216A0952DC For $d\geq 1$, $r_0=p-d\geq 0$, and $0<a<R$,

3B4962C3AEC1 6.41.8
\begin{equation} \int _{0<|z|<a}T\wedge \alpha ^{r_0}\wedge \beta ^ d =a^{2d}D_ T(0,a). \end{equation}

Proof. Put $\rho =|z|^2$. Since $\beta =\rho \alpha +\rho ^{-1}d\rho \wedge d^ c\rho $ and the second summand has square zero, direct expansion gives

\[ \alpha ^{p-d}\wedge \beta ^ d =d\bigl (\rho ^ d d^ c\log \rho \wedge \alpha ^{p-1}\bigr ). \]

For smooth $T$, Stokes on an annulus $b<|z|<a$ and the sphere identity in theorem 6.33.1 give its integral as $a^{2d}D_ T(0,a)-b^{2d}D_ T(0,b)$. For arbitrary $T$, first mollify and pass on annuli whose boundary spheres carry no trace mass. Positivity and smooth coefficient domination there justify this passage. Approximate the endpoints from below to retain the open outer endpoint and closed inner endpoint exactly as in that theorem. Now let $b\downarrow 0$. The density is bounded above at any fixed larger radius, and $d>0$, so the inner term tends to zero. This proves the formula, including every outer radius by continuity from below. $\square$

Proposition 6.41.9.BAA80DBD5575 Let $T_ S$ be the slices just constructed and let $\nu _ q$ be invariant probability on $G(q,n)$. For every $0<a<R$,

D71CDC8687A9 6.41.10
\begin{equation} \int _{G(q,n)}D_{T_ S}(0,a)\, d\nu _ q(S)=D_ T(0,a). \end{equation}

In particular $\int \nu (T_ S,0)\, d\nu _ q(S)\leq \nu (T,0)$.

Proof. The endpoints $c=0$ or $q=n$ are immediate, so use the notation of the preceding incidence proof. The invariant probability is a smooth positive top form on the Grassmannian. Away from zero,

\[ (\pi _0)_*\sigma ^*\nu _ q=\alpha ^{n-q}. \]

Indeed testing a smooth compact form gives the average of its integrals on the planes by ordinary fiber integration over $\sigma $. Thus the identity is precisely the punctured restriction of theorem 5.57.11, with the same constant. Fubini in submersion charts, or the same product-measure projection formula as above, now gives

\[ \int _{G(q,n)}\int _{S\cap B(0,a)\setminus \{ 0\} } T_ S\wedge \beta _ S^ d\, d\nu _ q(S) =\int _{0<|z|<a}T\wedge \alpha ^{n-q}\wedge \beta ^ d. \]

There is no missing mass on the zero section in this formula: the zero extension has no coefficient mass there, and smooth wedging preserves that property. Its integrated slice trace has no mass there by (6.41.3) applied to the Borel zero section. If $d>0$, the individual closed positive slice trace also has no atom at zero by corollary 6.33.5; the added section term occurs only when $d=0$.

For $d>0$, divide the preceding identity by $a^{2d}$ and apply lemma 6.41.7, since $n-q=p-d$. For $d=0$, the slices of $[E]$ are $\delta _0$ and the zero extension's slices have no atom there for almost all $S$, by the same Borel identity. Their added mass is exactly $\nu (T,0)$. The punctured right side is $D_ T(0,a)-\nu (T,0)$, proving the formula in that case as well. Measurability follows from the parallel slicing theorem. As $a\downarrow 0$ the densities converge to their Lelong numbers and are dominated by a fixed larger-radius density whose integral is finite. Dominated convergence in fact gives equality of their averages with $\nu (T,0)$; the stated inequality alone will suffice below. $\square$

Lemma 6.41.11.CE378B92124B Write $z=(w,v)\in \mathbb C^ q\times \mathbb C^ r$, with $r=n-q$, and let $p=r+d$, $1\leq d\leq q$. On the punctured space put

\[ \phi _\varepsilon =\log (|w|^2+|v|^2/\varepsilon ),\quad \gamma _\varepsilon =dd^ c\phi _\varepsilon ,\quad \eta _\varepsilon =d^ c\phi _\varepsilon \wedge \gamma _\varepsilon ^{p-1}. \]

As $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, these odd-degree currents satisfy

FCD4E7278D0F 6.41.12
\begin{equation} \eta _\varepsilon \ \longrightarrow i_*\bigl (d^ c\log |w|^2\wedge \alpha _ S^{d-1}\bigr ) \quad \hbox{on }\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} , \qquad S=\{ v=0\} . \end{equation}

Proof. For $r=0$ this is the identity. For $r>0$, away from $S$ the limit is zero smoothly: after subtracting the irrelevant constant $-\log \varepsilon $, the potential tends smoothly to $\log |v|^2$, and its displayed boundary form of degree $2p-1$ vanishes because $p>r$. Near $S$ in the punctured space, keep $|w|$ bounded below and put $v=\sqrt\varepsilon y$, $a=|w|^2$, $\Phi =a+|y|^2$. Direct differentiation, with the square-zero $d\Phi \wedge d^ c\Phi $ term killed by the first factor, gives

\[ d^ c\log \Phi \wedge (dd^ c\log \Phi )^{p-1} =\Phi ^{-p}d^ c\Phi \wedge (\beta _ w+\beta _ y)^{p-1}. \]

In its pairing with a fixed test form, a coefficient containing $k$ normal real differentials contributes a factor $\varepsilon ^{r-k/2}$ after change of variables. The remaining coefficient is bounded by a constant times $(1+|y|)(a+|y|^2)^{-p}$, integrable in $\mathbb R^{2r}$ uniformly for $a$ in a compact subset of $(0,\infty )$: at infinity its radial bound is $O(t^{-2d})$, and $d\geq 1$. Thus every term with $k<2r$ tends to zero, even with the changing compact support of the scaled test. A term with $k=2r$ must take the tangential part $d^ ca$ and exactly $r$ factors $\beta _ y$. Its integral is

\[ {p-1\choose r}d^ ca\wedge \beta _ w^{d-1} \int _{\mathbb C^ r}\frac{\beta _ y^ r}{(a+|y|^2)^ p} =a^{-d}d^ ca\wedge \beta _ w^{d-1}. \]

For completeness, normalized polar coordinates give the scalar integral as

\[ 2r\int _0^\infty \frac{t^{2r-1}}{(a+t^2)^ p}\, dt =\frac{r!(d-1)!}{(p-1)!}a^{-d}. \]

The beta integral here follows, for integers $r,d\geq 1$, by the substitution $s=t^2/a$ and $u=s/(1+s)$, followed by $r-1$ integrations by parts in $\int _0^1 u^{r-1}(1-u)^{d-1}du$. The binomial coefficient cancels it exactly. The resulting odd form is $d^ c\log a\wedge (dd^ c\log a)^{d-1}$ by the same square-zero expansion. Dominated convergence also replaces the scaled test by its restriction to $v=0$. This proves the identity, with the complex normal orientation and with no uncomputed constant or limiting point mass on this punctured domain. $\square$

Lemma 6.41.13.A33ABBC95853 Suppose $T$ is smooth on $B(0,R)\setminus \{ 0\} $, closed positive on the whole ball, and $q>c$. For any plane $S$ for which its concurrent slice exists,

\[ \nu (T_ S,0)\geq \nu (T,0). \]

Proof. The trivial endpoints having already been treated, take $1\leq p<n$ and unitary coordinates with $S=\{ v=0\} $. Set $r=n-q$ and $d=p-r>0$. Use the potentials of the preceding lemma. For a fixed $\varepsilon >0$, the classical product $\Gamma _\varepsilon =T\wedge \gamma _\varepsilon ^ p$ exists on smaller balls by theorem 7.31.2. Its atom at zero is $\nu (T,0)$: the weight is $\log |z|^2+O_\varepsilon (1)$, so this is corollary 6.34.11 and equation 6.34.4. We need its full boundary primitive, rather than a convergence assertion tested against the possibly singular $T$ at zero.

The coefficient bounds $\gamma _\varepsilon \leq C_\varepsilon |z|^{-2}\beta $ and $|d^ c\phi _\varepsilon |\leq C_\varepsilon |z|^{-1}$, with the ball bound for $\mu =T\wedge \beta ^ p$, show that $T\wedge \eta _\varepsilon $ has integrable coefficients near zero: its dyadic shell mass is $O_\varepsilon (s)$ at outer radius $s$. Likewise $T\wedge \gamma _\varepsilon ^{p-1}$ has shell trace mass $O_\varepsilon (s^2)$, and its multiplication by $\phi _\varepsilon $ has mass $O_\varepsilon (s^2(1+|\log s|))$. These are the actual classical products, agreeing with their smooth punctured formulas, since their positive bidimensions exclude any coefficient atom at zero. Insert cutoffs vanishing on $B(0,s)$ and equal to one outside $B(0,2s)$ in their distributional $d^ c$ identity. The error involving the derivative of the cutoff is $O_\varepsilon (s(1+|\log s|))\to 0$. Therefore the classical coefficient recursion gives, on the whole ball,

\[ \Gamma _\varepsilon =d(T\wedge \eta _\varepsilon ). \]

Choose $0\leq \chi \leq 1$ smooth, equal to one near zero and supported in $B(0,a)$, $a<R$. Keeping the original pole atom yields

\[ \nu (T,0)\leq \Gamma _\varepsilon (\chi ) =-\int d\chi \wedge T\wedge \eta _\varepsilon . \]

On the fixed annular support of $d\chi $, $T$ is smooth, so (6.41.12) legitimately passes this boundary integral to

\[ -\int _ S d\chi \wedge T_ S\wedge d^ c\log |w|^2 \wedge \alpha _ S^{d-1}. \]

Apply the same primitive argument to the closed positive current $T_ S$, smooth off zero and of positive bidimension $d$. This last integral is the full classical radial measure $T_ S\wedge \alpha _ S^ d$ evaluated on $\chi |_ S$, including its origin atom. It is at most $D_{T_ S}(0,a)$ by lemma 6.33.8. Let $a\downarrow 0$. This proves the inequality. No modification or removal of the singularity of $T$ has occurred. $\square$

Theorem 6.41.14.5BC026092C60 For a closed positive current $T$ of bidimension $(p,p)$ on $B(0,R)\subset \mathbb C^ n$, and $n-p\leq q\leq n$, the normalized concurrent slices defined by (6.41.6) satisfy

\[ \nu (T_ S,0)=\nu (T,0) \]

for almost every $S\in G(q,n)$. Their finite-radius densities satisfy (6.41.10).

Proof. For $q=c$, the zero extension's slice measure has no atom at zero for almost every $S$ and the added zero section gives exactly $\nu (T,0)\delta _0$, as proved above. The identity and constant endpoints also follow from that construction. Assume $q>c$ in the remaining nontrivial range.

Let $\rho _ j\geq 0$ be smooth probability densities on $\operatorname {GL}_ n(\mathbb C)$ supported in shrinking neighborhoods of the identity and set

\[ T_ j=\int g^*T\, \rho _ j(g)\, dg. \]

On each smaller ball these are closed positive, tend weakly to $T$, and are smooth away from zero. To verify the last assertion, near $z_0\ne 0$ the map $g\mapsto gz_0$ is a submersion (a matrix variation can send $z_0$ to any prescribed vector). Submersion coordinates for the action, with a finite partition on the compact support of $\rho _ j$, write the averaging operator as integration against smooth kernels in the image variable. Their derivatives in $z$ remain smooth kernels with common compact support locally, so distributional input coefficients produce smooth output coefficients. This also accounts for the smooth matrix factors in pullback. All constructions are on a common smaller ball before passing to a countable exhaustion.

Coordinate invariance in theorem 6.33.10 gives $\nu (g^*T,0)=\nu (T,0)$. This equality passes through the average: with $g$ in the fixed small compact identity neighborhood, change of variables gives

\[ \int _{B(0,a)}g^*T\wedge \beta ^ p =\int _{gB(0,a)}T\wedge (g^{-1})^*\beta ^ p \leq \| g^{-1}\| ^{2p}\int _{B(0,\| g\| a)}T\wedge \beta ^ p. \]

Consequently $D_{g^*T}(0,a)\leq C D_ T(0,C'a)$ uniformly for sufficiently small $a$. Thus these densities have a uniform finite bound, and dominated convergence as $a\downarrow 0$ proves $\nu (T_ j,0)=\nu (T,0)$, including the normalization constant one.

The action lifts to the incidence bundle by $g_ Y(z,S)=(gz,gS)$. For $q>c$, the unique closed order-zero extension property proves the exact identity

\[ \widehat{T_ j}=\int g_ Y^*\widehat T\, \rho _ j(g)\, dg. \]

Both sides are closed positive extensions of the same punctured submersion pullback; the difference is supported on the zero section and is zero by that uniqueness. In particular $\widehat{T_ j}\to \widehat T$ weakly. The parallel slicing subsequence theorem gives $(T_ j)_ S\to T_ S$ weakly for almost every $S$, after one subsequence on all compact fiber charts. The preceding punctured-smooth inequality and upper semicontinuity in theorem 6.33.13 then imply

\[ \nu (T_ S,0)\geq \limsup _ j\nu ((T_ j)_ S,0) \geq \nu (T,0) \]

for almost every $S$. Finally their average is at most $\nu (T,0)$ by the average-density proposition. A nonnegative measurable function bounded below almost everywhere by that constant and having integral at most the same constant equals it almost everywhere. This finishes the proof. $\square$
