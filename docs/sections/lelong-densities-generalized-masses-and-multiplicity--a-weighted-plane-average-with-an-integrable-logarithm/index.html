Lemma 6.44.1.C6BF252FC877 Let $Z\subset (\mathbb C^ n,0)$ be an analytic germ with $\dim _0Z\leq n-p$, where $1\leq p\leq n$. There is a proper analytic subset $E\subset G(p,n)$ such that, for every $S\notin E$, $S\cap Z=\{ 0\} $ as germs, if $0\in Z$. On a relatively compact neighborhood of such an $S$, one radius works uniformly for the absence of other intersection points. The complement of $E$ is connected and $E$ has invariant probability zero.

Proof. Discard any zero-dimensional branches. Blow up the origin in $\mathbb C^ n$, using the universal property of analytic blowup, and take the strict transform of each remaining branch: its analyticity follows from analytic closure after removing an analytic subset. It is pure of the dimension $d$ of that branch, because its dense complement of the exceptional divisor is the original punctured branch. The exceptional divisor contains no branch of the strict transform. The hypersurface-dimension theorem therefore bounds their intersection by dimension $d-1$. In the point-blowup coordinates $(z,[z])$, its fiber $D$ over zero is exactly the compact set of limiting projective directions of $Z\setminus \{ 0\} $. Thus $D$ is a compact analytic subset of $\mathbb P^{n-1}$ of dimension at most $n-p-1$; it is empty when there is no positive-dimensional branch.

The incidence of $\ell \in D$ and $S$ containing the line $\ell $ is analytic. Over a line its Grassmannian fiber has dimension $(p-1)(n-p)$, by graph coordinates. Hence its dimension is at most $p(n-p)-1$. Its projection to $G(p,n)$ is proper, so Remmert's proper mapping theorem, dimension bounds for holomorphic images make its image $E$ a proper analytic subset. This argument uses only a point blowup and analytic incidence, not an algebraicity assertion about an unproved tangent-cone scheme. If $S_ j\to S\notin E$ and nonzero $z_ j\in S_ j\cap Z$ tend to zero, a subsequence of $[z_ j]$ tends to a point of $D\cap \mathbb P(S)$, a contradiction. Compactness gives the uniform-radius assertion. Proper analytic subsets of a complex manifold have smooth volume zero, by analytic stratification. The trace inner product on $\operatorname {Hom}(S,S^\perp )$ defines a unitary-invariant smooth Riemannian metric on the Grassmannian. Its finite normalized volume is invariant, and uniqueness of invariant probability identifies it with $\nu _ p$. Thus $E$ is null. The Grassmannian is connected; the complement of a proper analytic subset of a connected complex manifold remains connected, using the connected punctured coordinate balls in bounded holomorphic removal and connected complements and a chain of such balls. $\square$

Theorem 6.44.2.F3138F8B5A26 Let $F=(F_1,\ldots ,F_ N)$ be holomorphic near zero, with $F(0)=0$ and $\operatorname {codim}_0\{ F=0\} \geq p$, $1\leq p\leq n$. After shrinking the coordinate ball put $u=\log |F|^2$ and $Q=(dd^ cu)^ p$. For invariant probability $d\nu _ p$ on $G(p,n)$, the following is an equality of positive measures on that ball:

CEB19C266692 6.44.3
\begin{equation} Q\wedge (dd^ c\rho )^{n-p} =\int _{G(p,n)}\iota _{S,*} \bigl (dd^ c(u|_ S)\bigr )^ p\, d\nu _ p(S). \end{equation}

The intrinsic slice products exist for almost every $S$ and are measurable, with locally integrable mass. In particular

\[ \nu (Q,0)=\int _{G(p,n)} \bigl ((dd^ c(u|_ S))^ p\bigr )(\{ 0\} )\, d\nu _ p(S). \]

No bound proportional to the volume of a neighborhood of bad planes is needed in this identity.

Proof. Shrink so that $Z=\{ F=0\} $ has dimension at most $n-p$ throughout the ball. The $p$ copies of $u$ and the $n-p$ copies of $\rho $ meet the mutual analytic codimension conditions: a subfamily of $j\leq p$ copies of $u$ has poles in $Z$ of codimension at least $p\geq j$; a subfamily including $\rho $ has common pole set $\{ 0\} $, of codimension $n$. Thus the classical products exist, and $\rho $ is coefficient-integrable against

\[ R_ j=(dd^ cu)^ p\wedge (dd^ c\rho )^ j\qquad (0\leq j<n-p) \]

by theorem 7.28.1.

For a unit vector $a$ write $\ell _ a(z)=\langle z,a\rangle $. The actual logarithmic identity and absolute estimate of lemma 5.57.1 say

\[ \int \log |\ell _ a(z)|^2\, d\sigma (a)=\rho (z)+c_ n, \qquad \int |\log |\ell _ a(z)|^2|\, d\sigma (a) \leq |\rho (z)|+C_ n. \]

Consequently absolute Fubini is legitimate against every coefficient variation of $R_ j$, on a compact test support. Differentiating this integrable weighted-current identity gives

FEF5A57A0E5E 6.44.4
\begin{equation} dd^ c(\rho R_ j) =\int dd^ c\bigl (\log |\ell _ a|^2R_ j\bigr )\, d\sigma (a). \end{equation}

The added constant has zero Hessian because $R_ j$ is closed.

We apply the same argument successively to the averaged currents. For almost every tuple of $k\leq n-p$ linear forms, every intersection involving $j\leq p$ copies of $Z$ and any $l\leq k$ of their hyperplanes has codimension at least $j+l$ when $j+l\leq n$. Here is the precise genericity check. A new hyperplane through zero contains a positive-dimensional analytic component only when its coefficients lie in the proper linear subspace of forms vanishing on that component. There are only countably many components in the fixed ball. Outside this null union, the hypersurface-dimension theorem drops every such component's dimension by one. Zero-dimensional components different from zero are avoided outside another countable null union; the point zero is allowed to persist. Induct, and take the finite intersection of the full-measure conditions for all subfamilies. The forms are also linearly independent almost surely, as the dependent tuples are the zero set of nontrivial minors. All mixed products for these tuples therefore exist, and their symmetry allows the linear divisor factors to be put first. Their product is the integration current of their common linear kernel, with coefficient one, by successive coordinate Poincare–Lelong. Recursion with the remaining $u$ factors is exactly the pushforward of the intrinsic product on that kernel.

For completeness, the successive Fubini step does not assume uniform estimates for each kernel. Suppose the average after $j$ steps is $R_ j$. Positivity and trace domination identify integration of a nonnegative Borel scalar against that average with the iterated integral against its member measures, first for continuous scalars, then for Borel scalars by monotone approximation. Apply this to $|\log |\ell _ a|^2|$ and then average $a$. The displayed absolute bound and the already proved $\rho $-integrability against $R_ j$ give finite total integral. Thus the weighted currents are integrable for almost every tuple; applying $dd^ c$ and the generic mixed-product equality proves the $(j+1)$st step. Weak measurability follows at each step by truncating the logarithm: the truncated scalar is continuous in $(a,z)$, uniformly on compact sets, and can be approximated there by finite sums of separated continuous functions. Passage to its integrable limit then gives measurability of every weighted-current action. Open-ball masses are measurable by increasing continuous tests. This also establishes the Borel and locally integrable mass assertions in the theorem.

At $k=n-p$ the common kernel has dimension $p$. Its distribution is unitary invariant and has total mass one, so the uniqueness of invariant Grassmann probability identifies it with $\nu _ p$. This proves (6.44.3). Apply equality of the measure integrals to the Borel set $\{ 0\} $. The left atom is $\nu (Q,0)$ by the radial classical-pole identity; the product has exactly its full pole mass, as already checked. This proves the last formula without exchanging an uncontrolled weak limit with a point evaluation. $\square$

Theorem 6.44.5.9944A8825328 Under the preceding hypotheses, set

\[ \mu _ p(F,0)=\nu \bigl ((dd^ c\log |F|^2)^ p,0\bigr ). \]

It is a positive integer. For almost every $p$-plane $S$, $F|_ S$ is finite at zero and

\[ \mu _ p(F,0)=d(F|_ S,0). \]

For each such $S$, this is the local sheet number of $PF|_ S$ for a generic cone-adapted $p$-row linear target projection $P$. All numbers are invariant under holomorphic changes of source and target coordinates. Set $\mu _0(F,0)=1$.

Proof. Apply lemma 6.44.1 to $Z=\{ F=0\} $. On the connected complement $M$ of its proper analytic exceptional set, $F|_ S$ has the point fiber and proposition 6.43.1 makes $m(S)=d(F|_ S,0)$ a positive integer. In local holomorphic graph coordinates choose a parametrization $g_ S:\mathbb C^ p\to S$. The function $\log |F(g_ S\zeta )|^2$ is jointly plurisubharmonic, its exponential is smooth, and its poles on one fixed small $\zeta $-ball are precisely $\zeta =0$, uniformly on a smaller parameter neighborhood. The last assertion uses the uniform radius in the lemma, followed by the positive minimum of $|F|$ on the buffered boundary annulus. It proves actual low-level support properness on this fixed domain. Thus theorem 6.39.11 makes every $\{ S:m(S)\geq c\} $ analytic locally on $M$, hence analytic on $M$. Let $b$ be the minimum of these positive integers. The set $\{ m\geq b+1\} $ is a proper analytic subset of the connected $M$, so it has smooth volume zero. Therefore $m(S)=b$ almost everywhere. The weighted plane-average atom formula now gives $\mu _ p(F,0)=b$. The cone-projection clause follows from the finite-germ proposition. Ordinary Lelong coordinate invariance and the bounded-change comparison for target coordinate changes prove the last assertion. The zeroth-power case is the local ambient cycle, with density one. $\square$

Corollary 6.44.6.143206769B43 Let $F:(\mathbb C^ n,0)\to (\mathbb C^ N,0)$ have zero-fiber codimension at least $p$. Put $F^{[a]}=(F,z_1^ a,\ldots ,z_ n^ a)$. For all sufficiently large integers $a$,

\[ \mu _ p(F^{[a]},0)=\mu _ p(F,0). \]

Proof. For $p=0$ both sides are one. Otherwise choose a plane outside the countable union of the null exceptional sets for $F$ and all $F^{[a]}$. On this plane the germ $F|_ S$ has isolated zero. The analytic Nullstellensatz gives powers of its coordinate functions in the ideal generated by $F|_ S$. Bounded coefficients in these finitely many identities give $|F(z)|\geq c|z|^ b$ on $S$ near zero for some integer $b$. For $a\geq b$, the norm of $F^{[a]}|_ S$ is comparable with that of $F|_ S$. Their logarithmic weights differ by a bounded function, so their point masses agree. The generic-slice theorem identifies these point masses with the two asserted multiplicities. No exponent uniform over every plane is required. $\square$
