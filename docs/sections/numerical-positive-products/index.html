13.32 Numerical positive products12DED59717CB

12DED59717CB

Throughout, $X$ is a nonempty connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$. Fix a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$. We use $dd^ c=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial /(2\pi )$, and all products of singular currents are non-pluripolar products. Write $V_\theta $ for the normalized potential with minimal singularities in the big class $\alpha =\{ \theta \} $, and set $\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha )=\int _ X\langle \theta _{V_\theta }^ n\rangle $. For pseudo-effective classes the volume is defined by decreasing Kähler perturbations, as in positive mixed intersection numbers.

Lemma 13.32.1 (Strict gentle approximation of a minimal potential).B89CF500D374 For every big $\alpha =\{ \theta \} $ there are potentials $a_ j$ with gentle rational analytic singularities such that $\theta _{a_ j}$ is a Kähler current and $a_ j\to V_\theta $ in $d_{S,\theta }$.

Proof. Choose a Kähler current $\theta _ v\geq \delta \omega $ in $\alpha $. Minimality permits a constant shift making $v\leq V_\theta $. For integers $l>1$, put $z_ l=(1-l^{-1})V_\theta +l^{-1}v$. These potentials increase almost everywhere to $V_\theta $, and $\theta _{z_ l}\geq (\delta /l)\omega $. By increasing multiplier-ideal envelopes, increasing continuity in the singularity metric, increasing multiplier envelopes and increasing $d_ S$ continuity give

\[ P_\theta [z_ l]_{\mathcal I}\longrightarrow V_\theta \quad \hbox{in }d_{S,\theta }. \]

Here $P_\theta [V_\theta ]_{\mathcal I}=V_\theta $: the minimal potential is a normalized competitor for this envelope and dominates every normalized $\theta $-psh competitor, even before the multiplier condition is imposed.

For fixed $l$, apply gentle quasi-equisingular approximation to $z_ l$ with background $\theta -(\delta /l)\omega $. On a tail its currents in background $\theta $ are at least $(\delta /2l)\omega $. The strict-current argument in the proof of analytic approximation of multiplier-ideal-good singularities shows that this quasi-equisingular sequence converges in $d_{S,\theta }$ to $P_\theta [z_ l]_{\mathcal I}$, without assuming that $z_ l$ is $\mathcal I$-good. The chosen regularization has gentle analytic singularities, so its residual on a resolution is smooth. Choose one member $a_ l$ at distance below $1/l$ from this multiplier envelope. The triangle inequality proves the asserted convergence. $\square$

Lemma 13.32.2 (Simultaneous exceptional correction).85B840A3A734 Let $\pi :Y\to X$ be a finite composite of smooth-center blowups. There is an effective rational exceptional divisor $F$ and a Kähler form $\gamma $ in $\pi ^*\{ \omega \} -\{ F\} $. If finitely many smooth closed forms satisfy $S_ i\geq \delta _ i\pi ^*\omega $, with $\delta _ i>0$, then

\[ S_{i,t}=S_ i+t(\gamma -\pi ^*\omega ) \]

are Kähler for $0<t\leq \min _ i\delta _ i$, and their classes are $\{ S_ i\} -t\{ F\} $. Any fixed finite list of their mixed intersection numbers, including intersections with fixed smooth pulled-back forms, converges to the corresponding uncorrected list as $t\downarrow 0$.

Proof. For one blowup Kähler metrics on smooth-center blowups gives a Kähler class $\pi ^*\{ \omega \} -q\{ E\} $ for every sufficiently small $q>0$; choose $q$ rational. Inductively pull back the divisor already chosen and add a sufficiently small positive rational multiple of the new exceptional divisor. Pullback preserves effectiveness, and the same blowup construction preserves a Kähler representative. Isomorphism steps add nothing. For the identity map take $F=0$, $\gamma =\omega $. Now $S_{i,t}\geq (\delta _ i-t)\pi ^*\omega +t\gamma >0$. Each claimed integral is a polynomial in $t$, so finite simultaneous control follows by taking one sufficiently small positive rational $t$. No bound uniform over all modifications is asserted or used. $\square$

Theorem 13.32.3 (Same-class simultaneous approximation with finite tests).AD36F0CA2A0B Let $\alpha _1,\ldots ,\alpha _ r$ be big classes, and let $\eta _1,\ldots ,\eta _ N$ be fixed smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms. There are finite smooth-center modifications $\pi _ j:Y_ j\to X$, Kähler forms $\kappa _{i,j}$, and effective rational divisors $E_{i,j}$ such that

\[ \pi _ j^*\alpha _ i=A_{i,j}+\{ E_{i,j}\} ,\qquad A_{i,j}=\{ \kappa _{i,j}\} . \]

Every top mixed intersection, with repetitions, converges to the mixed mass of currents with minimal singularities in the corresponding classes. Moreover, for every $(i_1,\ldots ,i_{n-1})$ and every $b$,

\[ \int _{Y_ j}\kappa _{i_1,j}\wedge \cdots \wedge \kappa _{i_{n-1},j} \wedge \pi _ j^*\eta _ b \longrightarrow \int _ X\langle T_{i_1,\min }\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{i_{n-1},\min }\rangle \wedge \eta _ b. \]

For $n=1$ the latter expression means the empty product $1$; it is already unchanged under a modification.

Proof. For each $i$, choose the strict gentle sequence of the first lemma in a fixed smooth representative $\theta _ i$ of $\alpha _ i$. For top mixed masses their convergence follows from continuity of mixed masses in the singularity metric. To obtain each additional test, choose $C_ b$ so large that $C_ b\omega +\eta _ b$ is Kähler, and write $\eta _ b=(C_ b\omega +\eta _ b)-C_ b\omega $. Use continuity of mixed masses in the singularity metric again with the final entry the fixed smooth potential $0$ in each of these two Kähler backgrounds. All backgrounds in these applications are big, as required. There are finitely many tests and index tuples, so one common diagonal selection suffices.

Apply principalization of a coherent ideal successively to all chosen gentle ideal types, retaining the accumulated boundary divisor. This produces a common smooth-center modification on which

\[ \pi _ j^*\theta _{i,a_{i,j}}=S_{i,j}+[B_{i,j}], \]

where $B_{i,j}$ is effective rational and $S_{i,j}$ is smooth. By gentle analytic singularities, the residual is smooth on a resolution of each ideal; it remains smooth under the later pullbacks. Off the divisor strictness implies $S_{i,j}\geq \delta _{i,j}\pi _ j^*\omega $ for some $\delta _{i,j}>0$; smoothness extends this inequality across the divisor. By the local calculus of non-pluripolar products, non-pluripolar products under modifications, locality, divisorial removal and modification invariance identify every uncorrected mixed integral with the corresponding downstairs non-pluripolar mixed mass. Smooth densities charge no divisor.

Apply the preceding correction lemma on this common model. Choose a positive rational $t_ j\leq \min _ i\delta _{i,j}$ so small that every top integral and every listed test changes by less than $1/j$. Take $\kappa _{i,j}=S_{i,j}+t_ j(\gamma _ j-\pi _ j^*\omega )$ and $E_{i,j}=B_{i,j}+t_ jF_ j$. The class identities are exact, while all claimed limits follow from the diagonal convergence and this error bound. In particular no curvature margin, exceptional coefficient or intersection polynomial is assumed uniformly bounded in $j$. $\square$

Definition 13.32.4 (The movable class in bidimension one).9CAC26397732 For big classes $\alpha _1,\ldots ,\alpha _{n-1}$ set

\[ \langle \alpha _1\cdots \alpha _{n-1}\rangle =\{ \langle T_{1,\min }\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1,\min }\rangle \} \in H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbb R). \]

For $n=1$, the definition is the class of $1$ in $H^{0,0}$.

Proposition 13.32.5 (Numerical cofinality and movability).37818757E0D6 The preceding class is independent of the chosen minimal currents. One can choose the same-class approximations so that, simultaneously,

\[ A_{i,j}^ n\longrightarrow \operatorname {Vol}(\alpha _ i),\qquad (\pi _ j)_*(A_{i_1,j}\cdots A_{i_{n-1},j}) \longrightarrow \langle \alpha _{i_1}\cdots \alpha _{i_{n-1}}\rangle . \]

The latter limit is in cohomology, not an assertion of monotonicity or convergence of particular current representatives. It belongs to the closed cone $\mathcal M$ generated by pushforwards of products of Kähler forms on modifications.

More precisely, for every Kähler class $\beta $, pairing the movable class with $\beta $ is the supremum of the corresponding mixed nef-model intersections with $\pi ^*\beta $, over simultaneous decompositions $\pi ^*\alpha _ i=A_ i+\{ E_ i\} $ with $A_ i$ Kähler and $E_ i$ effective rational. The analogous supremum of $A^ n$ equals $\operatorname {Vol}(\alpha )$.

Proof. By mass comparison for bounded singularity differences, bounded-type mixed-mass invariance, with one final smooth Kähler factor, makes every pairing with a Kähler class independent of the minimal representatives. Kähler classes span $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$: for any smooth closed real $\eta $, both $C\omega +\eta $ and $C\omega $ are Kähler for sufficiently large $C$. By Hodge theory and integration duality for a flat bundle, the real Kähler Hodge decomposition, the complementary-type Poincaré pairing is nondegenerate and the cohomology spaces are finite dimensional. These identifications also hold for closed currents: extend the Green operators to distributions by duality and transpose their identities, obtaining $S=\Pi S+\mathrm d(\mathrm d^*GS)$ for a closed current $S$, with $\Pi S$ smooth harmonic. Thus the pairing proves independence of the cohomology class. Choose finitely many smooth closed $\eta _ b$ representing a basis of $H^{1,1}$. The finite-test theorem proves the displayed convergence; each approximant belongs to the generating set of $\mathcal M$, so closedness gives membership in $\mathcal M$.

For any admissible model let $R_ i=\pi _*(\kappa _ i+[E_ i])$. Its class is $\alpha _ i$, and it is closed positive. On the isomorphism locus of $\pi $, removing the divisors in the non-pluripolar product identifies its mixed product with that of the $\kappa _ i$. These currents have no mass on the excluded analytic set: downstairs this is the non-pluripolar property, and upstairs the trace against $\pi ^*\beta $ is a smooth density giving every analytic set zero mass. Thus their total mixed mass against $\beta $ equals $\int _ Y\kappa _1\cdots \kappa _{n-1}\pi ^*\beta $. The minimal-current bound of the positive mixed-mass bound puts this below the claimed pairing. The finite-test construction, with $\beta $ included, attains the bound in the limit. The same argument in top degree proves the volume supremum. This is cofinality for the relevant numerical suprema; no claim that the sequence eventually dominates each divisorial singularity type is needed. $\square$

Proposition 13.32.6 (Order and boundary limits).B28D656FB542 Pairings of $\langle \alpha _1\cdots \alpha _{n-1}\rangle $ with a fixed Kähler class are nonnegative, homogeneous in each argument, superadditive in each argument, and increase when a pseudo-effective class is added to any argument. The class-valued product is continuous on the product of big cones. For pseudo-effective classes it has the well-defined extension

\[ \langle \alpha _1\cdots \alpha _{n-1}\rangle =\lim _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \langle (\alpha _1+\varepsilon \{ \omega \} )\cdots (\alpha _{n-1}+\varepsilon \{ \omega \} )\rangle . \]

The limit is independent of the perturbing Kähler class, agrees with the big definition, and lies in $\mathcal M$. Boundary convergence is asserted for these decreasing Kähler perturbations; arbitrary continuity at every boundary point is not asserted.

Proof. Nonnegativity, homogeneity and pseudo-effective order follow from the positive mixed-mass bound, choosing the reference Kähler metric to represent the test class. Superadditivity follows by adding two minimal currents in the replaced argument: the minimal current of the sum class has at least their mixed mass, and multilinearity expands that mass into the two original terms.

Fix a big class $\alpha $ with $\alpha -\delta \{ \omega \} $ pseudo-effective, $\delta >0$. If $\beta \to \alpha $, finite-dimensional form comparison gives $-\varepsilon \{ \omega \} \leq \beta -\alpha \leq \varepsilon \{ \omega \} $ in pseudo-effective order, with $\varepsilon \to 0$. For $\varepsilon <\delta $ this implies

\[ (1-\varepsilon /\delta )\alpha \leq \beta \leq (1+\varepsilon /\delta )\alpha . \]

Indeed $(\varepsilon /\delta )\alpha -\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $ is pseudo-effective. Apply these bounds in every argument and use homogeneity and order. Each pairing with a fixed Kähler class is squeezed between factors tending to $1$ times its value at the original tuple. A finite Kähler spanning family proves cohomological continuity.

At the boundary these pairings decrease as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ and are nonnegative, so they have finite limits. A finite Kähler basis defines the limiting class, and every other pairing converges by linearity. Two Kähler forms satisfy $c^{-1}\omega \leq \omega '\leq c\omega $ for some $c>0$; the same order squeeze proves independence of the perturbation, including different fixed positive coefficients in different arguments. For big tuples the already proved continuity gives agreement. Finally $\mathcal M$ is closed and contains every big perturbation. $\square$
