9.38 Continuation on normal complex spaces48351356D0F9

48351356D0F9

All complex spaces in this section are reduced, Hausdorff and have a countable basis. Normality is understood stalkwise; in particular, every normal point has an irreducible germ. A meromorphic function is a section of the sheaf of total rings of fractions of the structure sheaf, so its local denominators are nonzerodivisors. On a normal space the local rings are domains. These are the conventions of reduced spaces and normalization.

Corollary 9.38.1.512196636E86 Let $X$ be a normal complex space, and let $A\subset X$ be an analytic subset such that

\[ \dim (A,x)\leq \dim (X,x)-2\qquad (x\in A). \]

Every holomorphic function on $X\setminus A$ has a unique holomorphic extension to $X$.

Proof. The assertion is local at points of $A$. Near such a point, $X$ is an irreducible pure-dimensional normal space. The codimension hypothesis therefore holds on each local branch. If $f$ is holomorphic off $A$, the function $|f|^2$ there is locally the restriction of the squared modulus of an ambient holomorphic function, hence is locally plurisubharmonic. By upper bounds near analytic subsets of codimension at least two it is locally bounded above near $A$. Thus $f$ is locally bounded, and the bounded extension on normal spaces in the normal locus and bounded holomorphic continuation extends it holomorphically. The complement of $A$ is dense; uniqueness follows from continuity, so the local extensions glue. In local dimensions zero and one the hypothesis forces $A$ to be empty. $\square$

For a meromorphic function $f$, its pole set is $P_ f=\{ x:f_ x\notin \mathcal O_{X,x}\} $. This is a failure to be a holomorphic germ, not merely the zero set of a chosen denominator.

Lemma 9.38.2.DA978F0F96EA On a normal complex space $X$, the pole set of a meromorphic function is a nowhere dense analytic subset. Near a point where $X$ has dimension $n$, this pole set is either empty or pure of dimension $n-1$.

Proof. Work on a small irreducible normal neighborhood on which $f=g/h$, with $g,h$ holomorphic and $h$ a nonzerodivisor. Define the ideal sheaf

\[ \mathcal J=\{ u\in \mathcal O_ X:uf\in \mathcal O_ X\} . \]

It is the first-coordinate image of the kernel of $(g,-h):\mathcal O_ X^2\to \mathcal O_ X$, since $ug=vh$ is equivalent to $uf=v$. Consequently it is coherent by kernels and exact sequences of coherent modules. Its definition is independent of the representation of $f$, and

\[ P_ f=\{ x:\mathcal J_ x\ne \mathcal O_{X,x}\} =\operatorname {Supp}(\mathcal O_ X/\mathcal J). \]

The coherent-support criterion proved in the normal locus and bounded holomorphic continuation makes this set analytic. It is contained in $\{ h=0\} $, which is nowhere dense because $h$ is a nonzerodivisor.

Suppose that a local branch $D$ of $P_ f$ has dimension at most $n-2$. Choose a regular point of a small representative of $D$ outside its other pole branches. After shrinking there, the whole pole set has dimension at most $n-2$. On its complement $f$ is holomorphic, so corollary 9.38.1 gives a holomorphic extension across it. This extension agrees with $f$ as a meromorphic function: after multiplying a local difference by a nonzero denominator, its holomorphic numerator vanishes on a dense set and hence everywhere. The chosen point is therefore not a pole, a contradiction. No branch has dimension $n$, since $P_ f$ is nowhere dense. Thus all nonempty pole branches have dimension $n-1$. This also includes isolated poles when $n=1$; for $n=0$ the local ring is $\mathbb C$ and there are no poles. $\square$

Theorem 9.38.3.3F94BF9B4F4B For a normal complex space $X$, the singular locus satisfies

\[ \dim (X_{\mathrm{sing}},x)\leq \dim (X,x)-2 \qquad (x\in X_{\mathrm{sing}}). \]

Proof. A zero-dimensional reduced germ is a point, and a normal one-dimensional space is regular by parametrization of irreducible analytic curves. We may therefore work on an irreducible pure $n$-dimensional normal neighborhood, $n\geq 2$. The singular locus is analytic of smaller dimension by analytic singular strata and Hausdorff dimension. Suppose that it has a branch of dimension $n-1$. Choose a regular point of that branch outside the others and shrink once more. We obtain a neighborhood $U$ for which $D=U_{\mathrm{sing}}$ is a nonempty connected complex manifold of dimension $n-1$, closed and analytic in $U$.

Take the proper bimeromorphic resolution $\pi :M\to U$ of the stated resolution-of-singularities input. Its image is closed and contains the dense regular locus, so it is surjective. Every component of the manifold $M$ meets the dense isomorphism locus and therefore has dimension $n$. The analytic set $E=\pi ^{-1}(D)$ is nowhere dense by nowhere-dense inverse images under bimeromorphic maps, so all its branches have dimension at most $n-1$.

Fix a compact neighborhood $K$ of a point in the manifold $D$, with nonempty relative interior. By local finiteness of irreducible components and compactness of $\pi ^{-1}(K)$, only finitely many irreducible components $E_1,\ldots ,E_ s$ of $E$ meet this inverse image. Each $E_ j$ is closed in $M$, so its restriction of $\pi $ is proper; hence $\pi (E_ j)$ is analytic by Remmert's proper mapping theorem. If this image is not all of $D$, it has dimension at most $n-2$. Call $E_ j$ dominating if $\pi (E_ j)=D$. Such a component has dimension $n-1$, since the dimension of an analytic image is at most that of its source by dimension bounds for holomorphic images.

We describe an analytic exceptional subset $Q_ j$ of each dominating $E_ j$, including its singular locus and the critical points of $\pi |_{(E_ j)_{\mathrm{reg}}}$, by local determinants. Since $E_ j$ is a hypersurface in the smooth $n$-fold $M$, a local generator $e$ of its reduced ideal exists by local equations and currents of irreducible hypersurfaces. The same lemma gives $\mathrm de\ne 0$ exactly on the regular part of $E_ j$: there it is a unit times a transverse coordinate, and a nonzero differential conversely gives a submanifold by the implicit function theorem. Near $\pi (y)\in D$, choose ambient holomorphic functions $t_1,\ldots ,t_{n-1}$ whose restrictions are coordinates on $D$. Define $Q_ j$ locally on $E_ j$ by

22FF23DD18BA 9.38.4
\begin{equation} \mathrm de\wedge \mathrm d(t_1\circ \pi )\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm d(t_{n-1}\circ \pi )=0. \end{equation}

In a chart of $M$ this is the vanishing of one holomorphic determinant, together with $e=0$. At a singular point of $E_ j$, $\mathrm de=0$. At a regular point, the determinant vanishes precisely when the restriction of $\mathrm d\pi $ to $T E_ j$ has rank less than $n-1$. Thus its zero set is intrinsic on the regular part and contains the whole singular locus, independently of these choices. The local analytic sets agree on overlaps and define a closed analytic subset $Q_ j$ of $E_ j$.

This subset is proper. Otherwise the differential of $\pi $ on $(E_ j)_{\mathrm{reg}}$ would have rank at most $n-2$ everywhere. Its image would be $\mathcal H^{2n-2}$-null by dimension bounds for holomorphic images; the image of the singular locus is also null, by its smaller dimension and the analytic stratification of analytic singular strata and Hausdorff dimension. Countably many charts suffice for these conclusions. The union could not equal the manifold $D$, contradicting dominance. Consequently $\dim Q_ j\leq n-2$, by purity and dimension of irreducible analytic sets. Its proper image $\pi (Q_ j)$ is analytic of dimension at most $n-2$ by the same image theorems.

Choose $a$ in the relative interior of $K$ outside the finite union of the sets $\pi (E_ j)$ for nondominating $E_ j$ and $\pi (Q_ j)$ for dominating ones. All these sets have smaller dimension than $D$, so such $a$ exists. Every point $y\in \pi ^{-1}(a)$ lies on one of the listed components, and every listed component through $y$ is dominating, with $y\notin Q_ j$. Near $y$ its map to $D$ is therefore a local biholomorphism, by the holomorphic inverse function theorem. Local finiteness allows a neighborhood of $y$ that meets only the finitely many components through $y$; their fibers over $a$ are isolated there. Thus the whole compact fiber $\pi ^{-1}(a)$ is discrete and hence finite.

Take pairwise disjoint coordinate balls $B_1,\ldots ,B_ l$ around its points. Properness gives a neighborhood $V$ of $a$ with $\pi ^{-1}(V)\subset \bigcup _ i B_ i$: use $V=U\setminus \pi (M\setminus \bigcup _ i B_ i)$ and shrink if necessary. Each fiber over $V$ is compact, and its part in any $B_ i$ is compact as well, since the balls are disjoint and cover that fiber. It is a compact analytic subset of a coordinate ball, hence finite by the maximum principle on analytic branches. Therefore $\pi :\pi ^{-1}(V)\to V$ is finite and bimeromorphic. The target is normal, so finite bimeromorphic maps to normal spaces makes it an isomorphism. Since its source is smooth, $a$ is regular in $U$, contradicting $a\in D$. No singular branch of codimension one can occur. $\square$

Theorem 9.38.5.F35BA7139EC4 Let $X$ be a normal complex space, and let $A\subset X$ be analytic with $\dim (A,x)\leq \dim (X,x)-2$ for every $x\in A$. Every meromorphic function on $X\setminus A$ extends uniquely to a meromorphic function on $X$.

Proof. Uniqueness is local. The difference of two extensions has, near any point, a nonzero holomorphic denominator and a holomorphic numerator that vanishes on a dense subset of $X\setminus A$. The numerator vanishes everywhere by continuity. Thus it suffices to construct an extension near each $x\in A$. Work on an irreducible pure $n$-dimensional normal neighborhood $U$ of $x$. Here $n\geq 2$, since $A$ is empty in the lower dimensions, and shrink so that $\dim (A\cap U)\leq n-2$.

Let $f$ be the given meromorphic function. By lemma 9.38.2, its pole set $P\subset U\setminus A$ is either empty or pure of dimension $n-1$. The Remmert–Stein theorem the Remmert–Stein extension theorem makes its closure $D$ in $U$ analytic. Every local branch of $D$ has dimension $n-1$ and is not contained in $A$. Indeed, branches contained in $A$ could be discarded without changing the dense subset $P$, contradicting irredundancy of the branch decomposition; every remaining branch has regular points outside $A$ and the other branches, where $D$ coincides with $P$, and purity fixes its dimension. Shrink $U$ so that $D$ has only finitely many irreducible components $D_1,\ldots ,D_ r$, all meeting $x$. Such a shrinking follows from local finiteness of global components. If $D$ is empty, $f$ is holomorphic on $U\setminus A$ and corollary 9.38.1 already gives the required extension.

Since $(D,x)$ is a proper analytic subgerm of $(U,x)$, some local holomorphic defining function has a nonzero germ in $\mathcal O_{U,x}$. Choose a representative $h$ and shrink so that $h$ vanishes on all of $D$. The function $h$ is not identically zero on any nonempty open subset of the irreducible space $U$, by the identity theorem on its connected regular locus and density. Its germ is therefore a nonzerodivisor at every point of $U$.

Each $D_ j\setminus A$ is irreducible. To see this, its regular locus contains $(D_ j)_{\mathrm{reg}}\setminus A$, which is connected by bounded holomorphic removal and connected complements, since $(D_ j)_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is connected and $A$ meets it in a proper analytic subset. This set is dense in $D_ j\setminus A$. A decomposition into two proper closed analytic subsets would restrict to a decomposition of that connected manifold into two proper analytic subsets, which is impossible; if one contained the whole manifold, density would make it all of $D_ j\setminus A$.

By theorem 9.38.3, each $D_ j$ has regular points of $U$ outside $A$ and the other components. Choose such a point on its own regular locus. In a manifold coordinate neighborhood there, write $D_ j=\{ \tau =0\} $ and $f=a/b$ with $a,b$ holomorphic and $b\ne 0$. Expanding in $\tau $ gives $b=\tau ^{m_ j}b_0$ with $m_ j\geq 0$ finite and $b_0|_{\{ \tau =0\} }$ not identically zero. Move the chosen point slightly within $D_ j$, keeping it away from $A$, the other components and $U_{\mathrm{sing}}$, so that $b_0$ is nonzero. Call the resulting point $q_ j$. Then $\tau ^{m_ j}f$ is holomorphic near $q_ j$. Since $h$ vanishes on $D_ j$, the coordinate ideal formula gives $h=\tau h_1$ there, and $h^{m_ j}f$ is holomorphic near $q_ j$ as well. This construction uses only one local quotient at each of the finitely many components; no global numerator or denominator for $f$ on $U\setminus A$ is assumed.

Put $m=\max (1,m_1,\ldots ,m_ r)$ and $g=h^ m f$ on $U\setminus A$. Its pole set is contained in $P$, is pure of dimension $n-1$ when nonempty, and avoids each $q_ j$. Every irreducible component of this pole set is contained in some $D_ j\setminus A$, by the finite-union property for irreducible analytic sets. Both have dimension $n-1$; by irreducibility of $D_ j\setminus A$ and strict dimension drop, that component must be all of $D_ j\setminus A$. This contradicts holomorphy near $q_ j$. Thus $g$ has no poles and is holomorphic on $U\setminus A$.

Extend $g$ holomorphically to $G$ on $U$ by corollary 9.38.1. The quotient $G/h^ m$ is meromorphic on $U$, since every germ of $h$ is a nonzerodivisor, and it agrees with $f$ as a meromorphic function on $U\setminus A$. The local extensions glue by uniqueness. $\square$
