9.13 Holomorphic hulls and domains of holomorphyB07D353797AE

B07D353797AE

For a subset $K$ of a complex manifold $X$, write

\[ \widehat K_ X=\{ x\in X:|f(x)|\leq \sup _ K|f| \text{ for every }f\in \mathcal O(X)\} . \]

The manifold is holomorphically convex if the hull of every compact $K$ is compact. The hull is closed in $X$, contains $K$, and applying its inequalities twice gives the same hull. Holomorphic maps take hulls into hulls, by composition of the testing functions. In an open subset of $\mathbb C^ n$ the hull is bounded: the coordinate functions bound every coordinate.

Proposition 9.13.1.36B688C7F361 A second countable holomorphically convex complex manifold has a smooth nonnegative plurisubharmonic exhaustion function. On a holomorphically convex open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, arbitrary values may be interpolated on any closed discrete set of distinct points.

Proof. Choose compact sets $K_\nu $ exhausting $X$ such that $K_\nu =\widehat K_\nu $ and $K_\nu \subset \operatorname {int}K_{\nu +1}$. To construct them, take at each stage the hull of the union of a compact neighborhood of the previous set with the next member of an ordinary compact exhaustion. For every point of $L_\nu =K_{\nu +2}\setminus \operatorname {int}K_{\nu +1}$, separation from $K_\nu $ gives a holomorphic $g$ with $\sup _{K_\nu }|g|<1$ and $|g|>1$ near that point. A finite selection $g_{\nu ,l}$ covers $L_\nu $. Raising all these functions to a common sufficiently large power $p_\nu $ gives

\[ \sum _ l|g_{\nu ,l}|^{2p_\nu }\leq 2^{-\nu }\text{ on }K_\nu , \qquad \sum _ l|g_{\nu ,l}|^{2p_\nu }\geq \nu \text{ on }L_\nu . \]

Their sum over $\nu $ converges locally uniformly to a continuous nonnegative plurisubharmonic function $\rho $. It is an exhaustion: outside $K_{N+1}$, a point first enters some $K_ m$ with $m\geq N+2$, so it lies in $L_{m-2}$ and $\rho \geq m-2\geq N$. The sum is in fact smooth. In a relatively compact coordinate polydisc, regard $(g_{\nu ,l}^{p_\nu })_ l$ as a finite Hilbert-valued holomorphic function. Its squared norm is at most $2^{-\nu }$ on a fixed larger polydisc for all sufficiently large $\nu $. The iterated Cauchy integral and the triangle inequality for that norm bound every derivative on the smaller polydisc by $C_\alpha 2^{-\nu /2}$, independently of the number of components. Cauchy–Schwarz and the product rule then bound each derivative of its squared norm by $C'_{\alpha ,\beta }2^{-\nu }$. Thus every differentiated series converges locally uniformly, proving smoothness.

For interpolation in $\Omega $, let $(z_ j)$ be distinct with no accumulation in $\Omega $, and let $a_ j\in \mathbb C$. Put $K_0=\varnothing $ and let $\nu (j)$ be determined by $z_ j\in K_{\nu (j)+1}\setminus K_{\nu (j)}$. Then $\nu (j)\to \infty $. Choose $g_ j\in \mathcal O(\Omega )$ with $g_ j(z_ j)=1$ and $\sup _{K_{\nu (j)}}|g_ j|<1$; for $\nu (j)=0$ take $g_ j=1$. A polynomial $P_ j$ with $P_ j(z_ j)=1$ and $P_ j(z_ i)=0$ for $i<j$ is obtained by multiplying linear functions that separate $z_ j$ from these finitely many other points and normalizing. Inductively choose

\[ \lambda _ j=a_ j-\sum _{i<j}\lambda _ iP_ i(z_ j)g_ i(z_ j)^{m_ i}, \qquad \sup _{K_{\nu (j)}}|\lambda _ jP_ jg_ j^{m_ j}|\leq 2^{-j}. \]

The second inequality follows by taking $m_ j$ large, once $\lambda _ j$ is fixed. The resulting series converges normally on $\Omega $ and has the required values, since every later polynomial vanishes at each earlier interpolation point. $\square$

A domain of holomorphy here means an open set $\Omega $ for which there are no connected open set $U$ crossing its boundary and nonempty open $V\subset U\cap \Omega $ such that every function in $\mathcal O(\Omega )$ extends from $V$ to $U$. A domain of existence of a single function is defined by the same condition for that function. A distance function $\delta $ will mean a continuous function on $\mathbb C^ n$, positive off zero, with $\delta (tz)=|t|\delta (z)$; convexity or the triangle inequality is not required. Put

\[ \delta _\Omega (z)=\inf _{w\notin \Omega }\delta (z-w), \qquad \inf \varnothing =\infty . \]

There are positive constants $c,C$ with $c|z|\leq \delta (z)\leq C|z|$, by compactness of the Euclidean unit sphere. When the complement is nonempty, the distance is continuous and positive on $\Omega $: locally its infimum may be restricted to a fixed bounded compact part of the complement, and uniform continuity there proves continuity. Its open balls are translates and dilates of the balanced open set $B_\delta =\{ \delta <1\} $.

Theorem 9.13.2.8FBCDB96A2E2 For an open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ and any distance function $\delta $, the following conditions are equivalent:

  1. $\Omega $ is a domain of holomorphy;

  2. $\Omega $ is holomorphically convex;

  3. for every compact $K\subset \Omega $ and $f\in \mathcal O(\Omega )$, $|f|\leq \delta _\Omega $ on $K$ implies $|f|\leq \delta _\Omega $ on $\widehat K_\Omega $;

  4. $\Omega $ is a domain of existence of one holomorphic function.

Moreover a domain of holomorphy satisfies $\inf _{\widehat K_\Omega }\delta _\Omega =\inf _ K\delta _\Omega $.

Proof. Assume the first condition, and fix $K,f$ as in the third condition. For $h\in \mathcal O(\Omega )$, $\xi \in B_\delta $ and $k\geq 0$, the function

\[ A_{k,\xi }(z)=\frac1{k!}D^ kh(z)(f(z)\xi )^ k \]

is holomorphic in $z$. For $0<r<1$, the points $z+t f(z)\xi $, with $z\in K$, $|t|\leq r$ and $\xi \in \overline B_\delta $, form a compact subset of $\Omega $. Indeed their $\delta $-displacement is at most $r\delta _\Omega (z)$; none can be in the complement. Cauchy's estimate on the parameter disc gives $|A_{k,\xi }|\leq M_ r r^{-k}$ on $K$, uniformly in $\xi $, where $M_ r$ bounds $h$ on that compact set. The hull inequalities transfer this bound to $\widehat K_\Omega $.

At $a\in \widehat K_\Omega $ with $f(a)\ne 0$, the homogeneous Taylor series for $h$ therefore converges normally on $a+f(a)B_\delta $: on every compact subset the ratio $\delta (z-a)/|f(a)|$ is bounded by some number less than $r<1$. It agrees with $h$ near $a$. This gives, for every $h$, an extension from a common neighborhood of $a$ to that connected balanced open set. The domain-of-holomorphy condition forces the latter to be contained in $\Omega $. Hence $|f(a)|\leq \delta _\Omega (a)$. The same conclusion is automatic if $f(a)=0$, proving the third condition. Taking constant $f$ of any modulus smaller than $\inf _ K\delta _\Omega $ also proves the final distance equality. In particular the hull has positive Euclidean distance from the complement. It is bounded and closed in $\Omega $, so compact. This proves the second condition; the third condition alone gives the same argument.

Under the second condition the preceding proposition gives arbitrary discrete interpolation. We show that it supplies the fourth condition, including extension from just one local component. Give $\mathcal O(\Omega )$ its complete metrizable compact-open topology; completeness follows from the locally uniform Cauchy formula. Consider the countably many pairs of rational balls $b,B$ with $\overline b\subset B\cap \Omega $ and $B\not\subset \Omega $. For an integer $N\geq 1$, let $E_{b,B,N}$ consist of functions admitting an extension from $b$ to $B$ bounded there by $N$. This set is closed: a convergent sequence of its functions has, by Montel on $B$, a subsequence of bounded extensions converging locally uniformly, with the correct restriction to $b$.

It has empty interior. Let $W$ be the component of $B\cap \Omega $ containing $b$. It has a boundary point $a$ inside $B$, since it is a nonempty proper open subset of the connected ball $B$; such a point lies in $\partial \Omega $. Choose distinct $z_ j\in W$ tending to $a$. They have no accumulation in $\Omega $, so interpolation gives $h(z_ j)=j$. This $h$ cannot extend from $b$ to $B$, since the identity theorem would make its extension equal to $h$ on $W$. If $E_{b,B,N}$ contained a neighborhood of $f_0$, then both $f_0$ and $f_0+t h$ would be in it for sufficiently small nonzero $t$; subtracting their extensions would extend $h$, a contradiction.

The Baire theorem now gives a function outside all these closed sets. Here Baire follows directly by choosing nested closed balls in a complete metric, with radii tending to zero and each ball disjoint from the next specified nowhere dense set. If this function extended from a nonempty open subset of $U\cap \Omega $ to a connected open $U$ crossing the boundary, identity would give equality on the corresponding component $W$ of $U\cap \Omega $. Choose $a\in \partial W\cap U$, a rational ball $B\Subset U$ containing $a$, and a rational ball $b\Subset W\cap B$. The extension is bounded on $B$ after choosing its closure in $U$, contrary to avoidance of $E_{b,B,N}$. This proves the fourth condition. It plainly implies the first. If the boundary is empty the condition is vacuous; the same proof or any entire function handles $\Omega =\mathbb C^ n$. The empty open set likewise has all four properties under the displayed definitions. $\square$
