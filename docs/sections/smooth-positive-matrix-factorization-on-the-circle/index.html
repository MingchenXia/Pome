9.23 Smooth positive matrix factorization on the circleB4D2D1F160F7

B4D2D1F160F7

Theorem 9.23.1.35AF621E1787 Let $m\geq 2$ and let $W:S^1\to \operatorname {Herm}_ m$ be smooth and positive definite at every point. There is a matrix function $H$, holomorphic on the unit disc and smooth on its closure, such that $H$ is invertible everywhere on the closed disc and

\[ W=H^*H\quad \hbox{on }S^1. \]

Any two such factors differ by multiplication on the left by a constant unitary matrix. This assertion is about smooth data; no density assertion in a full Hölder norm is needed.

Proof. Use normalized angular measure and the Fourier boundary calculus of Proposition 9.20.1. Let $\mathcal H$ be the closed subspace of $L^2(S^1,\mathbb C^ m)$ whose negative Fourier coefficients vanish. Parseval's identity makes the projection $P$ onto the nonnegative coefficients an orthogonal projection of norm one. The Fourier series identifies each element of $\mathcal H$ with a holomorphic function on the disc: on $|\zeta |\leq r<1$ its power series converges uniformly by Cauchy–Schwarz and $\sum r^{2j}<\infty $. Its value at zero is its constant coefficient, a continuous map on $\mathcal H$.

Compactness and positivity give $0<cI\leq W\leq CI$. Define the bounded self-adjoint operator $T f=P(Wf)$ on $\mathcal H$. It obeys

\[ \langle Tf,f\rangle =\int f^*Wf\geq c\| f\| _2^2, \qquad \| Tf\| _2\geq c\| f\| _2. \]

The second inequality makes its range closed: a Cauchy sequence of images has a Cauchy sequence of preimages. The orthogonal complement of its range is the kernel of its adjoint, which is zero by the first inequality. Consequently $T$ is onto and $\| T^{-1}\| \leq c^{-1}$.

For each $v\in \mathbb C^ m$, minimize $\int f^*Wf$ over $f\in \mathcal H$ with $f(0)=v$. This is a nonempty closed affine subspace in the equivalent weighted Hilbert norm. The Hilbert projection theorem gives its unique minimizer; variation gives orthogonality to the subspace with zero constant coefficient. The minimizer depends linearly on $v$, since these orthogonality equations and the value at zero are linear and uniquely determine it. Let $F$ be the matrix of minimizers for the standard basis vectors. Then $F(0)=I$, and variation by every $h\in \mathcal H$ with $h(0)=0$ gives

\[ P(WF)=M \]

for some constant matrix $M$. Indeed these $h$ span exactly the positive Fourier modes. All equations apply column by column.

We next prove that $F$ is smooth, rather than assume a boundary regularity theorem. Write $\tau _ h f(\theta )=f(\theta +h)$ and $\delta _ h=(\tau _ h-I)/h$. Translation preserves $\mathcal H$ and commutes with $P$. Translating the last equation and subtracting it gives

\[ T(\delta _ h F)=-P((\delta _ h W)\tau _ h F). \]

The right hand side is uniformly bounded in $L^2$, since $W$ is $C^1$ and translations preserve the $L^2$ norm. The inverse estimate for $T$ bounds $\delta _ hF$ in $L^2$. If $a_ j$ are its Fourier coefficients, Parseval and Fatou give $\sum j^2|a_ j|^2<\infty $ by letting $h\to 0$ in $\sum |(e^{ijh}-1)/h|^2|a_ j|^2$. This is precisely the first weak angular derivative in $L^2$; the difference quotients converge to it by dominated convergence using $|e^{ijh}-1|\leq |jh|$.

Inductively suppose the first $k$ weak derivatives are in $L^2$. Differentiation of $P(WF)=M$ in distributions is legitimate, since $P$ commutes with the angular derivative and $W$ is smooth. For $k\geq 1$ it yields

\[ T F^{(k)}=-\sum _{\ell =1}^ k\binom {k}{\ell } P(W^{(\ell )}F^{(k-\ell )}). \]

This right hand side has one $L^2$ derivative under the induction hypothesis. Difference quotients of this equation therefore give

\[ T\delta _ h F^{(k)} =\delta _ h\! \left(-\sum _{\ell =1}^ k\binom {k}{\ell } P(W^{(\ell )}F^{(k-\ell )})\right) -P((\delta _ h W)\tau _ h F^{(k)}), \]

whose right hand side is uniformly bounded in $L^2$. The same Fourier argument proves the next derivative. Thus $F$ has angular derivatives of every order in $L^2$. For every integer $r\geq 0$, Cauchy–Schwarz with a Fourier weight of order greater than $r+1/2$ gives $\sum _ j(1+j)^ r|a_ j|<\infty $. Its holomorphic power series and all derivatives consequently converge uniformly on the closed disc. This proves the asserted smoothness.

The Fourier coefficients of $WF$ have indices at most zero. Those of $F^*$ do also; the latter are absolutely summable to every order. Hence $F^*WF$ has only nonpositive Fourier modes. It is Hermitian on the circle, so the coefficient of index $-j$ is the adjoint of the coefficient of index $j$. Every nonconstant coefficient vanishes. Write $F^*WF=A$, a constant Hermitian matrix. For $v\in \mathbb C^ m$,

\[ v^*Av=\int (Fv)^*W(Fv)\geq c\| Fv\| _2^2 \geq c|F(0)v|^2=c|v|^2. \]

Thus $A$ is positive definite and $F$ is invertible on the circle.

It is invertible in the interior too. If $F(a)v=0$ with $v\ne 0$, then $a\ne 0$, and $f=Fv$ is the unique minimizer with $f(0)=v$. For $b_ a(\zeta )=(\zeta -a)/(1-\overline a\zeta )$, the function $\widetilde f=b_ a(0)f/b_ a$ is holomorphic, smooth on the closed disc and has value $v$ at zero. The removable zero at $a$ is used componentwise. Since $|b_ a|=1$ on the circle, its weighted squared norm is $|a|^2$ times that of $f$, strictly smaller. This contradicts minimality. Therefore $\det F$ never vanishes on the closed disc. Its inverse is holomorphic inside and smooth up to the boundary by the adjugate formula. Set $H=A^{1/2}F^{-1}$, using the positive square root of the finite positive Hermitian matrix $A$. The identity $F^*WF=A$ proves $W=H^*H$.

For uniqueness let $H_1,H_2$ be two factors and put $G=H_2H_1^{-1}$. It and its inverse are holomorphic and smooth on the closed disc, and $G^*G=I$ on the circle. The scalar maximum principle applied to $u^*Gv$, for fixed unit vectors $u,v$, gives $\| G(\zeta )\| \leq 1$; applied to its inverse it gives $\| G(\zeta )^{-1}\| \leq 1$. Thus all singular values are one at every interior point. Each column has constant squared norm one; applying the planar Laplacian shows that the sum of the squared absolute values of its holomorphic derivatives is zero. Every entry is constant. The constant matrix is unitary, as required. $\square$

Sources. The smooth-data case of the matrix factorization used in Lempert, La métrique de Kobayashi et la représentation des domaines sur la boule [Lempert1981] (1981), Section6 Theorem B and Appendix Theorem B, is reconstructed here by weighted Hilbert minimization and Fourier regularity. This proof does not establish the full finite Hölder version or the nonlinear stationary-disc deformation theorem.
