7.28 Classical products with controlled unbounded loci42FC136317B9

Theorem 7.28.1.1BBBF8109419 Let $T$ be closed positive of bidimension $(p,p)$ on a complex manifold, let $1\leq q\leq p$, and let $u_1,\ldots ,u_ q$ be plurisubharmonic. Interpret the Hausdorff nullness locally in any coordinate chart. If

\[ \mathcal H^{2p-2m+1} \bigl (L(u_{j_1})\cap \cdots \cap L(u_{j_ m})\cap \operatorname {supp}T\bigr )=0 \quad (1\leq j_1<\cdots <j_ m\leq q), \]

the coefficient product $u_1dd^ cu_2\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cu_ q\wedge T$ is locally integrable and has locally finite variation. Its $dd^ c$ defines a closed positive classical product with $q$ Hessian factors. These products are symmetric, multilinear, local, and invariant under adding constants to the Hessian potentials. Both the weighted and unweighted products are continuous under simultaneous decreasing plurisubharmonic approximations to the $u_ j$. The approximants may also be unbounded. Their pole mass is retained.

Proof. Everything is local in coordinates; put $A_ j=L(u_ j)$ and $S=\operatorname {supp}T$. Work with negative potentials on a compact coordinate neighborhood. The preceding estimates apply uniformly to their bounded truncations, decreasing smooth regularizations and all decreasing approximants, because these dominate the limits and have the same outside-$V_ j$ lower bound.

Construction and integrability. Induct on the total number $q$ of potential slots, counting the weight as one slot. For $q=1$, the uniform weighted estimate and monotone convergence for the fixed trace of $T$ show $u_1\in L^1(T)$. Then $u_{1,j}T\to u_1T$ by dominated convergence, and $dd^ c(u_1T)$ is positive closed by bounded truncation. Suppose the theorem is proved with fewer than $q$ slots. Put $R=dd^ cu_2\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cu_ q\wedge T$, already positive closed by induction. The analogous $R_ N$ for simultaneous bounded truncations converge to $R$. For fixed $m$ and $N\geq m$,

\[ -\max \{ u_1,-m\} \leq -\max \{ u_1,-N\} . \]

The uniform weighted estimate bounds the latter weight against $R_ N$. For that fixed bounded lower semicontinuous weight, weak convergence and decreasing continuous majorants of upper semicontinuous functions give the limiting integral inequality. Then let $m\to \infty $ for the fixed limiting measure. This proves coefficient integrability and its uniform local bound. Define the new Hessian product by $dd^ c(u_1R)$. Bounded truncations of the first potential against this fixed $R$ converge in coefficient $L^1$, so positivity and closedness follow from the bounded recursion. A compact test cutoff and the weighted bound also control its trace mass. Thus every product used below is defined before its continuity is asserted. The same argument gives uniform weighted bounds for arbitrary decreasing families.

The local induction for continuity. Induct additionally on the number $m$ of limit functions unbounded near the point under consideration. When $m=0$ the bounded Bedford–Taylor theorem applies. Before treating $m>0$, replace each function bounded near the point, and all its approximants, by a global maximum with a fixed plurisubharmonic quadratic function on the working coordinate neighborhood. Choose that quadratic below the common lower bound near the point and above the common upper bound outside a slightly larger ball. The new functions agree with the old ones near the point, are bounded on the entire working neighborhood and are equal to the fixed smooth quadratic on an outer corona. These modifications preserve decreasing limits. Only the other $m$ functions can now be unbounded anywhere used in the argument. Their unbounded loci still satisfy the hypotheses.

Choose the modifications and cutoffs together. If a bounded slot is present, first fix a cylinder and make its quadratic modification in concentric balls compactly contained there; choose its cutoff with compact support and derivatives in that quadratic corona. This cutoff supplies the required compact support by itself. If every slot is unbounded, use the sphere-avoidance cylinder of the preceding proof. For any other bounded slot use the same construction. For the other functions derivatives are supported away from $A_ j$. Use the closed, positive test form

\[ \alpha =\chi (z')(dd^ c|z'|^2)^{p-q}\wedge dd^ c|z_{p-q+1}|^2. \]

All localized primitives have compact support relative to $S$. Smooth approximants of a bounded modified function can be chosen equal to its fixed quadratic on that derivative support, using the same smooth-maximum construction as in theorem 7.29.5.

We record precisely how derivative remainders pass to the limit. If a remainder contains $B_{\chi _ j}$ for one of the $m$ unbounded slots, its support has a neighborhood where that slot is uniformly bounded. The local representation (7.30.2) expresses it as bounded plurisubharmonic Hessians with smooth coefficients. On each such neighborhood at most $m-1$ slots remain unbounded, so the induction at this same $q$ applies. If the derivative belongs to a slot bounded from the outset, the error is a fixed smooth form, and removing it leaves fewer than $q$ potential slots. The induction on $q$ applies. For several errors use a finite partition on their joint supports and the same representation for every bounded error slot. The transformed potentials decrease and have empty local unbounded locus, so all intersection hypotheses are inherited from the remaining original slots. This proves convergence of every remainder, also with any mixture of fixed limits and decreasing approximants, and with sequential smooth regularization parameters.

Localized potentials $\chi _ j u_ j$ cause no additional product premise: near $A_ j$, $\chi _ j$ is constant, and elsewhere $u_ j$ is bounded. There they are finite differences of bounded plurisubharmonic functions by the same square decomposition. Their lower-slot products are therefore defined by expansion and are independent of the expansion by coefficient recursion and linearity. All lower-slot integration by parts below is legitimate for these closed signed currents as well.

Every weighted weak limit has the right upper bound. Write $R_ j=dd^ cu_{2,j}\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cu_{q,j}\wedge T$, so $R_ j\to R$ by the already-proved theorem with $q-1$ slots. The positive currents $-u_{1,j}R_ j$ have uniformly bounded local mass. Choose any subsequential weighted limit $W$. Fixed upper semicontinuous truncations of the weight and their decreasing continuous majorants, followed by fixed-measure monotone limits, give $W\leq u_1R$, exactly by decreasing continuous majorants of upper semicontinuous functions. Thus $D=u_1R-W$ is positive.

The reverse localized inequality. For a vector $v=(v_1,\ldots ,v_ q)$ define

\[ J(v)=\int \chi _1v_1\, dd^ c(\chi _2v_2)\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ c(\chi _ qv_ q)\wedge T\wedge \alpha . \]

Its main term is the expected weighted product times $\chi _1\cdots \chi _ q$; all other terms are the remainders just treated. Fix an approximant index $j$ and choose smooth plurisubharmonic majorants $w_ l\downarrow u_{l,j}$, with the fixed quadratic branch on bounded-slot derivative supports. Starting with $J(u)$, replace the weight $\chi _ lu_ l$ by $\chi _ lw_ l$ in turn, then interchange it with the next potential by integration by parts. The term in that replacement containing only $\chi _ i dd^ cv_ i$ is nonnegative, since $w_ l\geq u_ l$. Every other term contains a cutoff derivative. Hence after all $q$ replacements,

For completeness, each interchange has smooth first factor $\chi _ lw_ l$ and second factor $\chi _{l+1}u_{l+1}$, against the closed signed current containing $q-2$ other localized Hessians and $T\wedge \alpha $. The second factor is coefficient-integrable by the already-proved theorem with $q-1$ slots and the local difference description above. Its $dd^ c$ is also a defined lower-slot current. The primitive has compact support by the cylinder construction. Distributional integration by parts, testing with a compact cutoff equal to one there, proves the interchange. Thus no unproved unbounded product identity is used in (7.30.9).

Hold $w_1$ fixed and let the other smoothing parameters tend to zero. The $q-1$ localized Hessian factors in $J(w)$ converge by the unweighted theorem with $q-1$ slots, and the weight is smooth. Then let $w_1\downarrow u_{1,j}$: the signed lower-slot measure is fixed and its coefficient integrability follows from the main weighted bound and the remainder bounds, so dominated convergence applies. Consequently $J(w)\to J(u_{\bullet ,j})$. The remainder convergence proved above gives limits $e_{l,j}$ for $E_ l$ in the same order. As $j\to \infty $ they tend to zero: the two terms coming from $w_ l-u_ l$ have identical limit in every lower-$m$ or lower-$q$ remainder. Therefore

\[ J(u)\leq \liminf _ j J(u_{\bullet ,j}). \]

Expand both sides into their main term and remainders. Along the subsequence defining $W$, all remainders converge to the same values. It follows that

\[ \int \chi _1\cdots \chi _ q\, D\wedge \alpha \leq 0. \]

This is a nonnegative measure integral, and all cutoffs equal one near the target point. Use finitely many good unitary systems whose degree-$(p-q+1,p-q+1)$ forms span, as in the estimate proof. The trace of $D$ vanishes near that point, and coefficient domination gives $D=0$. Every subsequence has the same further limit, proving weighted convergence. Applying $dd^ c$ proves unweighted convergence with $q$ Hessian factors. Smooth approximation now gives symmetry; the coefficient recursion gives multilinearity, constants, locality and compatibility on overlaps. This completes both inductions. $\square$
