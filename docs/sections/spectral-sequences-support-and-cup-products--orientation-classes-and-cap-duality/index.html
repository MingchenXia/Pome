Lemma 10.71.1.603753CCA942 The groups $\tau _{X,x}=H_ n^{\mathrm{sing}}(X,X\setminus \{ x\} ; \mathbb Z)$ form a locally constant sheaf of infinite cyclic groups. There is a canonical identification $\tau _ X\otimes \tau _ X=\underline{\mathbb {Z}}$, sending $o\otimes o$ to $1$ for either local generator. For every compact $K\subset X$ there is a unique class

\[ [X]_ K\in H_ n^{\mathrm{sing}}(X,X\setminus K;\tau _ X\otimes R) \]

whose local value at each $x\in K$ is $1$ under this identification. These classes commute with restriction to smaller compact sets and with open excision. For any local module system $A$, $H_ j(X,X\setminus K;A)=0$ for $j>n$, and a class in degree $n$ is determined by all its point restrictions on $K$.

Proof. A point has singular homology only in degree zero: the complex for a constant module has differential alternating between the identity and zero in positive degrees. Contracting a ball to a point by the prism gives the same conclusion for balls. The open hemisphere Mayer–Vietoris sequence computes the reduced homology of spheres inductively from $\widetilde H_0(S^0,R)=R$; it is $R$ in the sphere dimension and zero otherwise. Radial retraction of a punctured ball onto a sphere and the pair sequence now give the asserted local infinite cyclic group. In dimension zero the pair is simply a point relative to the empty set.

A nonempty compact convex set $K\subset \mathbb R^ n$ has the same relative homology as a point in it. Indeed, with center $x\in K$, choose a sphere of radius greater than every distance from $x$ to $K$. Both $\mathbb R^ n\setminus K$ and $\mathbb R^ n\setminus \{ x\} $ retract radially onto this sphere. The first retraction stays in the complement: a ray from a point of a convex set cannot reenter it after leaving it, and the chosen sphere is beyond the whole set. The retractions commute with the inclusion up to these explicit radial homotopies. The pair sequences therefore give an isomorphism on relative groups, which are the coefficient module in degree $n$ and zero elsewhere. Local systems on the chart are constant by transport. Restriction from a closed ball to every point of its interior consequently supplies compatible local generators. On an overlap two such generators differ by $+1$ or $-1$, locally constantly, since comparison can be made on one smaller closed ball inside the overlap. These are exactly the transition maps defining $\tau _ X$. Their squares are one, proving the canonical tensor identification.

We give the compact-set extension argument in detail. For compact $K,L$ the relative Mayer–Vietoris sequence has the segment

\[ \begin{aligned} H_{j+1}(X,X\setminus (K\cap L);A)& \longrightarrow H_ j(X,X\setminus (K\cup L);A)\\ & \longrightarrow H_ j(X,X\setminus K;A)\oplus H_ j(X,X\setminus L;A)\\ & \longrightarrow H_ j(X,X\setminus (K\cap L);A). \end{aligned} \]

It is obtained from the two open complements by the small-chain argument. Thus, if the higher vanishing and local uniqueness hold for $K,L,K\cap L$, they hold for $K\cup L$. Compatible degree-$n$ classes on $K,L$ glue uniquely, since the left group in degree $n$ is zero. Induction proves these assertions for finite unions of compact convex sets in a chart; their finite intersections are again compact convex sets, with the empty case giving zero.

For an arbitrary compact $K$ in a chart, let a relative cycle $z$ have boundary disjoint from $K$. Choose finitely many small closed balls centered at points of $K$ whose interiors cover $K$ and whose union $L$ misses that boundary. Then $z$ is already a relative cycle for $L$. Above degree $n$ its class is zero by the preceding finite union case. In degree $n$, if all its point values on $K$ vanish, its restriction to each chosen ball vanishes because it vanishes at that ball's center. Local uniqueness for the finite union makes its class zero on $L$, and hence on $K$. Existence of the canonical class on a compact set in a chart follows by restricting the class of one larger closed ball. The coefficient system $\tau _ X\otimes R$ makes its local value independent of the sign of the chosen coordinate orientation.

Finally cover an arbitrary compact $K$ by finitely many compact subsets $K_ i$ contained in coordinate balls; obtain them by shrinking a finite coordinate cover on the compact Hausdorff subspace. The assertions are known for every $K_ i$ and for every intersection with the union of its predecessors, since that intersection is a compact subset of the same chart. The relative Mayer–Vietoris argument proves vanishing, uniqueness and existence successively for their unions. Uniqueness also proves compatibility with smaller compact sets. Open excision has the same point values, so it preserves these classes. This completes the construction, including the integral orientation twist. $\square$

Lemma 10.71.2.3C7389B6FCBC The homological orientation system of a smooth manifold agrees with its usual determinant orientation. In particular a smooth coordinate change preserves a local homology generator exactly when its derivative has positive determinant. The group $\operatorname {GL}^+(r,\mathbb R)$ is path connected.

Proof. Gram–Schmidt writes a positive-determinant matrix as an orthogonal determinant-one matrix times an upper triangular matrix with positive diagonal. Linear interpolation contracts the latter to the identity through such triangular matrices. For $r\ge 2$, successive plane rotations take the first column of the orthogonal factor to the first coordinate vector, and then act on its orthogonal complement. Induction gives a path to the identity. Rank one is the path of positive scalars; rank zero is immediate.

A linear map of positive determinant is thus homotopic through invertible linear maps to the identity, and induces $+1$ on local homology. A reflection in one coordinate induces $-1$. Indeed, it exchanges the two hemispheres of the surrounding sphere and fixes their equator; in the reduced Mayer–Vietoris sequence, exchanging the two summands negates the connecting map. For the zero-sphere this is directly the exchange of its two points in reduced $H_0$. The radial local-homology identification gives the same sign for the reflection. Factoring a negative-determinant matrix as a reflection times a positive-determinant one proves the linear case.

Finally let a smooth coordinate change fix the origin, with invertible derivative $T$ there. On a sufficiently small ball write $f(v)=Tv+e(v)$ with $|e(v)|\le \frac12\| T^{-1}\| ^{-1}|v|$. For $0<t\le 1$ set $f_ t(v)=t^{-1}f(tv)$ and set $f_0(v)=Tv$. The first-order remainder estimate makes this a continuous homotopy; the displayed bound makes $f_ t(v)$ nonzero for $v\ne 0$ throughout. It is therefore a homotopy of the punctured-ball pairs defining local homology. The local action of $f$ is the action of $T$, whose sign was just computed. Compatible positive smooth charts and compatible positive homological generators consequently specify the same orientation. $\square$

For a $j$-simplex with a parallel coefficient $e$, and a $p$-cochain $u$, define the cap product by the back face with coefficient transported to the shared vertex:

\[ (e\sigma )\frown u= t_{0,p}\bigl (e\otimes u(\sigma [v_0,\ldots ,v_ p])\bigr ) \sigma [v_ p,\ldots ,v_ j]. \]

It is zero for $p>j$. Evaluation of a Hom coefficient on $e$ may replace the tensor in this formula. Expanding the two alternating boundaries gives

5E4EEAACF262 10.71.3
\begin{equation} \partial (c\frown u)=(-1)^ p\bigl ((\partial c)\frown u -c\frown \delta u\bigr ). \end{equation}

Indeed faces strictly before the shared vertex cancel against the coboundary sum, faces strictly after it give the boundary of the back face, and the two occurrences of the shared face have opposite signs. All coefficient transports coincide inside the original simplex. The same front/back subdivision gives

6F3FE0A6CE1E 10.71.4
\begin{equation} \langle c\frown u,v\rangle =\langle c,u\smile v\rangle , \qquad (c\frown u)\frown v=c\frown (u\smile v). \end{equation}

These are equalities of coefficients, not just cohomology classes. Cap is natural for maps with the indicated coefficient maps: $f_*(c\frown f^*u)=f_*c\frown u$.

Theorem 10.71.5.9EC6A8A9270A For every local $R$-module system $A$ on $X$, cap product gives natural isomorphisms

\[ D_{X,A}:H_ c^ q(X,A)\xrightarrow {\sim } H_{n-q}^{\mathrm{sing}}(X,\tau _ X\otimes A). \]

There is no torsion-free, finite-generation or orientability hypothesis. The maps commute with open extension and the induced homology maps, and with coefficient maps. On a compact oriented manifold this is cap with the integral fundamental class tensored with the coefficient ring.

Proof. Use the supported singular comparison already proved. A cochain $u$ vanishing on $X\setminus K$ is capped with a representative $c$ of $[X]_ K$. Its cap is a cycle by equation 10.71.3: $\partial c$ lies in the complement where $u$ vanishes. A change by a cochain boundary or by a relative chain boundary changes this cycle by a boundary, by the same identity. Chains in the complement cap to zero. Compatibility of $[X]_ K$ for increasing $K$ proves that this construction defines $D_{X,A}$ on the directed limit. Open excision and the carrier property prove naturality for open extension; no properness of an open inclusion is asserted.

On a coordinate ball $\Omega \simeq \mathbb R^ n$, fix a point $x$. Closed Euclidean balls centered there are cofinal among compact subsets. The radial calculation gives $H_ j(\Omega ,\Omega \setminus K;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$ in degree $n$ and zero otherwise, for each such ball $K$ and also for $K=\{ x\} $. The relative homology maps to the point pair preserve the local generator and are isomorphisms. These relative chain complexes are free, being freely spanned by the simplices not lying in the complement. Thus corollary 10.68.9, over $\mathbb Z$, gives relative cohomology with any constant coefficient module only in degree $n$, where it is that module, compatibly under these maps. The compact-support limit has the same groups.

Represent the point-pair local generator by an embedded identity simplex with $x$ in its interior, and its boundary in $\Omega \setminus \{ x\} $. The boundary-sphere computation identifies it as a generator. The same universal coefficient map supplies a relative cocycle with any prescribed value on it. Capping gives that value at the last vertex, hence the corresponding $H_0$ class of $\Omega $. The orientation coefficient makes this independent of the sign used for the local generator. Therefore $D_\Omega $ is an isomorphism. This argument uses only pairs of open manifolds, and does not assume a singular/sheaf comparison on a disk boundary pair.

We verify the connecting square required to glue balls. Suppose $X=U\cup V$, $K\subset U$ and $L\subset V$ are compact, and put $P=X\setminus K$, $Q=X\setminus L$. A relative $p$-cocycle $\phi $ vanishing on $P\cap Q$ can be written $\phi =\phi _ P-\phi _ Q$, with $\phi _ P$ vanishing on $P$ and $\phi _ Q$ on $Q$: choose its value simplex by simplex according to whether the simplex is in $P$, in $Q$, or in neither. Then $\delta \phi _ P=\delta \phi _ Q$ vanishes on chains in both $P$ and $Q$, and represents the cohomology Mayer–Vietoris boundary. Replace those complement chains by small chains where necessary; the preceding homotopies justify this replacement on the relative complex as well.

Subdivide a representative of $[X]_{K\cup L}$ into $c=c_{U\setminus L}+c_{U\cap V}+c_{V\setminus K}$. These three opens cover $X$. The middle chain represents the class at $K\cap L$, and the sum of the first two represents the class at $K$, by local uniqueness and excision. In particular its boundary lies in $U\setminus K$; this follows also by subtracting the third chain, whose boundary is in $X\setminus K$, from $\partial c$. Cohomology boundary followed by cap gives $[c_{U\cap V}\frown \delta \phi _ P] =[\partial c_{U\cap V}\frown \phi _ P]$ by equation 10.71.3. Cap followed by homology boundary, using the first summand as the $U$ part, gives

\[ [\partial (c_{U\setminus L}\frown \phi )] =(-1)^ p[\partial c_{U\setminus L}\frown \phi _ P] =(-1)^{p+1}[\partial c_{U\cap V}\frown \phi _ P]. \]

The omitted $\phi _ Q$ term is zero on $U\setminus L$, and the last equality uses the just established boundary in $U\setminus K$, where $\phi _ P$ is zero. Thus the connecting square commutes with exactly the unit sign $(-1)^{p+1}$. The other two squares follow from restriction and extension on representatives. Taking directed limits over $K,L$ gives the compact-support Mayer–Vietoris diagram. Every compact subset of $U\cup V$ is the union of compact subsets in the two opens, by a finite shrinking; intersections are cofinal for compact subsets of $U\cap V$. The five lemma, adjusting the stated sign, proves duality on a union when it holds on both opens and their intersection.

Finite unions of convex balls in $\mathbb R^ n$ now follow by induction: intersection with the last ball is a union of fewer convex opens. Arbitrary Euclidean opens follow by their directed union of such finite unions. On both sides of duality this passage is valid because every compact support, singular cycle and bounding chain is contained in a finite subunion. The same argument for a finite collection of coordinate charts works because every finite intersection is an open subset of the first chart. The manifold is the directed union of finite chart unions. This proves the isomorphism everywhere, with arbitrary local coefficients and with the already defined natural maps. $\square$

Theorem 10.71.6.61D073B63D13 For every compact $K\subset X$ and every local system $A$, there is a natural isomorphism

\[ D_ K:H^ q(K,A|_ K)\xrightarrow {\sim } H_{n-q}^{\mathrm{sing}}(X,X\setminus K;\tau _ X\otimes A). \]

For $K\subset L$ its restriction map corresponds to the quotient map of relative homology groups. This statement uses sheaf cohomology on the possibly non-locally-contractible compact set $K$; it makes no unsupported singular comparison on that compact set.

Proof. Compact strong-paracompactness and theorem 10.60.1 identify the left side with the direct limit of $H^ q(U,A)$ over neighborhoods of $K$. On such a neighborhood use the proved singular comparison and cap with $[U]_ K$. Excision maps the result to the displayed relative homology group. The cap boundary formula proves well-definedness, and uniqueness of compact fundamental classes proves independence of the neighborhood. It also gives the stated restriction naturality.

For a nonempty compact convex $K$ in a chart the left side is $A$ in degree zero and zero otherwise, by constant-sheaf homotopy invariance. The right side has exactly those groups by the radial calculation in lemma 10.71.1. Capping a degree-zero constant multiplies the relative generator by its value, so $D_ K$ is an isomorphism in this case.

Here is the compatibility needed for finite closed unions. Cohomology of $K\cup L$ has the closed-cover Mayer–Vietoris sequence: the sheaf sequence with terms $A$, $i_{K*}A|_ K\oplus i_{L*}A|_ L$ and $i_{K\cap L,*}A|_{K\cap L}$ is stalkwise exact, and closed direct images are exact and preserve flabbiness. It can equally be computed by taking neighborhood limits of the open-cover sequence. Those limits are cofinal: given a neighborhood of $K\cap L$, disjoint neighborhoods of the compact sets outside it produce neighborhoods of $K,L$ whose intersection lies inside it. The relative homology sequence of their complements is the one used in the compact orientation lemma.

To check its connecting square, represent a degree-$(q-1)$ class near $K\cap L$ by $w=u-v$, with $u,v$ cochains near $K,L$. Then $z=\delta u=\delta v$ on the overlap represents its cohomology boundary near $K\cup L$. Choose a representative $c$ of $[X]_{K\cup L}$ and subdivide it for the covers by each of these neighborhoods and the corresponding complement. Extend the cochains arbitrarily elsewhere. Every resulting simplex meeting the relevant compact set lies in its prescribed neighborhood; all other simplices vanish in the relevant relative quotient. In the relative chain Mayer–Vietoris sequence, $c\frown w$ lifts to the pair $(c\frown u,c\frown v)$. By equation 10.71.3 its two boundaries are the image of $(-1)^ q c\frown z$, since $\partial c$ is in the complement of $K\cup L$. Thus $\partial _{\rm MV}D_{K\cap L}[w] =(-1)^ qD_{K\cup L}\delta _{\rm MV}[w]$. This gives the precise connecting sign; the other squares follow from the cap formula. The five lemma proves the union assertion. Induction therefore proves it for finite unions of compact convex sets inside a chart, including their intersections.

Every compact set in a chart has a cofinal family of neighborhoods that are finite unions of closed small balls, with the compact set in their interiors. The neighborhood-continuity theorem identifies the direct limit of their sheaf cohomology with $H^ q(K,A|_ K)$. Their complements increase to $X\setminus K$: any finite chain in this complement is disjoint from one sufficiently small such neighborhood. Consequently the corresponding relative chain complexes have directed limit $C(X)/C(X\setminus K)$, and their homology has the same limit. The natural maps $D$ commute with these limits, proving the assertion for arbitrary compact sets in a chart. Finally take a finite compact coordinate cover of a general $K$ and use the closed-union argument. Each new intersection is a compact subset of one chart, where the theorem is already proved. This proves the theorem without any regularity assumption on $K$. $\square$

Theorem 10.71.7.211197321C0C If $X$ is connected and noncompact, then $H^ n(X,A)=0$ for every locally constant module sheaf $A$.

Proof. A connected paracompact manifold has a countable basis. To see this, shrink a locally finite refinement of a relatively compact coordinate cover, cover each of its compact smaller closed sets by finitely many coordinate balls inside the assigned refinement member, and retain those balls. This gives a locally finite cover by connected relatively compact coordinate balls. Each compact ball closure meets only finitely many cover members. The intersection graph is therefore locally finite and is connected, since the union of the members in a graph component is both open and closed in $X$. Starting at one vertex, the vertices reachable in at most $r$ steps are finite; their countable union is the entire graph. The countable union of the countable coordinate bases is a base of $X$.

Use lemma 9.52.5 to obtain a compact exhaustion $K_ j\subset \operatorname {int}K_{j+1}$ such that no component of $X\setminus K_ j$ has compact closure. In particular every such component meets $X\setminus K_{j+1}$. The dimension bound of theorem 10.62.2, transferred on coordinate charts and globalized by theorem 10.61.8, gives a soft resolution $A\to L^0\to \cdots \to L^ n\to 0$ by theorem 10.61.7. Put $E_ j^\bullet =\Gamma (K_ j,L^\bullet |_{K_ j})$. Its terms are acyclic on the compact set and its cohomology is $H^ q(K_ j,A|_{K_ j})$. Its restriction maps are onto degree by degree: a germ section on the closed set $K_ j$ extends to $X$ by softness, then restricts to $K_{j+1}$.

Compatible sections on the $K_ j$ glue on their interiors and give exactly a section on $X$. Thus the following is a short exact sequence of complexes:

\[ 0\longrightarrow \Gamma (X,L^\bullet )\longrightarrow \prod _ jE_ j^\bullet \xrightarrow {\, 1-s\, }\prod _ jE_ j^\bullet \longrightarrow 0, \qquad s((e_ j))_ j=e_{j+1}|_{K_ j}. \]

Surjectivity of $1-s$ follows by recursive lifting: after choosing $e_ j$, lift $e_ j-b_ j$ to $e_{j+1}$. Products of module sequences are exact, so the cohomology of either product complex is the product of the cohomology groups.

By compact local cap duality, $H^ n(K_ j,A)=H_0(X,X\setminus K_ j;\tau _ X\otimes A)=0$. Indeed the complement is nonempty, and every point of the connected manifold can be joined to a point of that complement; its transported coefficient represents the same $H_0$ class. The quotient relative $H_0$ is therefore zero. The triple sequence gives a surjection

\[ H_1(X,X\setminus K_{j+1};\tau _ X\otimes A) \longrightarrow H_1(X,X\setminus K_ j;\tau _ X\otimes A), \]

since its next term is $H_0(X\setminus K_ j,X\setminus K_{j+1};\tau _ X\otimes A)=0$. For this last equality each path component of the first open set meets the second, and the same transport argument applies within that component. Naturality of compact local duality identifies these surjections with the restrictions on $H^{n-1}(K_ j,A)$. The recursive lifting argument therefore makes $1-s$ onto on the product of those degree-$(n-1)$ groups as well. The long exact sequence of the displayed complexes, with vanishing product in degree $n$, gives $H^ n(X,A)=0$. A connected zero-dimensional manifold is a point and cannot be noncompact, so the stated case has $n\ge 1$. No countability of a stalk, finite generation of homology, or dual reflexivity is used. $\square$
