02B93EEDEB97

Lemma 7.31.1.FDDB630F0AA9 Suppose a neighborhood of a compact subset $K$ of a complex manifold carries a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function $\rho $. Every plurisubharmonic function defined near $K$ is, on a fixed smaller neighborhood of $K$, the decreasing limit of smooth plurisubharmonic functions.

Proof. We first localize the finite-cover proof of decreasing smooth regularization with a vanishing curvature error. For bounded $v$ on a slightly larger compact neighborhood and fixed $\eta >0$, that proof gives smooth $a_{\eta ,\delta }\downarrow v$ on a fixed open neighborhood $O$ of $K$, with $dd^ ca_{\eta ,\delta }\geq -\eta \, dd^ c\rho $. Here is why no compact-without-boundary assumption is needed for this use. Take finitely many nested charts $V_ i\Subset W_ i\Subset U_ i$ whose $V_ i$ cover a fixed larger compact neighborhood. The bounded coordinate-convolution comparison gives uniformly vanishing overlap errors. Add fixed small chart penalties, zero on $V_ i$ and negative near $\partial W_ i$, and take the smooth regularized maximum. Where a branch leaves $W_ i$ in $\overline O$, another branch has zero penalty and dominates it; the removal property of that maximum glues the formulas. All relevant boundaries intersecting $\overline O$ are compact. The curvature error is at most $\eta \, dd^ c\rho $ after choosing the penalties small enough. For the fixed cover and penalties the local convolutions and maximum decrease with $\delta $, and the zero-penalty branches give limit $v$ and the majorant inequality. These are exactly the local steps of the cited proof, on the fixed buffered compact neighborhood.

Add a constant to $\rho $ so that $\rho \geq 0$ on that neighborhood. Put $v_ N=\max \{ u,-N\} $, choose $t_ N\downarrow 0$, and use the preceding construction with $\eta _ N=t_ N/2$. Choose its smoothing parameter so that

\[ w_ N=a_{\eta _ N,\delta _ N}+t_ N\rho +2^{-N} \]

decreases strictly with $N$ on a fixed compact neighborhood of $K$, and $\| a_{\eta _ N,\delta _ N}-v_ N\| _{L^1}\leq 2^{-N}$ there. The choice is possible inductively: the pointwise limit of the candidate functions for index $N$ is $v_ N+t_ N\rho +2^{-N}$, below the previous smooth majorant by at least $2^{-N}$. Increasing open sets where the candidate lies below that previous function cover the compact set, so one sufficiently small parameter works everywhere. The $L^1$ condition is the bounded decreasing convergence for this fixed $v_ N$. Each $w_ N$ is smooth plurisubharmonic, since $dd^ cw_ N\geq (t_ N/2)dd^ c\rho $. Also $w_ N\geq u$, and $w_ N\to u$ in $L^1$, because $v_ N\downarrow u$ in $L^1$. The decreasing plurisubharmonic limit is therefore the canonical representative $u$ at every point, by decreasing nets of plurisubharmonic functions. Restriction to the interior of the fixed compact neighborhood proves the lemma. $\square$

Theorem 7.31.2.F6EE68FA9FDA Let $T$ be closed positive of bidimension $(p,p)$ on $X$. Suppose $X$ is covered by relatively compact open sets $D$, each carrying a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion, such that

\[ \partial D\cap L(u_ j)\cap \operatorname {supp}T=\varnothing \]

for every member of a finite plurisubharmonic family. Then all conclusions of theorem 7.29.5 hold: products through $p$ Hessian factors, coefficient integrability of an additional weight through $p-1$ factors, and simultaneous decreasing continuity. In particular, for $p\geq 1$ a single such potential is coefficient-integrable for $T$.

Proof. Fix a desired compact subset of one $D$. The closed set $\operatorname {supp}T\cap \bigcup _ jL(u_ j)\cap \overline D$ is compact and disjoint from $\partial D$. Choose a sublevel $\Omega =\{ \rho <c\} \Subset D$ of its strict exhaustion containing that set and the desired compact subset. Put $\psi =\rho -c$ and $\omega =dd^ c\psi >0$ near $\overline\Omega $. No regular-value choice is needed below: every integration by parts uses compact current differences rather than boundary integration.

Take a smaller sublevel containing all these relative unbounded loci and the target compact set. Near $\operatorname {supp}T$ in the remaining closed collar, the limit functions are bounded below uniformly, and so are all their decreasing approximants. Subtract constants for a uniform negative upper bound. On an open neighborhood $U$ of $\operatorname {supp}T\cap \overline\Omega $, leave the functions unchanged in the inner sublevel and use $\max \{ u,A\psi \} $ in that collar neighborhood, choosing $A$ large enough that the formulas agree on the overlap. They are plurisubharmonic on $U$ and equal $A\psi $ near $\operatorname {supp}T\cap \partial \Omega $.

The preceding regularization lemma supplies smooth decreasing plurisubharmonic majorants of each original function near $\overline\Omega $. Smooth-maximum gluing in the collar gives smooth decreasing majorants of its modification on the same $U$, equal exactly to $A\psi $ on a smaller relative collar. Uniform lower bounds give the inner branch gap, and the uniform negative upper bound gives the outer branch gap, just as in the coordinate collar construction. Every product on $U$ is supported in $\operatorname {supp}T$. It extends by zero to $\Omega $: each boundary point of $U$ inside $\Omega $ is outside the closed support of $T$, so all these currents already vanish near that point. This extension introduces no boundary derivative.

We verify the exact hypotheses used in the proof of theorem 7.29.5. Bounded products exist on $U$ by the canonical bounded theory and the just-described extensions. Their difference from the corresponding $A\psi $ product has compact support in $\Omega $. Hence testing $dd^ c$ against closed powers of $\omega $ proves the mass identity (7.29.2) on $\Omega $. The $L^1$ collar exchange uses only a compactly supported coefficient-integrable difference times a closed current, so its distributional proof also holds on $U$ with zero extension. Using the smooth function $A\psi $ gives the weighted identity (7.29.3), with $M_ T=\int _\Omega T\wedge \omega ^ p<\infty $. All constants are independent of interior truncation and approximant indices. Closedness and positivity of $\omega $, rather than constant coefficients, are all that those identities require.

The complete induction of that theorem now applies with these data. Explicitly, the weighted identity first gives $uT\in L^1$ by fixed-measure monotone convergence. Weighted continuity with fewer Hessian factors gives the next unweighted product and its symmetry. The same bound with simultaneous bounded truncations then proves integrability of the next weight. Fixed USC truncations bound every weighted weak limit from above. Smooth majorants with the fixed relative collar, the $L^1$ exchange and the strictly lower-factor sequential limits give the reverse mass inequality. All its base currents remain supported in $\operatorname {supp}T$, and every degree inequality remains $r\leq p-1$ for an unbounded weight. Splitting integrals with a compact cutoff is valid because the relative collar currents are fixed and finite. Thus each step and each limit of that earlier proof has its required premise here; no global coordinate chart or unproved smoothing premise is added. Locality returns the original functions near the target set, and the covering by $D$ proves the theorem on $X$. $\square$

Corollary 7.31.3.CF64F301AEAF Let $D\Subset X$ admit a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion. For $1\leq j\leq q\leq n-1$, let $u_ j$ be plurisubharmonic near $\overline D$, with $\partial D\cap L(u_ j)=\varnothing $. Every plurisubharmonic function $V$ defined near $\overline D$ is coefficient-integrable for $dd^ cu_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dd^ cu_ q$ locally in $D$.

Proof. For a given compact target choose a strict exhaustion sublevel $\Omega \Subset D$ containing it and all the unbounded loci in $D$, as above, and write $\psi <0$ for its defining exhaustion level and $\omega =dd^ c\psi >0$. Modify the $u_ j$ outside the target to a common $A\psi $ collar. The preceding theorem defines the closed positive product $R$; it is $A^ q\omega ^ q$ on that collar. Choose $\Omega _\delta =\{ \psi <-\delta \} $ containing the target and the region where the factors differ from $A\psi $, and a nonnegative compact cutoff $\chi $ in $\Omega $ equal to $-\psi $ near $\overline{\Omega _\delta }$. By the regularization lemma, after subtracting a constant there are smooth nonpositive plurisubharmonic $V_ k\downarrow V$ near $\overline\Omega $. Distributional integration by parts gives

\begin{align*} \int _{\Omega _\delta }(-V_ k)R\wedge \omega ^{n-q} & =\int _\Omega \chi \, dd^ cV_ k\wedge R\wedge \omega ^{n-q-1}\\ & \quad -A^ q\int _{\Omega \setminus \Omega _\delta } V_ k\, \omega ^{n-1}\wedge dd^ c\chi . \end{align*}

The first integral is uniformly bounded by the collar mass identity with base $dd^ cV_ k$ of bidimension $(n-1,n-1)$; it applies since $q\leq n-1$. Its bound is $\| \chi \| _\infty A^ q\int _\Omega dd^ cV_ k\wedge \omega ^{n-1}$, uniformly finite by local weak convergence of these positive Hessian currents and a fixed outer trace cutoff. The second integral is uniformly bounded by the local $L^1$ convergence of $V_ k$ against smooth volume. Monotone convergence now uses the fixed trace measure of $R$. It proves $V\in L^1(R\wedge \omega ^{n-q})$ on the target, and coefficient domination finishes the proof. $\square$

Corollary 7.31.4.EBA900FA96EF If $L(u)\cap \operatorname {supp}T$ is contained in an analytic set of dimension at most $p-1$, then $uT$ is coefficient-integrable and $dd^ c(uT)$ is a closed positive current for $p\geq 1$. For $T=1$, the mixed products of $q\leq n$ potentials exist and have the preceding decreasing continuity whenever $L(u_ j)\subset A_ j$ are analytic and every $m$-fold intersection of distinct $A_ j$ has codimension at least $m$.

Proof. The analytic stratification and Hausdorff bounds of analytic singular strata and Hausdorff dimension give exactly the zero measures in (7.30.4); apply theorem 7.28.1. In the first assertion with $p=0$, the dimension condition instead forces $L(u)\cap \operatorname {supp}T=\varnothing $. Then $u$ is locally bounded on the support, so $uT$ is defined and its $dd^ c$ vanishes by degree. This is not a general integrability assertion for an unbounded weight against a top-degree measure. $\square$
