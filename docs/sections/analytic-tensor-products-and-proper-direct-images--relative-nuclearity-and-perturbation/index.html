9.46 Relative nuclearity and perturbation3E8D1F9F84EF

3E8D1F9F84EF

For Fréchet modules over any unital Fréchet algebra $A$, a map is $A$-nuclear if it has a series

\[ u(x)=\sum _ j\lambda _ j\xi _ j(x)y_ j,\qquad \sum _ j|\lambda _ j|<\infty , \]

where the $A$-linear maps $\xi _ j:E\to A$ form an equicontinuous family and $(y_ j)$ is bounded. It is $A$-subnuclear if it becomes $A$-nuclear after a continuous open module surjection from a Fréchet module; if the source is nuclear this covering module is required nuclear. These definitions do not require a Banach algebra to be nuclear as a complex vector space.

Proposition 9.46.1.4BBFDD591C1E Within nuclear Fréchet modules, relative nuclear and subnuclear maps are preserved by continuous module precomposition and postcomposition. If $v:V\to W$ is complex-nuclear, then $I_ A\widehat\otimes v:A\widehat\otimes V\to A\widehat\otimes W$ is $A$-nuclear. In the analytic setting of the preceding proposition, restriction $\mathcal F(S\times U)\to \mathcal F(S\times U')$ is $\mathcal O(S)$-subnuclear when $S\Subset X$ and $U'\Subset U\Subset Y$ are strict-PSH open sets. For the structure sheaf it is $\mathcal O(S)$-nuclear.

Moreover let $A,B$ be nuclear Fréchet algebras, $\rho :A\to B$ a complex-nuclear homomorphism, $E,F$ nuclear $A$-modules and $F_1\subset F$ a closed $A$-submodule. Suppose $B$ is transverse over $A$ to $E,F,F/F_1$. If $u:E\to F$ is $A$-subnuclear with image in $F_1$, then the induced map $B\widehat\otimes _ A E\to B\widehat\otimes _ A F_1$ is $B$-subnuclear.

Proof. Postcomposition sends the bounded vectors to bounded vectors; precomposition sends the coefficient family to an equicontinuous family. For subnuclear precomposition along $v:S\to E$, pull the chosen open cover $M\to E$ back to $S$. The closed pullback in $S\times M$ is Fréchet, maps onto $S$ openly, and is nuclear if $S,M$ are nuclear. The lifted map is a restriction of a nuclear composition. Finite direct sums use the direct sums of the covering modules and the concatenated finite list of summable series; coefficient maps project to their source summand. This proves the ideal and finite-direct-sum assertions. For the tensor assertion extend the coefficients $\eta _ j:V\to \mathbb C$ of a nuclear series to $I_ A\widehat\otimes \eta _ j$ with target $A$, and take vectors $1\otimes w_ j$. Their equicontinuity follows from the projective seminorm bounds and their boundedness from continuity of tensor insertion.

Compact coherent restrictions are complex-nuclear by lemma 9.43.3. The structure sheaf formula follows by the external-product isomorphism. For $\mathcal F$ choose finitely many global generators near the compact $\overline S\times \overline U$ exactly as above. Coherent vanishing on $S\times U$ makes the free section map onto $\mathcal F(S\times U)$ an open surjection. In the restriction square the free vertical map is $\mathcal O(S)$-nuclear and the lower quotient map is continuous. This is the required subnuclear covering.

For the last assertion write first $u$ as $A$-nuclear and $\rho (a)=\sum _ k\mu _ k\eta _ k(a)b_ k$ complex-nuclear. The map $E\to B\widehat\otimes _ A F$, $x\mapsto 1\otimes u(x)$, is complex-nuclear, with the actual series

\[ \sum _{j,k}\lambda _ j\mu _ k (\eta _ k\circ \xi _ j)(x)[b_ k\otimes y_ j]. \]

The scalar coefficients form an equicontinuous family and the bracketed vectors a bounded family. Absolute convergence in every target seminorm proves the equality by the balanced relation. By transversality and(1) of the preceding calculus theorem, $B\widehat\otimes _ A F_1$ is a closed subspace of $B\widehat\otimes _ A F$. Nuclear maps from nuclear Fréchet sources remain nuclear when their range is regarded in a closed target subspace, by the proved Banach-factor range criterion of theorem 2.13.3. If $u$ was only subnuclear, do this on its nuclear covering module and descend through the closed kernel using the same Banach-factor quotient criterion. Thus $j:E\to B\widehat\otimes _ A F_1$, $j(x)=1\otimes u(x)$, is complex-nuclear in either case. The map $I_ B\widehat\otimes j$ is then $B$-nuclear. Compose with the action $B\widehat\otimes (B\widehat\otimes _ A F_1) \to B\widehat\otimes _ A F_1$. It factors through the open surjection $B\widehat\otimes E\to B\widehat\otimes _ A E$ and gives the required base-changed map. The covering space $B\widehat\otimes E$ is nuclear, as required. $\square$

Lemma 9.46.2.627F10C620CF Let $A\to B$ be a homomorphism of nuclear Fréchet algebras and let $B$ be transverse to the nuclear modules $E,F$. An $A$-subnuclear $u:E\to F$ induces a $B$-subnuclear map $u_ B:E_ B\to F_ B$, where $E_ B=B\widehat\otimes _ A E$. It can be tested on a nuclearly free covering of $E_ B$.

Proof. Take the nuclear covering $M\to E$ in the definition and compose it with the action $P=A\widehat\otimes M\to M$. This gives an open surjection $p:P\to E$ with $P$ nuclearly free and $up$ $A$-nuclear. Its closed kernel is transverse to $B$ by the transversality calculus, so $P_ B\to E_ B$ is an open surjection. Write $P=A\widehat\otimes M$. For an equicontinuous family $\xi _ j:P\to A$, the formulas

\[ \xi _{j,B}(b\otimes m)=b\rho (\xi _ j(1\otimes m)) \]

form an equicontinuous family $B\widehat\otimes M\to B$. Indeed each target seminorm is bounded using continuity of multiplication by one product of a $B$-seminorm and a common $M$-seminorm; the latter comes from equicontinuity of the original family. The bounded vectors of the nuclear series remain bounded under $F\to F_ B$. The same series is thus $B$-nuclear on $P_ B$, and proves the claim. This argument also gives the nuclear series on a free base change to an arbitrary Banach algebra $B$, whenever the target free base change is being used. $\square$

Lemma 9.46.3.5C1AB5BDC27C Let $B$ be a unital Banach algebra, $S$ a Fréchet $B$-module and $v:S\to S$ a $B$-nuclear map. Then $\operatorname {coker}(I-v)$ is finitely generated as an algebraic $B$-module. More strongly, for every continuous unital map $B\to C$ to a complete Fréchet algebra, the actual balanced quotient $S_ C=C\widehat\otimes _ B S$ satisfies

\[ \operatorname {coker}(I-v_ C)\text{ is finitely generated over }C. \]

Neither cokernel nor $S_ C$ is required Hausdorff in this assertion. Here the unbalanced tensors use the projective topology, and the balanced quotient uses the actual image.

Proof. Write $v(x)=\sum _ j\lambda _ j\xi _ j(x)y_ j$. The maps

\[ \alpha :S\to \ell ^1(B),\quad \alpha (x)=(\lambda _ j\xi _ j(x))_ j, \qquad \beta :\ell ^1(B)\to S,\quad \beta ((b_ j))=\sum _ jb_ jy_ j \]

are continuous $B$-linear. Equicontinuity bounds $\alpha $ by one source seminorm. For every seminorm $q$ of $S$, joint continuity of its action gives $q(by)\leq K_ q\| b\| r_ q(y)$ for a source seminorm $r_ q$; boundedness of $(y_ j)$ then proves convergence and continuity of $\beta $. We have $v=\beta \alpha $ and $w=\alpha \beta $ is $B$-nuclear on the Banach module $L=\ell ^1(B)$, with coefficient maps $\xi _ j\beta $ and vectors the standard unit vectors $e_ j$.

Split $w=w'+w''$ at a large finite index $N$. On the Banach space $L$, equicontinuity of the coefficient maps is a uniform operator norm bound, so $\| w''\| <1$ for large $N$. The Neumann series makes $T=I-w''$ a continuous $B$-linear automorphism with continuous inverse. Write $w'=J\Lambda $, where $J:B^ N\to L$ inserts the first $N$ vectors and $\Lambda :L\to B^ N$ is the finite list of coefficient maps including the $\lambda _ j$. The exact continuous identity

08CCD417EB8B 9.46.4
\begin{equation} I=(I-w)T^{-1}+J\Lambda T^{-1} \end{equation}

proves finite generation of its cokernel. The maps $\alpha ,\beta $ induce inverse algebraic cokernel isomorphisms for $I-v$ and $I-w$: they intertwine the two operators, and their compositions $v,w$ act as the identity modulo the respective actual images.

Retain this identity before passing to any cokernel. Every continuous $B$-linear map and each of its inverse identities induces a map on the actual balanced quotient with $C$. The free-factor formula gives $C\widehat\otimes _ B B^ N=C^ N$. Thus the induced identity is

\[ I=(I-w_ C)(T^{-1})_ C+J_ C\Lambda _ C(T^{-1})_ C. \]

For precision this equality is not inferred merely by continuity from a dense subset of a non-Hausdorff target. The original operator identity is first tensored as an identity on the Hausdorff unbalanced completion $C\widehat\otimes L$, where it holds by density. The factorization through $C^ N$ then follows from the exact balanced relation: for a continuous $B$-linear coefficient $\ell :L\to B$, the difference between $c\otimes \ell (x)e_ j$ and $c\rho (\ell (x))\otimes e_ j$ is the image of the completed relation map applied to $c\otimes \ell (x)\otimes e_ j$. This formula is an identity of continuous maps before quotienting. Hence the residual summand really has image in the module generated by the finitely many classes $[1\otimes e_ j]$. The induced $\alpha _ C,\beta _ C$ still intertwine and have the same composition identities, giving the cokernel isomorphism with $I-v_ C$. This proves the stronger assertion without tensoring a possibly non-Hausdorff cokernel. $\square$

A fully nuclear algebra is a unital Fréchet algebra $A$ whose topology is defined by increasing multiplicative seminorms $p_ t$, $0<t<1$, with nuclear Banach links $A_{p_{t'}}\to A_{p_ t}$ whenever $t<t'$. A countable cofinal set of indices defines the same topology. Let $A_ t$ be the Hausdorff completion for $\{ p_{\lambda t}:0<\lambda <1\} $, where $0<t\leq 1$; thus $A_1=A$. This is again fully nuclear, $(A_ t)_ s=A_{st}$, and $A_{t'}\to A_ t$ is complex-nuclear for $t<t'$. Indeed choose $t<u<u'<t'$ and factor that map through the nuclear Banach link $A_{p_{u'}}\to A_{p_ u}$; all maps to and from these completions are bounded by their indicated seminorms. A nuclear $A$-module is fully $A$-transverse if it is transverse to every $A_ t$. Put $E_ t=A_ t\widehat\otimes _ A E$. The transversality calculus and the free-resolution associativity show that $E_ t$ is fully $A_ t$-transverse and $(E_ t)_ s=E_{st}$. All these identifications are topological and use a Hausdorff intermediate quotient.

Theorem 9.46.5.741AFF17E7BD Let $A$ be fully nuclear, $E,F$ fully $A$-transverse nuclear modules, $f:E\to F$ an open surjective module map and $u:E\to F$ $A$-subnuclear. Then for every $t<1$

\[ \operatorname {coker}(f_ t-u_ t) \text{ is a finitely generated algebraic }A_ t\text{-module}. \]

No Hausdorffness of this cokernel is asserted.

Proof. Use the preceding free-cover construction to replace the source by a nuclearly free $P=A\widehat\otimes M$, so that $u$ becomes $A$-nuclear and $f$ is still onto. The kernel of the cover is fully transverse by applying (1) of the calculus theorem to every $A_ t$; hence the cover remains onto after each base change. Proving finite generation for the replaced source therefore proves it for the original map.

In an $A$-nuclear series for $u$ arrange $y_ j\to 0$. To do so choose $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$ slowly enough that $\sum _ j|\lambda _ j|/\varepsilon _ j<\infty $, replace $y_ j$ by $\varepsilon _ jy_ j$ and $\lambda _ j$ by $\lambda _ j/\varepsilon _ j$. Boundedness of the old vectors proves convergence of the new vectors to zero in every seminorm. Open surjectivity of $f$ gives lifts $x_ j\to 0$ with $f(x_ j)=y_ j$: a decreasing countable zero-neighborhood basis has open images, and successively late terms $y_ j$ can be lifted in successively small such neighborhoods. Then $v(x)=\sum _ j\lambda _ j\xi _ j(x)x_ j$ is an $A$-nuclear endomorphism of $P$ and $u=fv$. The kernel of $f:P\to F$ is fully transverse, so $f_ t$ remains onto. Thus the cokernel of $f_ t-u_ t=f_ t(I-v_ t)$ is an algebraic quotient of that of $I-v_ t$.

Fix $t<1$ and let $B=A_{p_ t}$, a Banach algebra. There is a continuous homomorphism $B\to A_ t$ because each $p_{\lambda t}\leq p_ t$. Free base change identifies $P_ B=B\widehat\otimes M$ and makes $v_ B$ $B$-nuclear: its coefficient maps are $b\otimes m\mapsto b\rho (\xi _ j(1\otimes m))$, uniformly bounded by a projective seminorm, and its vectors are the bounded images of $x_ j$. Apply the strengthened Banach perturbation lemma with $C=A_ t$. Its free-factor collapse identifies $A_ t\widehat\otimes _ B(B\widehat\otimes M) =A_ t\widehat\otimes M=P_ t$. Both iterated base changes of $v$ agree on elementary free tensors and hence on this Hausdorff completion. The lemma therefore proves finite generation of $\operatorname {coker}(I-v_ t)$ and then of the desired cokernel. At no point is a non-Hausdorff cokernel placed in the nuclear Fréchet tensor category. $\square$

Proposition 9.46.6.06B015B619A5 Let $X$ be a closed analytic subspace of an open $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, with any coherent defining ideal $\mathcal J$, and $D=D(a,R)\Subset \Omega $ a polydisc. Put $U=X\cap D$ and $U_ t=X\cap D(a,tR)$. The quotient seminorms on $A=\mathcal O(U)$ of $p_ t(f)=\sup _{\overline{D(a,tR)}}|f|$ make $A$ fully nuclear, and $A_ t=\mathcal O(U_ t)$ topologically. If $Y$ has a strict-PSH exhaustion, $V\Subset Y$ is a strict-PSH open and $\mathcal F$ is coherent on $X\times Y$, then $\mathcal F(U\times V)$ is fully $A$-transverse and its $t$th base change is $\mathcal F(U_ t\times V)$.

Proof. Coherent vanishing gives an open algebra quotient $\mathcal O(D)\to \mathcal O(U)$ with closed kernel $\mathcal J(D)$. Its quotient seminorms are multiplicative: take holomorphic lifts of two classes, multiply them and take the infimum of their norm products. They define its quotient Fréchet topology. For $t<t'$ insert one radius $s$ strictly between them. The two Banach polydisc restriction links at $t'>s>t$ in the direction from larger to smaller radii are nuclear by the explicit polydisc series. After projecting the target to its quotient by the closed ideal image, their composition vanishes on the closed source ideal image. The proved two-Banach-link quotient criterion in theorem 2.13.3 makes the descended map between the two quotient Banach completions nuclear. These completions are precisely those for the quotient seminorms. Thus every indicated link is nuclear.

For clarity about the intermediate completions, the restriction $\mathcal J(D)\to \mathcal J(D(a,tR))$ has dense image by the full coherent $1$-Runge theorem: concentric smaller polydiscs are $1$-Runge complete. For a compact $L$ in a smaller polydisc choose $r<s<t$ containing $L$ in the radius-$r$ polydisc. For large $m$, $\sum _ i|(z_ i-a_ i)/R_ i|^{2m}$ is less than $s^{2m}$ on $L$ and its $s^{2m}$-sublevel is inside the radius-$s$ polydisc. Add a sufficiently small positive multiple of a nonnegative strict-PSH exhaustion of $D$. This preserves the strict margin on $L$ and makes that sublevel relatively compact inside the radius-$t$ polydisc. The completion of $\mathcal O(D)$ for the seminorms with indices below $t$ is $\mathcal O(D(a,tR))$ by Taylor density. Its closed ideal is therefore the closure of the image of $\mathcal J(D)$. Taking the quotient commutes with this completion in this specific situation. Indeed each quotient seminorm is the infimum over that ideal; taking its closure does not change the infimum, and the closed quotient is Fréchet complete with the original quotient image dense. This proves the claimed completion identity, including nonreduced ideals and components missed by a smaller sublevel.

Choose a slightly larger concentric polydisc $D^+$ with $\overline D\subset D^+\Subset \Omega $; compactness of $\overline D$ inside $\Omega $ permits this enlargement. Then $X\cap D^+$ is a closed analytic subspace of a strict-PSH polydisc and inherits its exhaustion. Restrict $\mathcal F$ to this space times $Y$ and apply proposition 9.45.4 to $U_ t\subset U\Subset X\cap D^+$ for $t<1$. It gives all positive Tor vanishings, Hausdorffness and the claimed section identification. For $t=1$ it is the free identity $A\widehat\otimes _ A E=E$. This is full transversality in the stated nonreduced category. $\square$

Theorem 9.46.7.71936EFE5494 Let $A$ be fully nuclear and $E^\bullet ,F^\bullet $ bounded-above complexes of fully $A$-transverse nuclear modules. Suppose a continuous $A$-linear chain map $f:E\to F$ is subnuclear in each degree and induces cohomology isomorphisms in all degrees. For every $t<1$ there is a bounded-above complex $L^\bullet $ of finite-rank free $A_ t$-modules and a continuous chain map $h:L\to E_ t$ inducing all cohomology isomorphisms. No closedness of the original cohomology boundary images is required or asserted.

Proof. We first prove a top-degree finite-generation statement. Suppose $E,F$ are acyclic above degree $q$. For every $s<1$, $H^ q(E_ s)\simeq H^ q(F_ s)$ is finitely generated over $A_ s$. To see this choose $s<r<1$. Backward transversality induction shows that $Z^ j(E)$ and $Z^ j(F)$ are fully transverse for $j\geq q$: use the strict sequences $0\to Z^ j\to E^ j\to Z^{j+1}\to 0$, beginning above the last nonzero term. Their base changes identify $(Z^ j)_ r=Z^ j(E_ r)$ and the corresponding $F$ cycles. The quotient $F^ q/Z^ q(F)=Z^{q+1}(F)$ is fully transverse. The map $f^ q$ restricted to $Z^ q(E)$ is subnuclear into $F^ q$ and has image in $Z^ q(F)$. Since $A\to A_ r$ is complex-nuclear, the closed-range relative nuclearity proposition makes its induced map into $Z^ q(F_ r)$ $A_ r$-subnuclear. Base change preserves the quasi-isomorphism by the transversality calculus, applied in every degree. Hence

\[ d_ F^{q-1}\oplus f_ r^ q: F_ r^{q-1}\oplus Z^ q(E_ r)\longrightarrow Z^ q(F_ r) \]

is onto. Both source and target are fully $A_ r$-transverse, and $0\oplus f_ r^ q$ is $A_ r$-subnuclear. Apply the perturbation theorem with scale $s/r<1$. The perturbed map is $d_ F^{q-1}\oplus 0$, whose actual cokernel is $H^ q(F_ s)$. This proves the finite-generation statement, including when that cokernel is non-Hausdorff.

Choose $N$ with $E^ j=F^ j=0$ for $j>N$, and numbers $t<t_ q<t_{q+1}<1$ for $q\leq N+1$, tending to $t$ as $q\to -\infty $. For example take $t_ q=t+(1-t)2^{q-N-2}$. Start with no free terms in degrees at least $N+1$. Its corrected cone into $E$ is $E[-1]$ and is acyclic in degrees greater than $N+1$. Inductively suppose a finite tail $L^{\geq q}$ and a map into $E_{t_ q}$ have been constructed whose cone $M_ q$ is acyclic in degrees greater than $q$. Form also the cone $N_ q$ of its composite into $F_{t_ q}$. The map $M_ q\to N_ q$ is $I_ L\oplus f_{t_ q}$. Every term is fully $A_{t_ q}$-transverse. Its maps are $A_{t_ q}$-subnuclear: identity on a finite free module is a finite nuclear series, and the remaining summand is subnuclear by the free-cover base-change lemma. It is a quasi-isomorphism, by the natural long exact cone sequences and the quasi-isomorphism of $f_{t_ q}$; the elementary kernel/image chase in these two exact sequences gives injectivity and surjectivity of the middle cohomology map. Thus $N_ q$ too is acyclic above $q$. The just proved statement, applied over $A_{t_ q}$ and shrunk to $t_{q-1}$, makes $H^ q((M_ q)_{t_{q-1}})$ finitely generated.

Choose cycle representatives of finitely many module generators, defining a continuous $A_{t_{q-1}}$-linear map

\[ (a,b):L^{q-1}\longrightarrow L^ q_{t_{q-1}}\oplus E_{t_{q-1}}^{q-1} =(M_ q)_{t_{q-1}}^ q \]

from a finite free module. Being cycles says exactly $d_ La=0$ and $ha-d_ Eb=0$. Set the new differential $d_ L^{q-1}=a$ and the new chain-map component $h^{q-1}=b$. The new cone has the same cycles and boundaries above $q$; in degree $q$ its boundaries additionally include these representatives, so its cohomology is zero there. It is therefore acyclic above $q-1$, completing induction.

Finally base change every constructed finite tail to $A_ t$. The tails agree in their overlapping degrees by construction, and define a bounded-above complex of finite free modules and a chain map $h:L\to E_ t$. Every cone tail stays acyclic in its proved degree range by the transversality calculus, since its terms are fully transverse and $t<t_ q$. For any fixed degree $j$ choose a tail starting below $j-1$. Its cone agrees with the full cone in degrees $j-1,j,j+1$, so the full cone is acyclic in degree $j$. The corrected cone criterion implies $H^ j(h)$ bijective in every degree. Only these finite neighboring degrees were used; no inverse limit of cones, completion of a spectral page, or infinite total diagonal is involved. $\square$
