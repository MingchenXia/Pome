10.36 Smooth bundles, connections and holomorphic extensions684270C4A4BA

684270C4A4BA

Manifolds are Hausdorff and second countable. Write $\mathcal A^ k(E)=C^\infty (M,\Lambda ^ kT^*M\otimes E)$, with complex coefficients when $E$ is complex. Matrix products of forms include wedge product in the displayed order.

Definition 10.36.1.50F3CC97C433 A smooth vector bundle of rank $r$ over $M$, over $\mathbb K=\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$, is a smooth projection $\pi :E\to M$ with $\mathbb K$-vector-space fibers and local trivializations $\theta _ i:E|_{U_ i}\to U_ i\times \mathbb K^ r$ whose restrictions to the fibers are linear isomorphisms. A $C^ k$ section over $U$ is a $C^ k$ map $s:U\to E$ with $\pi \circ s=\operatorname {id}_ U$, where $0\leq k\leq \infty $. A frame is a tuple of sections forming a fiber basis at every point. A sheaf of modules is locally free of rank $r$ if it is locally isomorphic to the direct sum of $r$ copies of its ring sheaf. Ranks may vary between connected components, and rank zero is allowed.

See Proposition 10.93.1.

Proof. On a trivializing cover coefficient columns of a section satisfy $s_ i=g_{ij}s_ j$, where $g_{ij}g_{jk}=g_{ik}$ and the matrices are invertible and smooth, or holomorphic in the holomorphic case. Conversely glue $U_ i\times \mathbb K^ r$ by $(x,v)_ j\sim (x,g_{ij}(x)v)_ i$. The cocycle identity makes these identifications consistent, and each inverse image of $U_ i$ is exactly $U_ i\times \mathbb K^ r$. Points with different base points are separated on the base; points over the same base point are separated in such a product chart. A countable trivializing subcover gives a countable atlas. Thus the glued total space is a Hausdorff second-countable smooth or complex manifold with the required vector-bundle charts.

A morphism between free sheaf modules is a matrix of functions: its columns are the images of the constant basis sections, and linearity determines every other section. Applying this to local bases of a locally free module gives the preceding invertible transition matrices. The reconstructed bundle's section sheaf is the original module by these same local identifications. Morphisms glue by the same matrix rule, proving full faithfulness as well as existence. In particular sections are $C^ k$ exactly when their coefficient columns are $C^ k$. For the trivial bundle these are arbitrary $C^ k$ maps to $\mathbb K^ r$. In a coordinate chart $x:U\to \mathbb R^ m$, the tangent trivialization is $v\mapsto (\pi (v),dx(v))$. Its transitions are the coordinate Jacobians, and the chain rule is exactly their cocycle identity. Dual and exterior powers give the cotangent and differential-form bundles; their sections have the usual coordinate coefficients. In the holomorphic case the same constructions use holomorphic frames and give the existing sheaf $\mathcal O(E)$. $\square$

Definition 10.36.2.7346D78B2C78 A connection on $E$ is a $\mathbb K$-linear operator $D:\mathcal A^\bullet (E)\to \mathcal A^{\bullet +1}(E)$ satisfying

\[ D(\alpha \wedge s)=d\alpha \wedge s+(-1)^ p\alpha \wedge Ds \quad \text{for }\alpha \in \mathcal A^ p(M,\mathbb K). \]

Its covariant derivative along a real vector field $\xi $ is $D_\xi s=(Ds)(\xi )$ for sections $s$. Its curvature is the endomorphism-valued two-form representing $D^2$, as established below. A connection is flat if this curvature is zero.

Proposition 10.36.3.090E2091E078 Every smooth bundle has a connection; its connections form an affine space over $\mathcal A^1(\operatorname {End}E)$. A connection is locally $D=d+A\wedge $ and has curvature $F_ D=dA+A\wedge A$. It induces connections on sums, tensors, duals, Hom and exterior powers, and on smooth pullbacks. For a Hom-valued $k$-form $B:E\to F$ the induced differential is

EB79762053F3 10.36.4
\begin{equation} D_{\operatorname {Hom}(E,F)}B =dB+A_ F\wedge B-(-1)^ k B\wedge A_ E. \end{equation}

One has the Bianchi and covariant-derivative identities

\[ D_{\operatorname {End}E}F_ D=0,\qquad [D_\xi ,D_\eta ]-D_{[\xi ,\eta ]}=F_ D(\xi ,\eta ). \]

Proof. The Leibniz rule makes $D$ local. If a section vanishes near $x$, multiply it by a smooth cutoff supported in that neighborhood and equal to one near $x$; applying the rule shows $Ds(x)=0$. The graded Leibniz rule gives $D=d+A\wedge $ from its values on a local frame. If new columns are $\widetilde s=gs$, compatibility gives $\widetilde A=gAg^{-1}-(dg)g^{-1}$. Squaring and using $d(A\wedge s)=dA\wedge s-A\wedge ds$ gives $D^2s=F_ D\wedge s$. It follows either from this equality or the transformation law that $\widetilde F_ D=gF_ Dg^{-1}$. In rank one $A\wedge A=0$; $F_ D=dA$ is a globally defined closed scalar two-form. The matrix $A$ itself is defined only on the chosen frame domain.

For existence, take trivial connections $d_ i$ on a trivializing cover and a subordinate smooth partition $(\chi _ i)$, as in proposition 10.43.10. The locally finite sum $D=\sum _ i\chi _ i d_ i$, each summand extended by zero where necessary, is smooth and has the required Leibniz rule because $\sum _ i\chi _ i=1$. The difference of two connections is linear over smooth functions on sections, hence is an endomorphism-valued one-form. Its action in higher degrees is left wedge multiplication by that form, by the graded rule. Conversely adding any such form gives a connection.

The transition matrices for tensor, dual and exterior bundles are $g_ E\otimes g_ F$, $(g_ E^{-1})^{\mathsf T}$ and $\Lambda ^ kg_ E$. For a direct sum use block diagonal matrices and the componentwise connection. On tensor-valued forms the defining identity is $D(s\otimes t)=Ds\otimes t+(-1)^{\deg s}s\otimes Dt$, where the form factors are wedged in that order. The induced connection forms are respectively $A_ E\otimes I+I\otimes A_ F$, $-A_ E^{\mathsf T}$ and the derivation of $A_ E$ on the exterior factors. The local graded product rule verifies that they glue and uniquely determines them. Their curvatures are respectively

\[ F_{E\otimes F}=F_ E\otimes I+I\otimes F_ F, \qquad F_{E^*}=-F_ E^{\mathsf T},\qquad F_{\det E}=\operatorname {tr}F_ E. \]

The exterior curvature acts by the sum of its actions on each factor; there are no extra signs because curvature has even degree. Applying the product rule to $B s$ proves (10.36.4), and gives $F_{\operatorname {Hom}(E,F)}B=F_ FB-BF_ E$ for zero-form $B$.

For $F_ D=dA+A\wedge A$, direct expansion yields $dF_ D=dA\wedge A-A\wedge dA$ and $A\wedge F_ D-F_ D\wedge A=A\wedge dA-dA\wedge A$. This proves Bianchi by (10.36.4). Finally $D_\xi =\xi +A(\xi )$, so subtracting $D_{[\xi ,\eta ]}$ from the commutator gives

\[ \xi (A(\eta ))-\eta (A(\xi ))-A([\xi ,\eta ]) +[A(\xi ),A(\eta )]=F_ D(\xi ,\eta ), \]

with the indicated sign on the derivative term.

For a smooth map $f:N\to M$, the fiber product $f^*E=\{ (y,v):f(y)=\pi (v)\} $ has charts $(y,v)\mapsto (y,\operatorname {pr}_2\theta _ i(v))$ over $f^{-1}U_ i$. The first coordinate is $y$, not $f(y)$. Its transitions are $g_{ij}\circ f$ and its connection forms are $f^*A_ i$; the gauge rule verifies their compatibility. The pullback identities for $d$ and wedge products give $D_{f^*E}f^*s=f^*D_ Es$ and $F_{f^*E}=f^*F_ E$. No immersion assumption on $f$ is required. $\square$

Lemma 10.36.5.6DF73544DD2F A smooth connection has unique invertible parallel transport along piecewise smooth paths: a starting vector extends uniquely to a section $u$ along the path with $D_ tu=0$. Transport respects concatenation and reversal and depends smoothly on smooth finite-dimensional families of paths, piecewise on a fixed finite subdivision near each parameter value.

Proof. In a local frame the equation is $U'=-B(t,z)U$, $U(0,z)=I$, with

\[ B(t,z)=A_{\gamma _ z(t)}(\dot\gamma _ z(t)). \]

On a compact interval and compact parameter set the iterated integral series

\[ U(t,z)=I+\sum _{k\geq 1}(-1)^ k \int _{0<t_ k<\cdots <t_1<t} B(t_1,z)\cdots B(t_ k,z)\, dt_ k\cdots dt_1 \]

converges uniformly: the kth norm is at most $(Mt)^ k/k!$. It satisfies the integral equation and hence the differential equation. After any fixed number $a$ of parameter derivatives, the kth term is bounded by $C_ a^ k(1+k)^ a t^ k/k!$, after increasing $C_ a$ to bound all coefficient derivatives of order at most $a$. Thus those derivatives also converge uniformly. Time derivatives follow from the equation, proving smooth parameter dependence. The same construction solves $V'=VB$, $V(0)=I$; differentiating $VU$ proves $VU=I$. For uniqueness the difference of two solutions with zero initial value satisfies its homogeneous integral equation. Iterating it bounds the difference by $(Mt)^ k/k!$ times its bounded supremum, which tends to zero. Initial values enter linearly.

A compact path is subdivided into finitely many frame neighborhoods. Gauge transformations take the local equations to each other, so uniqueness glues the solutions. The same finite subdivision works near any fixed member of a smooth path family, proving the assertion for global families as well. Concatenation and reversal follow from uniqueness and the chain rule. The argument also works over the reals and on reversed time intervals. $\square$

Inner products on complex bundles are linear in the first argument. A smooth Hermitian metric is a smoothly varying positive definite Hermitian form on the fibers. A connection is metric-compatible when

\[ d\langle u,v\rangle =\langle Du,v\rangle +\langle u,Dv\rangle \]

for sections. On bundle-valued forms the pairing wedges the first coefficient with the conjugate of the second, and the corresponding rule has the sign $(-1)^{\deg u}$ before the second term. For line bundles this is the quadratic metric of quadratic Hermitian metrics on line bundles; its squared norms, rather than their square roots, occur in curvature weights.

Theorem 10.36.6.08655B144691 A smooth connection is flat exactly when it has parallel frames locally. For a flat connection, parallel transport depends only on the endpoint-preserving continuous homotopy class of a path. It has a parallel frame on every connected simply connected open set. On a connected base, rank-$r$ flat bundles with connection correspond to representations of $\pi _1(M,a)$ on $\mathbb K^ r$, up to conjugacy. A Hermitian bundle with a flat metric-compatible connection corresponds to a unitary representation, with unitary conjugacy for isometric connection isomorphisms.

Proof. In real dimension zero each component is a point and every assertion is immediate. To obtain local parallel frames from $F_ D=0$ otherwise, work in a coordinate box and solve $\partial _1G=-A_1G$, $G(0,x')=I$. The lemma makes $G$ smooth and invertible. Replace a frame $e$ by $eG$, so new coefficient columns are $G^{-1}$ times the old ones. In this new frame the first connection coefficient is zero. The $(1,j)$ flat-curvature equation then gives $\partial _1\widetilde A_ j=0$. On the hyperplane $x_1=0$ repeat in the remaining variables; the residual connection is flat there and the resulting frame change is independent of $x_1$. Induction makes every coefficient zero. Conversely a parallel frame has $A=0$ and therefore $F_ D=0$. Two parallel frames differ by a matrix $g$ satisfying $dg=0$, so their transition is locally constant.

In these frames transport extends even to continuous paths: keep the coefficient vector constant on each subpath in a frame neighborhood. Refining the finite subdivision changes nothing, because the overlap transitions are locally constant along that subpath. It agrees with ODE transport on piecewise smooth paths. For a continuous homotopy, subdivide its compact square sufficiently finely that each small rectangle maps into a single frame neighborhood. Transport around its boundary is the identity. Replace two consecutive edges of a rectangle by its other two edges and sweep through the grid. Each replacement preserves transport; interior backtracks cancel by reversal, and the fixed endpoint edges have identity transport. This proves invariance along the two paths. Paths can also be replaced by piecewise smooth ones by such coordinate subdivisions. On a simply connected open set, transport of one fiber basis is consequently independent of path and gives a smooth parallel frame, as is visible in the local parallel frames just constructed.

We give the covering construction to specify the representation convention. Multiply based loops by composition: $\gamma \delta $ means first $\delta $, then $\gamma $. The set $\widetilde M$ of endpoint-preserving homotopy classes of paths $p$ starting at $a$ is covered by endpoint charts over simply connected coordinate balls: append any path inside the ball. Such appended paths are equivalent exactly when their endpoints agree. The charts are compatible by locally constant changes of sheet. They make the endpoint map a covering; distinct points over one endpoint are separated by disjoint sheets. They give a smooth Hausdorff manifold. It is second countable: choose a countable coordinate-ball cover. Components of its pairwise intersections are open and hence at most countable; subdivision of paths shows that a sheet is specified by a finite word of these chart and intersection choices. Indeed, in each ball two intervening paths with the same endpoints contract, and moving a crossing point inside its intersection component does not change the sheet. There are countably many such words and endpoint charts. This cover is connected, since a defining path lifts from the identity sheet to its own class. A loop there projects to a null-homotopic loop: its class must be the identity because its lift closes at the identity sheet. Lifting a contracting homotopy, by subdivision into covering charts, contracts the original loop. Thus this is the universal cover, with the usual equivalent base points handled by conjugating paths.

There is a right action $p\cdot \gamma =p\circ \gamma $ of $\pi _1(M,a)$ on $\widetilde M$, free and transitive on every endpoint fiber. Set $\rho (\gamma )=T_\gamma $. The concatenation identity makes $\rho $ a representation. The map $(p,v)\mapsto T_ pv$ identifies $E$ with the quotient by the right action

\[ (p,v)\cdot \gamma =(p\circ \gamma ,\rho (\gamma )^{-1}v). \]

It is invariant by the transport rule. In each sheet chart it is a smooth fiberwise isomorphism, and its local frames are parallel. Conversely for any representation this quotient is a smooth vector bundle: the sheet charts have locally constant transition matrices. Their trivial connections glue, are flat, and have the prescribed monodromy. A connection isomorphism conjugates the representations by its value at $a$; conversely such a fiber intertwiner extends by transport to an isomorphism. These constructions are inverse.

For a metric-compatible connection and sections $u,v$ along a smooth path,

\[ \frac d{dt}h(u,v)=h(D_ tu,v)+h(u,D_ tv). \]

Thus parallel transport is isometric. The flat frames may be chosen orthonormal at one point and are then orthonormal everywhere, with unitary constant transitions. Conversely unitary transitions glue the fixed fiber metric and its trivial compatible connection. The quotient construction and its isomorphisms are then unitary exactly as asserted. The real Euclidean version has orthogonal transitions. $\square$

Over a complex manifold set $\mathcal A^{p,q}(E)=C^\infty (X,\Lambda ^{p,q}T^*X\otimes E)$. Projection onto types gives a unique decomposition $D=D'+D''$, where $D'$ raises $p$ and $D''$ raises $q$. Each satisfies the graded Leibniz rule with $d$ replaced by $\partial $ or $\bar\partial $. Conversely any two operators of these types and with these rules sum to a connection. Locally they are $\partial +A'\wedge $ and $\bar\partial +A''\wedge $, respectively.

Proposition 10.36.7.CCF7A9609341 For every smooth Hermitian bundle over a complex manifold and every prescribed $(0,1)$-connection $D''$, there is a unique compatible connection having that $(0,1)$ part. No integrability of $D''$ is required. On a real base a connection is compatible precisely when its one-form matrix in a unitary frame is skew Hermitian. Compatible connections exist, and their differences are exactly the skew-Hermitian endomorphism-valued one-forms.

Proof. Differentiating the inner product in a unitary frame gives compatibility exactly when $A+A^*=0$, where adjoint conjugates the one-form as well as transposing its matrix. This test on sections gives the full graded metric product rule on forms by the ordinary graded Leibniz rule. On a complex base write the prescribed part in such a frame as $\bar\partial +B$. The unique skew-adjoint matrix with $(0,1)$ part $B$ is $A=B-B^*$, its $(1,0)$ part being $-B^*$. This local construction glues: on an overlap the transformed compatible connection has the same prescribed $(0,1)$ part, and local uniqueness applies. This proves existence and uniqueness. Local unitary frames exist by smooth Gram–Schmidt. Smooth metrics exist by summing local Euclidean metrics with a smooth partition of unity, exactly as in the line case smooth line-bundle metrics and curvature changes. Over a real base the partition is supplied by proposition 10.43.10. Averaging the trivial connections in unitary frames by that partition gives a compatible connection, since their metric product rules sum with coefficients whose sum is one. The difference assertion follows from the unitary-frame test and the preceding affine-space description.

Compatibility also makes $F_ D$ skew Hermitian, by applying $d^2$ to the metric product rule. In rank one a unitary frame has imaginary $A$ and real $\mathrm iF_ D$. On a simply connected open set a unitary gauge $g$ has a smooth real phase $g=e^{\mathrm i\varphi }$: its local phases have the same closed differential $-\mathrm i g^{-1}dg$, whose path integrals are independent of path by the coordinate-square homotopy argument above. Adjust the additive constant to recover $g$. The gauge rule is then $A=\widetilde A+\mathrm i\, d\varphi $. For a holomorphic bundle the prescribed canonical coefficientwise $\bar\partial $ gives precisely its already-defined Chern connection. Its curvature has type $(1,1)$: the $(0,2)$ part is $(D'')^2=\bar\partial ^2=0$, and skew-adjointness makes the $(2,0)$ part zero as well. $\square$

Corollary 10.36.8.DD24E61268DB A Hermitian holomorphic bundle has zero Chern curvature if and only if it has orthonormal holomorphic frames locally. On a connected simply connected open set, zero Chern curvature gives a single such frame. On a connected manifold these bundles correspond to unitary representations of its fundamental group, up to unitary conjugacy for isometric holomorphic isomorphisms.

Proof. If the Chern curvature vanishes, the parallel frames of theorem 10.36.6 can be chosen orthonormal. Their sections are holomorphic because their Chern $(0,1)$ derivative is zero. Conversely the trivial connection in an orthonormal holomorphic frame is compatible and has canonical $(0,1)$ part; uniqueness in proposition 10.36.7 makes it the Chern connection, so its curvature vanishes. An isometric holomorphic isomorphism preserves that unique connection. Conversely unitary locally constant transition matrices are holomorphic and give this Chern connection. The representation statement therefore follows from the same flat-bundle correspondence. $\square$

Remark 10.36.9.597030C175B7 For a holomorphic bundle $E$, the canonical coefficientwise $\bar\partial $ is independent of holomorphic frame, since its transition matrices are holomorphic. It squares to zero. Its cohomology on global $(p,q)$-forms is denoted $H^{p,q}(X,E)$. Write $\Omega _ X^ p$ for the holomorphic bundle $\Lambda ^ pT^{1,0*}X$ or its sheaf of holomorphic sections, according to context. The existing differential-resolution comparison corollary 10.43.11 is precisely

\[ H^{p,q}(X,E)=H^ q(X,\Omega _ X^ p\otimes \mathcal O(E)); \]

in particular $H^{p,0}(X,E)$ is the space of holomorphic sections of $\Lambda ^ pT^{1,0*}X\otimes E$. This notation does not impose a metric, positivity or compactness condition.

Theorem 10.36.10.B7A8F8D8A536 Let $L$ be a smooth complex line bundle on an oriented real manifold $M$, and let $s$ be a smooth section transverse to the zero section. Then $Z=s^{-1}(0)$ is a closed smooth submanifold of real codimension two, with its normal orientation induced by the complex line $L$. Orient $Z$ so that the oriented normal plane followed by $TZ$ gives the orientation of $TM$. For any connection $D$ on $L$, the one-form $u=s^{-1}Ds$ off $Z$ extends with locally integrable coefficients, and as complex currents on $M$

3E0F17FBE3F5 10.36.11
\begin{equation} du=2\pi \mathrm i[Z]+F_ D. \end{equation}

If $D$ is compatible with a smooth Hermitian metric, the real locally integrable form $\alpha =(2\pi )^{-1}\operatorname {Im}u$ satisfies

\[ d\alpha =[Z]-\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }F_ D. \]

Consequently $[Z]$ represents the real image of $c_1(L)\in H^2(M,\mathbb Z)$ under the de Rham comparison. No compactness is required.

Proof. Write $s=fe$ in a smooth complex frame, and $D=d+A$ there. At a zero, $Ds=df\otimes e$, independently of the connection. Transversality says that $(\operatorname {Re}f,\operatorname {Im}f)$ has rank two there. The real inverse function theorem, applied after completing these two differentials to a basis, gives local coordinates $(x,y,t)$ with $f=x+\mathrm iy$. The orientation rule makes $dx\wedge dy$ followed by the orientation of the $t$ coordinates the positive orientation of $M$; in dimension two the latter factor is the signed orientation of a point. A change of frame multiplies the normal derivative by a nonzero complex scalar at a zero, whose real determinant is positive; hence this normal and induced orientation is intrinsic. These charts also prove that $Z$ is a closed embedded submanifold and its integration current has locally finite mass.

On the complement of $Z$,

\[ u=\frac{df}{f}+A. \]

In the coordinates just chosen, $df/f=(dx+\mathrm i\, dy)/(x+\mathrm iy)$ has coefficients bounded by a constant times $(x^2+y^2)^{-1/2}$. They are locally integrable in the two normal variables, hence also in all variables by Fubini. Thus $u$ defines an intrinsic locally integrable current: its expressions agree off $Z$, and $Z$ has Lebesgue measure zero.

We check the distributional derivative, including its orientation. On the plane let $z=x+\mathrm iy$ and let $\varphi $ be a smooth compactly supported test function. With the convention $T_\eta (\psi )=\int \eta \wedge \psi $, the derivative of a one-current satisfies $dT_\eta (\varphi )=T_\eta (d\varphi )$. On a punctured disc containing the support, $d(dz/z)=0$ classically, so Stokes' formula Stokes' formula on compact domains with corners gives

\[ \left\langle d\left(\frac{dz}{z}\right),\varphi \right\rangle =\lim _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \int _{|z|>\varepsilon }\frac{dz}{z}\wedge d\varphi =\lim _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \int _{|z|=\varepsilon }^{\rm counterclockwise}\varphi \frac{dz}{z} =2\pi \mathrm i\, \varphi (0). \]

The boundary sign is positive because the inner boundary of the punctured disc is clockwise, whereas $d(\varphi \, dz/z)=-dz/z\wedge d\varphi $. The last equality follows by setting $z=\varepsilon e^{\mathrm i\theta }$; the integrand converges uniformly to $\mathrm i\varphi (0)\, d\theta $. The first limit is justified by the local integrability just proved.

In the product coordinates $(x,y,t)$ the coefficients are independent of $t$, so all their distributional $t$ derivatives vanish, by integration by parts in those variables. Applying the plane identity to the coefficient of a test form containing only the $t$ differentials and using Fubini gives $d(df/f)=2\pi \mathrm i[\{ x=y=0\} ]$, with exactly the specified orientation: if the coordinate orientation differs from that of $M$, both this integral and the induced integral over $Z$ acquire the same sign. All other components of a test form contain $dx$ or $dy$ and contribute zero. Since $F_ D=dA$ for a line connection, this proves (10.36.11) near $Z$; off $Z$ it is the ordinary identity $du=dA$. Equality of currents is local, so the formula holds on all of $M$.

Choose a compatible connection, which exists by proposition 10.36.7. Its scalar curvature is imaginary. Taking imaginary parts and dividing by $2\pi $ gives the formula for $d\alpha $, because $\operatorname {Im}F_ D=-\mathrm iF_ D$. The current and smooth-form de Rham comparison of corollary 10.43.11, and the integral comparison theorem 10.40.5, give the asserted class. If $Z$ is empty, $u$ is smooth globally and the same proof applies. In real dimension less than two transversality forces $Z$ to be empty, and all two-forms and two-currents vanish, so the statement still holds. $\square$

Proposition 10.36.12.84111D436822 For a holomorphic exact sequence $0\to S\xrightarrow jE \xrightarrow gQ\to 0$, give $S,Q$ the induced and quotient metrics. Use the smooth orthogonal identification $E=S\oplus Q$. Let $\beta \in \mathcal A^{1,0}(\operatorname {Hom}(S,Q))$ be the second fundamental form, so that the established block connection is $D_ E=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}D_ S& -\beta ^*\\ \beta & D_ Q\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Put $b=\beta ^*$. The full map derivative identities are

\[ \begin{array}{ll} D'j=g^*\beta ,& \bar\partial j=0,\\ D'g=-\beta j^*,& \bar\partial g=0,\\ D'j^*=0,& \bar\partial j^*=b g,\\ D'g^*=0,& \bar\partial g^*=-j b. \end{array} \]

Here each operator is the induced Hom connection. Moreover $\bar\partial _{\operatorname {Hom}(Q,S)}b=0$ and

\[ F_ E=\begin{pmatrix} F_ S-b\wedge \beta & -D'_{\operatorname {Hom}(Q,S)}b \\ \bar\partial _{\operatorname {Hom}(S,Q)}\beta & F_ Q-\beta \wedge b \end{pmatrix}. \]

The orthogonal splitting is holomorphic exactly when $\beta =0$.

Proof. The block connection and diagonal curvature identities are those already proved in proposition 10.34.9; their proof imposes no positivity condition. Here $j$ and $g$ and their adjoints are the constant inclusion and projection matrices. Apply (10.36.4) in degree zero to each. The four results are respectively $(0,\beta )^{\mathsf T}$, $(-\beta ,0)$, $(0,b)$ and $(-b,0)^{\mathsf T}$, proving all types and signs displayed above. Squaring the block connection gives upper-right entry $-D_{\operatorname {Hom}(Q,S)}b$ and lower-left entry $D_{\operatorname {Hom}(S,Q)}\beta $ by the degree-one formula. Chern curvature has type $(1,1)$; its $(0,2)$ upper-right part forces $\bar\partial b=0$, and its $(2,0)$ lower-left part forces $D'\beta =0$. This proves the matrix, including the minus sign in its upper-right entry. Finally $\bar\partial g^*=-jb$ vanishes exactly when $b=0$, since $j$ is injective. This is exactly when the orthogonal right inverse is holomorphic. $\square$

Theorem 10.36.13.304DF496E0BF With $S,Q$ fixed, isomorphism classes of holomorphic extension diagrams $0\to S\to E\to Q\to 0$, with identity maps on their ends, are in bijection with $H^{0,1}_{\bar\partial }(X,\operatorname {Hom}(Q,S))$, and hence with $H^1(X,\mathcal O(\operatorname {Hom}(Q,S)))$. The class is $[\beta ^*]$. It is independent of all metrics and vanishes exactly for a holomorphically split extension.

Proof. A smooth right inverse $h$ exists by orthogonal splitting. For the orthogonal one the preceding proposition says $\bar\partial h=-jb$, with $b=\beta ^*$ closed. For any smooth right inverse define $b=-j^{-1}\bar\partial h$: the expression lies in $S$ because $g\bar\partial h=\bar\partial (gh)=0$. Any other right inverse is $h+jv$, with a global smooth $v:Q\to S$, so its form is $b-\bar\partial v$. This proves independence of splitting and metric. Transporting a splitting under an extension isomorphism proves invariance as well.

In the splitting coordinates the Dolbeault operator is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\bar\partial _ S& -b\\ 0& \bar\partial _ Q\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. A smooth extension isomorphism inducing the identity on the ends has the form $\Psi =\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I& v\\ 0& I\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. From the coefficient product rule it is holomorphic from $E_ b$ to $E_ c$ precisely when

\[ \bar\partial v-c+b=0. \]

Thus equal Dolbeault classes give isomorphic diagrams, and a split diagram has class zero. This also proves the converse splitting claim.

For surjectivity take any closed smooth $b$. On the smooth bundle $S\oplus Q$ use the displayed operator. It squares to zero because $\bar\partial b=0$. More concretely, in local holomorphic frames the local complex exactness local Dolbeault exactness for forms and currents gives $b=\bar\partial v_ i$. The matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I& -v_ i\\ 0& I\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ intertwines this operator with the usual one on the local split bundle, by the same calculation. On overlaps these trivializations differ by holomorphic upper-triangular matrices, since $\bar\partial (v_ i-v_ j)=0$. They therefore define a holomorphic structure, with holomorphic inclusion and projection, and with the prescribed class $[b]$. This is an explicit triangular construction; no general integrable-connection theorem is needed. The final sheaf identification is corollary 10.43.11. Zero ranks give the unique trivial extension and zero cohomology. $\square$

Proposition 10.36.14.732A25F31FC3 Extension diagrams with fixed end bundles $S,Q$, allowing arbitrary holomorphic automorphisms on both ends, are classified by the orbits

\[ H^1(X,\mathcal O(\operatorname {Hom}(Q,S))) /(\operatorname {Aut}(S)\times \operatorname {Aut}(Q)), \qquad (u,v)[b]=[u b v^{-1}]. \]

This gives a surjection onto the isomorphism classes of their abstract middle bundles, which need not be injective. For line bundles on a compact connected complex manifold the diagram orbit set is $\{ 0\} \sqcup \mathbb P(H^1(X,\mathcal O(\operatorname {Hom}(Q,S))))$, where the first element is the split diagram.

Proof. A diagram map with end automorphisms $u,v$ has smooth block matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u& w\\ 0& v\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. It is holomorphic exactly when $\bar\partial w-cv+ub=0$. Since $u,v$ are holomorphic and invertible, this is equivalent to $[c]=[ubv^{-1}]$, and the equation constructs the isomorphism whenever that class equality holds. Forgetting the inclusion and projection gives the asserted surjection. For line bundles each end automorphism is a nowhere vanishing holomorphic function; on a compact connected manifold it is a nonzero constant by the local maximum principle. Its action is scalar multiplication, whose orbits are zero and the projective lines of nonzero classes. If the cohomology vanishes its projective space is empty.

Here is a counterexample to injectivity after forgetting the diagram. On $\mathbb P^1$ let $S=\mathcal O(-2)$ and use the two maps into $E=\mathcal O^2$ given in homogeneous coordinates by

\[ j_1=(x^2,y^2)^{\mathsf T},\qquad j_2=(x^2,y^2+xy)^{\mathsf T}. \]

Each pair has no common zero and hence is a subbundle inclusion: locally one entry is a unit, and an invertible two-by-two matrix completes it to a frame. Each quotient is a line bundle with $Q\simeq \det E\otimes S^{-1}\simeq \mathcal O(2)$; this determinant isomorphism follows locally by wedging a frame of $S$ with any quotient lift. Choose such an identification for each quotient. Every automorphism of $\mathcal O^2$ is a constant invertible matrix, since its entries are global holomorphic functions on $\mathbb P^1$. It and a scalar automorphism of $S$ preserve the span of the two quadratic components of an injection. But

\[ \operatorname {span}\{ x^2,y^2\} \ne \operatorname {span}\{ x^2,y^2+xy\} , \]

by independence of the homogeneous monomials. Thus the diagrams lie in distinct diagram orbits while their middle bundles are identical. Both are nonsplit: a right inverse from $\mathcal O(2)$ to $\mathcal O^2$ would have components in $H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-2))=0$, by proposition 10.51.5. This counterexample also concerns nonzero extension classes. $\square$
