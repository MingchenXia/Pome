25.3 Nonlinear products, capacity, and mass

25.3 Isolated singularities and residual massE66D40EC5904

Residual Monge–Ampère mass is the atom retained at an isolated pole by the classical product. The zero-mass question of Guedj and Rashkovskii asks whether this atom must vanish when the ordinary Lelong number is zero. Li–Xia [LiXia2026ZeroMass] give explicit counterexamples in every complex dimension at least two: the pole is isolated, the exponential of the potential is continuous, the Lelong number vanishes, and the entire classical Monge–Ampère measure is a unit atom. The higher-dimensional construction uses the separate-variable maximum-product identity of Błocki [Blo00]. The non-pluripolar product removes the residual atom.

Quantitative control of radial growth gives sufficient conditions for zero residual mass. Deng–Li–Liu–Wang–Zhou [DLLWZ26] introduce the logarithmic truncation threshold. For isolated poles, finite threshold together with zero Lelong number forces the residual mass to vanish; mixed residual masses and Crofton's formula give a direct proof of this consequence. The uniformly directional Lipschitz condition considered by He–Li–Xu [HLX25] bounds the logarithmic radial derivative and implies a finite threshold. The Li–Xia potential therefore has infinite threshold and fails this radial regularity. Its failure of circular invariance can also be seen by evaluating the explicit iterates at two points on the same circle.
