25.2 Plurisubharmonicity, currents, and singularities

25.2 Currents, singularities and analytic multiplicityE49C1A0D4682

Demailly's Complex Analytic and Differential Geometry [Demailly2012] develops real and complex currents alongside plurisubharmonic functions. Regularization and homotopy relate currents to smooth differential forms, while positivity provides measure coefficients and local mass bounds. Extension across complete pluripolar sets and integration over analytic sets connect this calculus to complex geometry. Lelong numbers record the local concentration of positive closed currents; slicing, generic multiplicity and Siu decomposition relate those densities to analytic cycles.

The generalized Lelong–Jensen theory uses plurisubharmonic weights to measure concentration and construct boundary measures. Demailly's memoir [Demailly1985] and his work on Monge–Ampère and pluriharmonic measures [Demailly1987] are central sources for this development. The admissibility of a weight and properness on the relevant current support remain part of each comparison statement.

25.3 Nonlinear products, capacity, and mass

25.3 Classical products and mass at polesE66D40EC5904

The Bedford–Taylor calculus for locally bounded potentials is the starting point for the classical unbounded products treated in Demailly's exposition [Demailly2012]. Compactness or dimension conditions on the unbounded loci permit further products by controlling their mass. Such classical products can retain mass on a pole set. They therefore differ from the non-pluripolar construction, which removes that mass. Weighted boundary measures and Jensen identities belong to the same classical current framework [Demailly1985, Demailly1987]; their normalizations and pole terms are retained in the statements.

25.4 Positivity, Hermitian geometry, and volume

25.4 Hermitian operators, Hodge theory and projectivityF9B38BEB0BCF

Hermitian linear algebra, Chern connections and elliptic estimates support the Hodge and vanishing theory in Demailly's book [Demailly2012]. The Frölicher spectral sequence compares Dolbeault and de Rham cohomology on general complex manifolds. Bott–Chern and Aeppli groups retain further information, while the Kähler identities impose stronger relations. The Bochner–Kodaira identity and its torsion terms distinguish the Hermitian calculation from its Kähler specialization.

The $L^2$ method of Hörmander, holomorphic division and compact vanishing theorems connect curvature to global sections and jets. Kodaira's embedding theorem and its integral-curvature formulation then relate positivity to projectivity. Unique continuation is a separate analytic ingredient: Aronszajn's theorem [Aronszajn1957] is a classical source for scalar-principal second-order equations and coupled lower-order inequalities. Its hypotheses do not assert unrestricted unique continuation for every matrix-principal elliptic system.

Pliś's smooth elliptic counterexample [Plis1961SmoothElliptic] provides a concrete obstruction. Its fourth-order two-variable construction can be prolonged to an ordinary second-order elliptic system with a nonzero smooth solution vanishing on an open set. This separates general matrix-principal ellipticity from the scalar-principal hypotheses used for harmonic forms.

The spectral proof of holomorphic Morse inequalities uses a different asymptotic from the transcendental subtraction of nef classes. Demailly's magnetic-field method [Demailly1985MagneticMorse] counts low Dolbeault eigenvalues and gives weak and strong line-power cohomology inequalities with a fixed coefficient bundle on compact Hermitian manifolds. Its semipositive specialization yields the leading holomorphic-section growth without assuming a Kähler base.

Siu's work on semipositive line bundles over non-Kähler manifolds [Siu1984Semipositive] includes the one-point positivity variant in an added-in-proof announcement. Its geometric application joins section growth to Kähler modifications and coefficient-valued cohomological descent.

Hopf quotients illustrate the topological restrictions imposed by the Kähler condition. The normal-coordinate curvature expansion and Wirtinger's inequality connect the local Hermitian metric to curvature and complex-volume geometry in the exposition [Demailly2012].

25.5 Cohomology, compact curves, and complex tori

25.5 Cohomology, orientation and compact curves62EE456A4F3B

Sheaf resolutions, supported products and spectral sequences organize the cohomological part of Demailly's exposition [Demailly2012]. The de Rham comparison connects this language to differential forms. Integral orientation and cap duality require local coefficient systems and retain torsion information which real differential forms do not detect. Hatcher's Algebraic Topology [Hatcher2002] supplies a complementary account of the integral singular-topological theory. Compact-support duality, ordinary duality and their coefficient hypotheses are distinguished throughout these comparisons. Nuclear Fréchet tensor products and analytic direct images form a separate topological development: closed-range cohomology, actual balanced quotients and algebraic Tor groups keep their respective hypotheses.

For compact complex curves the analytic and topological theories meet in Riemann–Roch, Serre duality, periods and Jacobians. Picard and Albanese tori extend the period construction, while polarizations relate integral forms to projective embeddings. These topics are presented together in [Demailly2012]; their organization here follows the common cohomological and geometric structures.

25.9 Sections, convex bodies, and non-Archimedean geometry

25.9 Entire-function growth and arithmetic zero sets482A6169B3A6

Weighted Jensen formulas also lead to Schwarz-type bounds for entire functions vanishing on a finite set. Demailly's exposition [Demailly2012] relates polynomial degree, singular degree and weighted multiplicity in these inequalities. Waldschmidt's account [Waldschmidt1979] develops the connection with transcendence and algebraic groups, including the analytic method behind Bombieri's arithmetic zero-set theorem. A coarse degree bound, the optimal asymptotic Schwarz coefficient and an exceptional-locus refinement are different assertions. Star configurations of hyperplanes realize the proposed degree bound with equality in every dimension. This special case leaves the general historical conjectures separate from the proved estimates.

25.10 Singular spaces, traces, and locally conformally Kähler geometry

25.10 Complex spaces, coherent sheaves and convexityF14EBE94C297

The local geometry of analytic sets, normalization and modifications provides a common setting for extension and coherent-sheaf methods. Demailly's book [Demailly2012] joins these geometric constructions to Cartan's cohomology theorems, proper direct images and convexity estimates. Nilpotent structure requires the full quotient structure sheaf; the underlying zero set alone does not determine a complex space. Affine algebraicity also involves growth at infinity, whereas local analytic parametrization concerns a neighborhood of one point.

Projective models and Kähler modifications connect meromorphic maps to compact geometry. Demailly's author-hosted Analytic Methods in Algebraic Geometry [DemAnalyticOnline] is the specified reference for the elimination existence input. That existence theorem remains an explicit external input; reconstructing the surrounding analytic and descent arguments does not itself provide a proof of elimination.

Bounded strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustions on smoothly bounded pseudoconvex domains are the subject of Diederich–Fornæss [DiederichFornaess1977]. Their exponent and boundary hypotheses accompany the exhaustion estimate. This work is distinct from the later smooth nonpluripolar-curve example. Resolution and principalization remain explicit geometric inputs where they are used; passing to a smooth model still leaves the analytic extension and descent arguments to establish.
