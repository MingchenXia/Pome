25.3 Nonlinear products, capacity, and mass

25.3 Positive products and analytic approximationE66D40EC5904

Analytic Fujita approximation connects non-pluripolar masses with intersection theory on modifications. Boucksom's volume theory [Bou02b] and the work of Boucksom–Demailly–Păun–Peternell [BDPP13] develop this connection through positive currents and approximate Zariski decompositions. The movable part is represented by Kähler or nef classes upstairs, while an effective divisor records the remaining singularities. Controlling top volume and lower mixed products on the same approximating models is what makes the construction useful for variation formulas. Comparison theorems identify the maximal non-pluripolar mass with numerical approximation and with the current volume defined by the absolutely continuous determinant.

The algebraic construction of positive products by Boucksom–Favre–Jonsson [BFJ09] gives a direct approach to varying volume. A uniform quadratic lower error near a nef approximant, applied again with the endpoints exchanged, identifies the differential. This argument separates class-space variation from continuity for arbitrary weakly converging currents. Boucksom's appendix to Witt Nyström's projective duality theorem [WittNystrom2019Duality] relates orthogonality, differentiability and cone duality in the analytic setting. These developments complement the non-pluripolar mass comparisons and singularity-type continuity discussed above.

25.4 Positivity, Hermitian geometry, and volume

25.4 Volumes and dual conesF9B38BEB0BCF

Boucksom's divisorial Zariski decomposition [Bou04] distinguishes the divisorial negative part of a pseudoeffective class from its movable positivity. Orthogonality and the derivative of volume express this structure numerically. The differentiability formula of Boucksom–Favre–Jonsson [BFJ09], and its transcendental extension through Witt Nyström and Boucksom [WittNystrom2019Duality], identify the gradient with the positive product of degree one less than the dimension. Cao–Tosatti [CaoTosatti2026VolumeRegularity] establish the sharp local $C^{1,1}$ regularity once this formula is available. Their elementary cone estimate uses homogeneity and monotonicity, so the local Lipschitz bound for the gradient need not be deduced from a concavity theorem. The volume extends by zero across the non-big locus; its regularity there is a separate assertion from differentiability inside the big cone.

For nef transcendental classes on compact Kähler manifolds, Tosatti's sharp Morse inequality [Tosatti2026Morse] supplies the optimal subtraction bound for volume. Its current-concentration and separation argument extends the projective numerical picture to the full real $(1,1)$-space. Together with simultaneous analytic approximation, the inequality yields volume differentiation, orthogonality, and duality of the pseudoeffective and movable cones. The separating classes may already be taken as pushforwards of single Kähler powers from modifications. The spectral holomorphic Morse inequalities discussed earlier count a different cohomological asymptotic.

Restricted volumes record positivity along a specified analytic subvariety. Collins–Tosatti [CT22] develop their numerical analytic formulation and its relation to non-Kähler loci. Witt Nyström's deformation to the normal bundle [WittNystrom2024Restricted] and Vu's work on divisorial derivatives [Vu2025VolumeDerivative] connect these invariants with the first variation of volume. The formulation must retain the distinction between ordinary restriction outside the non-Kähler locus and the numerical extension to a subvariety in that locus. Birational reduction and the contribution of divisorial negative parts are part of that comparison.

Balanced metrics provide a smooth representative for another face of the same duality. Toma [Toma2010Mobile] establishes the relevant projective cone comparison, and Fu–Xiao [FuXiao2014Balanced] develop the compact Kähler version. Their separation arguments test closed strictly positive $(n-1,n-1)$-forms against positive $\partial \bar\partial $-closed currents. The behavior of these currents under modifications is studied by Alessandrini–Bassanelli [AlessandriniBassanelli1995Balanced, AlessandriniBassanelli2003Transforms]. Lifting these currents under a modification requires an additional theorem: $\partial \bar\partial $-closedness alone does not provide a local plurisubharmonic potential for pullback. This analytic input enters the identification of the closure of the balanced cone with the movable cone.
