25.4 Positivity, Hermitian geometry, and volume

25.4 History of relative positivity and foliationsF9B38BEB0BCF

The relative canonical construction, compact-center propagation and Kähler foliation applications follow Cao and Păun [CaoPaun2025]. Ou's work [Ou2025] supplies the restricted concentration mechanism and movable-slope context. The local reconstructions here develop the smooth-center Lelong bound, flat-twist section envelope, anisotropic order estimate, compact-center potential and uniform complex-submanifold mean-value argument.

The analytic variation theory comes from Berndtsson and Păun [BP2008] and Păun and Takayama [PT2014]. Hacon, Popa and Schnell [HPS2017] supply the multiplier-ideal direct-image formulation. The projective-fiber section argument is related to [Lemma 4.4, CCP2021]; the singular-metric vanishing and diagonal ideal calculations use the analytic framework of Demailly [DemAnalyticOnline]. The local-projectivity deduction uses Grauert's base-change and Euler-characteristic theorems [Grauert1960], read with the author's correction [Grauert1963].

The projective slope and rational-quotient arguments come from Campana and Păun [CP19]. Tensor semistability uses Toma's appendix to [CP11]. Campana's cycle-space constructions [Campana2004], the section theorem of Graber, Harris and Starr [GHS2001], and projective canonical pseudo-effectivity [BDPP2012] provide the geometric inputs. Fujiki's deformation theorem [Fujiki1981] yields the final family application. The direct rational Hodge choice and the comparison of saturated differential images explain the reductions used in this exposition.

These attributions identify the origins of the arguments and their stated external inputs. Exact consulted versions are distinguished in the bibliography; no equivalence with an unread later edition is intended.
